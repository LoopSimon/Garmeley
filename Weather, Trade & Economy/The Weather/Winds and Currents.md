<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Winds_and_Currents.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> <small><i>The winds and ocean currents of the world</i></small> </div>  <p>The winds and ocean currents of the <a href="Globus" class="internal-link">world</a> follow a pattern that seafarers have learned over centuries of voyages and losses. The world turns from west to east, its air is dense, and a steep tilt gives it strong seasons. Together these set the great belts of wind that circle the globe, the winds drive the great loops of the oceans, and between them they decide where rain falls, where fog lies, where deserts spread and which way a ship can sail.</p> <p>The air of the world is heavy, and a wind of any given speed strikes harder than its speed suggests. Gales tear sails and rigging more readily, waves build higher, and storms draw more strength from warm seas than their size alone would explain.</p> <h2>The Wind Belts</h2> <p>Six belts of wind circle the world, three in each hemisphere, mirroring one another across the equator. Their boundaries are not walls but broad zones, and they shift north and south with the seasons.</p> <ul> <li><strong>The Doldrums</strong> lie along about 3°N on average, where the trade winds of the two hemispheres meet. Here the air rises in towering clouds, and the sea knows long calms broken by sudden squalls and heavy rain. In the Tirean Ocean sailors call this belt the Tempest, and crossing it is slow and unpredictable.</li> <li><strong>The Trade Winds</strong> blow from the doldrums to about 26° in each hemisphere, from the north-east in the north and from the south-east in the south. They are the steadiest winds in the world, and a ship running west before them can hold the same course for weeks.</li> <li><strong>The Calm Latitudes</strong> lie at about 26° north and south, where air sinking from above spreads out over the sea. Winds here are light and fickle, and a ship may lie becalmed for weeks.</li> <li><strong>The Westerlies</strong> blow between about 26° and 56°, from the south-west in the north and from the north-west in the south. They are strong and stormy. In the south, where no land stands in their way, they are the strongest winds in the world.</li> <li><strong>The Polar Front</strong> runs at about 56° in each hemisphere, where cold polar air meets the milder air of the westerlies. It is a line of frequent gales.</li> <li><strong>The Polar Easterlies</strong> blow cold and gusty from the polar front to the poles, from the north-east in the north and the south-east in the south, and bring sea ice in winter.</li> </ul> <h2>The Turning Seasons</h2> <p>The world is tilted by 30°, and its year of 400 days is divided into four seasons of about a hundred days each. In the present age the southern hemisphere has the harsher seasons, with hotter summers and colder winters than the north.</p> <p>All the wind belts follow the Sun. In the northern summer they shift north, and in the southern summer they shift south. Over the open Tirean and Odian oceans the doldrums move from about 3–4°N in the average year to 9–11°N at the height of the northern summer and to 6–15°S at the height of the southern summer. Around Behera and the Sapphire Sea the doldrums lie south of the equator for most of the year, at about 2–4°S.</p> <p>Over land the swing is far greater. Each northern summer the rain belt is drawn deep into <a href="Nirydia" class="internal-link">Nirydia</a>, as far as 20–25°N, and pulls the winds of the surrounding seas in after it. This is the engine of the monsoons.</p> <h2>The Monsoon Seas</h2> <p>In the seas around <a href="Behera" class="internal-link">Behera</a> and <a href="Nirydia" class="internal-link">Nirydia</a> the winds and coastal currents reverse every half-year. Between the two monsoons come a few weeks of shifting, uncertain winds, when ships wait in port for the new wind to settle.</p> <h3>The Niryd and Sapphire Monsoon</h3> <p>Over the Niryd Ocean, the Sapphire Sea, the Sunny Sea and the Pilvian Gulf, the winter monsoon blows from the north-east, dry and mild, off the land. The summer monsoon blows from the south-west, stronger, wet but steady. Storms strike only the eastern side of the basin, around the Pepper Islands and the Bay of Kizac. The western side, the Citrine Sea, the Sunny Sea, the Bay of Azuv and the Strait of Meter, is the fair-weather side, and its calm, reliable winds made it one of the great trading waters of the world.</p> <h3>The Vanpur Monsoon</h3> <p>Over the Vanpur Ocean and Gavivag Bay the winter monsoon blows from the north and north-west, cold and dry, off the continent. The summer monsoon blows from the south and south-east, warm and humid, and begins with a spell of early-summer rains. In late summer and autumn typhoons come in from the Zefelli Sea. In summer the onshore winds drive ships toward the coasts of Gavivag Bay, so its harbours must be sheltered.</p> <h3>Where the Monsoon Ends</h3> <p>The monsoon stops at Behera's coast. The open Beheran Ocean keeps its ordinary trade winds all year, and Sirenna's north coast lies beyond the monsoon's reach.</p> <p>The monsoon also carries the spores of Drizan, the home to the <a href="Mogyr" class="internal-link">Mogyri</a>. Corrosive, toxic spores rise from Drizan's central mountains. The summer monsoon carries them north-east over the <a href="Sea of Spores" class="internal-link">Sea of Spores</a> onto the Living Coast, and the winter monsoon carries them south-west over the waters south of Drizan. No season leaves the Sea of Spores clear, and no ship crosses it.</p> <h2>The Ocean Currents</h2> <p>The winds drive the surface of the sea before them, and the turning of the world bends the moving water into four great loops in the open oceans. They turn clockwise in the north and counter-clockwise in the south. Warm currents run toward the poles along the western edges of the oceans, narrow, fast and deep, while cold currents run back toward the equator along the eastern edges, broad and slow. Around these loops run a great circling current in the far south, an eastward countercurrent in the doldrums, seasonal coastal currents in the monsoon seas and weak drifts in the polar seas.</p> <p>The <a href="Krestasi" class="internal-link">Krestasi</a> know these currents better than any surface people. Their drifting coral cities are grown to ride them, so that a Krestasi city may lie in a different sea from one season to the next.</p> <h3>The North Tirean Loop</h3> <ul> <li><strong>North Equatorial Current</strong>: warm, flowing west at about 17°N from off Rakh toward Peadyr.</li> <li><strong>Nyllena Current</strong>: warm and strong, running north close along Peadyr's east coast and then north-east past the Gulf of Nyllena. It is the fastest water in the Tirean Ocean.</li> <li><strong>North Tirean Drift</strong>: warm, running north-east to northern Ilrakhan. It keeps the Bloody Strait free of ice.</li> <li><strong>Wrahi Current</strong>: cold, flowing south along Rakh's west coast through the Wrahi Sea and past the Smoking Sea.</li> <li><strong>Wyrmrest Current</strong>: cold and weak, flowing south from the Wyrmrest Sea through the Gulf of Nyllena, inshore of the Nyllena Current. Where the two meet, fog is common.</li> </ul> <h3>The South Tirean Loop</h3> <ul> <li><strong>South Equatorial Current</strong>: warm, flowing west from the Beheran Ocean past the southern edge of the <a href="Immersong" class="internal-link">Immersong</a> to Eressa.</li> <li><strong>Beheran Drift</strong>: warm and weak, a slow westward drift across the open Beheran Ocean at about 22°S that joins the South Equatorial Current.</li> <li><strong>Eressan Current</strong>: warm, flowing south along <a href="Eressa" class="internal-link">Eressa</a>'s east coast.</li> <li><strong>Bricean Current</strong>: cold, flowing north along Bricea's west coast.</li> </ul> <h3>The North Odian Loop</h3> <ul> <li><strong>North Equatorial Current</strong>: warm, flowing west across the Odian Ocean at 13–17°N from off western Ligos toward Nirydia.</li> <li><strong>Vanpur Current</strong>: warm and strong, running north along Nirydia's east coast outside the islands of Ub and Ynkal, then out into the Odian at about 35–40°N. It is the fastest water in the Odian Ocean, and it runs north the whole year.</li> <li><strong>North Odian Drift</strong>: warm, flowing east across the northern Odian at 40–46°N toward Ligos.</li> <li><strong>Morrow Current</strong>: warm and weak, branching north into the Morrowsea.</li> <li><strong>Driftglass Current</strong>: cold, flowing south along western Ligos through the Driftglass Sea.</li> <li><strong>Beyond Current</strong>: cold and weak, flowing south from the Beyond, east of <a href="Zefellis" class="internal-link">Zefellis</a>, to meet the Vanpur Current. Where the two meet, fog is common.</li> </ul> <h3>The South Odian Loop</h3> <ul> <li><strong>South Equatorial Current</strong>: warm, flowing west across the Odian at 9–10°S from off Pagia to the Hyktar Ocean.</li> <li><strong>Hyktar Current</strong>: warm, flowing south through the Hyktar Ocean east of <a href="Sirenna" class="internal-link">Sirenna</a>.</li> <li><strong>Pagian Current</strong>: cold, flowing north along <a href="Pagia" class="internal-link">Pagia</a>'s west coast.</li> <li><strong>Sapphire Throughflow</strong>: warm, carrying water west from the Hyktar Ocean into the Sapphire Sea. Beyond it the Monsoon Drift runs along Behera's south coast and reverses with the monsoon.</li> </ul> <h3>The Austral Current and the Polar Seas</h3> <p>The Austral Current is the greatest current in the world. Cold and strong, it flows east all the way around the southern ocean at 46–52°S, driven by the southern westerlies with no land to stop it. It must squeeze through two gaps on its way: the Valev Narrows between Eressa and Valev, and the Bronze Passage between Sirenna and the southern ice.</p> <p>Closer to the poles the currents are weak and cold. The East Wind Drift runs west along the southern ice at 64–67°S, a small loop turns off the ice coast of Shellgrave, and in the far north the Polar Drift runs west at 80–81°N through the Frozen Sea, choked with ice.</p> <h3>The Lesser Currents</h3> <ul> <li><strong>Equatorial Countercurrent</strong>: warm and weak, running east at about 5°N inside the doldrums, through the Tempest toward the Sea of Clouds. It gives eastbound ships a lane against the trade winds.</li> <li><strong>Niryd Coastal Current</strong>: warm and weak, following the northern shore of the Niryd Ocean and running out through the Strait of Meter. It flows west in winter and east in summer.</li> <li><strong>Vanpur Coastal Current</strong>: warm and weak, running inside the islands of Ub and Ynkal from the Strait of Ynkal down to the Sea of Spores. It flows south in winter and north in summer.</li> <li><strong>Rim Current</strong>: cold and weak, turning anticlockwise around the rim of the Torn Sea.</li> </ul> <h2>Narrow Waters</h2> <h3>The Torn Sea</h3> <p>The Torn Sea, also called the Gullsong Sea, is a mostly enclosed sea at 35–49°N, deep in the westerlies. Its sailing season runs from spring to autumn, and its winters bring westerly storms and cold blasts from the north through the gaps in the Trykkerog. In hard winters the northern bays freeze, Melmers Bay, the waters around Juelfhuk and the lobe toward the Giants Steps, while the southern bays, the Bay of Uhl, Trazlov Bay and White Bay, stay open. Its tides are almost nothing.</p> <p>So many rivers feed the Torn Sea that its water is slightly fresher than the ocean, and its surface flows out through both of its gates all year. Leaving is easy, but entering needs a favourable wind, so the ports at the strait mouths are always full of ships waiting for one. The Bloody Strait in the north-west is the only gate that stays open in winter, warmed at its outer end by the North Tirean Drift, which gives whoever holds it a hold on the sea's trade. The Giants Steps, a chain of island gaps to the north, close with ice from the Frozen Sea every winter.</p> <h3>The Southern Passages</h3> <p>The Valev Narrows, between Eressa and Valev, are about 200–300 km wide and full of islands. The Bronze Passage is open water bordered by the southern ice. Ships sailing east through either are carried fast by wind and current together. Ships sailing west face both, and the westward passage is one of the most brutal in the world. Both are natural places for toll ports, shipwrecks and naval power.</p> <h2>Storms</h2> <p>The dense air of the world feeds storms well. Tropical storms rise over the warm waters on the western sides of the tropical oceans in each hemisphere's late summer: in the Zefelli Sea, in the western North Tirean off Peadyr, in the eastern Niryd Ocean, in the Hyktar Ocean and in the southern Beheran Ocean. Typhoons strike the Vanpur Ocean and Gavivag Bay in late summer and autumn, coming in from the Zefelli Sea. Like all great storms they turn anticlockwise in the north and clockwise in the south, drift west with the trade winds and die over cold water or land within days or weeks.</p> <p>The one exception is the <a href="Immersong" class="internal-link">Immersong</a>, a permanent storm at about 11°S in the South Tirean Ocean, north-east of Eressa. It has held the same place for all of recorded history. Its swell is felt thousands of kilometres away, the high cloud that spreads from its top hangs over the Sea of Clouds, and its edges serve sailors as lanes of their own.</p> <h2>Climate</h2> <p>The winds and currents explain much of where the world is dry, foggy or rich in fish.</p> <ul> <li><strong>Dry coasts</strong>: wherever a cold current runs along a coast it chills the air above it, and little rain falls. Western Ligos, Pagia's west coast, Bricea's west coast and Rakh's Smoking Sea coast are all dry for this reason.</li> <li><strong>The Ciador Desert</strong> covers Sirenna's north coast and interior, which lie under the calm latitudes and beyond the reach of the monsoon.</li> <li><strong>The Beheran Desert</strong> lies north of the rain belt for most of the year. When the rain belt crosses Behera each summer on its way north, the Cloudscratchers block the moist air, and the monsoon winds blow along Behera's coast rather than onto it.</li> <li><strong>Fog</strong> gathers where cold currents meet warm ones: off the Gulf of Nyllena, where the Wyrmrest Current meets the Nyllena Current, and off Zefellis, where the Beyond Current meets the Vanpur Current.</li></ul> <h2>Winds and the Sea Lanes</h2> <p>Ships under sail follow the winds and currents rather than the shortest line, and so most ocean crossings go out by one path and come home by another. Ships sail around the great loops rather than across them: west with the trade winds and the equatorial currents, east with the westerlies. They avoid the calms, which can hold a ship for weeks. They ride the fast warm currents along the western edges of the oceans. To reach a coast that lies upwind they swing wide across the trade winds into the westerlies and come at it from the far side. And they keep to the calendar: the high-latitude routes are sailed in summer only, the tropical routes avoid the late-summer storms, and the monsoon trade runs on a fixed half-yearly rhythm.</p> <p>The great sea lanes of the world are built on this pattern: the crossings to the New World, the <a href="Ziflis Trade" class="internal-link">Ziflis Trade</a> of the Niryd monsoon, the <a href="Vanpur Pepper Trade" class="internal-link">Vanpur Pepper Trade</a> and the <a href="Smoking Sea Trade" class="internal-link">Smoking Sea Trade</a>. The same winds explain why some crossings were made so late. The trade winds blow from Sirenna toward Behera all year, and ships that lose their rigging in the Beheran Ocean drift west into the gales of the Immersong, so Sirenna was reached from the Purple Sea only in recent years, by ships able to swing south into the westerlies and come at it from the far side.</p>
