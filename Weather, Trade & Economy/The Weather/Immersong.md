  <p>The Immersong is a massive storm centred around a gigantic vortex of water, located in the heart of the Tirean Ocean, north-east of the shores of <a href="Eressa" class="internal-link">Eressa</a>. Its exact make up and origin are unknown, as traversal is essentially impossible, although it has appeared in folklore for over 4,000 years as a religious symbol. The Immersong is worshipped as a deity and is part of various religious traditions in Eressa and <a href="Pagia" class="internal-link">Pagia</a>, as well as the central piece of the <a href="Crustlords" class="internal-link">Krestasi Myth of Doom</a>.</p> <h2>Description</h2> <p>Seen from its edges, the Immersong is a wall of cloud that fills the horizon. It is a great wheel of wind turning clockwise around a central eye, as every great storm of the southern seas does. Its violent core is about 800 km across, and gale-force winds reach out across some 2,000 km of ocean. Around the eye stands the eye-wall, the fiercest part of the storm, and beneath the eye the sea itself turns in the vortex that gives the storm its heart.</p> <p>The storm's high cloud spreads far out from its top and drifts away north-east, so that the waters beyond it lie under almost unbroken cloud. Sailors named them the Sea of Clouds.</p>  <p>The Immersong is never silent. Thunder rolls within it without end, and at night its lightning can be seen from about 400 km away, a flickering glow on the horizon long before the clouds themselves come into view. Beneath the thunder runs a deeper sound, a hum too low to be heard, felt instead as a pressure in the chest and a trembling in still water. Careful observers have detected that trembling on shores far beyond sight of the storm. Many take this endless thunder and hum to be the song in its name.</p>  
 <p>Every other great storm of the world is born over warm water in late summer, drifts west with the trade winds and dies within days or weeks over cold water or land. The Immersong breaks every part of this pattern. It does not move. The trade winds and the South Equatorial Current flow past its southern flank and should carry it west, yet it has held the same place for all of recorded history.It does not tire. A storm feeds on warm water, and a storm of this size churns cold water up from the deep, which should soon starve it. Yet the water beneath its eye-wall stays as warm as the warmest tropical seas, and the storm draws on the strength of the greatest storms of the world without pause, season after season.It does not change.Records and legends from Eressa and Pagia describe it in the same place and of the same size across thousands of years.</p>
 
 
 <h3>The Vortex and the Immereye</h3> <p>Beneath the storm the sea turns with it. The vortex reaches one to two kilometres down, drawing cold, nourishing water up from the depths at its centre and spinning it outward. No one has reached its centre and returned, and what lies at the bottom is known only from legend. Sailors' tales speak of an eye on the seabed beneath the storm, the Immereye, which holds the Immersong in place and keeps it alive. Whether it is a thing, a place or a power, and whether it was made or has always been, no one can say.</p> <h2>Effects on the Seas</h2> <p>The Immersong shapes the seas for thousands of kilometres around. Swell: the storm throws out a permanent swell that runs across the South Tirean Ocean. Eressa's north coast is pounded by heavy surf all year, and its harbours need breakwaters. Navigators far out of sight of the storm can fix their position from the direction of the swell. Lanes along its edges: on its northern side the storm's winds blow east, against the trade winds. Together with the Equatorial Countercurrent this gives ships an eastbound lane toward the Sea of Clouds and Behera. On its southern side its winds blow west on top of the trade winds and the South Equatorial Current, a fast but rough run toward Eressa. No ship cuts across the storm itself. Fishing grounds: the cold water drawn up by the vortex spreads out at the storm's edges, and the waters around it are among the richest fishing grounds of the Tirean Ocean, for those willing to work within reach of its gales.A trap for the lost: the Beheran Drift and the South Equatorial Current carry ships that lose their rigging in the Beheran Ocean slowly west into the Immersong's gales. For centuries, ships that set out from Behera toward Sirenna and turned back or were dismasted never returned, and the storm took the blame in the tales of every port on the Beheran shore.</p>  <h2>In Faith and Legend</h2> <p>For over 4,000 years the Immersong has stood in the folklore of the peoples who live within reach of its swell. It is worshipped as a deity in various religious traditions of Eressa and Pagia, where its unending thunder and its unchanging place are taken as signs of a power older than the world's peoples. Among the <a href="Krestasi" class="internal-link">Krestasi</a> it is the central piece of the <a href="Crustlords" class="internal-link">Myth of Doom</a>.</p>
