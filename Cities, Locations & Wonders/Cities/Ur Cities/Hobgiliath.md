<div class="wiki-infobox"> <h4 class="wiki-header">Hobgiliath</h4> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Country</strong> <span><a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a>, Unofficial Capital</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Government</strong> <span>Tribal seat of Boss Tenzea</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Population</strong> <span>15-30000 </span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Heraldic Symbol</strong> <span>N/A</span> </div> <hr class="wiki-hr"> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Primary Religion</strong> <a href="Kaladi Mysticism" class="internal-link">Kaladi Mysticism</a> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Primary Culture</strong> <span>Tirbi <a href="Hobgoblin" class="internal-link">Hobgoblin</a></span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Official Language</strong> <span>Overgob</span> </div> </div> <p>Hobgiliath is the largest settlement of <a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a> and the seat of Boss Tenzea, a hobgoblin city on the Trinmo that the Urien remember as Dursan, the capital of the Kingdom of <a href="Urland" class="internal-link">Urland</a> and the seat of the Gryphon Throne for nearly seven centuries. The Tirbi took it in 695PA and renamed it for their clan, and the Urien have never retaken it. It is called the capital of Ak-Saray, but only unofficially, since the tribes have no throne to sit on and the standing of the city comes from the custom that they gather here when a Great Boss is to be named.</p> <h2>Geography</h2> <p>Hobgiliath stands on the west bank of the Trinmo, on the confluence where Dursan stood, and it faces the Urien lands across the river. The walls of Dursan were burned and breached in the siege of 695PA, and the hobgoblins have mended them in their own manner, with timber and earth where the old stone could not be saved. No census of the city exists, and it is thought to hold something like fifteen thousand people, most of them Tirbi, with a population of Urna labourers and slaves on its edges.</p> <h2>History</h2> <p>Dursan was the seat of the Rassel kings and of the royal council known as the Rook, and it was the home of the Archbishop of Ur, whose cathedral stood beside the palace. It was besieged by the horde of Tirbo from the autumn of 694PA. Archbishop Felix of Preston wielded the Prophet's Light on the walls until he died there, Duke Oswald III of Turfield was killed by Korgaz in single combat, and King Uther IV, the Last Gryphon, perished in the storming of the city, which fell in the summer of 695PA. The Rassel dynasty was extinct, the Archbishop's seat was destroyed, and the city was renamed Hobgiliath after the clan of its conqueror. Hadley Lake, nearby, became Lake Tirbo.</p> <p>In 700PA Korgaz, the Blade of Noon, who had been wounded at the walls of this city, lost the champion's title here in ritual combat to a younger challenger and was slain, without a scar on his back. The Urien have never retaken the city.</p>  <p>Hobgiliath is the one place in Ak-Saray that the Urien would call a city, which sits oddly with a people who prefer small communities and cave halls. The tribes that come to trade or to attend a gathering keep to their own halls, and the city has always been busier at the times when a Great Boss is being named. Boss Tenzea has held it for more than two decades. The Urna of the city live in the quarters on its edge, as workers, servants and slaves.</p>
