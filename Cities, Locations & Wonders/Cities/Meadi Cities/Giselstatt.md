It is sometimes called the Pearl of the Valley, for from the city one can see the <a href="Kortopis" class="internal-link">Kortopi Mountains</a> on the southern horizon and the Trinwood on the northern. It is the centre of banking in the Kingdom of <a href="Meadrath" class="internal-link">Meadrath</a> and one of the most important crossings of the river, and it is a city of much history, famous for its three fortifications, its iron chains, its breweries and its festivals.</p> <h2>The City</h2> <p>The name of Giselstatt means the town of Gisel. It developed naturally, and whether it recalls a ruler of that name from older times or someone else entirely is unclear. The oldest records of the city go back to the 3rd century, when the first Kings of Gis built the White Keep at the river, together with a bridge, and declared that they would rule the valley from now on. Other cities of Gisvale, such as Druell or far Turv, may be older, but Giselstatt has long been the most important of them.</p> <p>The city boasts a record three fortifications, all built over the centuries. The White Keep stands in the north, and the City Hall lies beneath it. Castle Gis in the south was built by <a href="Marlina I" class="internal-link">Queen Marlina I</a> in 491PA to fortify the city, and it is the most military and defensible of the three, refurbished over the years. Castle von Mart in the east was built by Archduke Gisrol II in 880PA as a more spacious and luxurious residence for the ducal family, and the Archduke usually resides there. The three are now connected by a series of walls, although the city has long sprawled across them.</p> <p>The Trinmo is crossed here by the Gisbridge, and Giselstatt has forged massive iron chains that span the river at the eastern and western entrance to the city. They lie on the river floor, enchanted with magick, and can be raised at the behest of the Archduke. On the northern side of the Gisbridge stands the famous statue of <a href="Gismar" class="internal-link">Gismar</a> himself with his sword, which reminds the citizens of his mythical tale.</p> <h2>Rule and Trade</h2> <p>The city is in the hand of the Archduke, who is advised by the Great Guilds. A Deacon appointed by the Archduke handles most civil and criminal matters on the legal side, at present Deacon Falder von Irk, whose office is at the City Hall beneath the White Keep.</p> <p>Giselstatt is the heart of the banking world of Meadrath. It lies near mighty <a href="Uhl" class="internal-link">Uhl</a>, yet its banking is governed by its own commercial entities. Fairs are held in the city six times a year, and the merchants of the Gisvale Trade Company keep their houses here.</p> <h2>Festivals</h2> <p>Giselstatt is famous for its breweries, among them the Froh, Draffel, Betscher and Kleu Brewers Houses. The great beer and ale festival, also known as Goodmans March, is held in the first week of March.</p> <p>The festival of First Harvest is a harvest festival, celebrated with a great street parade in costume. The citizens emulate the movements of the massive <a href="Dragon" class="internal-link">Heximar</a>, and the parade ends when the hero, a burgher whom the Guilds traditionally choose, slays the beast as it finally crosses the Gisbridge.</p> <h2>History</h2> <p>In 589PA Diedrik of <a href="Medlan" class="internal-link">Medlan</a> supplanted King Tibor II of Gisvale, and for some seventy years Giselstatt was held by the Lords of Medlan. In the <a href="War of Trout and Drake" class="internal-link">War of Trout and Drake</a> it was the stronghold of Ringil I, and in 664PA the city was retaken, the line of Diedrik was extinguished and the Dragon Banner of Gismar was raised again. The banner is kept in Giselstatt as a relic of the Crown and flown on feast days. In 786PA the Treaty of Leis made Gisvale an Archduchy for the house von Mart.</p>
