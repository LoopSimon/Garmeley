<p>Ikara, often called the City of Fate, and known to its oldest residents by the ancient name Ikaharal, "Garden at the River" in the old Asefi tongue, is a walled city sitting on the Toumis Riverin the northern Asef Desert, at the crossroads of the roads from Erun to Larash and from Emiz to the border fortress of Dor Beher. It is administered by the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae Sultanate</a> under its Sanjak, <a href="Ilman Gorak" class="internal-link">Ilman Gorak</a>, and counts itself among the older surviving cities of the eastern Asef, younger than the ancient centres of Mereskis and Erun, but centuries older than most settlements in the region, having been founded in the early 8th century BA when the Sultanate itself did not yet exist and the Asefi peoples tracked their years by the Falconer's Calendar, a dating system still used locally whose origins predate Ikara itself. The city is most celebrated for the Falcon's Pit, its great hippodrome arena whose tournaments draw visitors from across the northern Asef and beyond, but Ikara is equally a city of artisans, caravans, ancient faith, and of the particular quiet pride that belongs to places that have endured longer than the empires that govern them.</p>

<h2>People, Culture and Faith</h2>

<p>The population of Ikara is predominantly Asefi Beheran human, the Asefi being one of the oldest continuous human cultures of the northern desert, the locals relationship with the Toumis River and the falcon veneration traditions  predate the city itself. The elves of the Sultanate form a significant but distinctly secondary presence: elven officials, administrators and upper merchants occupy the Palace, the Har Asafi and the finer villas of the walled city, while the overwhelming majority of those who work, trade, pray and live here are human. The Sanjak Ilman Gorak himself, approaching his hundred and sixtieth year and seventeen years into his tenure, is unusual among elven administrators in having cultivated genuine goodwill with the human population, his patronage of the Falcon's Flight tournaments earning him a reputation considerably warmer than most imperial appointees in the region enjoy. Ikara tolerates the Sultanate's governance without excessively embracing it. The city has been here since before the elves came but has never suffered under them.</p>

<p><a href="Ilonism" class="internal-link">Ilonism</a> is the dominant faith and the invisible architecture of daily life in the city. The three gods of the Wheel, Agmor of the past, Welmo of the present and Ruxis of the future  are worshipped in shrines deliberately placed outside the city's three gates, requiring a genuine journey from the worshipper as an act of intention. The three gates themselves are religious markers, their number being of a theological significance . The Temple of the Wheel in the southern Temple Quarter is the oldest standing structure in Ikara, and Wheel Square beside it is where the city's most significant communal religious life takes place. Ilonism here has absorbed centuries of Asefi falcon veneration into its practice, the Falcon's Pits tournaments carry ritual significance that their entertainment function does not fully explain, and the Ikaran Falcon in the city's heraldry pairs with the Ilonist Wheel as equal symbols of the city's identity. A small <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethric</a> community maintains the Candlehouse Chapel east of the walls under Father Timothy Mevan, practicing largely undisturbed in a city with no strong reason to persecute or really even to notice them.</p>

<h2>Economy</h2>

<p>Ikara's wealth flows from two sources that reinforce each other: the caravan trade and the Falcon's Pit. Its position on the crossroads between Erun, Larash, Emiz and Dor Beher makes it the natural resting point for commercial traffic crossing the northern Asef. The Ikara Caravanserai northwest of the walls is one of the largest such complexes in the region, and the Toumcross Market immediately north of the primary bridge captures the first commercial energy of arriving caravans before they reach the Magisters' Bazaar within the walls. The citys artisan trades, leather from the Grand Tannery, dyework from the Halls of Dyes, ceramics, carpets and metalwork from the workshops of the Bazaar Quarter and Riverside, give those caravans something worth taking out as well as bringing in.</p> 
<p>The Falcon's Flight, the great hundred day summer tournament season, transforms this already active commercial picture into something considerably more animated: visitors from across the northern Asef and beyond arrive for the games, fill the Northern and Western Camps, spend in the Magisters Bazaar around the clock, and provide the city's merchants and artisans with a concentrated seasonal revenue that sustains prosperity through the quieter months. The Telamon Family, whose holdings along the Toumis River predate the Sultanate's presence in the region, control significant farmland that feeds the city and remain the largest single private economic force in Ikara. <a href="Meana Telamon" class="internal-link">Meana Telamon</a>, the familys current head and the richest person in the city, sponsors the Skyrider Company of tournament athletes whose champion Umar Nasal was crowned Summer Race winner only a week ago. The <a href="The Alchemists Guild" class="internal-link">Gildia Alchemikow of Trazlov </a>maintains an outpost east of the Pens, a signal of Ikara's commercial reach far enough north to attract even that distant guild's commercial interest.</p>

<h2>Districts</h2>

<h3>The Temple Quarter</h3>

<p>The oldest part of the walled city occupies its southern reaches, organized around the Temple of the Wheel, a structure whose age exceeds reliable record, predating every administrative regime that has governed Ikara and serving as the spiritual anchor of the citys Ilonist identity through all of them. Wheel Square in front of the Temple is the most significant public religious space in Ikara, where the community gathers for the major ceremonies of the Ilonist calendar and where the Spear of the Wheel,  the religious military figure associated with the Temples protection maintains a visible presence. The Palace of the Sanjak sits within the Temple Quarter as well, its authoritative but luxurious character somewhat at odds with the spiritual gravity of its surroundings — though the rumours of undercity passages connecting the Palace to the Brass Dome south of the walls, if true, would suggest the Sanjak's relationship to that subterranean institution is more intimate than administrative protocol acknowledges. The Yeniren Barracks complete the Quarter as the locally recruited elite guard force of the Sanjak, Asefi human soldiers paid well enough that service in the Yeniren is one of the more dependable paths to a comfortable life in the city and the surrounding region.</p>

<h3>Old Ikara</h3>

<p>West of the Falcons Pit, the streets of Old Ikara are the administrative and magical heart of the walled city, narrower and older than the Bazaar Quarter across the arena, their stone worn smooth by centuries of foot traffic. The Tower of Goal rises here: the residence and school of Ikaras <a href="Magisters Ring" class="internal-link">Magisters</a>, those sanctioned practitioners of magic identified by their Malam talisman and operating under the authority of <a href="Karajal School of Insight" class="internal-link">Karajal</a> itself. The Tower is a small, self-contained community that acts simultaneously as  school, workshop, residence and something approaching a closed society all at once. Although the wealthier and more prominent Magisters maintain private villas elsewhere in the city, moving between their  obligations at Goal and a more comfortable patrician existence. The Har Asafi, the regional headquarters of various Sultanate officials from across the Asef, sits in Old Ikara as the administrative complement to the Sanjak's Palace, and the House of Justice completes the Quarters largest institutional buildings, where law is spoken, judgment delivered, and the Sultan's Law applied to the lives of thousands of people by the Sultans officials.</p>

<h3>The Bazaar Quarter</h3>

<p>East of the Falcons Pit, the Bazaar Quarter is the city's commercial and intellectual soul, the louder, brighter, more chaotic counterpart to Old Ikara's administrative gravity. The Magisters Bazaar dominates: a vast market open through the day and well into the night, where the smell of spices, fresh leather, dyed cloth and metalwork mingles with the noise of negotiation in Asefi Beheran, High Elvish and a dozen other languages spoken by those passing through. The Ikaran Arcades, multi-storey covered walkways stretching from the Falcon's Pit into the heart of the Bazaar, give the quarter its distinctive architecture, allowing commerce and movement to continue regardless of the desert heat and providing the city's merchants with covered frontage that transforms the approach to the arena into a continuous commercial experience. The Library of Maral occupies a refurbished palace near the Bazaars eastern edge, built by Sanjak Maral roughly a hundred and twenty years ago. By Ikaran standards it is a recent addition, though it has long since established itself as the city's primary repository of written knowledge and a point of some pride for the Magisters whose own studies it serves.</p>

<h3>The Falcons Pit</h3>

<p>The Falcons Pit is not a district but the city's organizing centre, the point around which Old Ikara and the Bazaar Quarter arrange themselves and toward the Ikaran Arcades draw both commerce and crowds. The arena itself is a massive hippodrome style structure whose scale is immediately apparent to anyone approaching the walled city from any direction. Its three premier events define the social calendar of the entire northern Asef: the The Grand Duelling Tournament, where individual champions meet in formal combat; the Summer Race, the chariot and mounted racing competition whose champion Umar Nasal of the  Skyrider Company was crowned only a week ago to considerable celebration; and the Cities Championship, where parties of fighters compete in team combat that the crowd regards as the most unpredictable and therefore most compelling of the three events. Local families of standing sponsor athletes and heroes throughout the Falcon's Flight, the hundred day summer season during which the tournaments reach their height, alongside external sponsors from across the Sultanate whose commercial and social interests find the Pit's visibility an attractive platform. The fighting portions of the current season are about to begin.</p>

<h3>Riverside and the Pens</h3>

<p>The Toumis River bisects the city along its eastern face, separating the main walled settlement from the streets beyond it. The Riverside district occupies the organized streets and residential buildings between the inner walls and the waters edge, working waterfront rather than wealthy quarter, its character shaped by the river traffic arriving at the Toumdocks adjacent to the great Toumcross bridge in the north. The Toumcross itself is the old and primary crossing, wide enough for loaded caravans and old enough that its stones have settled into the riverbed with a comfortable permanence., the newer Dogsbridge in the south handles the overflow. Across the Toumis, the so called "Pens" extend east from the far bank. These are working class quarters where the citys poorer residents live in proximity to the Cobalt Market, the east bank's own commercial centre, and where the density of permanent settlement thins gradually into something less organized. The Runt, spreading east from the Pens interior, is the citys least comfortable truth: poor neighbourhoods that spill into a disease ridden and crime infested tent settlement of those who cannot afford walls around them or roofs above them. The <a href="Red Honey" class="internal-link">Red Honey</a> epidemic currently gripping Ikara is nowhere more acute than in the Runt, where its effects compound an already precarious existence, though the drugs recent spread into the wealthier quarters of the walled city has complicated the Yenirens efforts to treat it as a problem of the poor alone.</p>

<h3>The Camps</h3>

<p>The Northern and Western Camps outside the city walls are a permanent feature of Ikara's character, the semi permanent tent settlements of Bedouin travellers, merchant caravans and seasonal visitors who make the city their base without making it their home. Their population is constantly changing and yet somehow constant: different faces occupying the same ground in the same patterns season after season, the Camps providing the commercial and social buffer between the organized city and the desert roads that feed it. During the Season of Falcon's Flight the Camps swell to their greatest extent as visitors arrive for the tournaments from across the northern Asef, their tents pressing against the city walls and spilling north toward the Ikara Caravanserai, whose vast complex northwest of the walls facilitates the logistical machinery of caravan travel with the practiced efficiency of an institution that has been doing exactly this for centuries.</p>

<h2>Outside the Walls</h2>

<p>The territory immediately surrounding Ikara carries as much character as the city itself. To the north, the Toumis River curves toward the Izker Moor,  the wetland formed where the Toumis meets the Izker River coming from the northeast, a marshy delta of reeds and standing water that defines the northern approaches to the city and accounts for why the road to Erun bends west before heading north. The small village of Yarol sits on the Toumis between the Moor and the city, a modest satellite settlement whose population serves the river trade and the farms of the Toumis valley. Telamon Keep stands southwest of the walls along the road to Larash, the fortress is older in its foundations than most of what now surrounds it, its current occupant Meana Telamon running the city's most significant mercenary operation in the Glimmercapes, alongside the familys agricultural holdings. Eastward, the Grand Tannery announces itself to approaching travellers well before it becomes visible, its smell carrying across a considerable distance; the Halls of Dyes sit southward of the Tannery,  with their well guarded warehouses of prepared colorant, and the Silver Garrison, named for its gleaming rooftops, houses the Third Battalion of the Dusk Army under Bey Razom, a quiet but explicit statement of the Sultanate's military presence in the region.</p>

<p>South of the walls, the Brass Dome dominates the lands entirely. The structure's visible surface, a dome of polished brass that catches the desert sun, gives little indication of its true nature, which is primarily subterranean: <a href="Golden Dusk" class="internal-link">Golden Dusk</a> Drow clerics and medicae practice their arts below ground here, almost never ascending to the surface, administering the healthcare of the city's more important citizens and overseeing the Fields of Silence, the Ikara's burial grounds which stretch south beyond the Dome in the geometric patterns characteristic of long established funerary tradition. Entry to the Brass Dome is forbidden without a writ of passage, and such writs are not easily obtained. Rumours of undercity passages connecting the Dome to the Sanjak's Palace within the walls have circulated in Ikara for years without ever being confirmed or definitively dismissed, part of elven conspiracies that are found anywhere within northern Behera. The Tower of Bar Shitim rises near the Isle of Mice where the Toumis bends south, and Bar Iroff  stands further east in the desert approaches, Bar Laban guards the road to Dor Beher and Bar Enjaz the path north. These Towers are older fortifications whose current military purpose is secondary to their role as landmarks on a flat desert horizon The Candlehouse Chapel, east of the walls and easily missed among its larger neighbours, is where Father Timothy Mevan tends to Ikara's tiny Gethric community, a congregation founded by pilgrims some three hundred years ago, closed for decades during the political upheaval of the <a href="Wars of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">Wars of Elbae Aggression</a>, and quietly reopened sixty years ago to a city that has largely forgotten it is there.</p>

<h2>Current Situation</h2>

<p>In the summer of 927PA Ikara is at its most alive and its most strained simultaneously. The Falcon's Flightis in full progress, the Summer Race freshly concluded with Umar Nasal of the Skyrider Company crowned champion to the delight of Meana Telamon's considerable network of supporters. The fighting portions of the season are about to begin, and the city's population swells with visitors while its merchants and artisans operate at full capacity. The western walls meanwhile, are undergoing refurbishment,  scaffolding and stonemasons occupying a stretch of the city's ancient perimeter in a project whose urgency may say something about the Sanjaks assessment of the regions security that official communications do not. Beneath the festival atmosphere, the <a href="Red Honey" class="internal-link">Red Honey</a> epidemic that has been spreading through Ikara troubles the Yeniren considerably. The drug crossing the social lines that usually contain such problems, found now in the wealthy villas of the Bazaar Quarter as readily as in the disease ridden tent sprawl of the Runt. A new variant known as Demon's Blood appeared across the Asef in the autumn of 926PA, distinguished from Red Honey by its reported amplification of magical ability alongside its destructive physical and psychological effects, whether it has yet arrived in Ikara is yet to be revealed, though the Magisters of the Tower of Goal are among those following its spread with particular attention.</p>
