<p>Uraz, commonly referred to as Uraz the Cursed, is an active stratovolcano situated beyond the southern margins of mainland <a href="Aisligos" class="internal-link">Aisligos</a>, visible from the open waters of the Silken Sea and from the broken rock formations of the Ruins of Naltor that surround its approaches. The eruption of Uraz in 283BA, preceded by a sequence of catastrophic earthquakes whose full extent reshaped the continental interior, constitutes the single most destructive event in the recorded history of Aisligos. The tectonic shifts that accompanied or triggered the eruption caused the collapse and submersion of the Naltor region, a lush lowland of valleys and arable plains that had served as the heartland of the ancient <a href="Alder" class="internal-link">Alder</a> civilisation and as the land bridge connecting mainland Aisligos to <a href="Peadyr" class="internal-link">Peadyr</a>. What had been the most storied landscape on the continent sank into the sea in the course of days, leaving only the highest peaks above the waterline and the submerged ruins of Alder cities beneath them, structures whose age and construction already predated the eruption by thousands of years, and whose loss removed the largest concentration of <a href="Aldergrounds" class="internal-link">Aldergrounds</a> from the reach of any who might have studied them.</p> 

<p>The eruption itself did not end with the initial cataclysm. Uraz continued to discharge ash clouds and volcanic material across a period lasting into the early centuries of the PA era, blanketing much of Aisligos in a sustained dry period that destroyed agricultural capacity across the continent for generations. The famines and population collapses of the Uraz Period effectively reset the post-Catarmon cultures of the <a href="Narzoi" class="internal-link">Narzoi</a> peoples, dismantling what settled structures had begun to emerge in the centuries prior and returning many communities to dispersal and migration. It was during this same period that the <a href="Geipra" class="internal-link">Geipra</a> undertook their migrations westward, and the fracturing of the continental interior produced the canyon system now known as the <a href="Red Scars" class="internal-link">Red Scars</a>. The volcano remains active and its silhouette, rising above the ruins it created, has become one of the defining images of the Aisligan world.</p>
