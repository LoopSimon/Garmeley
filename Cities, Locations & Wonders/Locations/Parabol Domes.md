<p>The Parabol Domes are the eight great domed works of <a href="Majir" class="internal-link">Majir</a>, on the southern shore of Lake Ganoa, in which the flowers of the lake are processed into <a href="Ganoan Dye" class="internal-link">Ganoan Dye</a>. They are the source of the wealth of the <a href="Ganoa Valley" class="internal-link">Ganoa Valley</a> and of the toxic fumes that hang over the southern shore, as well as of the hallucinogenic residue known as <a href="Para" class="internal-link">Para</a>. The Domes dominate the skyline of Majir and the life of its people, who call them the Lungs, and the city grew around them from a small ferry village after the first Domes were raised in 822PA.</p>  
<h2>History</h2>  
<p>The first Parabol Domes were raised in 822PA by the Masters of <a href="Jink" class="internal-link">Jink</a>, who wished to make the dye in greater quantities than ever before but had no wish to bring its fumes into their own city. They chose the ferry village of Majir on the far shore, downwind of Jink, and over the following decades built eight Domes there, ruled together with the growing town by a Domelord appointed by the Council of Jink from the Tower of Relativity. The workers of the Domes rose against Jink in 893PA, and by shutting the valves of the Domes and stopping the dye made them the centre of the war of 893PA to 895PA. Since the truce of 895PA the Domes have belonged to the free city of Majir, and proposals to raise Domes on the northern shore have so far come to nothing.</p>  
<h2>Workings</h2>  
<p>Each Dome is a great vault of brick and iron, built in a curving parabolic shape from which the works take their name, raised over furnaces and boiling vats in which the Ganoan Flowers harvested beneath the lake are rendered into dye. The vapours of the process rise into the vault and are drawn off through vents, valves and pipes, and the waxy Para settles on the vents and hardens within seconds, to be scraped off by the workers. What the vents cannot hold escapes as the smoke that the wind carries over Majir. The Domes run day and night, worked in shifts that are called by bells, and their furnaces are cooled only once a year, during the three days of the <a href="Long Breath" class="internal-link">Long Breath</a>.</p>  
<p>The Domes inevitably begin to leak with age and use, and keeping them stable is the charge of the <a href="Mystics Guild" class="internal-link">Mystics Guild</a>, whose chamber in the valley, the Twin Spires, holds a monopoly on their maintenance. Much of this work can only be done while the furnaces are cold, so that the Long Breath is also the busiest time of the year for the Mystics of the valley. The Guild's place in the Domes is resented by many in Majir, who accuse it of serving Jink and of coveting the Para of the vents.</p>  
<h2>The Domes</h2>  
<p>Three of the Domes are counted as great Domes and five as lesser ones, and all of them are known by names given to them by their workers. Since the war most of them have come under the control of the gangs of Majir, which supply their shifts, take a share of their wages and keep the Para of their vents, while the city still carries the cost of their upkeep.</p>  
<p>The Red Eye is a great Dome named after the round oculus of red glass at its crown, which glows through the night with the light of its furnaces and can be seen from Jink across the lake. It goes dark only on the Long Breath, and it is held by the Valvers. The Grandmother is the oldest of the Domes, raised in 822PA, a great Dome whose pipes are the worst and whose Para is held to be the richest in the city, so that the Mystics are seen there more often than anywhere else. It is held by the Greenhands. The Wheelhouse is the great Dome in which the valves were shut in 893PA, and its valve wheel is the one shown on the arms of Majir. Each Consul swears the oath of office beside it, and though it belongs to the city in name, it is held in practice by the Masks.</p>  
<p>The Kettle is the loudest of the lesser Domes, and its shift bell sets the time for the whole of the Dome belt. It is held by the Valvers. The Widow, named for the many workers it has killed, stands beside the Waxworks, and most of the Para that leaves Majir passes through it. It is held by the Greenhands. Mupat's Mouth is named after its great smoke vent, shaped like an open mouth, and is the holiest place of Mupat of the Deathwind in the city, where new workers are blessed before their first shift. It is held by the Masks. The Cinder is the easternmost of the Domes, standing beside the caravan camps of Cinderside, and is held by Rainpath. The Drowned Dome was built too close to the shore and has half sunk into it, so that it leaks into the lake. After the war it was sold to a Majiri trading house, which runs it to this day.</p>  
<h2>Workers</h2>  
<p>The lives of the dome workers are short and rough. The fumes of the Domes ruin the lungs of those who breathe them, and few workers live to see grandchildren, so that the Majiri say of a worker who dies of the fumes that he has been breathed, after Mupat of the Deathwind, whom the Domes have made the patron of the city. Workers wear cloth masks against the smoke and their hands are stained green by the dye, and the medicine they need against the fumes must be bought from Jink, which holds a monopoly over it. Still the Domes never lack for workers, as working them is the quickest way for the poor of the valley and the newcomers who arrive in Majir every year to earn coin and bread.</p>
