<p>The Great Kiln is the oldest temple of <a href="Sochor" class="internal-link">Sochor</a>, the centre of the faith and the seat of the Osafar. It stands in the jungle highlands of <a href="Byamon" class="internal-link">Byamon</a>, and it is the home of many theological debates on the teachings of the god.</p> <h2>The Ash Plain</h2> <p>No jungle grows around the Great Kiln. As an act of faith the priests have burned down everything within two miles of the temple and keep the ground bare, so that the temple stands on a wide plain of ash and black earth with the green wall of the jungle on every side. The plain is a place of pilgrimage, but no one lives on it except the priests and their guardians.</p> <h2>The Kiln and the Ash</h2> <p>The temple is built around the kiln that gives it its name, a fire that is held to have burned since the Holy Work began and that is never allowed to die. The ash drawn from it is carried to the temples of the <a href="Ashcult" class="internal-link">Ashcult</a> across Tal and the Purple Sea, where it is used at the great rites. The Osafar lives within the temple, today <a href="Hien-Ro" class="internal-link">Hien-Ro</a>, together with the Elders of Ash.</p> <h2>The Brandgivers</h2> <p>The priests of the Kiln are guarded by the Brandgivers, a military order that serves as the guard of the Osafar and lives with the priests on the ash plain. They are the armed force of the Ashcult, and no other soldiers keep watch over the Kiln.</p> <h2>The Schools</h2> <p>In the 9th century the debates of the Kiln gave rise to two Schools of Thought, the Dogmatists and the Radicals, who differ over the role that Chance plays in judgement (see Sochor).</p>
