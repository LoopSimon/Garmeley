 <p>The Eye of Kasimir is the great astrolabe of <a href="Szievek" class="internal-link">Szievek</a>, a tower of pale stone carrying a dial of bronze rings that stands in the middle of the city beside the University. It is the largest instrument of its kind in Vihr, and when the Vihri speak of the wonders of their realm it is one of the first things they name. It was begun in the reign of Orian II and finished in 899PA under Jakub I, and it is named for <a href="Kasimir" class="internal-link">Saint Kasimir the Martyr</a>.</p> <h2>The Instrument</h2> <p>The Eye is not a hand-held astrolabe made large but a building raised around one. The tower rises from a square in the centre of the city, and on its southern face hangs the dial: a ring of bronze taller than the houses about it, holding a pierced plate of the sky and a turning rule, all set to the latitude of Szievek. The pierced centre of the plate gives the Eye its name. A stair runs up inside the tower to a platform from which the astronomers of the University take their sightings, and a smaller dial set low on the tower gives the hour to anyone who crosses the square.</p> <p>There is no magic in it and the Eye was made by craft and mathematics alone, a point the University is quick to make, since it was raised under the <a href="Wermian Accords" class="internal-link">Wermian Accords</a> and in a city whose Bishop watched such things closely.</p> <h2>History</h2> <p>Ras Orian II began the work, the cost was shared between the crown, the University and the Bishopric, and it took the better part of a generation to build: the stone tower first, and then the bronze, cast in pieces and raised ring by ring. The Eye was finished in 899PA under Orian's brother Jakub I, the Good, two years before his grandson Vormil III, the Builder, came to the throne. <a href="Alfred the Younger" class="internal-link">Alfred the Younger</a>, who was Bishop of Szievek from 882PA to 909PA, blessed the instrument when it was done.</p> <p>The name is the saint's. In the Gethric canon Kasimir represents Weather and Community, and the University gave both reasons when it chose him. The Eye reads the sky for the farmers of the Lehva, and its small dial keeps the hours of the city for everyone in it. The name was also borne by two Szinmo kings, Kasimir I and Kasimir II, and some in Szievek have always found it fitting that a Ralpia crown should pay for a monument of that name in the Szinmo's own city. Nobody at court has ever said so.</p>  <p>The astronomers of the University keep the calendar of the Vadcy by the Eye, and each year they publish an almanac with the dates of the sowing and the harvest, the likely floods of the Lehva, and the feasts of the saints. Their reckoning is trusted along the river far beyond Szievek, and the barge masters of the Lehva consult it before the spring. Visitors to the city are taken up the tower as a matter of course, and the Eye stands beside the sheep on the banner as the other sign of Szievek.</p>
