<p>The Vihrmen Federation, formally the Vihri Tribal Federation, was the principal political organization of the Vihri peoples from approximately 130PA until its dissolution in 241PA and the proclamation of the <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Rascy zi Vihr</a>. It was not a state in any administrative sense but it functioned as a durable framework for collective military action and inter tribal arbitration among peoples who shared a language, a faith, and a century of experience fighting shared enemies. The Federations most significant act was not any policy it enacted in peace but the war it was prosecuting at the moment of the <a href="The Incursion" class="internal-link">Wermian Incursion</a> of 215PA, the conflict whose catastrophic interruption produced both the conditions and the justification for the unified kingdom that replaced it.</p>

<h3>Prehistory</h3>

<p>The history of the Vihri peoples before the Federations formation is known only in the broadest and most fragmentary terms from what the Turvik shamanic tradition preserved in oral form and from the occasional reference in Wermian administrative texts to the tribal peoples of the western river plain. What can be said with confidence is that the Vihri were never a single unified people in the but a constellation of related groups whose shared language and common Turvik religious framework provided cultural coherence without producing political organization. The culture groups that would later be formalized as the Vadcy of the Rascy emerged from this period as the crystallization of regional identities around the economic and military logic of specific territories, shaped by generations of movement, conflict, and seasonal aggregation that the written record does not preserve.</p>

<p><a href="Turvik Paganism" class="internal-link">Turvik Paganism</a> in its earliest form was a shamanic tradition with no permanent priesthood, fixed hierarchy, and magical practice that was rare, personally costly, and bound to the spiritual authority of individual shamans rather than to any school or institution. The great Turvik sacred sites, foremost among them the Ollkustone of <a href="Trydlew" class="internal-link">Trydlew</a>, the monument of Ollku the river god that drew pilgrims from across the Vihri world, served as the fixed religious anchors of a largely mobile culture. It was around such sites, and around trade nodes that developed at advantageous points on the river system, that the earliest Vihri settlements of any permanence took root. The cities of Trazlov, Trydlew, and Tlokyemy accumulated over generations as seasonal gathering places became permanent communities, drawing the roaming bands of tribal warriors and their kin into increasingly sedentary orbits. The Horn Lords transitioned gradually from mobile raiding commanders to regional authorities whose power was tied to territory as much as to personal military reputation.</p>

<p>The threats that shaped this transition were multiple and came from different directions. From the Yellow Moor, the Pilvian Vale, and the southern reaches of the Trollback and Grevy Mountains, the Ogrelords, powerful warlords commanding the brutal warrior cultures of their kind, raided the Vihri settlements of the Expanse with a regularity that demanded organized response beyond what individual tribal bands could provide. The Ravenleaf Forest to the southeast represented a different and less comprehensible kind of threat wich even the Ogrelords themselves avoided. From within the Forests margins the disturbing activities of <a href="Kortyr" class="internal-link">Kortyr Paleblood</a> haunted the southern Expanse. The allegedly half elven Priest whose radical cult, hemomantic practices, and devastating hunts of the sacred unicorns of the Expanse made him a figure of horror in the folk memory of the southern Vihri long after his presumed death in 190PA. From the east, the <a href="Wermia" class="internal-link">Kingdom of Wermia</a>, whose magocratic ruling class commanded capabilities no Vihri tribal force could match individually, pressed steadily into the contested river territories that both peoples regarded as their own.</p>

<h3>Formation and Structure</h3>

<p>The convergence of these pressures became, over the course of the decades around 40BA to 130PA, the Vihrmen Federation. The Battle of Wyt Hill in 32PA where a coalition of Vihri Horn Lords defeated a major Ogrelord incursion and drove the highland warlords back into the Moors and mountain ranges they had emerged from, showed both that the Vihri could act together effectively and that the Ogrelord threat could be contained. With their southern and western flanks secured, the Horn Lords turned their collective attention eastward, and the formal council structure that assembled at Tlokyemy around 130PA represented the diplomatic bond that the Ogre Wars had already developed in practice.</p>

<p>The Federations government consisted of the periodic assembly of the Horn Lords at Tlokyemy to deliberate on matters of common concern, primarily the coordination of military campaigns and the arbitration of inter tribal disputes. The city of <a href="Trydlew" class="internal-link">Trydlew</a> was home to the Ullkustone where the most significant pacts were sworn before Ollku, whose waters ran through the Vihri heartland. No single lord held authority over the others by formal right, though the most powerful military commanders exercised a de facto leadership. What the Federation represented was less a government than a set of understood obligations and recognized procedures depending on the Warlords self interest and rivalries.</p>

<h3>The Incursion and Dissolution</h3>

<p>The war the Federation prosecuted against Wermia across the early third century PA ended not through Vihri victory or defeat but through the catastrophic self destruction of their enemy. For the full account of the Wermian Incursion of 215PA and the events surrounding <a href="Gethric" class="internal-link">Gethric</a>s sacrifice in 240PA, see <a href="The Incursion" class="internal-link">The Incursion</a>. What the Incursion produced for the Federation specifically was a brutal distinction between those Vihri who had fought through the 25 years of war against the Horrors and those whose more distant territories had allowed them to withdraw and wait. Viago of the Zav, who had commanded the High Vihri host and the largest allied contingent through the full length of the conflict, emerged in 240PA as the dominant military figure in the Vihri world with a certain divine legitimacy. His Proclamation of Vihr in 241PA dissolved the Federation not through any formal deliberation but through the assertion that a new order had replaced the old. The lords who had not fought found themselves without the standing to object and without the allies to resist.</p>

<p>The Federation left behind it the cultural and geographic foundations of the early Rascy, the network of tribal territories that became the Vadcy, the city of Trydlew that retained its symbolic significance even as Zavlech displaced it as the political center, and the Turvik traditions that new Gethric policies would spend the following century attempting to suppress. The holy Stone of Trydlew was not destroyed in this process as the old faith persisted, quietly, in the Sarloni communities of the Breza hills and the Wyt territories of the southern Expanse long after the Horn Lords council had passed from living memory.</p>
