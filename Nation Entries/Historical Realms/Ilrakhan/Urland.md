<p>The Great Kingdom of Urland, named after the region of Urland between the Kortopi and Trollback mountains, was a feudal Kingdom that existed in the Silver and Steel Ages. With strong chivalric and martial traditions it was at times the strongest political entity on the face of Ilrakhan but would succumb to a splintering of its Urien subcultures following the <a href="Hobgoblin Invasion" class="internal-link">Fall of West Urland</a>, when the Hobgoblins of <a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a> invaded at the end of the 7th century. Conflicts arose earlier when the old faith of the urien peoples, belief in the Turvik Gods, was slowly but surely supplanted by the emerging <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethric Church.</a></p>
<p>Political consolidation of Urland was gradual and never fully complete, with major Dukes holding personal courts and even often establishing individual currencies and legal codes at times, although it is nearly impossible to brush over 600 years of urien history over a generalising comb like that. Periods of strong rulers like John II or Idward I saw Urland dominating Ilrakhan as one of, if not the single strongest and most influential nation of the continent. The End of the Dunling Dynasty in power in the late 5th century is where most people contest the eventual cracks in the realm to originate from, although that as well is easier said than proven considering powerful monarchs in the following centuries. King Versil I  became the first of the of Rassel Kings following the Dunlings demise and his line would rule over Urland until the apocalyptic invasion of the Kortopi Hobogblins at the end of the 7th century PA.</p>
<small><i>see <a href="Rulers of Urland" class="internal-link">The Rulers of Urland</a></i></small>
 <p>The Great Kingdom of Urland was governed as a feudal monarchy with a royal court centered at Dursan, seated on the confluence of the Trinmo River and later renamed Hobgiliath by the conquerors who destroyed it, and a layered hierarchy of noble territories extending to the kingdoms borders. The king's personal council, known as "the Rook", assembled the great lords and senior churchmen of the realm and was served by four household offices whose holders ranked among the most influential men in the kingdom: the Chancellor, keeper of the royal seal and head of the kingdoms administrative machinery; the Marshal, responsible for military organisation and the mustering of royal forces; the Constable, charged with the security of the royal household and the kingdoms castles; and the Steward, who managed the crown's estates and the practical running of the royal court. Political consolidation of Urland was gradual and never complete as the great Duchies, Earldoms and the southern Marches governed their territories with considerable autonomy particularly under weaker kings, and the crowns reach into the localities was mediated entirely through its vassals rather than through any direct royal officer in the shires. The two crown Duchies of Hadley, the ancestral Dunling demesne around Hadley Lake, and Greenslopes, the Rassel seat absorbed into crown lands at the dynasty's founding, provided the kings primary territorial revenue and military base, though the loss of both to the Hobgoblin Invasion in 690PA destroyed the realms foundations more thoroughly than any military defeat alone could have achieved.</p> <p>The <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethric Church</a> became an increasingly important institutional presence following the conversion under King Edric I "the Zealous" in the early 4th century. The first Gethric clergy in Ur were largely Vihri imports, sent by Iztaw to establish the faith in a newly converted realm, but within a generation a native Urien clergy had emerged and a fully established ecclesiastical hierarchy governed the kingdom's spiritual life, culminating in the High-Bisop's appointment of an Archbishop of Urwhose seat was established at Dursan alongside the royal court. The Archbishop held a permanent place on the Rook, giving the church direct institutional access to the crowns deliberations, an arrangement that mirrored the churchs political weight in Vihr and made ecclesiastical appointments a matter of ongoing negotiation between Dursan and Iztaw. The <a href="Knights of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">Knights of the Gryphon</a>, based in the Duchy of Ares around Whitelute, operated as a parallel institutional power whose authority sometimes exceeded that of local nobles, chivalric orders in Urland carried weight that the strictly feudal hierarchy did not always account for, and the Knights of the Gryphon in particular accumulated lands, political influence and military capacity that made them answerable more to their own Grand Master and the Archbishop than to any Duke or Earl in whose territory they operated.</p> <p>The kingdoms currency reflected its political structure. The royal <a href="Urien Gryphon" class="internal-link">Gryphon</a>, minted at <a href="Uhl" class="internal-link">Uhl</a> from the gold of the Talshol, Nestlemine, Ilsbreak and Songhole mines, was the kingdoms premier coin and the standard of international trade. Ducal minting rights were real and exercised, the <a href="Lind" class="internal-link">Lind</a>, minted by the Dukes of Nied at Lindahall, circulated widely in the southern Trinvale, while the <a href="Mark" class="internal-link">Garmish Mark</a> served the agricultural south and the commercial routes into Garmeley and beyond. Strong kings curtailed ducal minting and defended the Gryphons primacy while weaker ones tolerated the proliferation of baronial coin as the price of noble loyalty. The Invasion's seizure of Ilsbreak and Songhole, both within the crowns own demesne,  deprived the Gryphon of half its gold supply, though the mint at Uhl and the Talshol mines survived intact and the currency outlasted the kingdom that had created it, remaining in worldwide circulation under Uhls monopoly into the present day.</p>
