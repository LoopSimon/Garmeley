The name <a href="Pehki" class="internal-link">Pehki</a> covers all the native peoples of Nostroya, but Roygviil was specifically the realm of the Roymer. It held the forests and plains of central Nostroya until it fell to the Grachi and the Vihri in 690PA.</p> <h2>The Prince and the Court</h2> <p>The Roymer were a tribal community that paid homage to a prince, the Prince of Roygviil. He was elected from among the sons of the tribes at the festivals of <a href="Rynbrekka" class="internal-link">Rynbrekka</a> while his predecessor was still alive, so that the succession was secured before an old prince died. The prince and his court had no sedentary home. They roamed the lands and slowly visited every community and holy site in turn. Royba, the ancient town at the heart of the Roymer lands, was counted the capital, though the court visited it like any other place.</p> <p>The prince and his court were attached to <a href="Yaalwe" class="internal-link">Yaalwe</a>, lord of the winds, more closely than to any other of the gods of the Aeld, and the Roymer called it the Court of Winds. The name had a double meaning: it was the court of Yaalwe, and it was a court that never stood still. Tradition names Vytaur, a legendary champion of Yaalwe, as the first Prince of Roygviil.</p> <h2>The Legend of Vytaur</h2> <p>In the old stories the southern lands were ruled in the elder days by a Giant named Gyngir, and no clan could stand against him. Then Vytaur wrestled Yaalwe for four days and four nights. On the last night the god judged him worthy and showed him his true form, the great eagle, and Vytaur rose with the power of the winds, enough for himself and his clan to stand against Gyngir.</p>  <p>The Roymer followed the <a href="Aeldcult" class="internal-link">Aeldcult</a>, and the southern Aeld circles tended the sacred groves of their lands. Their arms showed an open hand over two oak leaves on a shield divided in blue and red. The two colours are probably a reference to an ancient ritual of the southern Aeldcults, in which blood was shed into the great rivers of the land to symbolise that they were lifegivers and holy to the natives.</p> <h2>History</h2> <p>Roygviil formed in the early Age of Silver from Pehki tribes settling as federations, in southern lands that the Giants of the age of <a href="Giantsrul" class="internal-link">Giantsrul</a> had long abandoned. In the 2nd century PA Vihri settlers led by Fidor of Trazlov founded Fidograd, the first village of the Grachi, and the growing numbers of settlers, merchants and refugees soon clashed with the Roymer over land and sacred sites. In 247PA the leaders of both peoples met at the town of Istrov and agreed on the Istrov Pact: the Grachi could settle and farm the lands south of the Gior River but were not to set foot in the sacred groves of the southern Aeld circles.</p> <p>In 487PA Prince Usse led his army south and destroyed the city of Modva, breaking the Pact. Who was to blame for its breaking is still among the most controversial questions of Nostroyan politics. The First Druid of the Aeld, Asria Mikkavo, kept the Aeldcircle neutral and <a href="Ukiolla" class="internal-link">Ukiolla</a> followed her, and in the Splintering of the Circles many southern druids rejected her stance and joined Roygviil. After two decades of war the Roymer conceded in 508PA and ceded vast stretches of land to the Grachi kingdom of <a href="Halgrach" class="internal-link">Halgrach</a>.</p> <p>In 601PA, under King <a href="Vlad V" class="internal-link">Vlad V</a> of Halgrach, thousands of enraged beasts, direwolves and owlbears among them, poured out of the deep eastern forests in the event known as the Day of the Elk and devastated the Grachi lands. Nobody knows who was behind it. It may have been rogue druids, the southern circles as a community, or even the northern circles secretly influencing the southern ones for reasons unknown, and many Grachi held the Prince of Roygviil responsible. Whoever was to blame, there was no turning back afterwards, and infighting among the Roymer would have achieved nothing, even if the Prince was surprised by it. Vlad had the southern groves burned, the beasts hunted and druidism outlawed, and in 628PA he and his two sons were killed by a circle of southern druids, a deed the Prince of Roygviil claimed as part of the Roymer war effort. The Grachi mobilised in earnest.</p> <p>In 690PA Grachian forces besieged Royba, and in the Battle of Royba the combined Vihri and Grachi armies, aided by powerful mages and clerics, broke the Roymer army and razed the town, ending the war and the Principality. Breel, a Roymer fortress to the south of Royba built around the White Tower, a druidic place of worship of the Aeld, fell in the same war.</p> <h2>Aftermath</h2> <p>Many Roymer fled north after the fall, among them the important clan leaders, and in Ukiolla its leader declared himself Grand Prince of all Pehki and Roymer in Ukiolla. The Roymer themselves had only ever had a Prince of Roygviil. Those who stayed were largely eradicated, subsumed or displaced by Grachian expansion, and few Roymer are alive today, some in small communities in <a href="The Jättilkivi" class="internal-link">the Jättilkivi</a> and others assimilated among the Pehki. The southern circles outlived the principality, and the Roymer and the militant circles who still resist within <a href="Grachy" class="internal-link">Grachy</a> count the traditionalist Aeldcircle and Ukiolla as traitors to their people and the land.</p> <p>The ruins of Royba lie in the northern Grachwoods, the Gram held by <a href="Ivanova Orin" class="internal-link">Ivanova Orin</a>, and Breel was rebuilt as <a href="Kharnov" class="internal-link">Kharnov</a>. The <a href="Great Eastern War" class="internal-link">Great Eastern War</a> between Grachy and Ukiolla is sometimes called the result of the long conflict between the Roymer and the Grachi.</p>
