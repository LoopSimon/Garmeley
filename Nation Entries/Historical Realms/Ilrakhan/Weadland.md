Its people are the northern cousins of the <a href="Meadi" class="internal-link">Meadi</a>, and the three great rivers that cross it, the Lot, the Frij and the Ostor, gave their names to the tribes of the land. The kingdom is long gone, but the name has stayed with the country, and most of it is now united in the <a href="Blijgrat" class="internal-link">Blijgrat League</a>.</p> <h2>The Land</h2> <p>Weadland is a country of marsh, mere and slow river, and the Weadi did not take it as they found it. With irrigation, ditches, dams and dikes they have molded it for as long as they have lived there, draining fens into farmland, holding back the tides and turning the rivers to their own purposes, and the windmills of the coast still turn above fields that were once sea. Here and there great hills rise out of the wetlands, and on them the oldest lords of the country built their seats, high above the water. That is why the lords of Weadland were called Highlords: they were not set above the other lords, they simply stood up high.</p> <p>The Lot runs through the north-west towards Zeeport, the Frij through the north and east towards Hevn, and the Ostor through the south-east to the sea at Schipwig. The Weadi know themselves as the Four Tribes of Wead, the Lotmer, Frijmer and Osmer, who are named after the rivers, and the Kranmer of the moors in the south, who have long been ruled by <a href="Lesmere" class="internal-link">Lesmere</a>.</p> <h2>The Highlords and the Kings</h2> <p>The Weadi fought beside the Meadi in the age of the <a href="Auguri Dominion" class="internal-link">Auguri Dominion</a>, and after the Sages broke in 260PA the tribes of the north were left to themselves. In 296PA Pieter the Rich became Highlord of Weadland, and the annals of the realm begin with him. He was a man of great influence, and tales of his wealth and riches reached far beyond Weadland, which is why he is remembered as Pieter the Rich.</p> <p>The power behind the realm was Raalbrug in the central Frijlands, a town first mentioned in 125BA. Its lords, later known as the van Raal, grew powerful and entrenched enough to pressure their neighbours, by force of arms or by the weight of their wealth, until the rivals around them swore fealty to them. Soon afterwards they proclaimed themselves Kings of the Weadi. The kingdom was feudal in form, a realm of lords who held their hills and their lands of a king, and its capital was Schipwig, where the Ostor meets the sea. Raalbrug itself was destroyed in the Great Raalbrug Fire of 516PA, a generation before the Baeling came. The people of the kingdom were Gethric and spoke Weadi.</p> <p>In its last fifty years the kingdom was in steady decline. The barons and local rulers of Weadland grew ever more irreverent of the crown and ignorant of its wishes, and the royal court moved from Schipwig to Grisden, where it stayed until the Baeling conquest.</p> <h2>The Baeling and the Cities</h2> <p>In 549PA the <a href="Baeling Empire" class="internal-link">Baeling Empire</a>, a sea empire of the north, conquered Weadland, a kingdom long in decline, and the kingdom ended with the conquest. For four decades the coast was held by the Baeling, until their house fell in 590PA. Weadland was not restored. Instead many petty lords rose up on their own, some of them Weadi and some of them warders from <a href="Trykkerog" class="internal-link">Trykkerog</a> who had been set over the land, and in 591PA the von Kran took the coastal lands of the south and founded the Duchy of Lesmere. A warder held Schipwig at the dusk of the century and turned away the children of Tibor II of <a href="Gisvale" class="internal-link">Gisvale</a> for fear of war.</p> <p>The raids began again as soon as the great sea empire had collapsed, and they did not end until the Baeling came back in the 7th century, when a new warlord took the name and the northern sea realm rose once more. In the years between, the country returned to the rule of individual cities and communities, and four of them, Schipwig, Hevn, <a href="Zeeport" class="internal-link">Zeeport</a> and Geldfields, grew ever richer and more influential. In 737PA Saint <a href="Yahrwick" class="internal-link">Yahrwick</a> freed Zeeport from the Skori, the last heirs of the Baeling conquests, and in 770PA the three greatest cities swore the Blijgrat Agreement on the farmland of Blijgrat, which became the League.</p>
