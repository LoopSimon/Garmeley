<p>The Great Kingdom of Skorland was a short-lived realm of the Skori in western <a href="Trykkerog" class="internal-link">Trykkerog</a>, proclaimed in 641PA by Haddr the Great, Korre of <a href="Vignasoya" class="internal-link">Vignasoya</a>, after he had subdued the jarls of the west and the jarldom of <a href="Rensköldsvik" class="internal-link">Rensköldsvik</a>. It marked the height of Skori power in the century after the fall of the <a href="Baeling Empire" class="internal-link">Baeling Empire</a>, but it rested on the strength of a single ruler and broke apart soon after his death in 667PA.</p>

<h2>Background</h2>
<p>The Skori came out of the fall of the Empire stronger than any other people of the island. Korre Ulfar II "the Wolf" had won back the Wolfsbay coast before 590PA, Skori ships held the port of <a href="Zeeport" class="internal-link">Zeeport</a>, and from 594PA Vignasoya governed the island of <a href="Droil" class="internal-link">Droil</a>. Melmers Bay to the east was divided among petty lords and mercenary captains in the Interregnum of the Runeblades, and no power on Trykkerog could challenge the Skori. Within the west, however, the Korre was only the first among many jarls, and the great jarldom of Rensköldsvik on the Rensvik Bay had ruled itself since the fall of the Empire.</p>

<h2>Haddr the Great</h2>
<p>Haddr became Korre of Vignasoya in 629PA. In the following decade he brought the jarls of the north-west to heel, partly by war and partly by marriages and gifts from the wealth of the overseas holdings, and in 641PA he turned on Rensköldsvik. After its jarl submitted that summer, Haddr was acclaimed at the Great Thing at Mannelik in 641PA not as Korre alone but as Great King of all the Skori, and the realm he ruled has been called the Great Kingdom of Skorland ever since. For a quarter of a century every Skori lord on Trykkerog and overseas acknowledged him, and Skori ships sailed from Rensköldsvik to Droil under a single banner.</p>

<h2>Collapse</h2>
<p>Skorland had no institutions of its own beyond the person of its king. The jarls had sworn their oaths to Haddr rather than to a crown, and at his death in 667PA his sons quarrelled over the inheritance while the jarls withdrew their oaths. Rensköldsvik regained its independence at once, and the title of Great King was never claimed again. The Korringe of Vignasoya survived the collapse, and Haddr's son Sigvat I succeeded him as Korre, but the unity of the Skori was never restored.</p>

<h2>Legacy</h2>
<p>Skorland is remembered among the Skori as their golden age, and the songs of Haddr's victories are still sung in the halls of the west. Its collapse began the long decline of the Korringe. The northern jarls who had been forced into Haddr's realm drifted away from his successors over the following century and became the <a href="Free Jarldoms" class="internal-link">Free Jarldoms</a>, while Rensköldsvik has kept a lasting distrust of Vignasoya. The overseas holdings that had paid for Skorland were lost in the 8th century, Zeeport in 737PA and Droil in 772PA.</p>
