Naigan, then a provincial administration under a Lord within the <a href="Golden Empire" class="internal-link">Golden Empire</a>, followed suit with other regional rulers and exploited the weakness caused by internal instability and external pressures within the Golden Empire and in 492PA, Lord <a href="Aung Shant" class="internal-link">Aung Shant</a> declared himself the King of Naigan.</p> <p>Over the course of its rule Naigan would become the greatest Yppori realm in recorded history, conquering the entire Purple Sea and eastern <a href="Kaito" class="internal-link">Kaito</a>. By 658PA, <a href="Bayratha" class="internal-link">Bayratha</a> of Naigan was declared the Divine Emperor of the Purple Sea and the envoy of <a href="Sochor" class="internal-link">Sochor</a> himself.</p>  <p>The power of Naigan lay in the might of its channelers, especially in the early years. They commanded powerful <a href="Elemental" class="internal-link">elemental</a> forces, and some of them even bound elementals themselves as tireless forces of war which gave the armies of Naigan the edge over every rival.</p>  <p>In 532PA, forty years after Aung Shant declared himself King and a decade and a half after his death, the armies of Naigan took Fiyang, the capital of the Golden Empire. The last Golden Emperor was killed, and in 532PA the Golden Rule ended. In the same war the ancient city of Char, where the Chagor of the elder days had once been crowned, was razed as a sign to all: Naigan meant the power of Tal to pass from the old mainland cities to the imperial island and its capital Kiamar,  the ruin of Char made that plain to all and the nexus of power went east.</p> 
<h2>The Empire</h2> <p>In 658PA Bayratha of the Aung Dynasty declared himself Emperor of the Purple Sea, and the Kingdom of Naigan became the Empire of Naigan. It was also called the Empire of the Four, after the four peoples of Tal that it united, the Talang, the Al Ypp, the Anpon and the Rytoku. It conquered the Safiri cities of the western Antulun Bay, and in 681PA it took the city of Gyedo, one of the last holdouts against it, and sacked it horribly, the great Channeler <a href="Szuan" class="internal-link">Tihir Szuan</a> would die in the storming of its districts. This was the Golden Age of the Naigan, when the Empire of the Four was the uncontested power of the Purple Sea and only one of the great cities of Tal escaped it. Naigan tried once to take Koshtir, but the Great Sage <a href="Hilcarax" class="internal-link">Hilcarax</a> bent the mist itself on a vast scale, and an attack on the city by sea or by land proved all but impossible. The Sage agreed to hail Naigan as the rulers of the Purple Sea, likely foreseeing that the Purple Empire would fall in any case, and Koshtir was left alone. In its later days the Ivory Crown of the old <a href="Dynasties of Tal" class="internal-link">Dynasties of Tal</a>, sealed in a vault for centuries and later stolen, resurfaced on the mainland, an event that many would count as an omen of the coming doom for the Purple Empire.</p> 

<h2>The Belen Dynasty and the Fall</h2> <p>Two dynasties ruled the Empire, the Aung, who had founded the kingdom, and the Belen, who arose from a junior branch of the Aung and ruled for only around four decades. <a href="Belen Yun" class="internal-link">Belen Yun</a>, the third of the Belen Emperors, took the throne around 747PA at the age of 18 and was the last of all. Following decades of strife and plague after the Naigan conquered the Safiri cities of the western Antulun Bay, the Imperial City of Kiamar entered a decade long sealing from 761PA, effectively quarantining itself on the imperial island. During this period various powerful warlords arose in the Empire and it gradually began falling apart, many of them initially raised as peacekeepers by the Emperor himself who then later turned on him. In 771PA, Warlord <a href="Piri Cao" class="internal-link">Piri Cao</a>, known as "the Hungering Wave", sacked the Imperial Palace at Kiamar and forced Belen Yun to abdicate, essentially leading the lands of the Purple Sea into the <a href="Period of the Bleeding Heron" class="internal-link">Period of the Bleeding Heron</a>. After proclaiming his abdication with his imperial seal, Belen Yun was sealed in his throne room, and he and his court of concubines and servants all died within it. The room was opened only a full year later by bandits and soldiers of fortune, who would be the first in a long line of vultures set upon the Imperial Isle.</p>  <p>Naigan is remembered as the golden age of Yppori greatness, and the Yppori Moons of its banner remain a common emblem across city gates, pottery, coin and art. The realms of today, from <a href="Hanshi" class="internal-link">Hanshi</a> to <a href="Byamon" class="internal-link">Byamon</a>, all claim its inheritance to varying degree  (see <a href="Tal" class="internal-link">Tal</a>) and the island of Naigan, with Kiamar upon it, is still often called the Imperial City. It has been looted many times since the fall of the Empire, and has recently become the home of a powerful sorcerer known as <a href="Jinùk" class="internal-link">Jinùk</a>, who bows to no land and worships golden Dragons.</p>
