<p>The Qahori Federation is the largest surviving community of the <a href="Orc" class="internal-link">Qari</a> who once called the island of Qa their home, a maritime federation on the Qahori Archipelago in the Niryd Sea, north of <a href="Il Haena" class="internal-link">Il-Haena</a>. The islands were divided among several individual chiefdoms, often in conflict in the past, until they were united by the House of Tihiok under one Sandimar, the king-title of old Qa. Excellent seafarers, many of the Qahori have given up the great dream of reconquering the Lost Lands of Qa and instead search for luck in the southern oceans of Behera.</p>
<p>The Federation is not a religious state. It is a state of ordinary people who live and breathe, build their cities and grow old together. Their faith is important to them, but they are at peace with their existence on the islands, and they do not have the luxury of their cousins in <a href="Kamerzin" class="internal-link">Kamerzin</a>, who are far from the Sultanate and can afford to act bellicose towards it.</p>
<h2>The Destruction of Qa</h2>
<p>For many Qahori the extraordinary seafaring tradition of the Federation is understood within the <a href="Qalmyr" class="internal-link">Qalmyr Faith</a> not as a cultural achievement but as a form of mourning, the wandering of a people driven from the land Qastor gave them and unable to return. The loss of Qa to the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae Sultanate</a> across the <a href="Wars of Qahori Subjugation" class="internal-link">Wars of Qahori Subjugation</a> of 413 - 457PA, the event the Qahori call the Destruction of Qa, was experienced as simultaneously a military defeat and a divine verdict. The Qalmyr Faith had counselled against organized resistance. Those who had argued loudest for war, most notably Kamer of the New Fire and the successive leaders of the Alliance of the Old Moon, had been proved unable to prevent the outcome their theology had predicted. That the elder Ranqar the Broken had begged them not to fight and had died watching the catastrophe unfold did not simplify the grief.</p>
<p>The survivors who took to the sea carried both the loss of their homeland and the question of what their own choices had cost them. The community that eventually became the Federation settled on the Qahori Archipelago and rebuilt around the faith's pacific principles, as excellent sailors and traders, maintaining mercantile relationships that the outside world found surprising given the hostility the Sultanate might have been expected to sustain. Their dream of returning home was present in every prayer and absent from every practical calculation.</p>
<h2>The Kamerzin</h2>
<p>A generation after the Destruction of Qa, the Federation's commitment to the pacific principles of its faith was tested by the emergence of the Kamerzin, a militant faction that could not accept the Federation's accommodation with survival. They gathered those among the diaspora who agreed and sailed east into the Niryd Sea with promises to return one day in conquest. They reached the island of Durennis in the far east of Nirydia and built a civilization there that has regarded the Sultanate as an enemy across the centuries since. The Federation and the <a href="Kamerzin" class="internal-link">Kamerzin</a> have not been the same people since the moment the fleet divided, and the Qahori do not speak of their eastern cousins with either approval or condemnation, only with the silence of a people that understands exactly why they left and cannot say they were wrong.</p>
<h2>Abaleq and the Second Path</h2>
<p>In the 8th century the Federation lost the Fazaar Isles, where a religious schism led by the prophet Glarraq, who became <a href="Elabar" class="internal-link">Elabar</a>, ended in the founding of the <a href="Path of the Second Moon" class="internal-link">Path of the Second Moon</a> and of the kingdom of <a href="Abaleq" class="internal-link">Abaleq</a>. The Federation regards the Zolraii as heretics and most devout Qahori do not trade with them, but it did not lose everything: the city of Yaran on the isle of Eheribi remains Federation, and it is there that the traders who do sail to the Fazaar Isles dock.</p>
<h2>Faith</h2>
<p>The Qahori follow the First Path of Qalmyr, the orthodox path of Qastor, in the form of the Enduring, who hold to the teaching of Ranqar the Broken that the path of Qastor demands endurance rather than the sword. It has no hierarchy beyond the elders of each chiefdom and port and the keepers of its shrines. A smaller number are adherents of the <a href="Path of the Third Moon" class="internal-link">Path of the Third Moon</a>, whose followers have formed the core of the <a href="Third Moon Fleet" class="internal-link">Third Moon Fleet</a>, an effort that does not sail under the flag of the Federation.</p>
<h2>Bone-Craft</h2>
<p>The Qahori keep the bone-craft of old Qa as the Qari everywhere do. Their sailors and guards wear armour of carved bone plates, which does not rust in the sea air, and the fittings, ornaments and navigators' charts of their ships are carved from bone. The finest bone, that of the <a href="Igulan" class="internal-link">Igulan</a> and the <a href="Ossur" class="internal-link">Ossur</a> of Il-Haena, reaches the islands through the ports of the Sultanate, and pieces carried out of Qa after the Destruction are heirlooms in the old families.</p>
<h2>Foreign Relations</h2>
<p>The Federation trades with the Elbae Sultanate, which holds Il-Haena and whose ports its ships use freely, and with anyone else who will buy. The <a href="Krestasi" class="internal-link">Krestasi</a> of the <a href="Risen Reef" class="internal-link">Risen Reef</a> are rarely traded with but not ignored. The Qarzi are hated, and the Zolraii of Abaleq shunned.</p>
