<p>In the far east lie the lands of Kherova around the grand lake Kher. The last bulwark of the so called civilized world, the people of Kherova have long kept to themselves and value tradition, honesty and hard work. An oddity in the grand stage of the Continent, Kherova is the only country in the world where <a href="Human" class="internal-link">humans</a> and <a href="dwarf" class="internal-link">dwarves</a> coexist in every place of society in a synallagma that predates most of history. The Khatrym is placed on the trade routes between eastern nirydia and western Ilrakhan and has acted throughout many times in history as a communicator and gateway between the two continents, similar to their elven neighbours across the southern waves.</p>


<h3>Geography</h3>
<p>The defining feature of Kherova is the great Lake Kher, which sits in the Kher-Basin at the southern slopes of the Mistspires. Often described as the Kher Sea, it is the world's largest lake by far and reaches depths of over 1000 metres.</p>
<p>The Kulum Lowlands in the south are the country's access to the rich trade of the Pilvian Gulf and the climate grows notably warmer here, whereas the eastern half of the nation reaches into the <a href="Nirydia" class="internal-link">Nirydian Steppe</a> and the desolated northern wastes of Thamiras Rest.</p>
<p>The lake itself is also bordered by the smaller Kurini mountains in the south.</p>
<p>Central Kherova is hilly and temperate, the lake itself creating a very welcoming and hospitable climate.</p>

<hr class="wiki-hr">
<h3>Demographics</h3>
<p>Due to the lack of a central census like in countries population numbers are merely estimates here, but the largest settlements have known to conduct countings of their own, so we can estimate a population of above 2 million.</p>
<p>Around 75% of the country's citizens are kher human, while kher dwarves make up almost the entire remaining quarter, although <a href="Orc" class="internal-link">orcish</a> and half orcish minorities are prominent in the southern towns.</p>
<p>The capital and largest city, Kher itself, is home to almost 60000 souls, making it one of the largest human settlements on the continent, while the towns of Erbil and Kairos are also sizable communities of likely between 15 and 20 thousand people.</p>
<p>The Dwarven Hold of Daban-Kol makes up for a vast majority of the dwarvish population of the country but still many humans have made their home here as well.</p>

<h3>Religion</h3>
<small><i>see <a href="Kethricism" class="internal-link">Kethricism</a></i></small>
<p>Almost the entire country worships the state religion, the Gethric denomination of <a href="Kethricism" class="internal-link">Kethricism</a>, with the head of the church being the Patriarch of Kher.</p>
<p>Remnants of Spiritworship are still present however, especially in very isolated communities like the mountain villages south of Lake Kher.</p>
<p>The country's power rests clearly within the hands of the Khat, the absolute ruler of Kherova, however the Patriarch of kher and the Kethricist Clergy enjoy many privileges and great influence, making them the strongest institution of the country after the actual government.</p>

<hr class="wiki-hr">
<h3>Politics</h3>
<p>Kherova is ruled by the Kath, currently Tiblin IX of the Heriod Dynasty that took power in the late 7th century PA.</p>
<p>The Khat of Kherova has always been a descendant of the legendary Hero Khariel, who slew the Wyrm Thamira and married her half-dragon daughter in the events of the legendary poem, “The fated journey of Khariel”.</p>
<p>The largest power struggle within the country is that of the Akaz of <a href="Daban-Kol" class="internal-link">Daban-Kol</a> and the Patriarch of Kher, so the conflict at the foot of the Throne between dwarves and Clergy.</p>
<p>This has stemmed largely from the divide between the fully integrated kheri dwarves of the country, and the more isolated citizens and population of Daban-Kol, especially as the current Patriarch Vitrik I is himself a dwarf who has made no secret of his disapproval of the semi autonomous status of the Hold.</p>
<p>The greatest noble houses of Kherova enjoy an immense amount of power and leeway, namely the of Erbil, Zigrati, Iblisi, Zirad and ALdani and Adsali with especially the Zigrati making up the largest opposition to the crown due to a dormant and mostly ignored claim to the Throne itself.</p>

<p>Sharing their roots as well as close cultural and religious ties with each other, the southern Principality of  <a href="Azuvke" class="internal-link">Azuvke</a> has always been the closest ally to the Khatrym and ever since the destruction of Kulum has enjoyed a great influx of trade from Kherova as it is now being used as the Khatrysm greatest Port, at least until New Kulum has been expanded.</p>

<h2>History</h2>
<small><i>see <a href="Rulers of Kherova" class="internal-link">The Rulers of Kherova</a></i></small>
<h4>The Ancient Kheri and the Age of Cities</h4> <p>The deep history of the Kheri peoples before the unification of their city states is known only in fragments, oral traditions preserved by the Ankheri communities of Kairos, mythological cycles of which the Fated Journey of Khariel is the most celebrated, and the records of outside observers who encountered the Kheri from across their borders. The most detailed of these external accounts come from the archival tradition of the <a href="Elven Dynasties" class="internal-link">Meter Dynasty</a>. These dynastic records describe a people already settled in recognizable form around Lake Kher for centuries before any documented conflict as organized into city states of which Kairos, likely founded in the 13th century BA, was the oldest and Kher itself, dating to the early 9th century BA, the largest and wealthiest. The Meter accounts portray the Kheri as deeply resistant to outside interference, "a people whose walls are their character as much as their stone," and note persistent clashes along the lake basins western approaches where elven administrative ambitions met Kheri grit.</p> <p>The Kheri were never absorbed. Their city states warred with each other, traded with each other, and maintained the deep mutual familiarity due to their proximity and shared language, a condition that persisted for centuries and required an external threat to finally overcome. The shadow that hung most darkly over the pre unification period was not any foreign power but an internal one: the rise of <a href="Merzigal" class="internal-link">Merzigal of Alahmid</a> in the northern reaches, whose blood sacrifices and daemon consultations cast a pall over the lake basin communities for generations and whose legacy would outlast him by centuries, shaping Kherovan attitudes toward arcane practice fundamentally.</p> <h4>Tiblin the Strong </h4> <p>The process that eventually produced the unified Khatrym began not with a political vision but with a military emergency. When the expansionist Kingdom of <a href="Rean" class="internal-link">Rean</a> pushed eastward into the Kheri river lands in the mid 2nd century PA, the city states of Kher, Erbil and Kairos set aside their habitual rivalries and met the invaders together at the Battle of the Yevan River in 156PA, where the coalition force under the command of Tiblin of Kher, later styled Tiblin the Strong, broke the Reani advance and drove it back permanently.</p>

<p>In the decades that followed, the city states began formalizing what the war had demonstrated in practice: trade agreements deepened into defensive pacts and Tiblins heirs as Lords of Kher across five generations gradually transformed the loose coalition into something approaching a confederal polity. The decisive moment came not from the Kheris own ambition but from the needs of their northern neighbours: when <a href="Daban-Kol" class="internal-link">Daban-Kol</a> called on the city states for assistance in its war with the Hold of <a href="Tizin-Kol" class="internal-link">Tizin-Kol</a>, the coalition responded as a single force, and upon the conflicts conclusion the four communities of Kher, Erbil, Kairos and the Ankheri settlements  agreed to make permanent what circumstance had repeatedly demonstrated to be necessary. The Day of Brothers in 262PA, still celebrated annually across Kherova, marked the formal establishment of the Khatrym under the rule of Kher as its strongest city, with Giorats son crowned as Tiblin II "the Unifier", the first formal Khat, taking his regnal name in deliberate honour of the ancestor whose victory at the Yevan River had made everything that followed possible.</p>

<h3>Bagsar III and the End of the Khari Line</h3> <p>The final reign of the Khari Dynasty was brief and in many respects its most consequential. Bagsar III, who took the throne in 581PA as the sixteenth Khat of his line, ruled for only twelve years but those twelve years closed a chapter that had been building since the days of Tiblin IV and resolved the question of what to do about the long shadow of <a href="Merzigal" class="internal-link">Merzigal of Alahmid</a>. The <a href="Kherovan Inquisition" class="internal-link">Kherovan Inquisition</a> was formally established in 585PA under Bagsars direct authority, charged with hunting the Abmerzigal, securing Merzigals lost relics and research, and addressing the accumulated occult dangers his legacy had left scattered across the lake basin. Two years later in 587PA the Inquisition conducted its first great act, the destruction and salting of the fortress of Alahmid itself, a centuries old monument to unchecked arcane power now finally reduced to rubble and guarded ruin. These were the Khari lines final acts of consequence, clearing debts that had gone unpaid for nearly three centuries. Bagsar III died in 593PA without a clear legitimate heir, his direct line ending with him, and the Khatrym he passed on was one whose greatest institutions had just been founded and whose throne was suddenly, catastrophically, without a clear successor.</p> <h3>The Era of the Empty Seat (593PA - 661PA)</h3> <p>The 68 years that followed Bagsar III's death were the most politically turbulent in Kherovan history. Two noble houses stepped forward simultaneously to claim nearest kinship to the extinct Khari line: House Zirad, lords of the eastern highlands around Pretas, and House Aldani, stewards of the southern coastal city of Igris. The remaining great houses of Kherova. Erbil, Zigrati, Iblisi and Adsal retreated into effective autonomous rule of their own territories, continuing to function as regional powers while the question of who sat the throne of Kher remained unanswered. The Khatrym did not fully fracture, held together less by any governing authority than by the inertia of a realm that had functioned for over three centuries and survived strenuously without direction from above.</p> <p>Into this vacuum stepped two institutions in very different ways. Patriarch Kelsar II of the Kherovan Patriarchate moved deliberately and without haste to consolidate the churchs position as the indispensable center of Kherovan civic life: mediating disputes between noble houses, providing administrative continuity where the crown was gone, and making it increasingly clear that the Patriarchates endorsement would be the necessary condition of any eventual Khat. The Inquisition, still young and still defining its own mandate, expanded its operations across the same years. While not taking sides in the succession dispute but using the absence of strong royal oversight to embed itself more deeply into the fabric of Kherovan institutions than a strong Khat might have permitted. By the mid 20s it was apparent to any observer that whoever emerged from the dispute would not merely need to defeat their rivals, they would need the Patriarchs blessing to be considered legitimate at all. The church had made itself a throne without sitting on one.</p> 
<p>The counterweight to this consolidation of clerical authority came from an unexpected direction. Erramun Borit, Akaz of <a href="Daban-Kol" class="internal-link">Daban-Kol</a>, declined to involve himself in the succession dispute but equally refused to withdraw from Kherovan political affairs . Operating on the long timescales that dwarven governance characteristically sustains, Borit positioned Daban-Kol as the neutral economic and arbitration anchor of a Nation whose political centre had collapsed. Trade agreements between fractious noble houses were brokered through the Hold. Disputes over resources and territorial margins that might otherwise have escalated found resolution through dwarven mediation whose neutrality none of the competing factions could seriously challenge. The mining city of Duv-Kol, founded in the late 4th century as the most visible symbol of Kheri human and dwarf unity, flourished precisely in this period. Duv-Kols marble trade expanding, its mixed population growing and its prosperity a living argument that Kherovas deepest strength lay not in the question of who sat the throne but in the productive cooperation that had defined the people of the lake basin for centuries. The Era of the Empty Seat did not break Kherova partly because Erramun Borit refused to let it. The Patriarch and the Akaz both emerged from the period stronger than they had entered it, and the rivalry between their institutions, each understanding itself as the true anchor of the Kherovan state, took root in these decades and has never fully resolved. It is the defining tension of Kherovan institutional life in 927PA as clearly as it was in 650PA.</p>

<h3>The Heriod Dynasty begins</h3> <p>The resolution of the Era of the Empty Seat came not through negotiation or the exhaustion of the rival claimants but through the emergence of a third line that had waited out the deadlock entirely. The House of Heriod, descended from Heriod Uraz Khari, a brother of Bagsar III who had never sought the throne in his own lifetime, had spent decades accumulating quiet influence while House Zirad and House Aldani consumed their political capital in mutual opposition. By the mid 7th century the heirs of both original claimants had inherited a dispute without the personal legitimacy or the coalition strength their predecessors had possessed, and the Heriod line, was in a position to act. Tiblin VII, taking the dynasty's most honoured name as his own, formally claimed the Khatrym in 661PA with the crucial endorsement of Patriarch Kelsar II, whose blessing transformed a dynastic argument into a legitimate succession and confirmed what the Era had demonstrated: that no Khat could rule Kherova without the churchs sanction. Tiblin VII understood that a throne won through clerical endorsement and careful politicking was not the same as a throne won through demonstrated leadership, and that the great houses  would remain restless until the new dynasty proved itself something more than an institutional compromise.</p>
<p> His answer was the <a href="Yellow War" class="internal-link">Yellow War</a>. Launched in 660PA the campaign itself serving as the proof of consolidation, the conflict with <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Grevenna</a> over the Yellow Moor and the lands surrounding <a href="Kulum" class="internal-link">Kulum</a> gave the broken apart Kherovan nobility a common enemy and Tiblin VII a stage on which to lead rather than merely reign. Grevennas armies dominated in open field, but their arcane advantages were neutralized entirely by Kherovas Spellbreaker regiments, and the long siege of Kulum failed to break the city. Eight years of grinding war ended at the peace table in 668PA, where Tiblin VII signed the settlement with Grand Lord Stefan I of Grevenna in person at Kulum. With Kulum acknowledged as Kherovan, the Opuzna delta and large stretches of the Yellow Moor conceded as de jure Grevenni. While it planted the seeds of future crisis in the arrangement it created it was Tiblin VII's peace, concluded under his authority and bearing his name. The Heriod Dynasty had its mandate.</p>

<h3>Division from Gethricism</h3> <p>The Kheri peoples converted to Gethricism in 284PA, but they arrived at the faith carrying two centuries of accumulated experience with what arcane power could produce, the shadow of <a href="Merzigal" class="internal-link">Merzigal of Alahmid</a> and the memory of the Wermian Incursion ensured that the Kherovan understanding of the Gethric message was, from the beginning, inflected by a horror of arcane practice that the Church of Iztaw never fully shared. For a century and a half this divergence was cultural rather than institutional until the canonisation of <a href="Tronos" class="internal-link">Tronos of the Veil</a> at the Conclave of 410PA forced the question into the open. To the Kherovan clergy, the canonisation of an arcanist whose research had contributed to the very tradition the Wermian Crimson Circle drew upon was not merely controversial but rath a demonstration that Iztaw and Kherova understood the Prophet's message in ways that could not ultimately be reconciled. Under <a href="Adzul I" class="internal-link">Archbishop Adzul I</a>, the First Council of Opuzna convened in 458PA and produced the Doctrine of Dualism, the formal theological declaration that divine and arcane power were categorically different in origin and spiritual consequence. When High-Bisop Taromir I of Iztaw rejected the Doctrine without qualification, the Second Council of Volazc convened in 471PA and issued the Kethric Declaration, establishing the Kherovan Patriarchate as a fully independent institution and Adzul I as its first Patriarch. The denomination that resulted, <a href="Kethricism" class="internal-link">Kethricism</a>, has governed Kherovan religious life for 456 years. For a full account of its theology and practice, see <a href="Kethricism" class="internal-link">Kethricism</a>.</p>
<h3>Relations with the orcs</h3>
<p>After the destruction of the orcish tribes of <a href="Wermia" class="internal-link">Wermia</a> (specifically the tribes of Qa) and their exodus to the <a href="Qahori" class="internal-link">Qahori</a> Archipelago south of the Kurini Mountain range, Kherova has taken a largely friendly and sympathetic stance to the newly established <a href="Qahori" class="internal-link">Qahori Federation</a>.</p>

<hr class="wiki-hr">
<h3>Wars of Elbae aggression</h3>
<p>When the elven <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Sultanat</a> attacked <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Grevenna</a> in what is now known as the 1st War of Elbae in 750PA, Kherovan forces joined in the defensive coalition together with the nations of <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a> and <a href="Grachy" class="internal-link">Grachy</a> to defend their neighbour.</p>
<p>After the conclusion of the war and the defeat of the Sultanate, Kherova gained international renown for its fierce warriors and extremely useful anti-magical expertise against the magical supremacy of the Sultanate.</p>
<p>Nineteen years later, Kherovan troops were once again ready to defend their western allies during the 2nd Elbae Invasion disaster struck: the ancient enemies to the east, the <a href="Bihra" class="internal-link">Bihra</a> Khans of <a href="Nirydia" class="internal-link">Nirydia</a> had united for the first time in recorded history under a creature by the name of Jigallor, his grandson had since taken control of this Khaganate and used the distracted position of Kherova to strike the eastern cities in 782PA.</p>
<p>Jigallors Horde devastated eastern Kherova, destroying the Fortress Cities of Adsal and Iblis, laying waste to Dalkil and laying siege to Erbil, one of the three jewels of Kherova.</p>
<p>The Invaders were beaten back, the siege relieved and Jigallor fell during the Battle of Erbil, however the sudden retreat of the extremely valuable kherovan forces spelled Doom for <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Grevenna</a>, and was largely responsible for the loss of the 2nd War of Elbae Aggression leading to hostilities and enmity between Grevenna and Kherova to this date.</p>
<p>Kherova would not join the 3rd Coalition and maintain neutral relations to the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Sultanat</a>, even going so far as to refuse to sign the <a href="Treaty of Danipol" class="internal-link">Treaty of Danipol</a> in 815PA.</p>

<hr class="wiki-hr">
<h3>Fall of Kulum</h3>
<p>In the Year 820PA the centuries old port City of Kulum, access point to Pilvian Gulf and all southern trade, gave rise to one of the most infamous Arcanists in known history: <a href="Ahrmiz Saceren" class="internal-link">Ahrmiz Saceren</a> also known as Ahrmiz the Conjurer.</p>
<p>His cult renounced the Kethricist principles and more or less enslaved the city, abusing a Crisis of government in Kher.</p>
<p>Ahrmiz ruled Kulum with fear but also a promise of worldly power, gathering a surprising amount of followers around him; it was only due to a party of brave heroes and the actions of the empowered <a href="Kherovan Inquisition" class="internal-link">Kherovan Inquisition</a> that what remained of Kulum could be freed, and although the Cult itself split apart and survived only in hiding, rumours of Ahrmiz's promised resurrection through occult means still persist.</p>
<p>The Inquisition ruled the city lost to the lingering curses and the Kherovan Government sponsored the settlement of New Kulum which grew rapidly in the last century.</p>
<p>The <a href="Treaty of Kulum" class="internal-link">Treaty of Kulum</a>, signed as a result of this crisis by most nations on the continent, gave Kherovan Inquisitors international legal authority to apprehend remnants of the Cult, a practice that is often criticised by local lords and authorities as well abused by individual agents of the Inquisition that use it as an opportunity to enact secret missions and witch-hunts across the Continent.</p>
