<p>The Great Khaganates of the Steppe were the realms of the Steppe <a href="Bihra" class="internal-link">Bihra</a> whose Khans ruled the whole of the <a href="Niryd Steppe" class="internal-link">Niryd Steppe</a>, and often vast lands beyond it, either directly or through tributaries. Each took its name from the Khan who founded it, and most did not long survive the death of the line that held it together, breaking apart into lesser Khaganates and tribes of would be rulers. The last of them was the <a href="Jigallor Khaganate" class="internal-link">Jigallor Khaganate</a>, which fell in 794PA, and since then the steppe has been divided between rival Khaganates, none of which has been able to claim the power of the old Great Khans.</p>  
<h2>The Great Khan</h2>  
<p>A Great Khan was raised by a mixture of conquest, the strength and mutation of their body and the acclamation of the shamans of <a href="Bihra Shamanism" class="internal-link">Bihra Shamanism</a>, in keeping with the steppe belief in the triumph of the physical. Where two Khans claimed the steppe, the matter could be settled by single combat, as at the Duel of the Great Khans in 513PA, but this custom has slowly faded over the last century. The lesser Khans who accepted a Great Khan owed him service in war and tribute, and in turn ruled tributaries of their own, from the farming communities of the steppe rivers to entire foreign realms.</p>  
<p>The power of a Great Khaganate rested on the person of its Khan and on the loyalty of the lesser Khans, and the death of a Great Khan in battle was often enough to break it in a moment, as happened to the Luchun at Verivale and to the Jigallor at Adsal.</p>  
<h2>History</h2>  
<h3>Genbela and Odovo</h3>  
<p>The first Great Khaganates in the records of the neighbouring peoples are those of Genbela, which rose in 298PA, and of Odovo, which rose in 415PA, though the <a href="Kheri" class="internal-link">Kheri</a> had fought the Khans of the steppe long before them. For a century the two Khaganates divided the steppe between them. It was in their time that the warrior Vulg, a Steppe Bihra beside whom even their greatest Khans were said to pale, was defeated by <a href="Ralond" class="internal-link">Ralond</a> of Erbil in 428PA. Both Khaganates came to an end in 513PA with the Duel of the Great Khans.</p>  
<h3>Luchun</h3>  
<p>Khan Luchun rose in 536PA and built the largest Khaganate of the Steppe Bihra ever assembled, ruling from Merasa. In the south the Luchun subjugated the Bihra realms of <a href="Ekkam" class="internal-link">Ekkam</a> in 551PA and <a href="Az" class="internal-link">Az</a> in 578PA after the Battle of Shaltrees, and the Ganoa Valley gave its harvests in tribute to the Khans for over a century. The Khans hunted the practitioners of the <a href="Soulbond" class="internal-link">Art of the Soulbond</a> throughout their southern lands. The Khaganate ended in 671PA when <a href="Gaden" class="internal-link">Gaden</a> of Drizan slew the Great Khan Luchun in personal combat at the Battle of Verivale, breaking his armies and his realm at once. Its southern ports passed to Gaden as a protectorate and later became the <a href="Kareli Hierarchy" class="internal-link">Kareli Hierarchy</a>.</p>  
<h3>Jigallor</h3>  
<p>Khan Jigallor rose in 685PA and founded the last of the Great Khaganates, which ruled the whole steppe and many lands beyond it, most of them through tributaries. Every Khan of the line bore the name Jigallor and all were of one blood. In Lihbare each was known as Jigallor who is of the Blood of the Jigallor, a title commonly translated as Jigallor son of Jigallor. The Khaganate kept no fixed capital.</p>  
<p>In 782PA, while the armies of <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Kherova</a> were fighting in the west during the <a href="Wars of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">2nd War of Elbae Aggression</a>, the reigning Jigallor struck the eastern Kherovan cities. His horde destroyed the fortress cities of Adsal and Iblis, laid waste to Dalkil and besieged Erbil, and although the siege was relieved and the Khan driven back, the sudden withdrawal of the Kherovan forces from the west contributed greatly to the defeat of Grevenna. Twelve years later, in 794PA, the last Jigallor fell at the Battle of Adsal and the Khaganate broke apart.</p>  
<h3>After Adsal</h3>  
<p>The heirs of the Jigallor divided the horde between them, and the steppe has not been united since. The <a href="Duol Khaganate" class="internal-link">Duol Khaganate</a> rose to primacy in 851PA and its Khan was at first accepted as overlord by the other great Bihra, but they later refused him service. The Duol Khan still styles himself the Khan of Khans, yet in the modern day the Duol, <a href="Anatai Khaganate" class="internal-link">Anatai</a>, <a href="Yadu Khaganate" class="internal-link">Yadu</a> and <a href="Gökol Khaganate" class="internal-link">Gökol</a> Khaganates are effectively rivals, and none is counted among the Great Khaganates by most scholars.</p>  
<h2>Other Khaganates</h2>  
<p>Many Khaganates of the steppe never ruled it as a whole. The Xungin Khaganate held the northern and north-western steppe and was among the largest of these, until it was defeated by the <a href="Twintail Imperium" class="internal-link">Twintail</a> Imperator Natan VII "the Red" in a campaign he began as crown prince in 338PA, whose conquests south of the Mondi River began the line that became the <a href="Red Iron Towers" class="internal-link">Red Iron Towers</a>. In the modern day the four great Khaganates each hold lesser Khaganates as vassals. The Tuchewa, vassals of the Yadu, occupied a contested zone south of the Tower line for several decades until <a href="Kubil no Vang" class="internal-link">Kubil no Vang</a> destroyed them at the Battle of the Amethyst Caverns in 914PA, and the Elek, also vassals of the Yadu, took the mountains and hills between the steppe and the Gorati Highlands in 912PA.</p>
