Unlike most of the Pea they belong to none of the three great federations, for their villages lay too far from other tribes for them ever to bother joining one, and they remain a small and independent people under their own chief, at present Hinmul. They speak the Pea Narzoi dialect and follow <a href="Chianism" class="internal-link">Chianism</a>.</p> <p>The Vudi were among the first peoples of the island to deal with the Old World. In 842PA the Turpadori explorer <a href="Jaime di Dilin" class="internal-link">Jaime di Dilin</a> negotiated with them, and they gave him great respect and enjoyed the trade he brought. When Alinanora was founded on their shores in 900PA the Vudi at first profited from the arrival of its settlers, but within a short time they had lost hundreds of their people to various diseases.</p> <p>Their fortunes have declined steadily since. The Vudi were the first victims of the <a href="Narzoi Slavetrade" class="internal-link">Narzoi Slavetrade</a>, and they are now regularly kidnapped and made to work as slaves, and their settlements are waning. The leadership of Alinanora refrains from openly attacking them, preferring to avoid the kind of confrontation that has brought the <a href="Odise" class="internal-link">Odise</a> and <a href="Almersport" class="internal-link">Almersport</a> to war, but a people once met with great respect is today treated increasingly worse.</p>
<p>Because the Vudi belong to none of the three great federations, they have no Ulechiam to speak for them and few neighbours near enough to come to their aid. Their people are taken from the villages of the delta and the coast in raids that are never openly attributed to Alinanora, and many are put to work for the settlement and in the households of its Turpadori masters, who call them Wards of the Candle and claim to be saving their souls. The Vudi themselves hold to Chianism, and with every village emptied more of their own ways are lost.</p>
