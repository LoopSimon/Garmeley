It is the successor of the <a href="Knights of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">Knights of the Gryphon</a>, who reconstituted themselves at Urbridge Keep in 736PA to destroy the hobgoblin occupation of the west and recover the lands beyond the Trinmo. The knights are the state: the Order is a stratocracy under a Lord-Protector, and its lands are held and ruled by its own members. It is far smaller than <a href="Eldmar" class="internal-link">Eldmar</a> or <a href="Gelmsburg" class="internal-link">Gelmsburg</a>, but its heavy cavalry and its saint, <a href="Dorian" class="internal-link">Dorian</a>, are known across Ur. Its people are Ares humans, like those of Eldmar, but unlike Eldmar, Urbridge affords women little in the way of rights.</p> <h2>Geography</h2> <p>The Order holds the middle of Ur, between the Trinvale cities on the Trinmo in the west and the Eld Valley in the east. Its capital, Urbridge, stands in the east of the realm, where the Eld River meets the mighty Melm on its way south into <a href="Garmeley" class="internal-link">Garmeley</a>. The realm is divided into three territories, Leston in the north, Urbridge about the capital, and Whitelute in the south, the old seat of the Knights and the capital of Arester before them. Starwish Lake lies inland, and from it the river Thim flows east to meet the Melm. Greenslopes in the north-west was once the seat of the Rassel dynasty, and Rassel, Fenpool and Alcast are towns of the Order. The Sourlands lie in the south-west, and beyond them and to the south rise the hills of the Lock.</p> <p>Gelmsburg lies to the north of the Order, Eldmar to the east, and Garmeley to the south, whose city of Jorrenstead stands just across the Melm from Urbridge. To the north-west are the <a href="Trifflands" class="internal-link">Trifflands</a>, a godless commune of warriors that the Order treats as a nuisance, and to the south-west the cities of the <a href="Trinvale" class="internal-link">Trinvale Republic</a>, the only great Urien successor state that openly trades with the hobgoblins, which is a constant source of friction with the Order. West of the Trinmo lie the hobgoblins of <a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a>.</p> <p>Borders mean little in the dangerous lands of Ur these days. No man's land lies between many a town and city of the Order, and the roads are hardly as safe as one would wish.</p>

<h2>Government and the Order</h2> <p>The Lord-Protector is the head of the Order and Master of the Knights of the Gryphon. The office has been held by several of the powerful dynasties that have established themselves within the Order, which are, ironically for a knightly brotherhood, very feudal in their ways. House Goodwind has given the Order five Lord-Protectors, all of whom took the name Gorlan as a mantle of pride, and the present one is Gorlan V. Lord-Protectors of other lineages have held the office as well. In practice the Order is an internally biased club, in which the great dynasties look after their own, and a son cannot count on following his father.</p> <p>The lands of the Order are led by three Landmasters, of Whitelute, Urbridge and Leston, who answer to the Lord-Protector and are comparable to a count or baron in their duties. Below them the countryside is held by barons. The barons are seen as rich landholders, and they keep their titles and many of their assets, but they pay tribute to the Order and are weak nobles, better than burghers of high standing in little more than name. No woman can rise in the ranks of the Order.</p> <h2>Chapters of the Order</h2> <p>The knights of the Order belong to chapters. The Shields of Summer, which Dorian founded within the Knights, were far more dominant in the early days of the Order than they are now, and in the Hobgoblin Invasion they were led in the field by Saint <a href="Kifrir" class="internal-link">Kifrir</a> the Mirror-Dwarf. Since then other chapters have established themselves, such as the Moonblades, who are associated with Saint <a href="Luca" class="internal-link">Luca</a>. In reality, however, the politics of the Order are controlled by the <a href="Scions of Markur" class="internal-link">Scions of Markur</a>, who operate within it in a variant of their own, the Sunwardens. Many knights belong to no chapter at all, but for obvious political reasons they do not tend to rise to power.</p> <h2>Religion</h2> <p>The Order is <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethric</a>. Its knights follow the example of Saint Dorian the Kind, a Garmish commoner who popularised knightly vows as Gethric oaths, and who stands for peace, patience and harmony with nature. Dorian is called the Prince of Summer and is shown with the sunflower. The Lord-Protector signs the <a href="Wermian Accords" class="internal-link">Wermian Accords</a>.</p> <h2>The Order at Arms</h2> <p>The strength of the Order is its heavy cavalry. The sun on the gleaming plate of its charges in the <a href="Wars of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">Wars of the Gryphon</a> gave rise to a famous saying, the Light of Urbridge, which is told of the dawn charge that broke the siege of Fort Garlin in 883PA. The estates of the Order support its horse, and its wealth is in warhorses, grain and the levies and dues of its towns. In numbers the Order is not at all on the level of realms such as Eldmar and Gelmsburg. It could not man Gobsfall Keep, which Arsin of Gilkeep had held in 696PA, and begrudgingly handed it over to Gelmsburg after the Second War, when it had lost too many knights at Merbridge. The Order keeps the tradition of the Gryphon riders, and knights still ride out to try to tame a <a href="Gryphon" class="internal-link">Gryphon</a>, almost always fatally.</p> <p>On a small island in Starwish Lake stands the Urvault. It holds a large array of enchanted weapons and armour and a variety of cursed objects that the Order says are too dangerous or too strong to attempt to destroy, and that some suspect it keeps for other purposes.</p> <h2>History</h2> <p>The Knights of the Gryphon were the foremost chivalric order of Urland, older than the kingdom itself, with their seat at Whitelute in the Duchy of Ares. When the hobgoblins came out of the Kortopi Mountains in the winter of 690PA, the Grand Master, Samson the Quick, was killed by Korgaz at the Battle of Hadley. Arsin of Gilkeep took his place and held Gobsfall Keep, where the horde was broken in 696PA. Harlan of the Reym, Duke of Ares, who had not fought at Gobsfall, tried to declare himself King of Arester from his intact lands, and Arsin deposed him, for the knights would govern themselves before they would serve a man who used catastrophe as an opportunity. Arsin is remembered in the Order as a great man, and Harlan as a lesser and corrupt example of what had allowed Urland to fall in the first place.</p> <p>After the Splintering of Urland, Duke Jakub III of Garmeley, whose knights had died at Dursan, took the Earldom of Dim in the Ares Highlands, which the Urien lords regarded as a betrayal. In 736PA the Knights, following the legacy of Arsin, formally reconstituted themselves as the Order of Urbridge, directed entirely at destroying the hobgoblin occupation and recovering the lands west of the Trinmo.</p> <p>The Order took part in the <a href="League of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">League of the Gryphon</a> from its assembly in 841PA on Pearfawl Field near Leston, where the knights and lords of the League swore the Oath at Pearfawl, and fought in both Wars of the Gryphon, in which the League won back significant territory but could not press its advantage west of the Trinmo. The Order is sometimes considered the leader of the League. It has always been less interested in ideas of Urien restoration than the other minors, such as the proud Kingdom of Gelmsburg, and what it wants is the hobgoblins gone and its own lands secure. Gorlan V has twice called for a Third War in recent decades. Both attempts failed, blocked by the rivalry between Gelmsburg and Eldmar.</p> <h2>Urbridge in 927PA</h2> <p>The Order has lost interest in being the wardens of the west. It does little against the hobgoblins, the sorcerers and bandits on the roads, or the ogres and goblins that come down from the Lock, and it has become in effect a body of military landholders trying to enrich themselves. Gorlan V is the exception in his hatred of the hobgoblins, which is obsessive. For about fifteen years he has been sponsored by Schnedder Banking, a new bank in Urbridge founded by the retired adventurer <a href="Harold" class="internal-link">Harold Schnedder</a>, who has always shown discontent for the hobgoblin menace with a passion.</p> <p>The Lord-Protector is about fifty-five years of age and very sick and ailing. He is working to make sure that his son, Aldric, will succeed him, but it is not as certain as he thinks. The Landmaster of Leston, Thadric "Silvernose" Idersby, named for his nasal prosthetic of pure silver, is his rival and is likely to succeed him.</p> <p>In early 926PA Gorlan pledged the Order to the Green Banner of <a href="Coster of Melmbirth" class="internal-link">Coster of Melmbirth</a> in the <a href="Garmish Succession Crisis" class="internal-link">Garmish Succession Crisis</a>, as did the Queen of Eldmar, and their support pushed the Trinvale Republic into the Garmish Alliance. The reasons are religious, but they are not only religious. Garmeley was for centuries a massively rich and powerful neighbour to the south, larger, richer, stronger and more prestigious than the Order, and an older country that was not tied to the failed realm of Urland. Urbridge sees an opportunity. It may look to the valuable Silvermines on the east side of the Lock, and perhaps to Jorrenstead itself, the Pearl of northern Garmeley, though the Lord-Mayor of Jorrenstead is a Green Banner ally. The knights are likely not above becoming vultures and claiming what they want if Garmeley collapses entirely. Meanwhile the war is draining the resources of the Order in the south, and it may spell ill fate for it.</p> <h2>People of Interest</h2> <p>Gorlan, fifth of his name of House Goodwind<br> Lord-Protector of Urbridge, Master of the Knights of the Gryphon</p> <p>Thadric "Silvernose" Idersby<br> Landmaster of Leston</p> <p>Aldric of House Goodwind<br> Son of the Lord-Protector</p> <p><a href="Harold" class="internal-link">Harold Schnedder</a><br> Founder of Schnedder Banking</p> <p>Arsin of Gilkeep<br> Grand Master of the Knights of the Gryphon at Gobsfall Keep</p>
