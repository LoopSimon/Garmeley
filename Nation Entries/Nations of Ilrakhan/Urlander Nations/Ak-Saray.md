It was born in 696PA out of the <a href="Hobgoblin Invasion" class="internal-link">Hobgoblin Invasion</a>, when the Tirbo Clan and the clans that followed it out of the caves settled the lands they had conquered, and the Urien have used the name for the whole country ever since. It has never been a state in the sense the Urien understand the word. Its people, who call themselves the Tirbi in honour of their first warlord, live in great tribes that each rule a region of their own and are divided into clans. Only a Great Boss, a rare figure who has won the following of all the tribes, can lead them as one, and the title is usually invoked only for war against outsiders. Between such wars the tribes are effectively independent realms, and they fight one another as readily as they fight the men across the river. There are hundreds of thousands of hobgoblins in western Ur, though nobody has ever counted them, and the Urien know them as brutal and extremely capable fighters.</p> <h2>Geography</h2> <p>Ak-Saray lies west of the Trinmo, which has been its border with the Urien lands since the Battle of Gobsfall Keep, and reaches back toward the Kortopis. It takes in the old Duchy of Hadley, the Earldom of Torcester and the country around Foarley, once among the quietest and richest lands of Urland. It is a land of lakes, river valleys and forest, cold and wet for much of the year, which is the climate the hobgoblins prefer. Lake Tirbo, which the Urien still call Hadley Lake, lies at its heart. In the north the great forest of Silenttree, which the Tirbi call the Korgaz, runs up toward the Trinbend. The old crown gold mines of Ilsbreak and Songhole, taken in the Invasion, lie in tribal lands. The largest settlement is <a href="Hobgiliath" class="internal-link">Hobgiliath</a>, formerly Dursan, and the other towns of note are Tehel, formerly Foarley, and Mara in the west, but most of the Tirbi live in villages, steadings and the cave halls that the hills give them.</p> <h2>Peoples</h2> <p>Most of the people of Ak-Saray are Tirbi hobgoblins, known in their own tongue as Dirgae, who grow to about two metres and live to around seventy-five years. The other people of the country are the Urna, the human descendants of the river tribe that gave Ur its name. Those who did not flee east between 690PA and 696PA stayed, and over the generations they have lived with and alongside the hobgoblins. Their lot differs from tribe to tribe. Some tribes keep them on reservations, some keep them as slaves, and some treat them decently, arguably better than the peasants of the more oppressive feudal societies such as <a href="Grachy" class="internal-link">Grachy</a>. There were slave revolts in the early 800s, and none of them succeeded. No Urna in western Urland has any illusion of rising up today. Small numbers of goblins and dwarves from the <a href="Underlands" class="internal-link">Underlands</a> come to trade, and a few humans and dwarves are said to follow the teachings of the <a href="The Malagul" class="internal-link">Malagul</a>.</p> <h2>The Tribes</h2> <p>The six greatest tribes are the Unogi of the north, the Hara-Vel, the Krezuke, the Farblades, the Balaqi and the Darkeyes, but they are only the greatest of many, and western Ur is thought to hold up to a hundred independent tribes in all. Each tribe rules a region of its own, each is divided into clans, and the head of a tribe is its Boss. Wars between tribes are common, over hunting grounds, river crossings and old insults, and no tribe answers to any other.</p> <p>Only a Great Boss can bring the tribes together. The title has been given rarely, and it is usually invoked for war against outsiders. The union of the clans under <a href="Tirbo" class="internal-link">Tirbo</a> during the Invasion was the first and greatest of such alliances of the hobgoblin peoples. In 927PA there is no Great Boss, <a href="Hobgiliath" class="internal-link">Hobgiliath</a> remains as the unofficial capital, because by custom the tribes gather there when a Great Boss is to be named, and Boss Tenzea, who has held the city for more than two decades, is counted as head of state by the Urien. Her word carries no further than her own tribe's lands and what she can win by patience, for which she is known. She is a cautious ruler rather than a conqueror.</p> <h2>Society and Culture</h2> <p>Tirbi society is built on the clan, the hall, the hunt and the duel. Ritual combat is the Dirgae way of settling title and honour, and the champion's tradition of annual challenge is still kept, the same custom under which <a href="Korgaz" class="internal-link">Korgaz</a>, the Blade of Noon, held his title for thirty-one years, longer than any champion in Dirgae memory. A champion who falls to a younger challenger with no scar on his back is honoured, and one who runs is not. The Relic Sword of Korgaz, a blade of <a href="Wyrdrock" class="internal-link">Wyrdrock</a>, is believed to remain in Ak-Saray, though outsiders have not seen it in decades. The language of the country is Overgob, and the Tirbi name their lakes, forests and cities after their heroes and clans, as in Lake Tirbo, the Korgaz forest and Hobgiliath itself.</p> <p>The Malagul, a very small community of hobgoblins who follow the teachings of Elamak, the Silence in the Storm, hold only a small part of Silenttree, around Naked Mountain. They refused to join the unified hobgoblin force of the <a href="Wars of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">Wars of the Gryphon</a>, they are more tolerant of other peoples than their kin, and they are left in peace by the neighbouring tribes and by the men across the river.</p> <h2>Religion</h2> <p>see <a href="Kaladi Mysticism" class="internal-link">Kaladi Mysticism</a></p> <p>The faith of the Tirbi is Kaladi Mysticism, the veneration of heroes and leaders who were deified after death, honoured in festivals of rhythm and sound. In Ak-Saray the foremost Kaladi is Tirbo, who sits somewhere between a god and an ancestral hero and who has taken the old place of Jibey of the Blue Fire, a great leader and mage from the time before the escape from the caves who was once the most worshipped of all. Jibey has become the Kaladi of the past and the dead, and the Tirbi turn to him in mourning and at graves. The foremost magical office of the country is the Wyrdspeaker, which joins spiritual counsel with the channeling of magic, and the present holder is <a href="Zurgush" class="internal-link">Zurgush</a>, the Hand of Blight.</p>  <p>The hobgoblins of Ak-Saray are renowned as extremely brutal and competent combatants. Since coming to the surface they have adopted modern plate armour, and they breed dire wolves, which they ride into battle in preference to horses. The few masters who know how to forge Wyrdrock have very little ore to work, and the last known object they made, the Forlorn Shield, dates to 782PA.</p> <h2>Economy</h2> <p>Ak-Saray is an agrarian society, and the tribes live from their fields, their herds and the hunt. Many clans also send their young out as prized soldiers to the rich courts of lands such as <a href="Gisvale" class="internal-link">Gisvale</a> and <a href="Battvia" class="internal-link">Battvia</a> and to the cities of <a href="Blijgrat" class="internal-link">Blijgrat</a>, and others found mercenary bands of their own, though they are usually unwelcome in Ur itself and hated there. Since the Invasion more and more hobgoblins, as small groups, as independent families or as whole clans, have left western Ur, and Ur altogether, to settle in surface realms elsewhere. The tribes also trade with the dwarven and goblin holds of the Underlands with which they have made contact, although the main tunnel to Mitimgar remains closed. The southern tribes, the Balaqi and the Darkeyes among them, also trade openly with the merchants of the Trinvale Republic, the only great Urien successor state that does so, to the hatred of its neighbours.</p>  <p>The Shroomfever and the collapse of Urgil Cave cut the Tirbo Clan off from the <a href="Mitimgar" class="internal-link">Mitimgar</a> cave network, and for a long time the hobgoblins above and the goblins below had no word of each other. That has changed. Small tunnel paths to the larger cave networks have been opened, relations between the Tirbi and the goblins of Mitimgar are surprisingly friendly, and efforts are under way to bring help from Ak-Saray to the city in its long <a href="Granite Crusade" class="internal-link">Granite Crusade</a> against the dwarves of <a href="Ogul-Vosko" class="internal-link">Ogul-Vosko</a>. The great cavern connection of old, at Urgil, is still collapsed.</p> <h2>History</h2> <p>The Tirbo Clan was born in the Dirgae Quarters of Mitimgar. The Shroomfever of 678PA and the collapse of Urgil Cave in 682PA cut it off from its own people, and in the winter of 690PA, under the warlord Tirbo, it came up out of the Kortopis into the western valleys of Urland. The Invasion lasted six years. Its great battles were Elpfield, Foarley and Hadley, the siege of Dursan ended in 695PA, and the horde was stopped at the Trinmo at Gobsfall Keep in 696PA, where Tirbo was killed by the dwarf <a href="Kifrir" class="internal-link">Kifrir</a>.</p> <p>Without Tirbo the united horde fragmented, and the clans settled the land west of the river, in the territory that became Ak-Saray in 696PA. Tirbo had already begun to organise the occupation, but the work was left unfinished, and it was never to be finished. Elamak, who had joined the campaign only out of necessity, refused to cross the Trinmo and founded the Malagul. Korgaz recovered from his wounds and lost his title in ritual combat in Hobgiliath in 700PA.</p> <p>In the following centuries the unity of the first generation dissolved into the tribes of today. The masters of Wyrdrock dwindled with the loss of the ore, and the last known work of their craft, the Forlorn Shield, was made in 782PA. In the early 800s the Urna rose in slave revolts that were put down, and from then on more and more hobgoblins went abroad to sell their swords.</p> <p>The <a href="League of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">League of the Gryphon</a> was assembled in 841PA to destroy Ak-Saray and win back the west. The First War of the Gryphon ended in 843PA with the reconquest of Turv. In the Second War, from 883PA to 888PA, the League again won ground, but the intervention of Zurgush, then nineteen years old, stopped any chance of the League capitalising on its momentum, and the coalition fell apart quickly for political reasons. Neither war reached the heart of the country, and the League's recent attempts to begin a Third War have failed.</p>  <p>The tribes are not ready to unite behind anyone except to defend against large armies of men, and nobody has risen who could ask them to do otherwise. As a structure Ak-Saray is becoming brittle, yet the hobgoblins themselves look strong, individually, as tribes. The war in Garmeley, in the <a href="Garmish Succession Crisis" class="internal-link">Garmish Succession Crisis</a>, has drawn Eldmar and Urbridge, two of the three members of the League, to look south, and some chiefs have noticed, but none has found a following to act on it.</p> <p>The most notable movement is in the north, where the Unogi tribe is leading part of its people back down into the Underlands in support of Mitimgar.</p>   <h2>People of Interest</h2> <p>Boss Tenzea<br> Boss of Hobgiliath, counted head of state of Ak-Saray</p> <p><a href="Zurgush" class="internal-link">Zurgush</a>, the Hand of Blight<br> Wyrdspeaker of Ak-Saray</p> <p><a href="Tirbo" class="internal-link">Tirbo</a><br> Warlord of the united Clans, foremost Kaladi of the Tirbi</p> <p><a href="Korgaz" class="internal-link">Korgaz</a>, the Blade of Noon<br> Champion of the Tirbo Clan during the Invasion</p>
