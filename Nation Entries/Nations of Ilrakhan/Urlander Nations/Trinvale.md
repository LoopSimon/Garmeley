Its position at the Pass of <a href="Ogul-Vosko" class="internal-link">Ogul-Vosko</a> makes it the main way through for the trade of <a href="Battvia" class="internal-link">Battvia</a> and the <a href="Paper Cities" class="internal-link">Buteran Gulf</a> into rich <a href="Garmeley" class="internal-link">Garmeley</a> and the other towns and cities of Urland. It was founded as a union of cities along the southern Trinmo in 775PA, following the Wealsworth Incident of 768PA, out of the old <a href="Nied" class="internal-link">Duchy of Nied</a>. Its two most prominent cities are the trading hubs of <a href="Niedrest" class="internal-link">Niedrest</a> and Calmbrook, but it includes many more towns and communities under its economic and military umbrella. It is less a state than a stitched carpet of many groups, and the individual freedom of its cities and merchants is both the source of its wealth and the reason it is slow to act.</p> <h2>Geography</h2> <p>The Trinmo runs through the Republic from north to south, and most of its cities lie on the river or close to it. Niedrest, the capital, stands on both banks where the Dunbridge crosses the water, and Calmbrook, the old capital of the Duchy of Nied, lies far to the south. Zacin stands in the north-west, the Niedwoods cover much of the west bank, and Lindahall and Gullspire stand on the east bank at the foot of the hills of the Lock. To the west the foothills of the Kortopis rise behind the Republic. There the Hollow Road, an icy canyon through the southern Kortopis, comes down towards Highslope. It is viewed as far too dangerous to cross and is certainly impossible for merchant caravans, though adventurers are said to have done it in the past, and there are rumours of many monsters in the canyon and of ancient ice trolls that have made their home in it.</p>

<p>The Republic faces the hobgoblins of <a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a> across the Trinmo in the north. North of Zacin stands Greenstone Tower, and farther up the river stand Fort Garlin and Merbridge. In the south the Republic borders the Kerlaen Wilderness, a region of rolling hills and dense forests that the Republic nominally owns, though in reality it is a no man's land. Robbers and bandits and worse creatures live there, and so do the warlocks and barbarians of the wild Kerlaeni, who bind the nurdjinn, the djinn of the <a href="Wasteland of Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakhi Wastes</a>, and are said to conduct ancient and horrible rituals of power. There are incidents and small attacks in the far south from time to time, but some small settlements still trade with the wild Kerlaeni.</p> <h2>Peoples</h2> <p>The people of the Republic are mostly Nied humans, the south Urien natives of the old Duchy. In Niedrest about four in five are human, with Garmish, Cevoli and north Urien minorities, and the city has a sizeable community of <a href="Gnome" class="internal-link">gnomes</a>, who live almost entirely in their own quarter, and smaller halfling and elvish communities.</p> <h2>Government</h2> <p>The Republic is an oligarchy, in which the rich merchant dynasties of the cities hold the power. Its head is the Citykeeper of Niedrest, who is by tradition also Lord-Mayor of the Republic and sits in the Glass Palace, the old residence of the Wealsworths. In practice the Union is a stitched carpet. The larger towns and cities are each ruled by a different organisation, some by patrician councils, some by guilds, and some by barons, such as the Baron who holds Falkmere from Falk Keep. Only Niedrest and Lindahall have ever held elections, and those are little more than bribery competitions between a handful of rich merchant dynasties. The Union asks little of its members, and individual freedoms are great. Merchant enterprises are not restricted to trade with anyone, as long as the Republics are not actively at war with them.</p> <p>The Republic has no united army. Zacin is the only city that maintains a standing force of any kind, and its general, Oleg of Zacin, is counted as General of the Trinvale. What makes the individual cities so rich and prosperous also makes them slow to act and averse to centralising ideas such as a common army. The Republic is rich enough to keep retainer companies and mercenaries in great numbers, and it will even hire hobgoblins.</p> <p>The Citykeeper today is Rimond von Junkel of the rich and old merchant dynasty of that name. The celebrations of his second marriage in 923PA were disrupted by the <a href="Niedrest Incident" class="internal-link">Niedrest Incident</a>.</p> <h2>Religion</h2> <p>The Republic is split fairly evenly between <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethricism</a> and <a href="Trinicism" class="internal-link">Trinicism</a>, though Calmbrook and Niedrest, as the biggest cities, are still dominantly high Gethric. Niedrest is the seat of the Bishopric of Saint Fredric's, held by <a href="Nestor IX" class="internal-link">Nestor IX</a> under the Church of Iztaw, which draws many pilgrims to the relic of <a href="Fredric" class="internal-link">Saint Fredric</a> and has long quarrelled with the burghers of the city.</p> <h2>Economy</h2> <p>The Trinvale is a rich region, and it owes this to its position. The trade of Cevolia and Ur meets here, and merchants come down from the Pass of Ogul-Vosko to sell to the towns of Urland and Garmeley. Niedrest and Calmbrook stand in direct competition with each other. Niedrest is known for its timber and carpentry, its artisan metalwork and its breweries, and Calmbrook for its cloth and textiles. The Dunbridge is the only stone bridge over the Trinmo for fifty miles, and its toll gives Niedrest a stranglehold on trade. There are local banks, and the House of Numbers in Niedrest is the largest of them. The local branch of the <a href="The Ottian Syndicate" class="internal-link">Ottian Syndicate</a> is among the most influential guilds of Niedrest.</p> <p>The Republic is the only one of the great Urien successor states that openly trades with the hobgoblins today, to the hatred of the others, and this has led to friction with <a href="Urbridge" class="internal-link">Urbridge</a> in particular. It trades mostly with the southern tribes of Ak-Saray, the Balaqi and the Darkeyes among the most powerful of them. This is possible because the Republic restricts no merchant from trading with anyone it is not actively at war with.</p> <h2>Greenstone Tower and the Northern Forts</h2> <p>Greenstone Tower is not a settlement but an isolated mage tower, and a member of the <a href="Orenburg Pact" class="internal-link">Orenburg Pact</a>. Its Archmage, Gregor Redsight, is firmly with the countries of the <a href="League of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">League of the Gryphon</a> and supports Urbridge and the Republic against the hobgoblins. The Tower's few dealings with the outside world run through Zacin. Before the First War the Tower stood within the hobgoblin lands, and the League's advance in 842PA brought it to the Urien side of the border. Bound by the Pact, its mages do not take the field, but it has tended the League's sick and supplied the garrison of Fort Garlin, which is held to this day by the remnants of the League's veterans and their adventuring followers. Fort Garlin, raised by the League in 842PA, and Merbridge, taken in 886PA, are in effect outposts, manned only by various groupings of adventurers, and today they are supported most actively by the Republic and by <a href="Gelmsburg" class="internal-link">Gelmsburg</a>.</p> <h2>History</h2> <p>The lands of the Republic were once the heart of the Kingdom of the Kerlaens, whose capital, Gibaresh, is today's Calmbrook. After the Kerlaens fell to Urland in 327PA, the Kings of Urland created the Duchy of Nied in 332PA to oversee the old Kerlaen lands, and Niedrest grew in the following years around Pale Rock Castle. When the Kingdom of Urland fell, Jasper of Nied declared in 703PA that he would rule the Duchy as a true successor to Urland until a rightful heir to the Gryphon Throne was found, and for some sixty-five years it was an autonomous realm of its own.</p> <p>In midsummer 768PA Valian Wealsworth, Count of Niedrest, killed Mary Gallersby, the young daughter of one of the city's richest patricians, after a party at the Glass Palace. What followed was a revolt of essentially all of the local patricians against the old nobility, whom the city had long seen as arrogant leeches. The Glass Palace was stormed, and the new regime, which called itself the Council of the Good and Free, executed any noble who would not support it. Duke Henry of Nied marched on Niedrest with an army of mercenaries and the Ducal Guard, but the Council had bought many of them out beforehand, and at Tongmill, a few miles south of Niedrest, the Duke's own warriors turned against him. The Duke was captured, and over the following years the old order was exchanged in the cities of the Duchy. In 775PA the appeased cities proclaimed the Union of the Trinvale Republics. The Maiden with the Bloody Sword on its arms is said to be Mary Gallersby, but she is also the city personified, and a mixture of many other things. The full account is told on the page of <a href="Nied" class="internal-link">Nied</a>.</p> <p>The Republic is not formally a member of the League of the Gryphon, but it supported it from the beginning. It allowed the League to cross its lands to the north, and the cities never mustered a united militia for it, but the Republic made a massive loan to the League, known as the Valesloan, that made the formation of the first League in 841PA possible and financed it. In that sense it can be argued whether the Republic joined or not. The loan was waived in the end, in part because the territories south of Fort Garlin had become farmsteads and river settlements that enriched the south of the Republic, and in part to celebrate the victory of the first League. Garlin and Merbridge were fought over in the <a href="Wars of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">Wars of the Gryphon</a>.</p> <h2>Trinvale in 927PA</h2> <p>In the Niedrest Incident of February 923PA the Kherovan embassy in Niedrest was attacked and its staff murdered. The city guard never found the culprits, and the Incident caused outrage in Niedrest and at the Kherovan embassies abroad. It cost <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Kherova</a> much of its influence in the city. In 925PA Kher condemned the Republic in full, and Kherova became the official financial and political backer of the Green Banner League in the <a href="Garmish Succession Crisis" class="internal-link">Garmish Succession Crisis</a>.</p> <p>The Republic is economically closely connected with the southerners, with Tringlen and <a href="Forrington" class="internal-link">Forrington</a> in particular, and it has a general political aversion to the supporters of the north. In early 926PA <a href="Urbridge" class="internal-link">Urbridge</a> and <a href="Eldmar" class="internal-link">Eldmar</a> pledged themselves to the Green Banner, which more or less forced the hands of Niedrest, Calmbrook and Zacin, and the Republic joined the Garmish Alliance, with Oleg of Zacin as its general. When <a href="Dolm" class="internal-link">Dolm</a> surrendered in late 926PA the Republic was at last connected by land to Hainach and the south of Garmeley. In January 927PA Trinvale contingents held the town of Karolrest against the Knights of Fortune of the north in the Slaughter of Karolrest. Since then reports have come that knights of Urbridge are marching through the Sourlands on Lindahall and later Niedrest, and the Lord-Mayor has hired a host of Balaqi hobgoblins to defend the north of the Republic, a choice that has drawn scorn from those in Ur who still despise the hobgoblins as inhuman monsters.</p> <h2>People of Interest</h2> <p>Rimond von Junkel<br> Citykeeper of Niedrest, Lord-Mayor of the Trinvale Republic</p> <p>Oleg of Zacin<br> General of the Trinvale</p> <p>Gregor Redsight<br> Archmage of Greenstone Tower</p> <p><a href="Nestor IX" class="internal-link">Nestor IX</a><br> Bishop of Saint Fredric's in Niedrest</p>
