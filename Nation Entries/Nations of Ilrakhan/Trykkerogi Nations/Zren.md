<p>The Zren is the name given to the <a href="Vannsi" class="internal-link">Vannsi</a> civilisation of north-eastern <a href="Trykkerog" class="internal-link">Trykkerog</a>, occupying the Zreni Wetlands, a geothermally warmed network of rivers, hot springs and marshlands that lies east of the central mountain range and below the Kaldverjod. Volcanic heat rising through the bedrock keeps the rivers from freezing and sustains an ecosystem of unusual warmth and richness for so northerly a latitude, creating conditions that have allowed a stable Vannsi culture to thrive here for centuries. The Zren represents the largest and most organised of the Vannsi cultures in the northern seas and serves as the primary point of contact between the Vannsi and the human world of <a href="Ilrakhan" class="internal-link">Ilrakhan</a>.</p>

<h2>Land</h2>
<p>The Zreni Wetlands stretch along the southern shore of the Kaldverjod, the deep inlet that cuts into the north-east of the island. Their lower lands are a maze of warm rivers, reed marshes, pools and channels in which the Vannsi move as easily as on land, and in the coldest winters steam rises over them while the sea beyond is frozen. East of the lower wetlands the ground rises to the geyser plateau, where columns of hot water burst from the earth and where the Inamid holds its gatherings. To the north, along the western shore of the Kaldverjod, lie the lands claimed by the <a href="Aelf" class="internal-link">Aelf</a> of <a href="Skemfing" class="internal-link">Skemfing</a>, and to the south and west the Furkenheights and the wilds of Fagnirs Rest separate the Zren from the Bykmåli kingdoms.</p>

<h2>Government</h2>
<p>The Zren is governed by the Inamid, a Council of seven Sages drawn from among the most accomplished and wise of the Zreni Tideseers. The Inamid gathers sporadically rather than continuously, convening at its seat on the geyser plateau, a place of considerable spiritual significance, where the columns of hot water rising from the earth testify to the geothermal forces beneath. When the Sages speak, all clans of the Zren defer to their judgement. Between gatherings, individual clans govern themselves through their own elders, who may bring disputes or troubles before the Inamid when the occasion demands. The Speaker of the Inamid leads the council, though decisions of consequence are made collectively. A Sage serves until death, at which point the remaining council members select a successor from among those they deem worthy, in a process that prizes wisdom and political judgement as much as shamanic ability, and one that many capable Tideseers deliberately avoid, preferring the freedom of seafaring life to a lifetime of service.</p>
<p>The human minority of the Zren holds a single seat among the seven, filled by one of its own elders. Beyond this the Hevri and Jueli of the wetlands are afforded all the privileges of the Vannsi and treated as their equals.</p>

<h2>People</h2>
<p>The Zren is home to a minority population of <a href="Bykmåli" class="internal-link">Hevri</a> and <a href="Bykmåli" class="internal-link">Jueli</a> Bykmåli who have settled among the Vannsi over several centuries and been gradually accepted into Zreni life. Relationships between humans and Vannsi are not uncommon, though the two peoples cannot produce children together, and humans remain a minority.</p>

<h2>Faith</h2>
<p>The Zreni Vannsi follow the <a href="Vannsi Deep Worship" class="internal-link">Vannsi Deep Worship</a> of Tunthelle, the Deep One, whom the Bykmåli equate with <a href="Nyllena" class="internal-link">Nyllena the Saltmother</a>. Sea burial is the universal funerary rite, and many Hevri and Jueli families of the Zren have adopted it over the generations. The humans of the wetlands otherwise keep the faiths they brought with them, the Jueli families mostly Gethric and the Hevri mostly faithful to the old gods of <a href="Bykmali Paganism" class="internal-link">Bykmali Paganism</a>.</p>

<h2>Relations</h2>
<p>The Zren maintains active relations with the Aelf of Skemfing to the north, with whom they share the practical knowledge of arctic survival. Zreni Vannsi, particularly wandering Tideseers, are a sought after presence on Aelf voyages into the frozen seas. Further contact is maintained with Bykmåli settlements to the south and with the <a href="Pehki" class="internal-link">Sehvki</a>, one of the Nostroyan peoples of the Waelajokull coasts and nearby islands. The traders of <a href="Juelfluk" class="internal-link">Juelfluk</a> hold a monopoly on the little exchange that is conducted with the Zren. To the broader kingdoms of <a href="Baeling" class="internal-link">Baeling</a> and Juelfluk, the Zren is a known but remote presence, spoken of in sailors' tales and occasionally visited by the most intrepid of traders, but rarely engaging with the politics of southern Trykkerog.</p>

<h2>History</h2>
<p>Vannsi clans have lived in the warm wetlands below the Kaldverjod since long before the Bykmåli kept records, but for most of their history they lived as separate clans without a common authority. The first council of the Inamid is thought to have met around 392PA, and the Zren as a polity is usually dated from the following century. From 574PA Hevri and Jueli settlers began to integrate into Zreni society, and over the following centuries the human families of the wetlands became a recognised part of the Zren, eventually receiving their seat in the Inamid. The Zren took no part in the rise and fall of the <a href="Baeling Empire" class="internal-link">Baeling Empire</a> or in the Wars of Baeling Succession, and its isolation has spared it most of the conflicts of the south.</p>
