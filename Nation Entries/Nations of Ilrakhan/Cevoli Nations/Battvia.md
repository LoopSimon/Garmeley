<p> The largest successor state of the Kingdom of <a href="Cevolis" class="internal-link">Cevolis</a>, the Land of Battvia is one of rich farmlands and endless coasts, but the history of the Cevoli people is marked by constant infighting and wars. Pressed against the Kortopi mountains, Battvia has lost virtually all control over the rich Paper Cities of the Bay of Butera, and is involved in a century long rivalry with the Duchy of <a href="Jeledo" class="internal-link">Jeledo</a> , the legacy of grand Cevolis slowly drifting further and further into history. Battvia is divided by the Pringlades Forest and the Iago River, its green rich hills plastered by the Ruins of older civilizations like ancient <a href="Orestis" class="internal-link">Orestis</a>.</p>

<h2>History</h2> <p>The defining catastrophe of Battvian history was the <a href="Battvian Civil War" class="internal-link">Battvian Civil War</a> of 792-800PA, in which Prince Guido II "the Lame" refused to honour the Great Privilege, the constitutional charter his grandfather Anselmo I had granted the eastern cities in exchange for increased taxation, over a marriage dispute, triggering an eight-year war that ended with the eastern cities independent as the <a href="Paper Cities" class="internal-link">Paper Cities Federal Union</a>. The loss was not merely territorial, as Battvia's entire eastern coastal territories passed from Battvian sovereignty in a single peace settlement. What remained was the Levi heartland, the Cevoli Highlands and the northern trade routes through the Kortopi passes, significant but a shadow of what Battvia had been. Guido II ruled nine more years as the worst Prince in Battvian history by general consensus and died without heir in 809PA, leaving the throne to his brother. The political relationship between Battvia and the Paper Cities has been defined by the Civil War and its unresolved tensions ever since, Battvia never relinquishing the ambition to recover what it lost, the Paper Cities never forgetting that Battvia has never truly accepted it. For the full account of the conflict see <a href="Battvian Civil War" class="internal-link">Battvian Civil War</a>.</p> <p>The second major conflict were the <a href="Vorellan War" class="internal-link">Vorellan Wars</a> of 910-919PA, in which the city of Vorella, the largest city near the Pass of Ott and the primary commercial gateway between the Kortopi Mountains and Meadland, attempted to secede from Battvia and join the Paper Cities. The Paper Cities supported the revolt militarily, seeing in it the final blow against an old enemy. The war lasted nine years across two fronts, introduced gunpowder weapons to Cevolian warfare for the first time through the Ottian mercenary company the Copperheads, and ended only when <a href="Meadrath" class="internal-link">Meadrath</a> intervened through the Pass of Ott under Marshall Felix von Praum, tipping the military balance decisively. Battvia won and Vorella was subjugated, but at a price that only barely exceeded the cost of losing.</p> 
<p>The realms economy was ruined, its treasury emptied, and its sovereign left so indebted to Meadrath that the political conditions of the following years were effectively set in meadi interests rather than Battvian ones. In 921PA the Lona Council, called by the newly elevated Bishop <a href="Stefano Maldini" class="internal-link">Maldini</a> of Velasangra Cathedral, formally restructured Battvia as a constitutional monarchy — the final end to the settlements that Prince Guido II had refused to accept a century earlier arriving now not as a concession but as the only viable arrangement left after the realm had nearly destroyed itself twice trying to avoid it. For the full account see <a href="Vorellan War" class="internal-link">Vorellan Wars</a>.</p>
