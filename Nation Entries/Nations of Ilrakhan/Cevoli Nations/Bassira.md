<p>Bassira occupies the northern shore of the Bay of Fideux, wedged between the territories of <a href="Jeledo" class="internal-link">Jeledo</a> to the north east and <a href="Turpador" class="internal-link">Turpador</a> to the west, a position that has historically made it a source of friction between the two powerful cevoli duchies and simultaneously the reason for its unusual political status. Mindano is a powerful city-state made up of a majority culturally turpadori humans with a strong niardi minority as well, the City is known for its Great Hall of Silk, one of if not the most famed Pleasure Houses in all of Ilrakhan.</p>

<p>In 740PA, the Ducs of Jeledo and Turpador convened to negotiate a comprehensive border and trade agreement covering their shared frontiers and commercial rights in the Servi Sea routes to <a href="Sertova" class="internal-link">Sertova</a>. Conte Albarico I di Bassira, whose family had long governed the city under disputed ducal suzerainty, seized the diplomatic moment with considerable skill, as the cost of either duchy securing Bassira for themselves was the permanent hostility of the other and the Conte made both parties understand this with sufficient clarity that granting the city full autonomy became the cleanest available resolution. The resulting Treaty of 740PA formally recognized Bassira as a self governing city-state under its hereditary Conte, bound by commercial treaties to both duchies but subject to neither. This settlement was later replicated on the greater Stage of ilrakhani politics concerning the realm of <a href="Uhl" class="internal-link">Uhl</a> during <a href="Treaty of Uhl" class="internal-link">Treaty of Uhl</a> that followed the enormous <a href="War of Horn and Fang" class="internal-link">War of Horn and Fang between Meadrath and Vihr</a>. Bassira has maintained this balance between Jeledo and Turpador ever since as a maritime city whose prosperity depends on remaining useful to both its large neighbors and threatening to neither, and whose Conte's diplomatic instincts have historically been as important to the city's survival as its harbour.</p>
