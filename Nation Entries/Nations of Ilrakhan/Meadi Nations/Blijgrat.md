<div class="wiki-infobox"> <h4 class="wiki-header">Blijgrat League</h4> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Government</strong> <span>Confederal Parliamentary Republic</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Head of State</strong> <span>Stadtholder <a href="Jan van de Spiegel" class="internal-link">Jan van de Spiegel</a></span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Capital</strong> <span>None (de jure), Schipwig (de facto)</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Heraldic Symbol</strong> <span>Green Merman on Red</span> </div> <hr class="wiki-hr"> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Primary Religion</strong> <a href="Trinicism" class="internal-link">Trinicism</a> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Primary Culture</strong> <span><a href="Meadi" class="internal-link">Weadi</a> <a href="Human" class="internal-link">Human</a></span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Official Language</strong> <span>Weadi</span> </div> <hr class="wiki-hr"> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Preceded by</strong> <span><a href="Weadland" class="internal-link">Kingdom of Weadland</a>,<br><a href="Baeling Empire" class="internal-link">Baeling Empire</a></span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Formed</strong> <span>770PA</span> </div> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Flags/Blijgrat_flag.png" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> </div> <p>The Weadi cities north of the Trinwood have united in a free and liberal republic, faced with the old threat of raiders and invaders from across the Bloody Strait and with the dominant might of the Kingdom of <a href="Meadrath" class="internal-link">Meadrath</a>. Only in unity, with ingenuity, shrewd mercantilism and elegant diplomacy, can this League of Cities hope to survive as an autonomous home for the Weadi peoples.</p> <p>The Blijgrat League is not only a cultural but also a business alliance of trading cities and their guilds, founded to join forces in promoting mercantile interests, defensive strength and political influence. By the 10th century it holds a near monopoly on trade in the Bloody Strait and the Gullsong Sea, above all since various northern settlements in <a href="Trykkerog" class="internal-link">Trykkerog</a> joined it, and together with Meadrath it dominates the trade and manufacturing of western Ilrakhan.</p> <p>In current times a strong national identity has developed within the cities, and movements to centralize administrative and military duties are growing in popularity. The foundation of the <a href="Geldfield Unie van Vrijhandel" class="internal-link">Geldfield Unie van Vrijhandel</a> in 861PA and its consolidation as part of the League in the Charter of 891PA have further contributed to a united sense of purpose and identity, for the dominant burgher class as well as for the peasants who form the agricultural backbone of the League.</p> <h2>The Land</h2> <p>The League holds the north-eastern coast of Meadland, from the Bloody Strait in the north to the Gullsong Sea in the east, and the flatlands behind it north of the Trinwood, which were once the kingdom of <a href="Weadland" class="internal-link">Weadland</a>. Three rivers carry its life, the Lot in the north-west, the Frij in the north and the Ostor in the south-east, and everything between the hills and the sea has been shaped by the hand of man. Dikes and dams hold back the water, irrigation ditches and canals cross the fields, and windmills turn along the coast. The great ports are Geldfields on the Strait, Hevn on its headland with the island of Riloord before it, and Grisden and Schipwig on the Gullsong Sea, and inland lie Lotsprings in the Frijlands, Fostwen on the Lot, Aisteep on the Ostor and Giel in the wetlands. In the west the Bela divides the League from <a href="Weisch" class="internal-link">Weisch</a>, and at the mouth of the Bela stands <a href="Zeeport" class="internal-link">Zeeport</a>, a Free City that the Crown counts as part of Weisch but that has a seat of its own in the Council. In the south-east the wetlands run on into the Duchy of <a href="Lesmere" class="internal-link">Lesmere</a>.</p> <h2>Government</h2> <p>The League is a confederal republic. Each city governs itself under its own council, its guilds and its patrician families, and sends its master to the Council of Cities, the parliament and governing body of the League. The Council meets in the Great Hall of Equals at Schipwig, and it is only because of this hall that Schipwig is a capital of sorts, for the League has none by law. The three founding cities, Schipwig, Hevn and Geldfields, hold their seats with full weight, and the cities that joined later sit as lesser members.</p> <p>At the head of the League stands the Stadtholder, who presides over the Council and speaks for the League abroad. He is chosen by the Council from among the masters of the cities and serves a term of twelve years. It is an almost institutional custom to elect an able Stadtholder again, unless he has grown considerably frail or old, and so in practice a Stadtholder rules until a majority of the Council forces him out. The present Stadtholder is <a href="Jan van de Spiegel" class="internal-link">Jan van de Spiegel</a>, who is also Stadtholder of Schipwig and is known as the Leader-Elect of the League. He has held the office since 909PA and was re-elected in 921PA. The old cities guard their autonomy, but a growing party of burghers around the Stadtholder and the Kamerspreker <a href="Pieter van Dielen" class="internal-link">Pieter van Dielen</a> wants a common administration and a common army.</p> <p><a href="Zeeport" class="internal-link">Zeeport</a> holds a unique place in the Council. Since the Zeeport Agreement of 800PA it has had as much voting power as the three founders, but it has no vote in any decision that touches the greater lands of the League or its other members. The seat belongs to the Baron of Zeeport officially, but in practice an envoy of the Burgherrad always holds it, introduced in a formality as the temporary representative of the Baron.</p> <h2>The Cities</h2> <p>Four cities stand above the rest. Schipwig, where the Ostor meets the Gullsong Sea, was the capital of old Weadland for most of its history and is the seat of the Great Hall of Equals and of the Bishop who bears the old title of Archbishop of Weadland. Hevn is the seat of the University of Hevn, whose Academy of Planar Education is known far beyond the League. Geldfields lies on the Bloody Strait and is the third of the founding cities. Zeeport, at the mouths of the Bela and the Lot, is the fourth and the freest, a Weadi city in culture and a tributary of the Crown in politics, governed by its Baron and by the Burgherrad, a council of its guild and town leaders.</p> <p>Fostwen on the Lot was the first city to join the founders, in 778PA, and others followed, among them Lotsprings, Aisteep, Grisden, Riloord and Giel. Almost all of them came by invitation and not by force, for they feared the conquest of the Meadi and depended on the great port cities in any case.</p> <h2>Society and Faith</h2> <p>Weadi society is ordered less by birth than by trade. The burgher class dominates the cities, and beside the merchant houses stand the patricians and the old landed families, who style themselves "van" in the manner of the Weadi. The peasants who work the dikes and the reclaimed fields are the agricultural backbone of the League. The Weadi are proud of their liberty and wary of kings, and their ingenuity, shrewd mercantilism and elegant diplomacy are as famous as their wealth.</p> <p>The League is Trinicist, and the Bishop of Schipwig holds the old title of Archbishop of Weadland without any dominion over the other bishops. Saint <a href="Yahrwick" class="internal-link">Yahrwick</a> of Zeeport, the pirate saint of liberation and air, is loved along the coast, and the heresy or true faith of <a href="Diarism" class="internal-link">Diarism</a> has found many followers in the west. The University of Hevn draws scholars from many lands, and its professors have included <a href="Jan van Swarn" class="internal-link">Jan van Swarn</a> and <a href="Korman van Schipwig" class="internal-link">Korman van Schipwig</a>.</p> <h2>Trade and the G.U.V.</h2> <p>The League lives from the sea and from the land it has won from the sea. Its shipyards and fisheries, its cloth, and the grain and dairy of the reclaimed fields are the base of its wealth, and its banking houses and guilds finance much of the trade of the western seas. In 795PA Weadi traders opened the Kelzen Route to the Gerjodi Islands and the northern shores of <a href="Aisligos" class="internal-link">Aisligos</a>, and in 834PA the Weadi seafarer <a href="Jurim Eykszoon" class="internal-link">Jurim Eykszoon</a> charted the shores of Peadyr.</p> <p>In the early 800s the trade between the ports of <a href="Sertova" class="internal-link">Sertova</a> and <a href="Othirdon" class="internal-link">Othirdon</a> and the nations of the Torn Sea grew so much that it broke the monopoly of the Cevoli ports, which had long held the merchants of those lands in an iron grip. This was the Opening of the West. Weadi traders had more and more success in the western ports, but they had to compete with the regional interests of <a href="Vignasoya" class="internal-link">Vignasoya</a>, Baeling and the Cevoli powers of the south, and the League came to need one body that could negotiate with all of them.</p> <p>That body was the Geldfield Unie van Vrijhandel, the G.U.V., named for the city of Geldfields and founded in 861PA as a union of the many old trading companies, shipping Kontors and merchant guilds of the League. It is one of the first joint stock companies in the world, modelled on the cartels of the dwarves and gnomes and on the example of the elven <a href="Grand Ziflis Company" class="internal-link">Ziflis Company</a>, and the first chartered trading company created by independent citizens. It grew so quickly that the Council of Cities feared it would outgrow the state, and in the Charter of 891PA it forced the Unie to hand at least 45 percent of its shares to the chambers of the greatest cities, 15 percent each to Hevn, Schipwig and Geldfields, shares that may never be diluted. The League in turn granted the Unie a monopoly of one hundred years on all trade in Othirdon, Cevolia and Sertova and the leadership of its colonial effort in Aisligos, where it founded <a href="Faarhovn" class="internal-link">Faarhovn</a> on Jurland in 878PA. Zeeport holds none of the city shares of the Charter, but many of its rich merchant houses are invested in the Unie all the same.</p> <p>The Unie is headed by the Kamer van gelijke belangen, the Chamber of Equal Interests, which since the Charter elects an official representative, the Kamerspreker, at present Pieter van Dielen. In this the Unie works surprisingly closely with the <a href="Gesellschaft für Westmeadischen Handel" class="internal-link">Gesellschaft für Westmeadischen Handel</a>, the K.G.W., of the Crown, which was largely created in its image.</p> <h2>Arms and War</h2> <p>The League has no united navy and no common army. Every city keeps its own sizeable guard, and most cities hire several loyal mercenary companies as well, which the phenomenal wealth of the League can easily afford. The ships of the League are largely private, the fleets of the cities and their merchant houses, and the nearest thing to a navy of the League is the fleet of the G.U.V., one of the largest in the known world. In the <a href="War of Horn and Fang" class="internal-link">War of Horn and Fang</a> the combined city fleets fought beside the Meadi navy at the Battle of the Bay, although Zeeport stayed out of the war.</p> <h2>History</h2> <p>After the fall of the Baeling Empire in 590PA the kingdom of Weadland was not restored, and the country returned to the rule of petty lords, cities and communities. The great ports of Schipwig, Hevn, Zeeport and Geldfields grew richer and more influential, and the raids of the north returned. In 770PA the three cities Schipwig, Hevn and Geldfields swore the Blijgrat Agreement at Blijgrat, a stretch of farmland in central Weadland favoured by apple farmers, whose name means "happier days" in Weadi. The fields are not otherwise remarkable, and the oath sworn there is the only reason the agreement, and with it the League, bears the name. The cities were moved by a plain sense that unity would be economically viable.</p> <p>The League soon grew, almost entirely by peaceful and diplomatic means, and in 778PA Fostwen became its first new member. Zeeport was a different matter. By 800PA the city was divided between a sizeable faction that wished to remain independent and others who wished to join the League, but only as an equal partner of the three founders. King Gildher III of Meadrath sent the Landgraf of Weisch with many knights and a sizeable army to bring peace to Zeeport, and the affair came close to catastrophe before all parties reached the Zeeport Agreement. Zeeport remained independent but pays tribute to Meadrath in exchange for protection, it was allowed to join the League commercially, and it received its unique seat in the Council. It governs itself as a Free City.</p> <p>The Treaty of Lotsprings, a direct consequence of the Charter of 891PA, was signed in the city of Lotsprings on the 16th of May 900PA by Queen Gisela III of Meadrath, Stadtholder Willem van Barnevelt, Hendrik Fagel for the G.U.V. and Count Heinrich von Redling for the K.G.W., and also by Baron Mattias van Zee of Zeeport, who had helped to arrange it as a semi neutral party. It divided the western trade and the new world between the League and the Crown, with Jurland and the lands south of it falling to the League and the G.U.V., and it bound the two to defend each other's company holdings.</p>
<p>In 909PA the Council of Cities voted to remove Stadtholder Willem van Barnevelt, who had argued against the expansion of the G.U.V. in the new world. The vote was unanimous, and it was orchestrated by <a href="Jan van de Spiegel" class="internal-link">Jan van de Spiegel</a>, the Stadtholder of Schipwig, and a cabal of his trusted men. Van de Spiegel was elected Stadtholder in Barnevelt's place.</p>
<h2>Foreign Relations</h2> <p>With Meadrath the League trades and competes. The Crown holds the Bela and the northern coast as the Landgraviate of Weisch and protects Zeeport in return for tribute, and the <a href="Treaty of Lotsprings" class="internal-link">Treaty of Lotsprings</a> ties the two powers together, in the face of the commercial unions of Othirdon, Sertova, Cevolia and Trykkerog that are their common rivals. The memory of the raiders of the north lives on in the League, which still fears invasion from across the Strait, and that fear is a part of why it has bound itself so closely to the Crown.</p> <h2>People of Interest</h2> <p><a href="Jan van de Spiegel" class="internal-link">Jan van de Spiegel</a><br>Stadtholder of Schipwig and Leader-Elect of the Blijgrat League, an extremely cutthroat man in league with various organisations that span continents, who is also known to keep contacts as far east as Grachy, some of them high in its government</p> <p><a href="Pieter van Dielen" class="internal-link">Pieter van Dielen</a><br>Kamerspreker of the G.U.V. since 920PA and a supporter of the Stadtholder</p> <p>Willem van Barnevelt<br>Stadtholder of the League at the signing of the Treaty of Lotsprings in 900PA, a conservative career politician who was voted out of office in 909PA</p> <p>Hendrik Fagel<br>Kamerspreker of the G.U.V. at the signing of the Treaty of Lotsprings</p> <p><a href="Jurim Eykszoon" class="internal-link">Jurim Eykszoon</a><br>Weadi explorer, born in Hevn, who charted the shores of Peadyr</p>
