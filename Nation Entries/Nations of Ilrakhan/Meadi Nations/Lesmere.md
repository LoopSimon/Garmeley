Often in minor conflicts with both Schipwig and <a href="Gisvale" class="internal-link">Gisvale</a>, it is the proud home of the Kranmer people, who have ruled themselves, at least somewhat autonomously, since the days of the Auguri Dominion.</p>  <p>Lesmere holds the north-east of <a href="Meadland" class="internal-link">Meadland</a> on the shore of the Bay of Uhl. Its heart is the Kranmoor, a vast morass of fen and standing water that runs towards the sea, and to the north the Weadi Wetlands, crossed by the Les, reach up to the lands of Schipwig and the cities of <a href="Blijgrat" class="internal-link">Blijgrat</a>. In the south the duchy follows the Bow river towards the lands of <a href="Uhl" class="internal-link">Uhl</a>, and the border with Gisvale runs along the edge of the moor. The Kranmer are the Weadi people of the moors, though they are ruled as a Duchy under Meadrath and their courts use High Meadi.</p> <p>The moor has its own lore. The Kranmer are known to traverse the moors better than almost anyone, yet strange creatures such as <a href="Ghant" class="internal-link">Ghants</a> haunt them. The Kranmer speak of Father Kran, who sleeps in the deepest morass and is supposedly an ancient Ghant, and in 657PA the necromancer <a href="Morfeld" class="internal-link">Morfeld</a> first surfaced there, purging villages with a coterie of the dead before he moved on across the Bow.</p> <h2>The House von Kran</h2> <p>The von Kran are a human dynasty that has held the Kran delta for hundreds of years. Their ancestral seat is Fort Kranig, the old castle on the coast south of the moor, but the administrative capital has been relocated to Dellweid, a much richer and larger port city, and it is there that the duchy is governed. In 591PA, with the end of <a href="Baeling Empire" class="internal-link">Baeling</a> rule over the coastal lands of former <a href="Weadland" class="internal-link">Weadland</a>, the von Kran laid claim to Dellweid, which was culturally largely Kranmer, and took control without notable resistance, declaring the Duchy of Lesmere in reference to the old Kingdom of the Les, which had risen around the Les River north of the moor in 277PA.</p> <p>The present Duke, Kasper von Kran, is one of the premier vassals of the Crown. The rivalry between the von Kran and the Archdukes of Gisvale is ancient, and the Dukes are proud of their independence and sit uneasily beside the Archduke to the south.</p> <h2>Trade and the Sea</h2> <p>The Kranmoor is home to a large peat and bog iron industry, and the wealth of the duchy beyond it comes from the sea. Dellweid is a great harbour of the Bay, and Fort Kranig keeps the coast. Around 900PA the military naval port of Princes Watch was founded on an island in the Bay of Uhl. It is administered by the royal navy under the authority of the von Kran, and it is one of the ports where the royal fleet docks.</p> <h2>History</h2> <p>Early in the reign of Queen <a href="Gisela II" class="internal-link">Gisela II</a> a faction of dukes and counts gathered at Dellweid and held what amounted to a second court, refusing to acknowledge the young queen. Their so called Lesmerey demand, that Gisela marry Duke Otto von Praum or be deposed, was a treasonous attempt to make her their puppet. The Praum Affair ended in the close avoidance of war and the arrest and execution of the ringleaders, von Praum among them, and Queen-Mother Margaret du Jera is largely credited with resolving it.</p> <p>In 719PA Duke Abelard II von Kran marched his armies to Hasterhall and across the Bow to "pacify and protect the poor souls of the land", a blatantly opportunistic conquest, and in 735PA his son Kasper I assembled troops to march even further east. The Count of Uhl then accepted the protection of Vihr, and Uhl was a Vihri protectorate for over a century. When the <a href="War of Horn and Fang" class="internal-link">War of Horn and Fang</a> broke out in 851PA, King Arngil II directed the Meadi war effort from Fort Kranig, the fleet shattered the blockade of the fort in the Battle of the Bay, and he died there in 858PA. Yet the von Kran, whose ancient rivalry with the Archduke of Gisvale proved stronger than the demands of a common war, withheld their troops from the army of Gisvale, and it was beaten alone at the Battle of Dilfields by the Urien garrison of Fort Dilwatch, whose lord had never forgiven the seizure of Hasterhall.</p>
