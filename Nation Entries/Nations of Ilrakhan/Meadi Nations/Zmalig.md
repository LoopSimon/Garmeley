It is a Trinicist Bishopric, established in 710PA by the 4000 faithful, a group of pilgrims who sought to tame these harsh climates. Zmalig is under the protection of the Crown of <a href="Meadrath" class="internal-link">Meadrath</a> and part of the informal Meadi Empire, but it acts as a separate entity in many ways, especially in the freedoms it affords its citizens and in the unique role of its clergy, in an otherwise rather subdued <a href="Trinicism" class="internal-link">Trinicist</a> church.</p> 
	<p>Zmalig is a small land, the city and its coast on the shore of the Bloody Strait. <a href="Mear" class="internal-link">Mear</a> lies to the west, and the hills of the peninsula behind the city belong to the duchy, while <a href="Weisch" class="internal-link">Weisch</a> borders it to the south and east. The harbour looks out over the strait towards Trykkerog, the home of the Bykmåli. Above it rises the Torch of Zmalig, the great lighthouse whose tower also serves as the tower of the cathedral, and which gives the city its arms.</p> <p>The pilgrims were Meadi and Weadi, and they settled the harsh coast under their first bishop at a time when the <a href="Baeling Empire" class="internal-link">Baeling</a> still held the northern shore, who tolerated them until the Battle of Zeeport in 737PA broke their hold. The Bykmåli, former settlers of the Baeling age, are a substantial part of the people today, and the speech of the city is the Hundi dialect of Meadi, as it is among the Hundi of the Whisperhills.</p> <h2>The Bishopric</h2> <p>The Bishop of Zmalig, at present Ungerim Lerosch, is head of state and rules the city. He is elected for life by the synod of the clergy together with the guildmasters of the city, and the Crown confirms the election and does not make it. <a href="Gisela IV" class="internal-link">Gisela IV</a> is the sovereign of Zmalig, and the city has its seat in the <a href="Royal Congress" class="internal-link">Royal Congress</a>.</p> <p>Zmalig is unique in giving its clergy a great amount of earthly power in a Trinicist region, where bishops elsewhere rule nothing and raise no tithe, and more than in many a traditionalist area of <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">High Gethric</a> faith. The charter of the city is as unusual as its church. There is no serfdom within its walls, every citizen is a free burgher in the eyes of the law, the customs of the Weadi and the Bykmåli are tolerated, and the church is governed by elected councils and not by the court of the bishop alone.</p>
