During the reign of <a href="Gisela II" class="internal-link">Gisela II</a>, known as "the Good" to posterity, the <a href="Yahrwick" class="internal-link">Saint Yahrwick of Zeeport</a> ffreed the city and the northern Bloody Strait from the hands of the Skori, the last heirs of the Baeling conquests, in 737PA and forever broke the hold of the northerners over the strait and the coast of Meadland, in what is now known as the Battle of Zeeport. Gisela acted quickly and declared all the lands north of the Bela river rightful claim of the Crown, dispatching various armies and lords to fortify and defend the northern frontier, not only against a Baeling resurgence but also against the emerging and powerful Weadi city states that had yet to unite as the League of Blijgrat.</p> <p>Queen Gisela created the Landgraviate of Weisch in 739PA, named after the Weisch River, and granted it further lands unified under a single title. Although nominally not of the same rank as a Duchy, the Landgraviate has held an extremely important role in Meadi politics and has acted as an extension of the personal power of the Crown, and as its most loyal subject, ever since.</p> <h2>Land</h2> <p>Weisch holds the northern coast of <a href="Meadland" class="internal-link">Meadland</a> from Altfeeld in the west, where the lands of <a href="Mear" class="internal-link">Mear</a>, Morelle and Weisch meet, east along the Bloody Strait past Visdack, Rendling and Welrad to the Bela. Between the sea and the hills lie broad sheep plains, and the Weisch River runs past Rendling down to the strait at Visdack. On the coast in the west the Landgraviate borders <a href="Zmalig" class="internal-link">Zmalig</a>. Lake Eisig, Stur Keep and Fulmer lie south of the border, in the Crownlands. At the mouth of the Bela stands <a href="Zeeport" class="internal-link">Zeeport</a>, which the Crown counts as part of Weisch, although it governs itself as a Free City. In 800PA the city was torn between those who wished to remain independent and those who wished to join the Blijgrat League as an equal of its founders, and King Gildher III sent the Landgraf with many knights and a sizeable force to bring peace to Zeeport. The affair came close to catastrophe, but the Zeeport Agreement that followed left Zeeport a Free City that pays tribute to the Crown in exchange for its protection.</p>
<h2>The House von der Rend</h2> <p>The first Landgraf was the second son of the Duke of <a href="Wargel" class="internal-link">Wargel</a>, a man who stood to inherit no title of his own, an eager servant of the Crown and a hero in his own right. The line he founded, the von der Rend, is therefore a cadet branch of the House von Buhr. The present Landgraf, Waldir von der Rend, rules from Visdack. The quartered inverse dragon in the arms of the house, as in those of Mear, proclaims loyalty to the Crown, and the marigold and the scissors recall the wool and the dyes of the sheep plains.</p> <p>The Landgraf nominally ranks below the Dukes, yet Weisch is practically the home of a sizeable portion of the levies and of the royal battalions, among them the Drakenhelms, a massive company of soldiers trained as the effective standing infantry army of Meadrath and known for their helmets in the shape of a dragon. The enemies of Meadrath no longer sit on its northern border, but Weisch is still the centre of its militarism. The Academy of Visdack, founded in 904PA, is a relatively new and modern institution that teaches the history of warfare, and it is modelled on the self reflective contemplations of <a href="Rivalt du Chian" class="internal-link">Rivalt du Chian</a>.</p>
