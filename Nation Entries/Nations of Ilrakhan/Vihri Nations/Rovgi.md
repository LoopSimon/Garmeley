<p> Rovgi is one of the major Vadcy within Vihr, holding the Oathhills north of the Tryd River, it is closely associated with the religious history of the Realm of <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a>.</p>
<p>Known for their stubborn attitudes and fierce loyalty, the warriors of Rovgi have fought both alongside and even against the Knights of the Silver Wheel to their west across the Rebka River, where true no mans land begins and stretches onward in hundreds of deep valleys, dangerous forests and unmapped hills. Isolated settlements and rural communities here have to fight off roaming ogre hordes from the Ruby Valley, the waking terrors of the Oathhills, and guard the roads west and north of Pjaz.</p>
<h2>Geography</h2>
<p>Rovgi is the Vadcy of the Oathhills and the moors west of the Tryd River, the highest and wildest of the Vihri heartlands, and the one that faces the old frontier with <a href="Wermia" class="internal-link">Wermia</a>. Its capital Wogrov stands north of the hills, and the roads west and north of the town of Pjaz carry the traffic of the western marches. West of Pjaz the Rebka River marks the edge of the settled country. Beyond it lies Castle Nurk, the High Castle of the <a href="Order of the Silver Wheel" class="internal-link">Order of the Silver Wheel</a>, and the Pilgrims Path that runs through the Desolation of Wermia to Iztaw. To the south-west the hills climb toward the Trollback Mountains and the Ruby Valley, and to the south-east the Tryd River runs past Ahmsk, with the capital Zavlech lying beyond.</p>
<h2>History</h2>
<p>The Oathhills were the frontier between the Wermians and the Vihrmen long before there was a Vihr. At its height the Kingdom of Wermia held lands as far east as the Tryd, but as the Horn Lords of the Federation pressed it back it was left struggling to keep its dominions at the hills at all. The hills were also the setting for the worst of the <a href="The Incursion" class="internal-link">Incursions</a>. In the summer of 226PA the town of Holweid, a former Wermian town to the north of the Oathhills, became a Gate to the Abyss. The few who saw it and lived all took their own lives within the year, and the host that came through spread out over the country and did not meet real resistance until it reached the walls of Tawring, the city now called Iztaw. No Vadcy has a deeper stake in the story of the Prophet than Rovgi, and the people of the Oathhills know it.</p>
<p>The old enemies in the hills were never only the daemons. The <a href="Ogre" class="internal-link">ogres</a> of the Ruby Valley are the last of the highland warbands that the Horn Lords drove back into the moors and mountains in the Ogre Wars, and they have been raiding the western settlements ever since. When the lords of the <a href="Vihrmen Federation" class="internal-link">Federation</a> became the lords of the Vadcy in 377PA, Rovgi was one of the eight, and its host had the shortest march of any on the day the lords came to Zavlech.</p>
<p>Rovgi has had a long and uneasy relationship with the Order of the Silver Wheel, founded in 398PA to guard the pilgrim roads across the frontier. The warriors of Rovgi have fought both alongside the Knights and against them, and which it was has depended less on faith than on the year and the Var.</p>
<h2>Ruling House and Politics</h2>
<p>The Vadcy is held by House Wogrov, whose seat gives the Vadcy its capital, and whose current head is Var Zeslav. House Wogrov is tied to the present royal line by marriage. <a href="Vormil III" class="internal-link">Vormil III</a> married Maroza zi Wogrov, and their four surviving children, Kisras Vasczo, Kisa Yelizaveta, Kis <a href="Jakub Ralpia" class="internal-link">Jakub</a> and Kisa Anissa, are her children as much as his. Zeslav was Maroza's brother, which makes him the uncle of all four, and Rovgi's ruling house is their maternal kin.</p>
<p>That connection has become delicate since the Gras's death. Vormil is estranged from both of his sons, and the Var of Rovgi sits in the <a href="Honoured Assembly" class="internal-link">Honoured Assembly</a> as the family of the heirs and not of the Ras. How Zeslav uses that position, and whom he favours, is a question that Zavlech watches closely.</p>
<h2>Society and Culture</h2>
<p>The people of Rovgi are the Higher Vihri of the Oathhills, famous for their stubbornness and their loyalty. The country is poor in good land and rich in strongholds, and the common settlements are built for defence: hold-towers, walled hamlets, and roads that are watched day and night. The arms of House Wogrov show a castle guarded by foxes.</p>
<h2>Economy</h2>
<p>Rovgi's wealth is hill pasture, wool, timber and the tolls on the western roads, and much of what the Vadcy earns it spends on the garrisons that keep the roads open. Few of its settlements are rich, and its Var is among the poorer of the nine.</p>
<h2>Places of Note</h2>
<p>Wogrov is the capital, a castle town north of the Oathhills and the seat of House Wogrov. Its cathedral is the seat of the Bishop of Wogrov, who is by tradition the Archbishop of Vihr. Pjaz is the last large settlement before the western frontier. The Rebka River divides the Vadcy from the country of the Silver Wheel, and Holweid, the town where the Second Incursion began, lies north of the hills. The Ruby Valley in the south-west is the home of the ogres, and Ahmsk is a town on the Tryd River in the east of the Vadcy.</p>
