<p>Iztaw rules over a conglomeration of territories in northern Vihr, largely autonomous within the greater Vihri Kingdom and exempt from almost all obligations such as taxes and levies. It is the holy city of the Gethric faith and the seat of its Church, and its ruler is the High-Bisop. As a part of the Rascy zi Vihr, Iztaw infamously lost extensive lands and holdings following the Council of 870 as part of the creation of the <a href="Torry" class="internal-link">Vadcy zi Torry</a>. For the Church and its ranks, see the <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">Church of Iztaw</a>.</p>
<h2>Geography</h2>
<p>Iztaw lies in the north of Vihr, near the old Wermian border. A short way to the north, on the coast, lies the port of Brov, which feeds the holy city. West of it is the Vadcy of Torry, cut out of its lands in 870PA, and to the east stand Trazlov and the Vadcy of <a href="Trazlova" class="internal-link">Trazlova</a>. The Pilgrims Path, which leads from Urland through the desolation of Wermia, ends at its gates.</p>
<h2>History</h2>
<p>The city was once Tawring, a fortified frontier town on the disputed ground between the Kingdom of Wermia and the Vihrmen. In 202PA it was besieged in the wars between the two, and in 227PA its walls held for almost four months against the Host of Daemons before the central keep was laid to ground. The farmer Gethric led as many survivors as he could out of the city before it fell, and when the forces of the living reclaimed it he slew the Great Daemon Vazmoris there. The city was renamed Iztaw, and its walls were rebuilt. In 240PA the lands of the Federation and of Wermia that had been won back were formed into the holy realm of Iztaw. One year after Gethric's passing, Volboris the Healer, his oldest friend, proclaimed the Church in the city and became its first High-Bisop.</p>
<p>The High-Bisops of Iztaw have often stood above the kings of Vihr. In 377PA Morzin I held Ras Zervind II through the <a href="Candleguard" class="internal-link">Candleguard</a>, until the eight great lords marched on Zavlech and the Church had to give way. Under Taromir I the High-Bisop rejected the Doctrine of Dualism, and in 471PA Kherova went its own way. In 763PA Manral I and the Conclave ratified the <a href="Gethric Pogroms" class="internal-link">Gethric Pogroms</a>, and the Great Schism of 764PA cost the Church its western half. The blow that fell closest to home came after the War of Horn and Fang, when feeling turned against a clergy accused of having betrayed and sabotaged the country, and the Council of 870 carved Torry out of the lands of Iztaw. In 915PA Orthel II was elected High-Bisop.</p>
<h2>Government and Politics</h2>
<p>The High-Bisop is elected for life by the Bishops of the Church, though not every vote counts alike, and the Archbishops weigh more than the rest. He is the head of the realm of Iztaw and the head of the Church, and so he is both the spiritual leader of the Ras and his subject. The Archbishop of Vihr is by tradition the Bishop of Wogrov Cathedral in Rovgi. For many decades of history the High-Bisop was the most powerful man in Vihr, and the quiet struggle between Iztaw and Zavlech still defines the reigns of the Ras. Today the High-Bisop is a figure of great importance at court, but the power rests with Vormil III. The Candleguard keeps the cathedral and the church grounds, and nothing beyond them.</p>
<h2>Society and Culture</h2>
<p>Iztaw is a city of pilgrims. Every Father of the Church must make the Uwazna, the pilgrimage to Iztaw, and be ordained here by the High-Bisop, and the lay faithful come in numbers to the Silver Procession, the week of celebration in the first month of the year, and to the Day of the Prophet at the end of summer. The practice of Reaffirmation, in which a believer confides doubt to a priest, is protected here as an institution. The people of Iztaw are Low Vihri and devout, and they are used to the sight of cloaked clerics and foreign bishops in their streets.</p>
<h2>Economy</h2>
<p>The wealth of Iztaw is the wealth of the Church: the tithe raised by Gethric Bishops across the faithful lands, the gifts of pilgrims, and the trade in candles, relics and holy books. The realm pays almost no tax to the crown. The port of Brov on the coast to the north serves the city.</p>
<h2>Places of Note</h2>
<p>The Voldom, named for Volboris, is the cathedral of the High-Bisop and the great church of the faith. The rebuilt walls of Tawring still ring the old city. The ruins of the old keep are kept as a shrine to the siege, and the Pilgrims' Gate is the end of the long road from Urland. Brov, the port on the coast to the north, serves the city.</p>
