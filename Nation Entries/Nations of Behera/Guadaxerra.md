<p>Guadaxerra is the only state of the hobgoblins of <a href="Tal" class="internal-link">Tal</a>, a highland realm of the Aït Xerra in the foothills of the Xermesis, west of the Great City of <a href="Koshtir" class="internal-link">Koshtir</a> on the Kaitan Gulf. Its name means "the river of the mountains" in Tal Gob, after the river Guadazgar, which falls out of the Xermesis and runs through the country to the gulf. It was founded in 851PA on land granted by the Great Sage <a href="Hilcarax" class="internal-link">Hilcarax</a> to the hobgoblin companies that had fought for Koshtir in the last war of the <a href="Period of the Bleeding Heron" class="internal-link">Period of the Bleeding Heron</a>, and it is the heartland of <a href="Takuzt" class="internal-link">Takuzt</a>, the faith of the four elements.</p>

<h2>Geography</h2>
<p>Guadaxerra lies where the jungle of <a href="Kaito" class="internal-link">Kaito</a> climbs into the eastern slopes of the Xermesis, a country of cloud forest, deep river gorges and terraced valleys that is cooler and wetter than the coast below. The hobgoblins have always preferred such a climate, and most of them live in the higher valleys, in cliff halls and cave halls cut into the gorges and in the fortified granary halls called agdirs. The capital, Almazgar, stands where the Guadazgar leaves the hills, a town of terraces and stone halls around the great agdir of the Aït Ouzal. Above it the old road to the west runs over the pass of Tizi n'Jibey into the high Xermesis, where the <a href="Hatara" class="internal-link">Hatara</a> live, and below it the river carries the trade of the country down to Koshtir.</p>

<h2>Peoples</h2>
<p>The people of Guadaxerra are the Aït Xerra, the highland branch of the hobgoblins of Tal, who also hold scattered halls along the slopes of the Xermesis and in the high valleys of the Ilkiztis to the north. Like their kin in the cities, the <a href="Mudaxari" class="internal-link">Mudaxari</a>, they descend from the Tirbi sellswords who came south from Ur in the 8th century PA, though they have long since grown apart from the hobgoblins of Ak-Saray. Their language is Tal Gob, a dialect of Gob that has taken in many words from Talang, Mirani and the speech of the Sultanate. A few Al Ypp and Safiri families live in the lower valleys, and the Hatara of the high passes come down to trade.</p>

<h2>Government</h2>
<p>Guadaxerra is governed by the Agraw, the assembly of the heads of its clans, which meets at Almazgar on the four quarter days of the year. The Agraw elects the Amghar, the head of the state, for life, and by custom it chooses an exarch of Takuzt, a master of at least two elements. The present Amghar is Saghor of Aït Ouzal, an exarch of Earth and Water, who has held the office since 909PA. The clans keep their own halls and lands and settle most matters among themselves, and the Amghar speaks for the country in war and before foreign powers. The arms of the state are the Four Stones, a square of four stones in the colours of the elements, set apart from each other so that none of them touches.</p>

<h2>Society and Faith</h2>
<p>Life in Guadaxerra is ordered by Takuzt. Children are tested by the teachers of the halls to find which element rules them, and every clan keeps a school in which its young learn to channel that element and to master its virtue. The exarchs are the most respected people in the country, and the clans compete to have them. Older customs of the Dirgae survive beside the faith. The ritual duel is still fought to settle title and honour, though an exarch may refuse it without shame, and the <a href="Kaladi Mysticism" class="internal-link">Kaladi</a> are honoured with drums at their festivals. Tazdirt the Unmixed, the founder of Takuzt, is honoured as a Kaladi of the whole country.</p>

<h2>Economy</h2>
<p>The clans of Guadaxerra live from terraced fields, herds and the forest. Its Earth channelers are famed as masons and as builders of terraces and roads, its Water channelers as makers of channels and cisterns, and many of them hire out their skills to the cities of the gulf. Timber, hides and cut stone go down the Guadazgar to Koshtir, and Guadaxerra still sends companies of soldiers to serve abroad, as its founders did.</p>

<h2>History</h2>
<p>The first hobgoblins in Tal were Tirbi sellswords who came south from the Elbae Sultanate in the early 8th century PA, by way of the Ilkiztis and the shores of the Kaitan Gulf, and who served the Empire of Naigan and later the warlords who divided it. In the middle of the century Tazdirt the Unmixed, a hobgoblin who had served Naigan beside the channelers of the Pillar of Earth, broke with the Arts of Change and wrote the Four Sayings, and her teaching spread through the hobgoblin companies in the strife that followed the fall of the empire in 771PA.</p>
<p>In the last years of the Period of the Bleeding Heron several hobgoblin companies fought for Koshtir in its war against <a href="Hanshi" class="internal-link">Hanshi</a> over the Ariho. After the Peace of Chezin in 849PA the Great Sage Hilcarax granted them the forested hills west of the city, which Koshtir had claimed but never settled, on condition that they keep the western roads and come to the defence of the city when called. The companies settled there with their families and the hobgoblins who followed them from the coast, and in 851PA their captains met at Almazgar as the first Agraw and elected the first Amghar. Guadaxerra has kept its bond to Koshtir ever since, and has sent soldiers to the city on more than one occasion. It is not counted among the heirs of Naigan by the other states of Tal, and it claims no part of that inheritance.</p>
