<p>The Mudaxari are the hobgoblins who live in the cities and kingdoms around the Kaitan Gulf and to the north of it, the lowland branch of the hobgoblins of <a href="Tal" class="internal-link">Tal</a> beside the Aït Xerra of the mountains. Their name comes from an old word of Tal Gob meaning "those allowed to stay", which was first used of the hobgoblin companies that settled in the towns they had been hired to defend. They have no state of their own and live as communities under the rule of others, in the Great City of <a href="Koshtir" class="internal-link">Koshtir</a>, in the Yppori kingdoms of the gulf coast, in the savannah of <a href="Yorgos" class="internal-link">Yorgos</a> and in the Emirate of <a href="Iz-Almadus" class="internal-link">Iz-Almadus</a> under the Elbae Sultanate. Like the Aït Xerra they follow <a href="Takuzt" class="internal-link">Takuzt</a>, the faith of the four elements, though most of them keep it more quietly.</p>

<h2>Communities</h2>
<p>The largest community of the Mudaxari lives in Koshtir, where the hobgoblins have held a quarter of their own below the Temple of the Singer since the wars of the 9th century, and where they are on good terms with the Mirani Safir and with their kin in neighbouring <a href="Guadaxerra" class="internal-link">Guadaxerra</a>. Smaller quarters exist along the coasts of the Bay of Anpon and the Bay of Tears, and a few families live in <a href="Rikarma" class="internal-link">Rikarma</a> and <a href="Chezin" class="internal-link">Chezin</a>.</p>
<p>In Yorgos the Mudaxari live in Jafur and the caravan towns of the savannah, where they serve as caravan guards and where their Water channelers are hired to find and keep wells. In Iz-Almadus they live in Almadin and in the foothills of the Ilkiztis beside the Ilkizti <a href="Gnome" class="internal-link">Gnomes</a>, with whom they have worked the mines and quarries of the mountains for generations. The Earth channelers of the Mudaxari and the artificers of the gnomes have long worked together there, and the two peoples are counted among the most loyal subjects of the Emir. The route by which the first hobgoblins came south from the Sultanate ran through the Ilkiztis, and the community at Almadin is held to be the oldest in the region.</p>

<h2>Society</h2>
<p>The Mudaxari have adapted more to the peoples around them than the Aït Xerra. They speak the language of their hosts beside Tal Gob, take surnames after the custom of the cities, often formed with the ending -ez after a father or a clan, as in Ouzalez, and dress after the fashion of the towns they live in. Many of them are masons, builders of channels and cisterns, kiln keepers and caravan guards, and the hobgoblin channelers of the gulf are sought after for any work that needs stone moved or water carried. Their halls are led by the teachers of Takuzt, and an exarch living among them is treated as the head of the community whatever the host state may say.</p>

<h2>Faith and Relations</h2>
<p>The Mudaxari follow Takuzt, but they live among peoples of other faiths and have learned to keep their own to themselves. In the Yppori kingdoms this caution is necessary, since Takuzt denies that ash is an element and the <a href="Ashcult" class="internal-link">Ashcult</a> regards that as heresy. In Rikarma, the Burning Kingdom, the few Mudaxari families take part in the public rites of Sochor and keep the quarter days of Takuzt behind closed doors. In Koshtir, which does not follow Sochor, and in the lands of the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae Sultanate</a> they practise more openly.</p>
<p>Relations with the Aït Xerra are close, and many Mudaxari families send their children to the schools of Guadaxerra to learn their element. The highlanders regard the city hobgoblins as softened by their hosts, and the Mudaxari regard the highlanders as stubborn, which both sides take as a comment on Earth and Water.</p>
