<p>The Elbae Sultanate, named after the ruling Dynasty is the largest elven Realm in the World, controlling the vast island of Il-Haena, most of the eastern Coast of Behera, the Asef and Hessef Deserts and the Drow  <a href="Underlands" class="internal-link">Underlands</a> directly, as well as exerting significant political influence throughout its subjects, like the  Eyalets of <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Hainach</a> and <a href="Qarzi" class="internal-link">Qarzi</a> or the <a href="Iz-Almadus" class="internal-link">Iz-Almadus</a> and <a href="Tusul" class="internal-link">Tusul</a> Emirates. Its past has been marked by imperialist ambitions across Behera, Ilrakhan and Nirydia. The capital of the Sultanate, Jiravid, is one of the largest cities in the known world with a population of ca 140.000 citizens.</p>

<h2>Economy</h2>
<p> The basis of the Elbae Sultanates economy is founded in mercantilism theory, stressing maximizing the trade inside the large country and trying to weaken international rivals, this basic mercantilist Theory was also largely imposed upon the Sultanates colonies.</p>
<p>During the 8th and 9th century, owing to a scarcity of precious metals, the economy grew faster than the money supply of the period. Few coins were in circulation and instead most transactions were made on credit, the numerous debts were usually settled during so called "Balancings" held at convenient intervals, where reciprocal debts were cancelled and only the difference had to be paid with cash, overall coins formed less than 20% of all exchanges during the period.</p>
<p>Exports increased significantly during the Modern Age, especially within the Elbae Sphere of influence, mostly privately owned companies traded with the colonies in Il-Haena, Nirydia and southern Behera.  The <a href="Grand Ziflis Company" class="internal-link">Grand Ziflix Company</a>, brought together the Sultanates leading overseas merchants in a regulated company during the middle 8th century in the nature of a guild. Its members main business was the export of cloth, and the import of a large range of foreign exotic goods. To increase beheran merchants involvement involvement in elbae trade, in 876PA a trade agreement was signed in Jiravid between the Sultanate and the <a href="Yorgos" class="internal-link">Yorgosi Marival</a>, Yorgosi merchants received privileges to carry out commercial activities within the sultanate, which in turn gained easier access to exotic beheran goods and their large gold exports.</p>

<p>The 10th century is shaping up to be a prosperous one for the elven State, as entrepreneurs extend the range of their business around the globe, the Headmaster of the Grand Ziflis Company Usval al Hamon had boasted about it "We are the most naturally diligent nation in the world, Vast trade, mighty wealth, rich manufactures and universal correspondence have been constant companions of Elbaes industrious citizens!" </p>
<p>The Empire uses its own currency, the Sultans Talent or simple, the <a href="Talent" class="internal-link">Talent</a>, that is overseen and minted by the Asafi of the Treasury.</p>

<h1>Administration & Governance</h1> <p>The Elbae Sultanate is the largest and most administratively complex political entity in the known world, governing a population roughly divided between Elves and humans, with a significant Drow minority concentrated in the Underlands and the upper echelons of the <a href="Golden Dusk" class="internal-link">Golden Dusk</a>. Its governance reflects centuries of institutional evolution from the fragmented successor states of the Meter Dynasty into a centralized imperial apparatus of extraordinary sophistication, one that has survived internal coups, succession crisis, devastating wars and the death of Sultans while maintaining coherent control over territories spanning three continents. At its apex sits the Sultan himself, seated upon the White Chair of Jiravid in the Palace of Eternity, theoretically the absolute and unquestioned ruler of all he surveys. In practice the Sultan governs through a layered system of councils, appointed officials and shadow institutions whose relationships to one another, and to the throne, define the real texture of Elbae power.</p> <h3>The Sultan and the Keeper of Eternity</h3> <p>The Sultan of Elbae is an absolute ruler in a sense that has no precise equivalent among the major powers of Ilrakhan, more completely sovereign in theory than even the most autocratic of the continents kings, the final authority on all matters of law, war, religion and commerce within his domain. This absolutism is not merely ceremonial. The Sultan appoints and dismisses the heads of every institution in the realm, sets the direction of the Dirhan through his Viceroy, and the <a href="Shuyat" class="internal-link">Shuyat</a>, the most unknowable intelligence apparatus in the known world, reports exclusively to him and operates on his mandate regardless of any other authority. The Elbae understanding of sovereignty is shaped by the tradition of <a href="Elven Paragonworship" class="internal-link">Paragonworship</a>, the Sultan is not merely a ruler but a living link in the chain of elven history that stretches back through the ancient dynasties to Jir himself, and his reign carries a sacred weight that no merely political institution could bear.</p> <p>The institution that most directly shapes the Sultans world is not the Dirhan but the person of the Keeper of Eternity, the Sultans mother. The Keeper holds formal control over the Palace of Eternity itself and commands the Ivory Guard, the Sultans personal elite bodyguard whose loyalty is to the institution of the throne rather than to any individual within it. This is not a ceremonial position. The Keeper of Eternity is routinely the most practically powerful individual in the Sultanate after the Sultan himself, managing the internal politics of the palace, shaping access to the Sultan, and exercising enormous influence over the question of succession, the single most consequential political decision of any reign. The Sultans wives accumulate influence gradually but only become true power players when a son of theirs inherits, at which point they may aspire to the Keepership themselves. If the previous Keeper is still alive when a new Sultan takes the White Chair, custom demands she commit ritual suicide, a practice that concentrates the minds of Keepers considerably on the question of which heir they support and how long their own son reigns.</p> <h3>Succession</h3> <p>Succession in the Sultanate is deliberately non formulaic, which is simultaneously its greatest source of stability and its most dangerous recurring vulnerability. The Sultan has no obligation to name his eldest son as heir and considerable incentive not to, elven lifespans mean that a Sultans eldest child may be a century old by the time the question of succession becomes pressing, while a younger grandchild might be more vigorous, more politically malleable, or more compatible with the institutional needs of the moment. Younger candidates, generally those below one hundred and fifty years of age, are strongly favoured as a matter of practical stability, since a young Sultan can be expected to reign for a century or more, providing the continuity that the empires vast administrative machinery requires. The <a href="Golden Dusk" class="internal-link">Golden Dusk</a> formally "recommends" a successor from among the Sultans children and grandchildren, a recommendation that carries significant institutional weight without being legally binding. The Keeper of Eternity manages this process from within the palace, shaping which candidates are seen as viable and which are politically neutralised before the question ever reaches formal deliberation. Peaceful successions, violent ones, successions by intrigue and successions by demonstrated merit have all occurred within the Elbae line, the dynastys longevity owes less to a consistent mechanism than to its consistent ability to produce at least one viable candidate around whom power can consolidate before the previous Sultans death.</p> <h3>The Dirhan</h3> <p>The Sultans executive council, the Dirhan from the ancient elvish for "chamber of voices", assembles in the Ar Dirhan in Jiravid, and is headed by the Viceroy. The Viceroy is the second most powerful person in the realm in ordinary times, administering the day to day functions of governance on the Sultans behalf and coordinating the eleven Asafi — the ministers who hold the great offices of state. Each Asafi  is appointed and dismissible by the Sultan alone, though in practice the Viceroys recommendation is rarely ignored.</p> <p>The eleven Asafi of the Dirhan are:</p> <p>The Asafi of Suhur, "Suhur" being the ancient elvish word for magic, is always the Sapphire Eye of Karajal of the <a href="Karajal School of Insight" class="internal-link">Karajal School of Insight</a>. The Asafi of Suhur serves as the Sultanates foremost advisor on magical matters, a role whose importance in a realm where magical power has repeatedly determined the outcome of wars cannot be overstated. The <a href="Institute for Practical Energies" class="internal-link">Institute for Practical Energies of Almadus</a>, while increasingly significant in the modern era, lacks comparable representation and its interests are typically filtered through the Asafi of Economy.</p> <p>The Asafi of Economy oversees the internal production, commerce and trade networks of the Sultanate, a domain of staggering complexity given the empires control of maritime trade routes connecting three continents. A dedicated representative of the <a href="Grand Ziflis Company" class="internal-link">Grand Ziflis Company</a> serves directly as advisor to this Asafi, a relationship that has made the boundary between state commercial policy and Company operations difficult to define.</p> <p>The Asafi of the Armies coordinates the Sultanate's four major land formations and their <a href="Masir" class="internal-link">Masiri</a>, the Field Marshalls who command each formation and report only to this Asafi and the Sultan directly, as well as all other land based military forces within the Realm. The Asafi of the Navy holds equivalent authority over the Elbae fleet, separate from the Army by design and reflecting the Sultanate's foundational identity as a maritime power whose commercial and military reach depend on naval supremacy.</p> <p>The Asafi of Taxation administers the collection of revenue across the empires territories, a function distinct from the Asafi of the Treasury, who manages the Sultanates accumulated wealth, controls the minting of the <a href="Talent" class="internal-link">Talent</a>, and maintains the financial instruments through which the empires commercial dominance is exercised. The distinction between these two offices, collection versus management, reflects hard won institutional lessons about the dangers of concentrating fiscal power.</p> <p>The Asafi of Law presides over the secular legal apparatus of the Sultanate, the "Sultan's Law", which was codified and substantially reworked in the 400s PA from the fragmented and inconsistent legal traditions inherited from the ancient dynasties and the various peoples absorbed into the empire. Beneath the Asafi sits a full hierarchy of ministers and judges whose authority extends across all of the Sultanate's territories, operating in parallel with but formally separate from the religious jurisdiction of the <a href="Golden Dusk" class="internal-link">Golden Dusk</a>.</p> <p>The Asafi of Truth controls the Sultanate's official internal security apparatus, the visible arm of the states capacity to police its own population and institutions. This is distinct from the <a href="Shuyat" class="internal-link">Shuyat</a>, which operates entirely outside the Dirhans structure. The events of the Crisis of the Golden Throne in the 4th century PA, in which a sitting Asafi of Truth staged a coup against the Sultan himself, prompted institutional reforms ensuring the position cannot be held by a direct blood heir of the reigning Sultan.</p> <p>Two permanent seats on the Dirhan are held by institutional representatives rather than appointed Asafi: the High-Priest of Jiravid, who serves as the political emissary of the <a href="Golden Dusk" class="internal-link">Golden Dusk</a> with the secular government; The Grand Hierophant holds  spiritual authority that in matters of religious rite nominally exceeds even the Sultans, but exercising that authority through the Office of High Priest is much more elegant in the political arena. As well as the Representative of the Underlands, an exceptional accommodation reflecting the uniquely tight cultural and religious bond between the surface elven civilization and the Drow communities of the deep. All other regional interests are channelled through the Great Masqar rather than the Dirhan directly, yet the Drow of the Underlands Tomb Cities hold a place within the Dirhan.</p> <p>Finally, a Representative of the Great Masqar sits on the Dirhan as a formal bridge between the two deliberative bodies, ensuring that the concerns of the empires noble and territorial interests reach the Sulta's council without requiring the Masqar itself to convene in Jiravid.</p> <h3>The Shuyat</h3> <p>No account of Elbae governance is complete without acknowledging the institution that operates entirely outside its visible structure. The <a href="Shuyat" class="internal-link">Shuyat</a>, "Shadow" in ancient elvish, is the Sultanates true intelligence and covert operations apparatus, and one of the most feared groups in the known world by those who have reason to know it exists. Its head reports exclusively to the Sultan and upon assuming the position gives up their name and former identity entirely, being known thereafter and only as the Shuyat. The institutions agents operate across the entire empire and its overseas interests, with unilateral authority that no Asafi, Viceroy or Masqar can compete with. What distinguishes the Shuyat from the Asafi of Truth, beyond the opacity of its operations, is the scope of its mandate: where the Asafi of Truth polices the realm, the Shuyat also watches the realms watchers. The Crisis of the Golden Throne demonstrated what the Shuyat is for. When every visible institution was paralyzed, the Dirhan by law, the Asafi of Truth by its own compromised head, the Golden Dusk by the limits of its religious authority, it was the Shuyat that observed, confronted the one person who had discovered the truth, coordinated the apprehension of the perpetrator, and then stepped back into anonymity, allowing a private individual to take the public credit while the institution itself vanished back into the shadows it had never left.</p> <h3>The Great Masqar</h3> <p>The Great Masqar convenes not in Jiravid but in Rahzalbis, one of the great cities of the Sultanate, a deliberate geographical separation that distinguishes it from the Dirhan and reflects its different character. Where the Dirhan is an executive body of appointed ministers, the Great Masqar is a deliberative assembly of the empires powerful, the great noble families of the Beheran heartland, the Emirs of subject territories, representatives of the major commercial interests, dignitaries of the Golden Dusk below the level of the High-Priest, and the Pashas of the core provinces. The Masqar does not govern but it advises, ratifies, debates and above all communicates, serving as the mechanism by which the empires vast and diverse holdings make their interests known to the centre and by which Jiravids policies are given the knowledge of broad institutional support before being enforced. A Sultan who consistently ignores the Masqar courts the kind of noble discontent that has historically preceded the worst succession crises. A Sultan who manages it well can present his decisions as emerging from consensus rather than egomania, which in a realm this size is practically indispensable.</p> <h3>Provincial Administration</h3> <p>The Sultanates territories are governed through a hierarchy of titles reflecting their relationship to the imperial center. The core Beheran provinces are administered by Pashas, regional governors appointed from the Sultan's court and answerable directly to the Dirhan, overseeing the major population centres and productive territories of the Hessef and Asef and beyond. Within cities and districts, Sanjaks hold local authority. Military frontier zones of particular strategic importance are placed under Beys, military and administrative commanders whose primary mandate is security rather than civil governance. Subject and autonomous territories, those with their own ruling dynasties or historical independence that have been absorbed into the Sultanates sphere, are governed by Emirs, who hold considerably more autonomy than Pashas but operate within the framework of the Sultanates law and owe military service and tribute to the White Chair.</p> <h3>The Military Formations</h3> <p>Beyond the many minor forces and local groups, the Sultanate maintains four major standing army formations, each a substantial military operation in its own right and each commanded by a Masir, a Field Marshall, who answers only to the Asafi of the Armies and the Sultan himself. The Dawn Army, stationed in Il-Haena and operating closely with the Elbae Navy under current Masir Muras al-Nala, is the projection force of elven power across the great island and its surrounding waters. The Dusk Army under current Masir Saharmir Kiran holds the fortifications of the Horad Strait and the empires northern border.. The Southern Army, currently the largest formation in the Sultanate, is stationed around Biadarkyr, Almadin and the occupied territories of Tusul under its current Masir Nayla al-Muhus, and now bears the weight of suppressing the Zipivi resistance that has characterized the region since the White Wars. Finally, the Army of the Serpent operates entirely within the Underlands, the vast cave systems below the Beheran Desert, currently under Masir Yashar Djakatt, composed entirely of Drow soldiers and guarding the empire's subterranean borders with the territories of Fogorix and Avdan-Kol against threats that the surface world rarely sees and only dimly understands.</p>

<h3>The Rise of Elbae</h3>
<p>Izzmu Alik, Head of the Elbae Dominion, which controlled the lands around Corbaik in the southern Deserts, during the later years of the Meter Dynasty, was a  gifted Politician and Spellbinder during the turn of the millennium.  Similar to most other surviving heads of families during the Great Fall of the Meter and the Eruption of Al-Akka, he had managed to find refugee in the underland Tomb City in time to avoid the most devastating fallout. Owing to ruthless opportunism and a powerful economic position, the Elbae dominion managed to assert its way over the other surviving splinters of the lost Elfrealm in Behera, notably with Izzmu Alik himself defeating the drow Wizard Abghazi in 22PA at the height of the the Wars of Ash in a personal duel. The Elbae established themselves as the successors to the Meter in 64PA, however Izzmu declared himself as Sultan instead of the ancient elvish title Jesshera. This rejection of uniform tradition with the pre Eruption Governance of the lands would be one of many in an effort to not only distance himself from the Meter line that was now viewed as cursed and unbecoming, but also to assert his dynasties rule as a final achievement and something greater than those that had come before.</p>
<h3>Lizun the Golden & the Paragon cult</h3>
<p>Lizun, later known as "the Golden", due to the circumstances sorrounding him during the Crisis of the Golden Throne, ascended the White Chair of Jiravid as the first of his name and the 4th Sultan of the Elbae in 320PA following his grandfathers, Merilas II, passing a year prior. Shortly after his ascension to power, Irimaa Anhur Elbae had awakened as a living Paragon and been enshrined within her Mausoleum in the Tomb City Biarol, below Biardarkyr. Irimaas Awakening would be the last officially recorded instance of this phenomenon as, in what likely was an effort to deify his whole bloodline further, Lizun declared the so called Doctrine of Elbae Exceptionalism, that would declare that henceforth only a blood relative of the Elbae line could possibly be considered worthy enough to be declared as a Paragon of elvenkind. While Lizuns power was absolute and unquestioned in practice, many criticized this decision even in Lizuns times, the Poet Orivin being a famous example of a  contemporary dissent. Those of Orivins mind called it a failed show of power that would not only permanently weaken the Sultanate in the future but also destroy and corrupt one of the single longest existing cultural practices within their entire species while at the same time failing at its purpose of power projection, instead betraying a frail weakness and narcissism within the Sultans Heart. Orivin would be exiled for over 30 years to the colonies where his poems and writings gradually shifted and embraced Lizuns rule and decision as wise all along, but he would never again return to the Beheran homelands and die in exile.</p>
<h3>The Crisis of the Golden Throne (382PA - 403PA)</h3> <p>The reign of <a href="Lizun I" class="internal-link">Lizun I</a> had, by the late 4th century PA, extended for over six decades, a stretch of rule whose length, combined with the profound resentment that the Doctrine of Elbae Exceptionalism had quietly accumulated among the Sultanate's noble families, created conditions that Lizun's own son chose to exploit. Inselim, known as "the Veiled," held the position of Asafi of Truth, head of the Sultanates official internal security apparatus while simultaneously being the Sultans heir apparent. He was a mage of considerable power, and in 382PA he used that power to transform his father into a statue of solid gold.</p> <p>The act was not murder, Inselim had deliberately left the process incomplete and Lizun remained alive within the statue, his consciousness suspended but intact, which meant that he remained, under every principle of Elbae law and custom, the Sultan of Elbae. No successor could be named. No regent could be formally appointed. The Dirhan was paralyzed, its authority derived from a Sultan who could neither speak nor act. The Golden Dusk could pronounce on matters of spiritual and religious concern but not resolve a political magical crisis of this character. The Ivory Guard, commanded by the Keeper of Eternity, answered to the Sultans household, but Lizuns own mother had been dead for generations given the length of his reign, leaving that institution without its traditional anchor. Every visible mechanism of Elbae governance had been simultaneously disabled by a single act.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Lizun.png" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Sultan Lizun I</i></small>  
</div> <p>What broke the Crisis open was not an institution but a person. Sareem ar Zaik, intimate companion of Inselims own son Irater, the future Sultan Irater I, had spent years embedded in the innermost circles of palace life and had grown deeply suspicious of Inselims conduct in the months before the statues appearance. When the <a href="Shuyat" class="internal-link">Shuyat</a>, identified Sareem as someone acting in ways that suggested knowledge of the plot rather than complicity in it, their agents confronted him directly. Sareem explained what he had pieced together. The Shuyat moved with the decisiveness that defines it: securing the palace, taking control of key dignitaries, and apprehending Inselim before he could act further, all within hours of Sareems disclosure.</p> <p>With Inselim in custody the question became one of establishing the full truth of what had been done and whether it could be undone. Rosham Anra, the Black Eye of <a href="Karajal School of Insight" class="internal-link">Karajal</a>, a wizard of such exceptional power that contemporaries described him simply as a monster walking among men, performed a complete arcane dissection of Inselim's mind: memory, intention, knowledge, and the precise magical architecture of the golden transformation laid bare in their entirety. Inselim did not survive the process. Rosham Anra then emerged from the Ar Dirhan and made a proclamation that would define the next two decades: Lizun I was alive. The process had been deliberately left incomplete and would at some point reverse itself. When that reversal would occur was beyond his knowledge. The Sultanate would wait.</p> <p>And so it did, for twenty one years. Sareem ar Zaik was presented publicly as the sole discoverer of the plot and the <a href="Shuyat" class="internal-link">Shuyat</a> vanishing back into the anonymity it had never truly left. The golden Lizun stood in the Palace of Eternity, technically supreme ruler of the largest empire in the world, while the Dirhan and the Great Masqar governed in his name as best they could. The Conflict in Il-Haena grinded to a halt without response. The Alliance of the Old Moon under Kamer of the New Fire took the city of  Timoq in 385PA, the Sultanate unable to mount a coherent military response with its command structure leaderless. The Crisis exposed with  clarity how much of the Sultanates coherence depended on the Sultan being a living, speaking, present person rather than a legal abstraction.</p> <p>Lizun returned to his body in 403PA. The manner of his return is not precisely recorded, whether it was gradual or sudden, whether he was aware of the passage of time or emerged as if no time had passed at all. What the records of his final two decades consistently reflect is a man changed : paranoid and irritable in ways he had not been before, deeply suspicious of those around him, but stripped of the grandiose certainty that had produced the Doctrine of Exceptionalism in the first place. The poet Orivin, whose exile Lizun himself had ordered thirty years before the Crisis, had written of the Doctrine as the work of a man "who confused the height of his chair with the height of his worth." Whatever Lizun became in his last years, he seems to have humbled himself. He ruled until 424PA, when Irater I, whose lover had saved the realm and whose father had nearly destroyed it, ascended the White Chair.</p>
<h3>The Conquest of Il-Haena </h3> <p>The island the <a href="Orc" class="internal-link">Qahori</a> called Qa had been home to their people since long before the first elven settlers arrived on its eastern shores in 278BA and named the coast Il-Haena. What followed was nearly seven centuries of pressure, demographic at first, then institutional, then military, against a people whose <a href="Qalmyr" class="internal-link">Faith</a> counselled against the organized resistance that might have halted it. The Sultanates early Rulers varied considerably in their posture toward the Qahori: Merilas I maintained genuine accommodation and was known to the Qahori as a friend, while his successor Merilas II reversed course immediately, overseeing the Slaughter of Timoq in 265PA that brought the Sultanates military directly into the islands affairs for the first time. Lizun Is Crisis of the Golden Throne provided an interval in which the Qahori Alliance of the Old Moon briefly recaptured Timoq under Kamer of the New Fire, demonstrating both that the Alliance could fight and that the Sultanates incomplete approach had left the conquest unfinished. Irater I launched the <a href="Wars of Qahori Subjugation" class="internal-link">Wars of Qahori Subjugation</a> in 413PA to complete it. Forty-four years later, under the command of Masir Amr al-Ziyar, the last organized Qahori resistance was destroyed at Zeqer and the island was declared fully incorporated into the Sultanate. The Qahori who remained were largely those who had collaborated, the <a href="Qarzi" class="internal-link">Qarzi</a>, granted their southeastern territories as reward. The rest were driven into the sea. For the full account of the military campaigns, see <a href="Wars of Qahori Subjugation" class="internal-link">Wars of Qahori Subjugation</a>.</p>
<h3>Arbalas Wars</h3> <p>The three <a href="Wars of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">Wars of Elbae Aggression</a>, fought across the reigns of Sultans Fasmer I, and Arbalas I, were the Sultanate's most sustained military effort and its most consequential failure at the same time. The commercial logic driving all three wars was the same: control of the Horad Isthmus and with it the chokepoint between the Stormy Sea and the Pilvian Gulf. The <a href="Grand Ziflis Company" class="internal-link">Grand Ziflis Company</a>, chartered during the First War in 754PA, was the commercial instrument built to exploit the dominance the military campaign was meant to secure. The First War ended in defeat at the Second Battle of Danipol, where Kherovan Elite regiments neutralized the Sultanate's arcane advantage and the deaths of Masir Hazir ab-Keyel and the Sultans own son Inrafir Elbae at the hands of <a href="Fredric" class="internal-link">Fredric Foehammer</a> broke the campaigns command. The Second War achieved the Sultanates first major territorial objective, the subjugation of <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Hainach</a> as an Eyalet via through a two theatre strategy that exploited the withdrawal of Kherova from the coalition and drove Masir Ulsun Paraks forces through the Mesovo River into Hainach whileFerik Mosul Horvanheld the Horad against the coalitions main northern force.</p>
<p> The Third War, commanded in its central theatre by the Sultan's third son Meas Arsifan Elbae, ended when the Ruby Eye of Karajal, <a href="Jurahim al-Biad" class="internal-link">Jurahim al-Biad</a>, completed an invocation the Sultan had ordered abandoned, sinking the Horad Isthmus, creating the <a href="Strait of Horad" class="internal-link">Strait of Horad</a>, and killing thousands on both sides including much of the assembled high command. The <a href="Treaty of Danipol" class="internal-link">Treaty of Danipol</a> of 815PA ended the wars without the territorial control the Sultanate had sought; the Strait that replaced the Isthmus proved, in the following decades, to entrench Elbae commercial dominance more thoroughly than any military victory could have done. For the full account of all three wars, see <a href="Wars of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">Wars of Elbae Aggression</a>.</p>
<h3>The Modern Sultanate</h3>
