The fighting began in 923PA and is often called the Garmish Civil War. It has split the Duchy in two. The Gethric north follows <a href="Coster of Melmbirth" class="internal-link">Coster of Melmbirth</a> and his Green Banner League, and the rich south, which still keeps the faith of the <a href="Kindly Lords" class="internal-link">Kindly Lords</a>, stands in the Garmish Alliance that <a href="Hynek of Forrington" class="internal-link">Hynek of Forrington</a> raised. Since early 926PA it has drawn in <a href="Eldmar" class="internal-link">Eldmar</a>, <a href="Urbridge" class="internal-link">Urbridge</a>, the <a href="Trinvale" class="internal-link">Trinvale</a> Republic, <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Hainach</a>, and the knights of <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a>.</p> <p>It is the first war fought on Garmish soil since the <a href="Eight Years War" class="internal-link">Eight Years War</a> ended in 652PA, and the first there in which firearms have been used in the field. Hynek was killed in his camp outside <a href="Redflow" class="internal-link">Redflow</a> in May of 927PA. At the end of that month the Alliance still lies round the city, the League still holds the Keep, and a child stands in Hynek's place.</p> <h2>Background</h2> <h3>The Riverthrone</h3> <p>For almost six centuries <a href="Garmeley" class="internal-link">Garmeley</a> was a kingdom of the Riverkings of Garm, who sat on the Riverthrone at Redflow after the oldest of the rites between the Garmish and the druids of the Gladewood, the rite of Glade and River by which <a href="Alfin I" class="internal-link">Alfin I</a> was crowned in 51PA. When King Ahlef II fell at Greyfields in 648PA the line of Alfin ended, and Tyla of Aldale refused the crown and proclaimed herself Duchess so that no rival claim could split the realm while the Vihri were still inside its borders. Her house took the name of the city, and the House of Redflow held the Riverthrone from the same Keep until 922PA (see <a href="Rulers of Garmeley" class="internal-link">The Rulers of Garmeley</a>).</p> <p>The Duchy is ruled through absolute cognatic primogeniture, by which the eldest child inherits regardless of sex. Since the end of the kingdom the Dukes have shared their power with the great lords of the Council of Lords, which includes the Counts of Melmbirth, Forrington and Thulmouth, the Countess of Dimglade and the Count of Koltree. The Duchy keeps no standing army beyond the household of the Riverthrone, and war is made by the levies and knights of the lords. A quarrel among the lords is therefore a quarrel among armies, and for nearly three centuries the lords have been too rich and too content to quarrel in earnest.</p> <h3>North and South</h3> <p>The rich south is the oldest part of the country. Around Lake Forr the flatlands feed the Duchy, the barges of the Trinmo carry its grain and silver to the sea, and the Counts of <a href="Forrington" class="internal-link">Forrington</a> are the richest of the Garmish lords. It keeps the old spirit faith of the Kindly Lords, which has no church and no ranks, and it is bound to <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Hainach</a> and the Trinvale cities by trade and in Hainach's case by faith as well. The north is poorer and thinly settled. Its people are mostly Gethric, its lords look toward Eldmar and Urbridge, and its towns, Melmbirth, Jorrenstead and Dimglade, are small beside Forrington.</p> 
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Agnes.png" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> <small><i>The late Duchess Agnes I of Garmeley</i></small> </div> 
<p>The east is a country of its own. The Kol runs from north to south through both Kolglade and Koltree and meets the Trinmo in the south, and the people who live along it are close in their customs to the druids of the nearby Gladewood.</p> <p>The divide between the two faiths has always followed the divide between the north and the south, and the quarrels over wealth and worship that ran along it were kept quiet through the long peace of the Dukes Albrecht V and Eadwin II and of Duchess Agnes. When Agnes died the old quarrel had no one to hold it.</p> <h3>The House of Redflow</h3> <p>Duke Eadwin II was the son of Albrecht V. He had three children, and the two eldest, Kazlav and Natalia, died before him, so that Agnes was the only one who outlived her father and took the Riverthrone. Eadwin II had a younger sister, Fiora, who married into the House of Forr and gave the Counts of Forrington their heir. That heir was Hynek, born in 891PA and five years older than his cousin the Duchess. His paternal grandmother had been a daughter of the House of Pikris, which is why the princes of Hainach count him a kinsman. His sister Regia was born about 909PA. Fiora died before her niece Agnes.</p> 
<h2>The Death of Agnes I</h2> <p>Duchess Agnes was killed at the Midsummer Festival of 922PA by <a href="Jonek Two Tongues" class="internal-link">Jonek Two-Tongues</a>, a rogue sorcerer who had escaped from prison. She was twenty-six years old, unmarried and without children. A party of adventurers who had been at the festival, <a href="Piero & Friends" class="internal-link">Piero and Friends</a>, hunted Jonek down and killed him in the Barrens, but the Duchess could not be brought back. The Duchy was left without a ruler, and a realm that was deeply divided by faith and by wealth.</p> <p>Under the law the Riverthrone passed to Agnes's closest kin in the line of Albrecht V, and that was her cousin Hynek. The Council of Lords had no need to choose between claimants, because the law named one. It was Coster of Melmbirth who refused to hear it.</p>

<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Hynek.png" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> <small><i>Count Hynek of Forrington, Leader of the Garmish Alliance</i></small> </div>  <h2>The Two Claims</h2> <h3>The Claim of Blood</h3> <p>Hynek was the eldest living child of Fiora and the closest living kin of the late Duchess, and on the law of the Duchy that made him heir. He was thirty-one years old in 922PA, the Count of Forrington and one of the richest lords of the Duchy. He was a fervent follower of the Kindly Lords, on good terms with the mages of <a href="Patwin Tower" class="internal-link">Patwin Tower</a>, the druids of the Gladewood and his Hainachi neighbours, and he was widely seen as the protector of the south. When the lords of the south gathered behind him they called themselves the Garmish Alliance.</p> <p>The weakness of his claim was that no one had ever tested it. Fiora had died, so the Riverthrone came to her son by representation, and in a country that had not seen a disputed succession for nearly three centuries the north could say that a law no one had needed was a thin thing to die for.</p> <h3>The Claim of Custom</h3>
 
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Coster.png" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> <small><i>Count Coster of Melmbirth, Leader of the Green Banner League</i></small> </div>  <p>Coster Riedek, then twenty-five and the Count of Melmbirth, called by his enemies Black Coster, had no claim in law that his enemies would admit. He claimed the Riverthrone by the ancient custom of the Riverkings, the rite of Glade and River. Alfin I had been crowned in that rite as the husband of a daughter of the Gladefolk. Coster went into the Gladewood and took a woman of the circles there by force, and held up the act as his right to the throne. The Gladefolk and the south call it an outrage, and the druids have fought against him ever since.</p> <p>The Count of Melmbirth is a devout Gethric and a proclaimed defender of the faithful, and the cause he raised was above all a religious one. He declared the Green Banner League soon after the death of Agnes, to speak for the Gethric north against the southern lords. His claim was vague and his position weak, and he could not win by the law. What he could do was find friends. In 919PA he had married Princess Lena, the youngest daughter of Queen Moira of Eldmar, and through her the League had a royal ally before it had a single battle.</p> <p>The Council of Lords, which might have settled the matter, stands divided by the quarrel. Forrington, Thulmouth and Koltree sat for the Alliance, and Melmbirth and Dimglade for the League, three seats to two. It has not settled anything.</p> <h2>The War</h2> <h3>923PA: The Outbreak</h3> <p>In February of 923PA Citykeeper Rimond of <a href="Niedrest" class="internal-link">Niedrest</a> held a two week celebration for his second marriage, and a <a href="Tourney at Niedrest" class="internal-link">tourney</a> at its end drew knights from all the realms. Gorlan V of Urbridge came, and Prince Jakub of Vihr, Talion of Gelmsburg and Sybilla of Pikris. During the tourney the Kherovan embassy in the city was attacked and its staff murdered. This was the <a href="Niedrest Incident" class="internal-link">Niedrest Incident</a>, and its consequences reached Garmeley two years later. Prince Jakub, who had come to joust, took up the cause of the Green Banner League that year.</p> <p>Later that year the League's forces raided the Vormy Estate and took Lady Regia, Hynek's fourteen year old sister, hostage. The southern lords condemned it unanimously. Hynek declared the League's lords traitors to the country and common criminals, and proclaimed open war on them in the name of the Garmish Alliance. The Regent Council of Hainach, acting for the child Prince <a href="Miriam II" class="internal-link">Miriam II</a>, declared its support for the Count of Forrington and invited intervention from other realms. The League, for its part, could not win the backing of any throne, and so it hired mercenary and adventuring bands with promises of loot and pay from the wealthy southerners. Prince Jakub led a force of knights for the Count of Melmbirth, though the royal family of Vihr refused to see his deeds as anything but the heroics of one man.</p> <p>In the early autumn the city of <a href="Dolm" class="internal-link">Dolm</a> declared for the Green Banner, largely to gain independence from Forrington and Tringlen. It lay in the hills of the Lock behind impressive mountain fortifications and a maze of tunnels, and the siege that Hynek began there was to last almost three years. Hynek remained with a smaller force at Dolm while Baron Roderick of Aldale gathered the main armies of the south at Forrington and in the western flatlands. Coster did not march to relieve the city. The southern armies were larger than his, and he decided that the odds were against him.</p> <p>Instead the League began a campaign of pillage in the east. The northern forces destroyed farmsteads and villages east of the Barrens in great numbers, and the campaign ended with the sack of Koltree. The druids of the Gladewood withdrew into the forest before the raiders, and survivors of the villages followed them in and began a guerrilla war from the shadows of the trees. The League's soldiers do not dare to follow them in, for fear of the druids and the spirits of the wood. The priests and druids of the Farrman took up arms openly for the south. Among the houses that fell was that of the Count of Kolglade, who died when his seat was burned. His son Jan, the last of the house, fought a guerrilla war against the raiders in eastern Garmeley for months, until he fled across the Trollbacks to Vihr in late 925PA.</p> <div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Garmeley Burns.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> <small><i>Garmeley Burns: northern and Vihri knights sack the countryside of the east.</i></small> </div> <h3>924PA: The Sack of Sunminster</h3> <p>In 924PA Baron Roderick of Aldale, a seasoned commander, secured Koltree and marched on <a href="Sunminster" class="internal-link">Sunminster</a>, the neutral holy city of the Kindly Lords. He denounced the northern lords as Gethric barbarians and villains, and the token force of the church city yielded after a minor engagement. Bishop Pityr was taken hostage and the cathedral was looted, a great store of relics and gold being shipped south to Forrington. The sanctuary of the Leafpriests was left untouched.</p> <p>In the north the sack was taken as proof of what the Alliance was. In the south it was said that the Alliance had spared what was holy. In the rest of the continent it was taken as the point at which a quarrel of lords became a war of faiths. One of the oldest bishoprics in the country had been stripped, and foreign sentiment began to shift.</p> <h3>925PA: Jestrick Fields</h3> <p>Following the taking of Sunminster, the southern lords met the combined forces of the Green Banner League in open battle at Jestrick Fields, north of Koltree, in 925PA. The southern army fought under Baron Roderick of Aldale, and a great contingent of Hainachi soldiers stood with him. The northern host was led by Kylian of Redflow, the Castellan of Redflow Keep. The battle was bloody and a devastating defeat for the north. Roderick was a huge man, and he fought in the front of his line with several great spears that he handled almost like lances, killing the knights of the north as if he were hunting boars. The south gave him the name of the Spear of Jestrick for the victory. Kylian fell, and his son Almos took the castellany. The command of the mercenary knights and the rest of the northern troops passed to Jill the Good. Countess Nadya of Dimglade lost both her sons and heirs. The southern army suffered only minor losses, but Count Miklas of Koltree was killed by Prince Jakub in the fighting. Jakub was forced to retreat. With Miklas dead and the heirs of Dimglade dead, the succession in both counties is in doubt, and two seats of the Council of Lords stand uncertain.</p> <div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Roderick.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> <small><i>Roderick of Aldale, the Spear of Jestrick.</i></small> </div> <div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/northern soldier.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> <small><i>Kylian of Redflow, Castellan of Redflow Keep.</i></small> </div> <p>The same year brought a change of another kind. Kher condemned the Trinvale Republic in full for the Niedrest Incident, and Kherova became the official financial and political backer of the Green Banner League.</p> <h3>926PA: The International Intervention</h3> <p>The sack of Sunminster and the defeat at Jestrick Fields spelled dire news for Coster, but they led to developments no one had foreseen. As the war grew more divided along religious lines, and not only cultural and political ones, foreign sentiment turned. In the early months of 926PA Queen Moira III of Eldmar and the old Lord-Protector Gorlan V of Urbridge pledged themselves and their realms to the Green Banner, and many adventurers came from Vihr to join what they saw as the heroic struggle of the young Prince Jakub against vile heathens. Urbridge and Eldmar siding with the north more or less forced the hands of the Trinvale cities. Niedrest, Calmbrook and Zacin, which had been the mercantile partners of the south through the Trinmo for decades, joined the Garmish Alliance, with Oleg of Zacin as their general.</p> <p>The escalation of the civil war into an international one brought an overall economic downturn to the whole region. Harvests were lost and farms were pillaged. Firearms and early artillery began to arrive from <a href="Battvia" class="internal-link">Battvia</a>, and elite gunpowder units became a terror to armoured knights, changing the way war was made. In these years magic made little difference to the war. Only Patwin Tower took a clear stand, and the international community urged strongly against the use of the arcane on the battlefield.</p> <p>In the east, in the same year, the Battle of Lanfield was fought. It was a minor battle between the Eldmari contingents and the Knights of the Unicorn on the northern side and southern troops under Count Nials of Gredec, who had the support of Hainach. Before it was decided Selizza the Morning Knight met Nials in single combat and defeated him, though the Count carried his famous relic sword, Windkiss, it proved that her sunlight blade was its equal and her skill superior. The north won the field after the duel. But Selizza was appalled by the conduct of the Knights of the Unicorn, whom she called perfumed robber barons, and she let Nials go and thanked him for an honourable duel. Prince Jakub and Redbor of Tanverley quarrelled bitterly over it, and Redbor, who defended Selizza, spoke the words that are now repeated in Eldmar: "The Blades of Eldmar are here to fight with honour or not at all."</p> <p>Dolm surrendered in late 926PA after almost three years, on terms that were harsh but fair. Noble hostages were taken and the mines of the city passed into the hands of the south, and rumour says that cells of resistance still hide in the deep network of tunnels in and under the town. The Trinvale cities were now joined to Hainach and southern Garmeley by land. The Alliance wasted little time, and in the last days of the year it marched north and laid siege to Redflow Keep.</p> <h3>927PA: The Siege of Redflow</h3> <p>Through the winter and spring the war went on in two places. In eastern Garmeley the terror campaign of Prince Jakub went on, carried out by Vihri adventurers, the knights of the Order of the Unicorn, and Eldmari troops, who raided once again along the Kol. Count Nials of Gredec holds the south's position in the east from a camp north of Gliwn. In the heartlands west of the Barrens, meanwhile, organised armies took the field against each other. In Redflow Almos Brezina held the Keep for the League, and the city round it, thirty thousand people of mixed faiths and now a refuge for the burned villages of the countryside, waited out the siege without taking a side.</p> <div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/southern soldier.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> <small><i>A southern infantryman of the Garmish Alliance.</i></small> </div> <p>The worst of these clashes was the Slaughter of Karolrest in January. Karolrest, the town south-west of Redflow, was held by southern forces, chiefly contingents from the Trinvale, and it was attacked by Knights of Fortune of the north, at least in name. It seemed that the defenders had won a decisive victory. Then one of the attackers, who seemed to be gifted in magic, lost control of the power and exploded in a fury of lightning and fire, which destroyed large parts of the town's outskirts, the farmland round it and many of the combatants. The attackers scattered. The victory had become a pyrrhic loss for both sides. The north formally denied any part in the attack and called the attackers brigands, so as to avoid a quarrel under the <a href="Orenburg Pact" class="internal-link">Orenburg Pact</a>.</p> <p>The road from Jorrenstead to Redflow is blocked. The Riverfleet of the south, a number of ships together with four mercenary companies, has built a blockade across the Melm and the road north of the city, and skirmishers attack it almost daily. It is held chiefly by two of those companies and by troops under Meynard of Thulmouth, the second son of Count Elric, who is twenty-two and is said to have a strained relationship with his father. The Gallant Hounds, still led by Horatio the Smiling Baron, are the company that left the field at the Battle of Vorella in the <a href="Vorellan War" class="internal-link">Vorellan Wars</a>, and they have been thought dishonoured ever since for what was seen as their betrayal. The Handeaters come from northern Hainach.</p> <p>The north means to break it. Jill the Good is organising the attempt to break through to Redflow, and she is arguably the most experienced commander that the north has. But Lord-Protector Gorlan V of Urbridge hates her, largely out of contempt for a woman in command, and the quarrel troubles the League.</p> <p>In the Barrens a rogue group of sorcerers has taken control of the heath. They call themselves the Harrowlords, and amid the chaos of the war they have set up a small domain of magical war baronies. The best known of them is Eronima the Red, said to be a <a href="Kerlaes" class="internal-link">Kerlaeni</a> warlock and an infamous binder of <a href="Djinn" class="internal-link">nurdjinn</a>, her motives unknown but her methods terrifying.</p> <div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Eronima.png" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> <small><i>Eronima the Red of the Harrowlords.</i></small> </div> <p>The fighting has also begun to spread beyond Garmeley. Reports say that knights of <a href="Urbridge" class="internal-link">Urbridge</a> are marching through the Sourlands on Lindahall and later on Niedrest. The Lord-Mayor of the Trinvale, Citykeeper Rimond of <a href="Niedrest" class="internal-link">Niedrest</a>, has hired a host of Balaqi hobgoblins from <a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a> to defend the north of the Republic. The choice has set off a chain of reactions, since many in Ur still despise the hobgoblins as inhuman monsters.</p> <p>In May of 927PA Count Hynek of Forrington, the heir to the Duchy and the leader of the Garmish Alliance, was killed in his camp south of the siege works by an unknown assassin. No credit was claimed by Coster or his allies, and rumours of their part in it spread at once. Hynek was survived by his young son Milos, and his sister Regia is still held by the League. The siege has continued without him.</p> <h2>The Foreign Powers</h2> <h3>Vihr</h3> <p>The Kingdom of Vihr has no part in the war. Ras <a href="Vormil III" class="internal-link">Vormil III</a> opposed it from the start, and the royal family has refused to acknowledge what its second son does in Garmeley as anything more than the heroics of one man. But Prince <a href="Jakub Ralpia" class="internal-link">Jakub</a>, who is second in line to the throne, a captain of the <a href="Knights of the Unicorn" class="internal-link">Knights of the Unicorn</a> and a successful commander and tournament knight, is there all the same, and the Order has committed its finances and its elite forces to him. Once more the knights of Vihr have crossed the Trollbacks into eastern Garmeley, as they did in 644PA. Among those seen in Jakub's retinue for much of his time in the war was the mercenary <a href="Orlatz" class="internal-link">Orlatz</a>, who left for the south without telling anyone in the Prince's company why.</p> <p>The truth is that Jakub's war is a struggle within Vihr. Master Dorman of the White Sea, the head of the Order, backs him and has been in many ways a second father to him. His elder brother, the Kisras Waśko, sees other countries such as Meadrath modernising and centralising while Vihr, with its old decentralised structure, falls behind, and wants reforms that will strengthen the crown. He sees no point in senseless wars with Garmeley, and looks south and east as much as inward. Waśko is backed by members of the Trazlov Consortium, and in truth most of all by the Sekretar of Vihr, Tadeuz Pilsud of the Vaergriff, who serves only the realm and sees Jakub as a cause of instability. The Ras, who has grown more uncertain with the years and less able to assert the strong will he once had, is caught between them. Jakub, for his part, is testing how far he can go and who will stand against him, and the Knights of the Unicorn seem to back him entirely. It is rumoured that Pilsud has sent agents of the Vaergriff to ride beside the Kis and report on him, without Jakub's knowledge.</p> <p>The <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">Church of Iztaw</a> has taken the side that the Ras has refused. High-Bisop Orthel II is too afraid to oppose Vormil III in the open, but the Church supports the north in effect, and even Jakub. Vihr is a powderkeg politically, and the Garmish war has been the spark.</p> <h3>Eldmar</h3> <p>Queen Moira III pledged herself to the Green Banner in early 926PA. The alliance was sealed by marriage, since Coster wed her youngest daughter, Princess Lena, in 919PA, and beyond the marriage the Queen is moved by the shared Gethric cause of the north and by the hope of lands in northern Garmeley. The knights of Eldmar are led by Redbor of Tanverley, the heir to the County of Tanverley, a renowned fighter, and they ride with Prince Jakub and the Knights of the Unicorn in the raids on eastern Garmeley. Their absence has left the Eld Valley thin. Among the knights of Eldmar the north has found a hero in Selizza the Morning Knight, a paladin of <a href="Volboris" class="internal-link">Saint Volboris</a> who fights with a Blade of Sunlight and who bested Count Nials of Gredec in single combat at Lanfield. Rumour makes her the lover of Redbor of Tanverley.</p>

<h3>Urbridge</h3> <p>Lord-Protector Gorlan V of Urbridge pledged the Order in early 926PA, and his support, with Moira's, pushed the Trinvale Republic into the Garmish Alliance. The Order has its own reasons. Garmeley was for centuries a massively rich and powerful neighbour, larger and more prestigious than the Order, and the Urien lords have long remembered that Duke Jakub III took the Earldom of Dim after the Splintering of Urland. Jorrenstead, the Pearl of northern Garmeley, stands just across the Melm from Urbridge, and its Lord-Mayor is a friend of the League. The valuable Silvermines on the east side of the Lock are a prize, and the knights are not above becoming vultures if Garmeley should collapse entirely. Reports say that the knights have begun to march through the Sourlands on Lindahall, and from there on Niedrest, which would carry the war out of Garmeley and into the Trinvale.</p> <h3>Kherova</h3> <p>The Khatrym of <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Kherova</a> has not sent an army. Its part is money and influence. After the Niedrest Incident the Kherovan embassies in Urland and Garmeley demanded a full inquiry, the resignation of the Citykeeper of Niedrest and reparations, and in 925PA Kher condemned the Trinvale Republic and made itself the official financial and political backer of the Green Banner League. The arrangement has another side. Some Inquisitors of the <a href="Kherovan Inquisition" class="internal-link">Kherovan Inquisition</a> have been promised to Coster as a temporary guard against witches and sorcerers on the other side, which is most likely meant to buy passage for Kherovan agents in the region and the goodwill of the Urien lords. Further parties of Inquisitors have been seen moving through the north. Their numbers and their errand are not known outside Kher, and Kherova has not explained them.</p> <h3>Hainach and the Sultanate</h3> <p>Hainach is the closest ally of the south. It shares the faith of the Kindly Lords, trades chiefly with Garmeley, and is bound to the House of Forr by blood, and it was the first realm to declare for Hynek. It is also strong enough to make war on its own. The Regent Council of Prince Miriam II, who is fourteen and not yet betrothed, is led by Prince Torgos of Diniv, and it sent a vast contingent of soldiers to Jestrick Fields. Many of the nobles of the Princedom see the child ruler and the war as an opportunity to take power for themselves.</p> <p>The war has brought a refugee crisis to Hainach, and many of the refugees have gone on across the Mesovo border into the Sultanate of Elbae. The Sultan is formally uninvolved, trades with southern Garmeley through ports such as Thulmouth, and has troubles of his own: a drug crisis is growing in the northern cities along the Stormy Sea coast.</p> <h3>Trinvale</h3> <p>The Republic did not choose the south at once. The cities of Niedrest, Calmbrook and Zacin had been the mercantile partners of southern Garmeley through the Trinmo for decades, but they joined the Garmish Alliance only in 926PA, when Eldmar and Urbridge went over to the League and left them little choice. Their trade is the road through the Pass of Ogul-Vosko and down the river, and the Republics took the side of those who bought their goods. When Dolm surrendered, the Republic was joined to Hainach and southern Garmeley by land. Trinvale contingents held Karolrest when the north attacked it in January 927PA, and with the knights of Urbridge on the move toward Lindahall the Lord-Mayor has hired a host of Balaqi hobgoblins to guard the north, an act that has drawn scorn from those in Ur who still call the hobgoblins monsters.</p> <h3>The Others</h3> <p><a href="Gelmsburg" class="internal-link">Gelmsburg</a> has taken no side. Its envoys sit at the court of Eldmar and with the Green Banner League, and Gelmish merchants sell grain, horses and arms to both camps. <a href="Uhl" class="internal-link">Uhl</a> and the Free Lands of Triff have taken no side either. <a href="Grivil" class="internal-link">Grivil</a> has kept out of the war, though its Baron, <a href="Polgo Fiago" class="internal-link">Polgo Fiago</a>, was a friend of Hynek and sympathised with him, and rumour says that the killers of the Count found refuge in Grivil or were sent from it. The hobgoblins of <a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a> have noticed that Eldmar and Urbridge look south, but no chief has found a following to act on it.</p> <h2>Faith and Magic</h2> <p>The war is not only a war of the north against the south, but of two faiths. The Kindly Lords have no church, and they have been divided by the war as much as the country has. The priests and druids of the Farrman, the water spirit of Lake Forr, fight in the open for the Alliance, which is unusual among the followers of the faith, and the druids of the Gladewood have withdrawn into the forest and fight the north from it. The Leafpriests of Sunminster are split. Their best known, Mirosh Gocar, has not yet declared for either side. In the north it is called a defence of the Gethric faithful, and the Church of Iztaw, in practice, agrees.</p> <p>Patwin Tower is the only institution of the arcane that has taken a side. Its Archmage, Vaelon Divis, declared for the Alliance, and the Tower's decision was seen as a step toward the use of magic in war, which the <a href="Orenburg Pact" class="internal-link">Orenburg Pact</a> forbids. So far the Tower has kept its mages off the field, and its enemies say that this will not last. The stand has divided the Tower itself. <a href="Gharel Talnod" class="internal-link">Gharel Talnod</a> left in opposition to its interventionism and now lives at Karajal, while <a href="Janez Vajkard Valvasor" class="internal-link">Janez Vajkard Valvasor</a> spoke for the southern cause at Reanport, denounced the scholars and mages fighting for Coster, and disappeared from public view a year later. The uproar among the scholars was at first confused with the silent revolution against the <a href="Wermian Accords" class="internal-link">Wermian Accords</a>, but it grew into a humanitarian reaction of the academies against the escalation of the war.</p> <p>Magic has not stayed off the field. At Karolrest in January 927PA one mage who lost control of the power destroyed much of the town and many of the men who were fighting over it, and the north has denied that the attackers were its own. In the Barrens the self-styled Harrowlords have made a small domain of their own from magical war baronies.</p> <h2>The Cost of the War</h2> <p>Garmeley, which was the jewel of Ur, is now a country of burned villages and refugees. The villagers of the east have gone into the Gladewood or into Redflow, whose streets are full of them, and many others have left the Duchy altogether for Hainach and beyond. Among them is <a href="Theresa Salbridge" class="internal-link">Theresa Salbridge</a>, a detective who left Grevec at the outbreak of the war, went south, and later crossed the Mesovo into the Sultanate. The fate of <a href="Elena Montegru" class="internal-link">Elena Montegru</a>, a duellist of Dolm, who came home to the city in 923PA, is not recorded, and she is not among the nobles known to be in custody.</p>

<p>The silver trade of the west was ruined by the siege of Dolm and is only now starting again under the Alliance. Harvests have been lost and the trade of the Trinmo has dwindled. Of the soldiers of fortune that Prince Jakub called across the Trollbacks, many turned to looting and brigandage, and wrecked the countryside even further.</p> <h2>The Situation at the End of May 927PA</h2> <p>Twelve days after Hynek's death, in the last days of May, the Alliance is without a head. His son Milos, a boy of six whose mother is dead, is Count of Forrington and the heir to the Riverthrone, and Elric of Thulmouth, the senior count of the Alliance, rules for him as regent. Baron Roderick of Aldale keeps the command in the field. Regia, who is eighteen now, is held at Waterbreak Keep in Melmbirth. The succession has become more uncertain than before, for a child can die. If Milos were removed, Regia would be the last of the line of Fiora, and whoever married her would have children with an undisputed claim to the Riverthrone. That makes her the most important person in Garmeley, and one of the least free.</p> <p>Coster holds court at Jorrenstead with the Eldmari and Urbridge contingents, and Almos Brezina holds the Keep at Redflow. The road between them is closed by the blockade of the south, which skirmishers attack almost daily while Jill the Good organises an attempt to break through. Bishop Pityr is held at Forrington. Count Nials of Gredec still holds the south in the east, from his camp north of Gliwn.</p> <p>No one knows who killed Hynek. The north is blamed, since it gains most from it, though neither Coster nor his allies have claimed the deed. Others point to Grivil, or to a lord of the south who gains by a child heir and a regency, or to foreign powers who would like to see rich Garmeley torn apart. It is not known what the Inquisitors of Kherova want, whether the Ras of Vihr can call back his son or whether the realm will split first, what the Sultanate will do if the refugees keep coming, what Gelmsburg is waiting for, how far the knights of Urbridge will carry the war beyond Garmeley, what the Harrowlords want, or whether the cells under Dolm will rise.</p> <h2>People of Interest</h2>
