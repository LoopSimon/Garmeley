<p>The Hobgoblin Invasion of Urland, beginning in the winter of 690PA and ending with the Battle of Gobsfall Keep in 696PA, was the catastrophic military event that destroyed the <a href="Urland" class="internal-link">Kingdom of Urland</a> and replaced it with the <a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a> confederation in the west and a fractured collection of successor states in the east. A kingdom that had stood for nearly seven centuries, the inheritor of the northern ancient Rakhian provinces and the cultural heartland of the <a href="Urien" class="internal-link">Urien peoples</a>, was brought down in six years by a force that emerged from the Kortopi Mountains without warning into territory that had known peace for generations. The Hobgoblin horde was stopped at the Trinmo River but everything west of it was lost, including the capital Dursan, the royal bloodline, the Archbishops seat, and the majority of the kingdoms senior nobility. The world that existed east of the Trinmo after 696PA was not the world that had existed before 690PA.</p>

<h2>Origins</h2>

<p>The <a href="Hobgoblin" class="internal-link">Hobgoblin</a> peoples of the Kortopi Mountains were not unknown to the inhabitants of Ur. The Mitimgar cave network, the vast subterranean system of interconnected caverns beneath the Kortopi through which Hobgoblin communities organized their society, trade and warfare, was understood as a known of occasional surface raids, a threat to be managed rather than anticipated as a civilizational danger. The deadly Shroomfever, breaking out in the Kortopi in 678PA, attacked the cave ecosystem that the Hobgoblin communities depended upon. The subsequent collapse of Urgil Cave, a primary junction in the Mitimgar network, severed the Tirbo Clan completely from the rest of their people. Cut off, facing the destruction of their underground world, under the leadership of their warlord Tirbo, they went up. While unknown to the humans of Ur, the Goblins of Mitimgar had actually faced the same threat before, their Hobgoblin cousins and neighbours cornered the food supply and threatened to keep their more numerous but weaker goblin neighbours hostage, in response the Ruler of Mitimgar, Kerez-Ik, simply collapsed the entire tunnel that connected Urgil cave where the Hobgoblins encamped with food supply but no hope of permanent survival, to the rest of Mitimgar.</p>

<p>What emerged into the western valleys of Urland in the winter of 690PA was not a raid but an entire culture with nowhere else to go, led by a figure whose authority among the Clans derived from a combination of military supremacy and something approaching divine regard. A warrior in red armour, likely enchanted,  that his followers believed grew stronger with every enemy it killed, who had led them through the Urgil Cave collapse, the Shroomfever and out the other side into the light. Advising him was Chiakal, known as Ken-Har, "who sees the path", the clan's Grand Priest at only twenty years old, who had reportedly foreseen the conquest before it began and who would later foresee his own death before it arrived. Fighting for him was Korgaz, the Blade of Noon and champion of the clan, holder of the inherited relic blade passed from champion to champion across generations, a warrior of fifty-four years who had defended his title in ritual combat annually for thirty one years, longer than any champion in recorded Dirgae history. The clan also carried with it a reluctant ally: Elamak, leader of a monastic sect whose Kaladi practice emphasized harmony and who had joined Tirbos campaign only out of necessity, participation being the price of his people's survival.</p>

<p>The lands they emerged into, the Duchy of Hadley around Hadley Lake and the Earldom of Torcester to its south, were among the most peaceful and prosperous in Urland. The old Urna heartland, settled for centuries, its farms and river towns wealthy from trade along the Trinmo. They had no fortifications, no standing armies, no framework for what was coming. The Hobgoblins swept through them like locusts, the phrase used in many urien contemporary account usually derogatory by human scholars, and by the time any organized military response was possible, much of western Urland was already gone.</p>

<h2>Initial Engagements</h2>

<p>The first organized Urien response came in the spring of 691PA. Earl Lodvig the Fat of Torcester, whose demesne had been among the first to fall, assembled a host and led it against the invaders at Elpfield. He underestimated them in a manner that would become a recurring feature of early Urien engagements with Tirbos Clan: the Hobgoblins were large, brutal in appearance and unfamiliar in their methods of war, and commanders who expected a disorganized warband found instead a disciplined force fighting under the direction of experienced leaders who had been living and breathing Dirgae military culture their entire lives. Korgaz killed Lodvig in the fighting and his assembled forces broke. Countess Maria of Torcester, Lodvig's wife, assumed leadership of the western nobility after her husband's death, organizing the evacuation of civilians, coordinating the surviving nobility, and maintaining a coherent political and military presence on behalf of the western territories as those lands ceased to exist under human rule.</p>

<p>The months that followed Elpfield were a grinding advance without a decisive engagement that could be called a battle. Forest communities in the north fell. River towns fell. The gold mines of Ilsbreak and Songhole, formerly the crown's personal source of wealth, were taken by people who had no particular interest in what they produced. Throughout this period, a figure made a choice that many historians have argued about since: <a href="Kifrir" class="internal-link">Kifrir the Mirror-Dwarf</a>, companion of the recently canonised <a href="Dorian" class="internal-link">Saint Dorian</a> and the de facto leader of Dorians Shields of Summer Company within the <a href="Knights of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">Knights of the Gryphon</a>, was faced with a decision when the main Urien military forces began assembling for what would become the Battle of Foarley. He could join the army or could lead the many defenceless civilians east across the Trinmo to safety. He chose the civilians, the farmers, commonfolk and village communities whose defenders had died at Elpfield and who had no one else and was not there for Foarley. He was not there for Hadley. He was not there for the siege. He was moving as many innocents across a river while kings and Dukes died, yet he would be the one who killed Tirbo when the moment finally came.</p>

<h2>The Battle of Foarley</h2>

<p>In 693PA a more capable force assembled under Crown Prince Berengar and Count Paul of Foarley, knights errant and adventurers alongside the levies of the surviving western lords, a harder body of fighters than Lodvigs host. It was shattered anyway, though the manner of its defeat revealed something about the Invaders that the Urien nobility had not yet understood. The Hobgoblins feigned a retreat from Foarley, drawing Berengars mounted knights in pursuit into the dense trees and swamps north of the city, where their horses were useless, their formation impossible to maintain, and the space they needed to fight in the style they had trained for did not exist. Then the ambush descended as the Urien force was broken in terrain that neutralized everything it had. Berengar escaped with a fraction of his host while  Count Paul of Foarley gave his life during the fighting after being dragged of his warhorse. The city fell and was renamed Tehel by the Hobgoblins, one of the first deliberate acts of cultural and religious renaming that would eventually give Dursan the name Hobgiliath. The Tirbo were not a warband but already a people making a new home, naming things accordingly.</p>

<h2>The Battle of Hadley</h2>

<p>The Battle of Hadley, fought on the shores of the lake that would bear Tirbos name thereafter, was the largest Urien military effort of the invasion and its most catastrophic defeat. The assembled force included Crown Prince Berengar, Duke Oswald III of Turfield, Grand Master Samson the Quick of the Knights of the Gryphon, Marshal Henry of Leston and "the Plated Lady", Countess Maria of Torcester, alongside Garmish knights sent by Duke Jakub III of Garmeley. Tirbo commanded the Hobgoblin forces but Korgaz himself was his strongest asset.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Rezen.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Rezen the Manbiter</i></small>  
</div>

<p>The day produced moments that entered the histories of both sides. Korgaz killed Grand Master Samson the Quick,  as the longest serving champion in Dirgae memory cut down the leader of Urlands premier military order in an honourable duel valued by both warriors.Rezen Manbiter, one of the clans great warriors, engaged Duke Oswald III of Turfield in single combat in yet another  the honour duel that both sides martial cultures usually understood as a thing apart from the battle around it. Crown Prince Berengar, watching it unfold as his forces were losing ground around him, drove his blade into Rezen from behind. When Rezen fell, Oswald turned and mourned the breach of the duel's terms aloud, within earshot of witnesses on both sides. Berengar had acted from love for his people rather than dishonour, and Oswald knew it, but the battle was still lost. The Urien army broke and fell back toward Dursan. Korgaz had killed in numbers that day that contemporaries struggled to account for. Marshal Henry of Leston organized the retreat as best a retreat from a battle of that magnitude could be organized and the survivors moved east toward the capital.</p>

<h2>The Siege of Dursan</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Siege of Dursan.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>The Capital burns</i></small>  
</div>

<p>The Hobgoblin horde laid siege to <strong>Dursan</strong> in the autumn of 694PA. The city that had been the seat of the Gryphon Throne for nearly seven centuries, the home of the Archbishop of Ur, the administrative center of a kingdom stretching from the Bay of Uhl to the Kerlaen Wilderness, was besieged by people who had emerged from a collapsed cave system nine months earlier and had not lost a significant engagement since. It held for the better part of a year.</p>

<p>Marshal Henry of Leston died in the sieges early days. Archbishop Felix of Preston wielded incredible divine magicks in the citys defence in ways that the accounts describe as called by the Prophets Light, relying on whatever powers his faith afforded him, supporting the defenders to the last. He died at the walls. Countess Maria of Torcester, who had led the western nobility through three years of retreat and organization, died from the sieges later diseases. Korgaz led the assault on the walls and killed Duke Oswald III of Turfield in personal combat, completing what the Battle of Hadley had interrupted and amending the Shame of Rezens defeat. Oswald wounded Korgaz severely enough that the champion would take no further part in the wars remaining battles. Chiakal Ken-Har, who had reportedly foreseen this moment months earlier and had declined every opportunity to avoid it, died at the siege as he had seen he would. Crown Prince Berengar died heroically defending the city he had failed to save at Hadley and Foarley. King Uther IV "the Last Gryphon" perished during the storming of his capital, his line extinguished. The Garmish knights that Duke Jakub III had sent in support were trapped inside the walls and fell with the garrison. Dursan fell in the summer of 695PA, the Rassel dynasty was extinct and the Archbishop's seat was destroyed. The Hobgoblins renamed the city Hobgiliath and Hadley Lake became Lake Tirbo.</p>

<h2>The Battle of Gobsfall Keep</h2>

<p>East of the Trinmo, the surviving lords of Urland had been assembling around the river crossings since Kifrir had been moving civilians across them. Arsin of Gilkeep, who had assumed the Grand Mastership of the Knights of the Gryphon after Samson the Quicks death at Hadley, held Gobsfall Keep, the fortification at the Trinmo crossing that controlled the most direct route from the fallen west into the surviving east. Around him gathered Theara of Hornsley, Countess of Eldshire, Jorwyle of Gelm the Duke of Gelm, Duke Jasper of Nied, the Earl Verly of Karsbyand Kifrir himself, whose civilian rescue was complete and who was now for the first time in the invasion at the place where the fighting was happening.</p>

<p>Tirbo led the Hobgoblin push across the Trinmo personally as he had led every major engagement of the invasion, undefeated in his merciless march east. Elamak and his monastic sect, who had joined Tirbos campaign only out of necessity, refused to cross the river with him, instead withdrawing from the horde rather than participate in what lay beyond the Trinmo. Whether Elamak understood what was coming is not recorded. Tirbo died at Gobsfall Keep when Kifrir slew him. As the dwarves magic wove with his dance of blood and rage, the warlord who had led the Tirbo Clan from their collapsed cave system into the light, through every battle of the invasion, died at a river crossing held by Kifrir who had spent the war saving the innocents as Dorian would have had him do.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Arsin.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Arsin of Gilkeep, Grand Master of the Gryphon</i></small>  
</div>

<p>Without Tirbo the unified Hobgoblin horde fragmented and the Trinmo held. The Hobgoblins settled west of the river permanently in the lands around Lake Tirbo,  in the city they called conquered, within the territory that became the <a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a> stratocratic confederation in 696PA. They did not cross the Trinmo in such force again. Korgaz, still recovering from the wounds Oswald III had given him at Dursans walls, was not at Gobsfall. He died four years later in ritual combat against a young champion, defending his title for the thirty second time, finally losing without a single scar in his back. The great forest north of Ak-Saray, known to Urien peoples as Silenttree, is called the Korgaz in Overgob, and has been since his death, he would be remembered as one of the finest warriors the Dirgae had ever known, and one of their greatest heroes.</p>

<h2>Aftermath</h2>

<p>Elamak, having refused to cross the Trinmo, established his own temples in the lands west of the river after the wars conclusion with his monastic sect becoming the foundation of the Malagul, a school of martial and contemplative practice that persists within Ak-Saray to the present day, its existence a reminder that the Tirbo Clan had never been entirely unified in its purposes.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Hobmonk.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Elemak, the Silence in the Storm</i></small>  
</div>
<p>East of the Trinmo the political situation deteriorated rapidly into something that the surviving lords found almost as threatening as the invasion itself. In 702PA, Ricter of Gelm attempted to use <a href="Morfeld" class="internal-link">Morfeld's Key</a>, an artifact of catastrophic necromantic power belonging to the necromancer <a href="Morfeld" class="internal-link">Morfeld</a> that Saint <a href="Lyandra" class="internal-link">Lyandra</a> had seen burned to death at Uhl decades prior, to summon energies that would destroy the Hobgoblin occupation and crown himself King of all Urland. The invocation required the sacrifice of the entire city of Lindmol, and there was no evidence that the resulting energies could have been controlled even had the sacrifice been completed. A group of adventurers foiled the plot and killed Ricter before either could occur. He is remembered in the histories as a lower villain, not a desperate patriot but a man who would have gladly sacrificed his own people as the price of his ambition.</p>

<p>Harlan of the Reym, Duke of Ares, attempted to declare himself King of Arester from his largely intact lands, an assertion of the old Arestian identity that the surviving Knights of the Gryphon, under Arsin of Gilkeep, refused to accept so Arsin, hero of the wars, simply deposed him. The knights would govern themselves before they would serve a man who used catastrophe as an opportunity for personal elevation and had elected to not fight at Gobsfall himself.</p>

<p>In 703PA the Splintering was practically formalized. Jorwyle of Gelm, Ricters younger Brother, crowned himself King of Urland in Gelmsburg, claiming succession through the ducal seat of the kingdoms most powerful surviving house. Countess Theara of Hornsley, who had assembled the eastern lords around a constitutional charter granting rights to Counts and Barons that Urlands crown had never codified, was crowned Queen Theara I of <a href="Eldmar" class="internal-link">Eldmar</a> in a ceremony that explicitly rejected Jorwyles claim. Jasper of Nied declared the Duchy of Nied autonomous. The Archbishops seat, whose incumbent had died at Dursan, was eventually relocated to Gelmsburg, an early act by the Kings of Gelm that gave them ecclesiastical legitimacy their rivals could not match and that shaped the successor states political relationships for generations as Gelmsburg is still the Seat of the Archbishop of Ur. Duke Jakub III of Garmeley, who had sent knights to the siege and watched them die there, refused to commit further forces to the Urien union afterward and instead turned to reclaim the old Earldom of Dim in the Ares Highlands, territorial opportunism that the surviving Urien lords regarded as a betrayal but Jakub regarded as pragmatic payment. In 736PA the Knights of the Gryphon, following Arsin of Gilkeeps legacy, formally reconstituted themselves as the <a href="Urbridge" class="internal-link">Order of Urbridge</a>,directed entirely at destroying the occupation that had ended the kingdom and recovering the lands west of the Trinmo that the Tirbo Clan had made their own.</p>
<p>Years later the <a href="League of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">League of the Gryphon</a> would be declared and attempt to take back what the Invaders conquered.</p>
