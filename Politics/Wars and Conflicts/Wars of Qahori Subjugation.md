What began as elven settlement on the eastern shores of the island the <a href="Orc" class="internal-link">Qahori</a> called Qa in 278BA had by the early 5th century PA become an organized effort by the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae Sultanate</a> to complete by military force what demographic pressure and selective occupation had not fully achieved. The wars lasted forty-four years, consumed four leaders of the Alliance of the Old Moon, desecrated two of the <a href="Qalmyr" class="internal-link">Qalmyr</a> faiths holiest cities, and ended with the surviving Qahori driven from their sacred island into the sea. The Sultanates armies were as much <a href="Behera" class="internal-link">Beheran human</a> as elven and the soldiers who besieged Ensihr and broke the walls of Zeqer came from both peoples. The Qahori did not distinguish between them in their memory of what was done. In Elbae court records the wars are a military campaign. In Qahori memory they are the Destruction of Qa, not merely a defeat but a theological verdict, the punishment they believed their own decision to take up arms had invited upon themselves.</p> <h2>Origins</h2> <p>The elven settlement of Il-Haena had been, from its beginning, a process that exploited a specific vulnerability in Qahori civilization. The <a href="Qalmyr" class="internal-link">Qalmyr Faith</a> counselled its adherents that organized resistance, coordinated war against an invader, was to heed the whispers of Zolra, the ruinous counterpart to the creator god Qastor, and invite divine punishment. The early elven settlers understood this constraint, or learned it quickly, and the expansion of the following centuries proceeded accordingly. By 161PA the east coast of Il-Haena was essentially fully under elven control without the Sultanate having committed minor if any military force. The method had been patience.</p> <p>The accession of Merilas II changed the character of Sultanate policy sharply. His predecessor Merilas I had maintained a posture of genuine accommodation toward the Qahori, the older Sultan knew the Qahori leadership by name and was known to them as something other than an adversary, and the transition between the two reigns was experienced by the Qahori as a sudden reversal rather than a gradual deterioration. Under Merilas II, elven radicals attacked Qahori merchants at the great northern trading city of Timoq, in 265PA. When Qahori retaliation followed, the Sultanate dispatched its forces to occupy the city, killing hundreds of locals, Lizun I continued this pressure. The Qahori response was the Council of the Old Moon in 312PA, where Baqamon united almost all the Qahori tribes in a defensive coalition, a profound theological compromise that split the orcish communities between those who believed resistance was necessary and those who believed it was sin.</p>

<p>The most prominent voice against resistance was Ranqar the Broken, a Qahori elder of considerable standing whose epithet reflected not physical injury but the grief his position cost him within his own community. Ranqar argued before the Council and repeatedly afterward that organized war against the elves was precisely what Zolra desired — that the path of Qastor demanded not the sword but endurance, displacement if necessary, the preservation of the faith's inner life over its territorial expressions. He was not without followers, but ultimately not heeded. Kamer of the New Fire, who emerged as the Alliances dominant military leader, carried the Councils mandate and the argument that a faith whose sacred birthland was being taken city by city could not survive on endurance alone. Ranqar lived to see the beginning of what he had predicted, the wars began, the cities fell, his people perished, and died of old age around wo generations into the catastrophe he had spent his life trying to prevent. The Qahori remembered him afterward as a prophet. They had not listened to him when it might have mattered.</p> <p>The Crisis of the Golden Throne in 382PA provided an unexpected interval. With the Sultanate paralyzed, Kamer led the Alliance to retake Timoq in 385PA, the greatest military achievement in Qahori history and the demonstration that the Alliance could fight effectively when the elven administrative apparatus was disrupted. When the Crisis ended in 403PA and Irater I took the White Chair, the lesson drawn in Jiravid was that minor occupation was insufficient. The Wars of Qahori Subjugation were launched in 413PA as a systematic campaign to finish the conquest.</p> <h2>The Southern Campaign and the Battle of Gerqom</h2> <p>The Sultanates opening move was a naval invasion of Il-Haenas southern coast under Ferik Sorshe Dol, a naval commander of considerable operational competence whose mandate was to establish a southern beachhead and push eastward along the coast while the main land forces pressed from the north west. The early phases of Sorshe Dols campaign proceeded largely as planned, coastal landings secured, fortified positions established, the Qahori communities of the south driven back from the shoreline. But the interior was different territory. Hundreds of skirmishes across broken ground, extended supply lines that required prolonged pauses to consolidate, and a Qahori Alliance that had spent decades preparing for exactly this kind of defensive warfare combined to slow the southern advance to a grinding pace that bore little resemblance to the swift decisive campaign Jiravid had projected.</p>

<p>The Battle of Gerqom was the southern campaigns defining moment and its effective end. Goriok of the New Fire, Kamers chosen heir and commander of the Alliance forces in the south, brought the Qahoris full southern strength to bear against Sorshe Dols advance at Gerqom, a position whose terrain negated the Sultanates naval supply advantages and forced an open infantry engagement where Qahori warriors, fighting for their sacred ground, proved devastating. Sorshe Dol was killed in the battle. It was the only major Qahori field victory of the wars and the only significant elven defeat, a moment of such consequence that Gorioks name entered the Alliances traditions alongside Kamers, and Sorshe Dols death remained the most prominent Elbae military loss of the entire 44 year campaign. The Sultanate reorganized its southern forces under new command and continued, but Gerqom had demonstrated that the conquest would be neither swift nor clean.</p> <h2>Amr al-Ziyar and the Fall of the Cities</h2> <p>The unification of the campaigns command under Masir Amr al-Ziyar marked the conflict's decisive phase. Al-Ziyar, known as the one "Who Devours the Moons" referencing the Qalmyr fait's twin lunar deities, was bestowed by his own soldiers as much as feared by his enemies as a commander of systematic brutality and total effectiveness. The Qahori called him Zolra's fury made flesh. The Elbae court celebrated him with the honours due a man who had accomplished what four decades of prior effort had not.</p>

<p>Al-Ziyars campaign proceeded city by city. Mahraq "the Sweet Hill", which had fallen early in the wars but insurrected repeatedly, was reduced to a garrisoned occupation that ended the cycle of rebellion through the kind of brutal measures that al-Ziyar would become known for. The Battle of the Red Sands, fought on the arid plateau between the Maqari Mountains and the eastern coast, was al-Ziyars masterpiece during the conflict, a battle in which the Qahori made their most ambitious attempt to break the Sultanates central advance and were defeated decisively, Goriok's successor Eleva of the New Fire barely escaping with the Alliance' remaining cohesion intact. The Siege of Ensihr followed. Ensihr was one of the two holy cities of Qalmyr, the closest settlement to the Maqari Mountains sacred summit, and its fall was resisted with the desperate intensity of religious tragedy. Al-Ziyar took it regardless. The city was left standing in a physical sense but was never rebuilt into anything resembling its former significance. Its ruins became a site of Qahori pilgrimage from across the sea, a practice the Sultanate neither encouraged nor found it practical to fully suppress. Great Qatir, the largest Qahori city ever built, was destroyed outright during this phase of the campaign. Its ruins remained on the islands northern coast as the most visible evidence of what the conquest had been.</p> <h2>Last Stand at Zeqer</h2> <p>The fourth and final leader of the Alliance of the Old Moon was Hearo of the New Fire, known in the Qahori tradition as "the Last Spark"  acknowledging without illusion what the Orcs situation had become. Hearo gathered what remained of the organized Qahori resistance at Zeqer, a large city on the islands northeastern coast that represented the final significant territory the free Qahori held. The Alliances decision to make its stand there rather than continue withdrawing was understood by everyone involved as a final act rather than a strategic calculation, Zeqer was where the remaining free orcs of Qa would fight and die, because there was nowhere left to withdraw to that was still Qa.</p>

<p>Al-Ziyar besieged Zeqer with the methodical patience of a commander who had been fighting this war for twenty years and did not intend to lose its final engagement to impatience. The siege was prolonged. When Zeqer fell, Hearo of the New Fire died in its defence. The Alliance of the Old Moon, founded by Baqamon a century and a half earlier as a theological compromise with pacifism, led through four generations of the New Fire lineage, and sustained against the full military weight of the largest empire in the known world for forty four years, ceased to exist as an organized force. The Sultanate declared the subjugation of Il-Haena complete in 457PA. Those Qahori who remained on the island were primarily those who had collaborated, the <a href="Qarzi" class="internal-link">Qarzi tribe</a>, granted their southeastern territories and the holy city of Benwaral in recognition of their assistance, in an arrangement that preserved their lives and earned them the hatred of every other Qahori alive. The rest sailed east into the Niryd Sea, carrying their faith and the memory of what had been lost, to build whatever life the waters would allow them.</p> <h2>Aftermath</h2> <p>Masir Amr al-Ziyar returned to Jiravid as a celebrated figure whose military record was without fault in the Sultanates history. He served for decades afterward as a prominent member of the Great Masqar, his counsel on military matters treated with the deference owed to a man who had won the conquest of Il-Haena. In the Qahori diaspora his name was used to explain to children what Zolras fury looked like when it wore an elven face. Both characterizations followed him to his death and both have followed his memory since. The wars he won remained the defining trauma of Qahori civilization, the event against which everything in their subsequent history such as the founding of the Federation, the split of the Kamerzin, the endless seafaring that the outside world admired as a cultural trait and the Qahori understood as a form of mourning, was measured. The Elbae court records of the Wars of Qahori Subjugation are detailed, precise, and almost entirely concerned with logistics, troop movements and administrative outcomes. They do not record what Ranqar the Broken said before the Council of the Old Moon, or what Hearo of the New Fire said at Zeqer when the walls came down. The Qahori oral tradition preserved both. It has preserved them carefully.</p>
