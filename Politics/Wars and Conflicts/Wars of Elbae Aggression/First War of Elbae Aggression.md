<p>The First of the three so called <a href="Wars of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">Wars of Elbae Aggression</a>, fought between 750PA and 762PA, was the opening conflict of the series of wars between the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae Sultanate</a> and the Reani Coalition, a coalition assembled from <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Grevenna</a>, <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a>, <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Hainach</a> and the <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Khatrym of Kherova</a>, over control of the <a href="Strait of Horad" class="internal-link">Horad Isthmus</a> and the commercial dominance of the seas connecting Behera to Ilrakhan. The war began with a sequence of Sultanate victories that brought elven forces deep into Grevenni territory before a combination of Kherovan anti-magic intervention, a miraculous defensive stand, and the arrival of a warrior the Church of Iztaw would shortly declare a Saint reversed the campaign's direction entirely. It ended at the Second Battle of Danipol with the Sultanate's retreat behind the Horad and the deaths of figures whose names would define the conflict's legacy for generations.</p>

<h2>Origins</h2>

<p>The wars causes ran deeper than any single incident. The Horad Isthmus, the narrow land bridge separating the Stormy Sea from the Pilvian Gulf, had been a point of competing ancient claims for centuries, with the Asefi peoples of Beheras north and the Pilvian peoples of southern Grevenna both asserting historical rights over its control. Cultural rivalries between the Horadi and Asefi humans and their Pilvian neighbours ran deeper still, predating the Sultanate itself. For the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae</a>, the economic logic of the coming wars was clear: the Sultanate was the worlds foremost maritime trade empire, its merchant networks spanning three continents, and full control of the Horad chokepoint represented a consolidation of mercantile power. That the <a href="Grand Ziflis Company" class="internal-link">Grand Ziflis Company</a> received its formal charter from Sultan Fasmer I in 754PA, four years into the fighting, was not coincidenc, the commercial infrastructure for Elbae dominance was being built alongside the military campaign to secure it.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Waskor.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Gri Waskor zi Treblin</i></small>  
</div>

<p>On the coalition side, the motivations were equally layered but differently weighted. <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Grevenna</a> stood most directly in the Sultanates path and had the most immediate territorial stake. <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a> had the deepest ideological investment. Vihri anti-elven sentiment had been building for centuries within a strain of <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethric</a> theology hardened by the Doctrine of Human Exceptionalism, first formally articulated in 254PA, into something approaching genuine religious hatred. Within Vihri culture, elves were often not merely foreigners but categorically threatening: described as demonspawn and demon-kin, their mixed-race children dismissed as half-breeds. Gri Waskor zi Treblin, who commanded Vihrs forces and would eventually assume overall coalition command, embodied this ideology so completely that his military decisions would at times be driven more by his racial convictions than by tactical judgment. Vihr promised full military support to Grevenna, and that promise made the coalition possible. Both sides had been prepared for war when the inciting incident finally arrived.</p>

<p>In February 750PA the elven vessel Niamor, commanded by the dignitary Meas ab-Koron, was seized at the Grevenni port of Sandri on charges of smuggling. Ab-Koron was imprisoned. He was found dead in his cell shortly after. The Sultanate declared war on Grevenna within days as spark had found its tinder.</p>

<h2>The Opening Campaign </h2>

<p>The Sultanate committed the full weight of its professional military under the overall command of Masir Hazir ab-Keyel, commander of the Dawn Army. Hazirs strategy was built around the Horads geography: secure Danipol at the isthmus's northern mouth, establish naval supremacy over the Pilvian Gulf, land forces on Grevenni soil, and push north before the coalition could assemble a coherent response. The arcane component of the campaign was entrusted to Mons Garyal, the White Eye of <a href="Karajal School of Insight" class="internal-link">Karajal</a>, an old and deeply eccentric mage who expressed all his magical workings through conjured butterflies, a personal aesthetic choice whose practical results were among the most devastating in the wars history. Ferik Herem ab-Faet, a Drow Oracle who had spent years bridging the Sultanates occult traditions and its material military command, led the arcane regiments. The Sultans own son, Inrafir Elbae, commanded the elite Falcon Brigade.</p>

<p>The First Battle of Danipol opened the war with a decisive Sultanate victory. Ferik Tashima Zun led the assault on Danipols southern approaches, and the coalitions initial defensive effort collapsed. Lorgis of Rofea, Grand Lord of Grevenna and the coalitions supreme commander, was killed in the fighting. His daughter Siari of Rofea, seventeen years old, inherited the Grand Lordship of Grevenna and with it command of a coalition reeling from its first major defeat.</p>

<p>With Danipol secured, Hazir turned to the Pilvian Gulf. At the Battle of the New Sun, the Sultanates fleet, with Mons Garyal's conjured swarms providing arcane support across the waters, defeated the coalition navy under Lady Gistan of Melldock, Grevennas admiral. The Gulf was effectively blockaded and Sultanate naval supremacy established. Elven forces landed near Sandri, and Inrafir Elbae with his Falcon Brigade took the city at the Battle of Sandri, securing the supply base from which the inland campaign would proceed. The Sultanate was now fighting on Grevenni soil, supplied by sea, and advancing north toward Dimelski.</p>

<h2>The Slaughter of Orom and the Tower of Seriak</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Israfer2.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Ferik Israfer Elbae</i></small>  
</div>

<p>The coalitions defensive line between Sandri and Dimelski met the Sultanates advance near the village of Orom. Hazir deployed his forces with the tactical precision that had defined his career, inflicting severe casualties on both sides but forcing the coalition into a fighting retreat toward Dimelski in an engagement that contemporaries called the "Slaughter of Orom" for the losses it produced. The battle was indecisive in the strict sense; neither side routed. Its strategic result was unambiguous: the coalition was falling back, and Dimelski was now directly threatened.</p>

<p>It was at Orom that a figure arrived who had not been part of any commander's plan. <a href="Fredric" class="internal-link">Fredric Foehammer</a>, a Urien warrior who described himself as called by the Prophet to defend Gethricism against the elven advance, appeared with fewer than two hundred companions and fought with a ferocity that the engagements size did not suggest was possible. He nearly turned the battle himself. Alas he did not, and the coalition still retreated, but he was noticed by the coalition commanders who understood what they had received, and by the Sultanate officers who would have cause to remember him.</p>

<p>What Fredric did next was the fulcrum on which the entire war turned. The Sultanates advance on Dimelski required the Tower of Seriak, the fortification controlling the chokepoint between Orom and the city. With Seriak open, Sultanate reinforcements could flow freely and the siege of Dimelski would be supported by fresh forces arriving continuously. Fredric took Seriak with his two hundred men and held it for seven days against nearly five thousand Sultanate soldiers. He did not fall back. The tower did not fall.</p>

<h2>The Siege of Dimelski and the Kherovan Arrival</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Zirvid.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Lord Zirvid "the Bear" of Mirzan</i></small>  
</div>
<p>Seven days was precisely what Lord Zirvid of Mirzan needed. The Kherovan commander, approaching sixty, described by those who served alongside him as genuinely mountainous in physical presence, arrived with a kheri army and his <a href="Kherovan Inquisition" class="internal-link">Spellbreaker regiments</a> and broke the Siege of Dimelski. The Sultanates reinforcements, delayed by Fredrics hold on Seriak, were too late. The coalition stabilized as Dimelski held. The strategic momentum of the wars first two years, Victories at Danipol, the New Sun, Sandri and Orom, was suddenly and completely reversed.</p>

<p>Gri Waskor zi Treblin coordinated the relief with whatever competence managing a successful siege break required, and with the limitations that would define his reputation across both wars. His racial convictions concerning elves were not strategically useful at Dimelski. The Kherovan Spellbreakers were.</p>

<h2>The Second Battle of Danipol</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Mons.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Mons Garyal, White Eye of Karajal</i></small>  
</div>

<p>The coalition regrouped and advanced south. The Sultanate, its northern campaign checked and its supply lines under growing pressure, consolidated at Danipol for the final engagement. At the Second Battle of Danipol the full coalition met the assembled Sultanate forces in the field, and both sides committed everything remaining to them.</p>

<p>Mons Garyal opened the engagement with what witnesses on both sides described as a spectacle of genuine terror. Thousands of conjured butterflies, luminous, impossible, rising in dense formations from the Sultanate's lines, drove into the coalitions formations and detonated in cascading explosions that shattered organized resistance wherever they struck. The White Eye of Karajal killed in hundreds before the Kherovan Spellbreakers reached him. He died on a mountain of his own dead, surrounded by two dozen Spellbreakers whose mirror shields had stripped him of the magical expression that defined his entire practice. Whatever he said in those final moments was not recorded as his butterflies vanished.</p>

<p>With Garyal dead and the arcane pressure lifted, the coalitions formations reformed. Fredric Foehammer drove directly into the Sultanates command structure. He killed Hazir ab-Keyel himself, the Masir who had planned and led the entire campaign, in personal combat then turned and duelled Inrafir Elbae, the Sultan's son and commander of the Falcon Brigade, slaying him as well. An arrow caught Fredric in the throat after he lost his helmet in the fight with Inrafir. He was dead before the battle ended. The Sultanate sounded the retreat and withdrew behind the Horad. The First War of Elbae Aggression was over.</p>

<h2>Aftermath</h2>

<p>The Conclave of 763PA canonised Fredric Foehammer as a Gethric Saint within a year of his death, the Hero of Danipol elevated to the canon, his pure White Foehammer declared a holy relic, his skull subsequently preserved in gold at the Cathedral of St. Fredric in <a href="Niedrest" class="internal-link">Niedrest</a>. The same Conclave ratified the Gethric Pogroms, the formal ecclesiastical declaration that drove Gethric communities across Ilrakhan to the persecution of elves and half-elves as demonspawn and lesser beings. The theological current that Waskor zi Treblin had ridden throughout the war was now official Church policy. The Great Schism of 764PA followed within the year, as <a href="Gisela II" class="internal-link">Gisela II</a> of Meadrath refused to accept the Pogroms and the Trinicist denomination emerged from the fracture. The First War had not resolved the tensions that produced it. It had sharpened them considerably.</p>

<p>Lady Gistan of Melldock had survived the Battle of the New Sun with her fleet broken but herself alive. She would face the Sultanates navy again unsuccessfully in the Second War, and that time not survive the meeting. Siari of Rofea, who had inherited Grevenna at seventeen and governed through the wars most desperate phase, emerged as a Grand Lady whose authority had been earned in the worst possible school. Herem ab-Faet, the Drow Oracle who had bridged the Sultanate's arcane and material command, survived and continued in the Sultanate's service. Lord Zirvid of Mirzan returned to Kherova and died years later during the Kobold Invasion, the man who saved Dimelski meeting an ending none of the histories of the First War had predicted for him. The Sultanate began preparing almost immediately for the <a href="Second War of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">next attempt</a>. It would not make the same mistakes.</p>
