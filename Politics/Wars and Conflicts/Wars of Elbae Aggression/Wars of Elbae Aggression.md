<p>The Wars of Elbae Aggression were a series of three conflicts fought between the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae Sultanate</a> and successive Reani Coalitions between 750PA and 815PA, sixty five years of intermittent war over the Horad Isthmus, the narrow land bridge separating the Stormy Sea from the Pilvian Gulf, and by extension over the commercial and political domination of the seas connecting Behera to Ilrakhan. The wars are named by the coalitions framing rather than the Sultanates, which initiated each conflict and preferred to describe them in terms of historical rights and legitimate commercial interests. The coalition, which fought them in defence of those it already held, named them for what it understood them to be and its naming has persisted.</p>

<p>Each war was fought under different conditions, by differently constituted forces, and produced a different outcome. The First was a coalition victory, secured by the arrival of Kherovan Spellbreaker regiments whose anti magic capabilities the Sultanate had not anticipated, and celebrated on the coalitions side through the figure of <a href="Fredric" class="internal-link">Fredric Foehammer</a>, who died at the Second Battle of Danipol killing both the Sultanates supreme commander and the Sultans own son. The Second was a decisive Sultanate victory, achieved through a two theatre campaign that exploited the simultaneous collapse of Kherovan participation, whether by design or fortunate coincidence remaining disputed, and ended with the <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Princedom of Hainach</a> subjugated as an Eyalet, its Reani population henceforth fighting for the empire their neighbours were still resisting. The Third produced no conventional military victor. It ended when <a href="Jurahim al-Biad" class="internal-link">Jurahim al-Biad</a>, the Ruby Eye of <a href="Karajal School of Insight" class="internal-link">Karajal</a>, completed an invocation he had been ordered to abandon, destroyed the Horad Isthmus, created the <a href="Strait of Horad" class="internal-link">Strait of Horad</a>, and killed thousands of soldiers on both sides including many of the war's most senior commanders. He perished with them.</p>

<h2>The Shifting Coalition</h2>

<p>One of the defining features of the Wars of Elbae Aggression as a series was the radical change in the coalitions composition and the Sultanates circumstances across all three conflicts. The First Coalition of 750PA, Grevenna, Vihr, Hainach and Kherova, was the broadest and ultimately most effective, its decisive capability residing in the <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Kherovan</a> Spellbreakers whose anti-magic capabilities neutralized the arcane advantage that the Sultanate had relied upon. The Second Coalition lost Kherova, forced out by the Bihra Khan Jigallors attack on its eastern frontier in 782PA and with it its elites, enabling the Sultanates two theatre campaign that subjugated Hainach. The Third Coalition gained Garmeley and Urbridge but fought without Kherova and with Hainach actively fighting against it, the Reani peoples of the subjected Princedom now facing their own cousins across the battlefield, one of the more bitter ironies a sixty five year war produced. The coalition that won the First War bore almost no resemblance to the coalition that faced the Third.</p>

<h2>The Wars and Their Legacy</h2>

<p>The three wars are treated in individual articles. For the full account of each conflict's campaigns, commanders and engagements, see <a href="First War of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">First War of Elbae Aggression</a>, <a href="Second War of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">Second War of Elbae Aggression</a> and <a href="Third War of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">Third War of Elbae Aggression</a>.</p>

<p>The <a href="Treaty of Danipol" class="internal-link">Treaty of Danipol</a> of 815PA, signed at the fortress whose name the wars had made famous, was the first formal peace agreement, the Treaty's terms established the framework within which the Sultanate and the surviving coalition powers have related to each other since: Elbae relinquishing its claim on Grevenna, Hainach's Eyalet status formalized, the <a href="Reanport" class="internal-link">Republic of Reanport</a> granted the remains of the destroyed Horad Isthmus and its created islands in exchange for guaranteeing free passage. Two years later, the <a href="Orenburg Pact" class="internal-link">Orenburg Pact</a> of 817PA established international constraints on magical practice.</p>

<p>The wars most unexpected legacy was commercial rather than military. The Strait of Horad, created by al-Biads invocation, transformed the Stormy Sea  into a connected waterway linking the markets of Ilrakhans interior to the Pilvian Gulf and the trading routes beyond. The ports that controlled the Straits approaches, <a href="Reanport" class="internal-link">Reanport</a> foremost, with <a href="Mereskis" class="internal-link">Meresksi</a> and Thulmouth growing alongside it, entered a period of commercial prosperity that none of the wars planners had predicted. The Sultanate, which had fought three wars to control the Isthmus, found itself instead controlling a Strait that served global commerce in ways the Isthmus never had. The <a href="Grand Ziflis Company" class="internal-link">Grand Ziflis Company</a>, chartered during the First War, was ideally positioned to exploit the new geography. The wars that were named for Elbae aggression produced, in their ending, a commercial architecture that entrenched Elbae dominance more thoroughly than any of the territorial objectives the Sultanate had originally sought.</p>

<h2>Memory and Interpretation</h2>

<p>The wars are remembered differently by every party that fought in them. In <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Grevenna</a>, the First War is a victory defined by sacrifice, the death of Grand Lord Lorgis at Danipol, the seventeen year old Siari inheriting a coalition at war, the canonisation of <a href="Fredric" class="internal-link">Fredric Foehammer</a> as the Hero of Danipol. The Second War is a near catastrophe averted only partially, with Danipol held but Hainach lost. The Third War is the conflict that no one won and everyone paid for. In <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a>, the wars carried an ideological weight that outlasted all three of them as the Gethric Pogroms of 763PA, ratified at the same Conclave that canonised Fredric, were the direct institutional product of the First Wars anti elven mobilization, and their legacy in Vihri religious culture endured long after the fighting stopped.</p> <p>In <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Hainach</a>, the wars represent the end of Reani sovereignty and the beginning of the complicated accommodation with Sultanate governance that defines the Princedoms present, complicated further by the conspiracy theories that still circulate in 927PA regarding the timing of the Bihra attack in 782PA, and by the anti-Kherovan sentiment that Kherovas withdrawal and subsequent separate peace embedded for generations. In the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae Sultanate</a> itself, the wars are a mixed legacy: the Second War a clear victory, the First a humiliation eventually avenged, the Third frankly a mess from which the Sultanate extracted unexpected commercial advantage while losing its most capable heir. The man who ended the wars, Jurahim al-Biad, is remembered in the Sultanate with the particular ambivalence reserved for those whose actions cannot be straightforwardly condemned or celebrated. He disobeyed his Sultan, killed thousands of his own soldiers, and accidentally created the conditions for Elbae commercial dominance that no military campaign had managed to achieve. The epithet history gave him, Urul Horad "He who swallowed the Horad" was not intended as a compliment. It has become one anyway, in certain circles, which says something about how the wars are ultimately understood.</p>
