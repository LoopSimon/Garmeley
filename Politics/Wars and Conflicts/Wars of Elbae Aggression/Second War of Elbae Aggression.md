<p>The Second War of the <a href="Wars of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">Three Conflicts</a> between Elbae and the Reani Coalition, fought between 781PA and 790PA, was the conflict the Sultanate had spent nineteen years preparing for following its defeat during the <a href="First War of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">First Conflict</a>. Where the First War had been fought on terms the coalition chose: a single theatre, Kherovan Spellbreakers neutralizing elven magic and the decisive engagement at Danipol, the Second War was fought on terms the Sultanate designed. It opened on two fronts simultaneously, exploited the collapse of Kherovas ability to participate, and ended with the <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Princedom of Hainach</a> subjugated as an Eyalet of the Sultanate and the coalition that had defeated the First War permanently fractured. The question of whether the circumstances that made this possible were fortuitous or engineered has never been definitively answered.</p>

<h2>The Kherovan Question</h2>

<p>The single most consequential event of the Second War occurred before the first major engagement: the attack by Bihra Khan <a href="Jigallor Khaganate" class="internal-link">Jigallor</a> on the eastern frontier of <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Kherova</a> in 782PA, only one year after the war began. The Kherovan Khatrym, already weakened by the Treeplague that had killed thousands across the previous decade and cost Khat <strong>Arsen II</strong> his life in the same year as Jigallor's attack, was forced to withdraw its forces entirely from the western coalition in order to defend its eastern frontier. The Kherovan Spellbreaker regiments, whose mirror shields and anti magic arsenals had been the decisive factor at the Second Battle of Danipol twenty years earlier, were gone. Kherova subsequently signed a separate peace with the Sultanate, a decision that <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Grevenna</a> and <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Hainach</a> experienced as a betrayal and that left anti Kherovan sentiment embedded in both nations cultures for generations.</p>

<p>Whether this sequence of events was coincidental has been disputed ever since.<a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Hainachs</a> own records notes that conspiracies around the timing of Jigallor's attack "are alive and well even in modern days." Ferik Tashima Zun, who reprised his role from the First War in a coordination capacity between the war' two theatres, carried persistent rumours of having made arrangements with parties capable of influencing Bihra movements, rumours that were never substantiated and that Tashima himself declined to address. If the Sultanate engineered the removal of Kherova from the coalition before the Second War began, it was the most consequential act of shadow politics in the wars history. If Jigallors attack was genuinely coincidental, the Sultanate was the beneficiary of extraordinary fortune. The historical record does not resolve the question, and the political descendants of those who suffered the consequences have not forgotten to keep asking it.</p>


<p>The Sultanates campaign plan for the Second War reflected a fundamental lesson drawn from the First, to not fight the coalition on a single front where Kherovan forces can be concentrated at the decisive point. Masir Ulsun Parak, commanding the Sultanates primary force, would drive into <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Hainach</a> through the Mesovo River crossing at Dor Beher, the staging ground the Sultanate had maintained on the river's eastern bank since before the First War. Simultaneously Ferik Mosul Horvan, a human officer, rare at the Ferik rank but Paraks trusted right hand, would hold the Horad Isthmus against whatever the coalitions main northern force could bring to bear at Dor Horad. Admiral Gazia ab-Erer would command the Sultanate's navy on the Stormy Sea, deny the coalition maritime supply lines, and ensure that whatever happened in Hainach happened without interruption from the water. The two theatres would prevent the coalition from concentrating its strength at any single point. If either held, the other could advance.</p>

<h2>The Opening at Mesovo and Horad</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/MesovoHold2.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Ulsuns Army crosses the Mesovo</i></small>  
</div>

<p>The Battle of Mesovo opened the Hainach campaign in 781PA as Paraks forces crossed the river from Dor Beher into Hainachi territory. The coalitions response was hampered from the outset by a critical timing failure: Count Heyrim of Thoulmouth, commanding the powerful Knights who represented Garmeleys contribution to the coalitions southern defence, arrived late. How late and why have been debated in Garmish military history since, but the effect was immediate, Hainachs forces were forced to fight without the Garmish reinforcement that had been expected, and the crossing succeeded. Ser Esper "Whiteshield" Theral, a Hainachi knight of considerable personal reputation, fought with distinction in Hainachs defence at Mesovo and would continue to appear at every subsequent major engagement of the campaign.</p>

<p>At the Horad Isthmus, Gri Waskor zi Treblin of Vihr, who had survived the First War as coalition commander and retained both his command and his fierce racial convictions about elves, led the coalitions attempt to break through at Dor Horad and relieve the pressure on Hainach. He did not succeed. Mosul Horvan held the fortification against sustained pressure, and Waskor zi Treblin died in the fighting at the Battle of Horads insurmountable fortifications, the Vihri Gri whose ideological hatred of elves had been a defining force in the coalitions character across both wars, killed at the bottleneck he could not breach. Lord Arjak of Bilin, commanding Grevennas forces at Horad, assumed what coordination remained possible for the northern theatre.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Horadhold.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Ferik Mosul Horvan holds Horad</i></small>  
</div>

<p>On the water, Admiral Gazia ab-Erer met Lady Gistan of Melldock for the second and final time at the Battle of the Storms. Gistan had rebuilt what she could of the coalitions naval capacity after the Battle of the New Sun nineteen years earlier and brought everything available to the engagement. The result was the same as before, and it was worse: the coalition fleet was destroyed and Gistan was killed. She had fought the elven navy twice and lost both times, not from incompetence but from facing a naval power that the coalition had consistently underestimated and failed to match. The Stormy Sea was now an elven lake. Arjak of Bilin, unwilling to accept the naval situation as the final word, led a small force of covert warriors in a raid on the island of Rozbarg, burning elven ships at anchor and retaking the islands fortifications in the wars only significant coalition victory, a moment celebrated mostly because everything else was going wrong.</p>

<h2>The Hainach Campaign</h2>

<p>With Kherovas withdrawal confirmed and the Horad held, Parak advanced deeper into Hainach. The coalitions rearguard tried to slow him at Rithea, where Count Heyrim of Thoulmouth, belatedly present after his late arrival at Mesovo, mounted an impressive defensive action to cover the Reani retreat. It cost him his life. Count Deydrik of Forrington, the other senior Garmish commander, took over after Heyrims death and fought at Rithea and subsequently at Ankros. Ser Esper Theral was present at both engagements, still fighting with honour and excellence on the losing side.</p>

<p>The years between Rithea and Ankros were years of slow and grinding Sultanate advance through a country whose population had not asked for this war, whose coalition allies were increasingly unable to support it, and whose commander, Prince Agia I of Pikris, was watching the options available to her narrow with each passing season. Kherovas separate peace removed any remaining hope of the spellbreakers intervention that had saved Grevenna in the First War. The coalition's northern forces were pinned at the Horad. Garmeley's most effective field commander was dead at Rithea and Parak was advancing slowly but certainly into Hainachs heartland.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Esper.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Ser Esper "Whiteshield" Theral</i></small>  
</div>

<p>The Battle of Ankros in 789PA was the decisive engagement of the western campaign, the moment Hainach's organized military resistance ended. Baron Marion of Triloska, Hainachs High General, was killed in the fighting. Ser Esper Theral was captured, kept alive on Parak's orders and treated with the honours owed to a Knight of his reputation, a gesture that reflected the deliberate character of Parak's entire approach to the campaign. Prince Agias army was destroyed as a coherent force. The path to Hainach's cities lay open. Ankros would be studied for decades later as a notable major engagement prior to the establishment of the <a href="Orenburg Pact" class="internal-link">Orenburg Pact</a> where no major arcane forces were present on either side, in a century whos wars were dominated by powerful Magi.</p>

<p>Parak did not treat Hainach without mercy; The occupation of the great cities of Triloska and Edgral proceeded without the devastation that successful sieges typically produced in this era, on Parak' explicit orders. He understood, and had understood since before Mesovo, that the objective in Hainach was not punitive destruction but permanent incorporation. A subjugated population that remembered its conquerors as restrained was a more manageable Eyalet than one that remembered burning cities. The political calculation was as deliberate as the military one.</p>

<h2>The Fall of Hainach and the Battle of the Rains</h2>

<p>With Triloska and Edgral occupied and the Sultanate's forces advancing on Pikris itself, Prince Agia I faced the question that Paraks campaign had been designed to make unavoidable. Count Deydrik of Forrington, his own political interests in Garmeley clearly requiring a different outcome, urged her not to surrender. "Let the cities fall and people burn if they must," he told her, in what became one of the more notorious statements in the war's record. Agia refused. She had watched what Paraks army had done at Triloska and Edgral: nothing. She made the calculation that Pikris would be treated the same way, and that the alternative Deydrik was proposing was not resistance but sacrifice of her own people for Garmish political purposes. She surrendered. Deydrik returned to Garmeley without ceremony. The House of Forrington's relationship with Hainach, complicated by this moment, would take generations to rebuild and Deydriks descendants to publicly disavow his words to amend.</p>

<p>The coalitions northern forces meanwhile, attempted to exploit Parak' deep commitment in Hainach by assembling a fresh army at the Horad near Danipol, a final effort to punch through Horvans position and relieve the pressure on the fallen Princedom. Horvan did not wait for them to assemble. He left his fortification, struck the gathering army before it could organize itself into a coherent fighting force, and dispersed it decisively at the Battle of the Rains. The Second War of Elbae Aggression was effectively over as Peace was signed, it would not be the last one.</p>

<h2>Aftermath</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Gazia.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Admiral Gazia ab-Erer</i></small>  
</div>

<p>The formal terms of Hainach's subjugation were generous by the standards of conquest, no tribute, economic integration into the Sultanate's commercial sphere, and the appointment of Masir Ulsun Parak himself as the first Pirash, the elven Overseer of Hainach, a role whose definition as military advisor and diplomatic envoy rather than occupying governor reflected the same restraint that had characterized the campaign. The Council of 818 would later formalize Hainachs internal reforms. The Sultans Overseers eventually became a permanent feature of Hainachi governance, backed by the prince rather than resented by the population in the way that harsher occupation tends to produce. Paraks approach defined the Hainach-Elbae relationship for the next century and a half.</p>

<p>Admiral Gazia ab-Erer returned to Jiravid to a position on the Dirhan, as high as any woman or man of the Sultanate could hope to rise, and spent the following decades on what became known as the Gazian Reforms of the Elbae navy: a systematic expansion of elven naval doctrine built on her conviction that maritime supremacy, not land power, was the foundation on which the Sultanates future dominance would rest. "Through the waves true dominance can be asserted in the centuries to come," she reportedly told the Dirhan upon her appointment.</p>

<p>Ser Esper "Whiteshield" Theral, released after the war with the honours Parak had extended to him at Ankros, returned to Hainach carrying a complicated legacy, a man who had fought in every engagement of the campaign and been present at its ending as a prisoner rather than a combatant. His reputation within Hainach was that of someone who had done everything that could be done and been defeated by circumstances rather than by personal failure. Kherova, by contrast, carried a different reputation: the nation whose Spellbreakers had won the First War had, in the Second, been absent at the decisive moment and had subsequently made separate terms with the Sultanate rather than continuing the fight. Whether by design or by misfortune, Kherova had left its allies to lose a war without it, and neither Grevenna nor Hainach forgot this. The coalition that had won the First War no longer existed as a functional alliance when the Third War began.</p>
