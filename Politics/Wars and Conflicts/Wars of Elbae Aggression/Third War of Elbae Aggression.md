<p>The Third War of Elbae Aggression, fought between 805PA and 815PA, was the final and most catastrophic of the <a href="Wars of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">three wars</a> between the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae Sultanate</a> and the Reani Coalition, a conflict that ended not through conventional military victory but through an act of magical destruction that changed the geography of the world permanently. Ten years of fighting across two theatres of war, encompassing some of the most complex campaigns and the most celebrated individual figures of the entire series of wars, concluded on the second day of the Battle of Danipol when <a href="Jurahim al-Biad" class="internal-link">Jurahim al-Biad</a>, the Ruby Eye of <a href="Karajal School of Insight" class="internal-link">Karajal</a>, completed the invocation he had been preparing for weeks and sank the Horad Isthmus into the sea, killing thousands of soldiers on both sides, destroying the landbridge between Behera and Ilrakhan, and creating the <a href="Strait of Horad" class="internal-link">Strait of Horad</a> that has defined the worlds commercial and political geography ever since. He did this in direct defiance of an explicit order from Sultan Arbalas I to abandon the invocationm whether he acted on personal conviction, arrogance, or on instructions from within the Sultanate's own command structure that bypassed the Sultan and the central theatre's commander, has never been conclusively determined.</p>

<h2>The Coalition and the Changed World</h2>

<p>The Third War began in a world that the <a href="Second War of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">Secodn</a> War had reshaped. <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Hainach</a>, which had fought in the first two coalitions, now fought for the Sultanate as an Eyalet, meaning that the Reani peoples who had settled there under Sultanate governance faced the prospect of fighting against their own cousins from <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Grevenna</a> and <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a>. <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Kherova</a>, whose Spellbreaker regiments had been the decisive factor in the First War, was absent and  bound by the non-aggression pact it had signed with the Sultanate after its forced withdrawal from the Second Conflict. The 3rd Coalition was therefore a different formation from its predecessors: <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Grevenna</a> and <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a> as its backbone, the <a href="Duchy of Garmeley" class="internal-link">Duchy of Garmeley</a> contributing its armies, and the <a href="Duchy of Urbridge" class="internal-link">Duchy of Urbridge</a> sending not a full army but a chapter of nearly one hundred and fifty knights under Sir William "the Staunch" of Merville, proud warriors who came at the call of Gethric declarations circulating through Vihr and Urland framing the war as a sacred duty to protect humanity from elven "heathen spellmongers." The <a href="Reanport" class="internal-link">Republic of Reanport</a> aligned diplomatically with the coalition, its interests in the Stormy Sea's future making the war's outcome a matter of direct commercial consequence.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Torsal.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">

</div>
<p>The coalitions supreme commander was Rezvyr zi Sribo, Var of Selbia, a Vihri military mind whose appointment reflected a genuine assessment of ability rather than political convenience, and who proved across the wars ten years to be the finest coalition commander of all three conflicts. Grand Lord Torsal I of Grevenna commanded his nation's forces, his sister Marfenna, a renowned duellist who had urged him to war with a persuasiveness that some later histories would hold partly responsible for what followed, fighting alongside him. The elder Lord Arjak of Bilin, Grevennas veteran of the First and Second Wars, had died before the Third began, leaving the coalition without the man who had achieved its only naval victory. On the Sultanates side, the central theatre at the Horad Isthmus was commanded by Masir Meas Arsifan Elbae, the Sultans third son and a former student of Masir Ulrun Parak, who himself managed the western theatre in Hainach at an older age, charged with holding the frontier against Garmish and Urbridge pressure.</p>

<h2>The Opening, Ialopis and the Crossing</h2>

<p>The war's first major engagement established the pattern for the western theatre. At the Battle of Ialopis in the Vale of Rean, Garmish and Urbridge forces defeated the combined Elbae and Hainach forces in an engagement where the Urbridge knights impact was disproportionate to their numbers as Parak's forces were pushed back, and the coalition established its presence in the Vale of Rean. It was a coalition victory, though not a decisive one.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Rezvyr.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Rezvyr zi Sribo, Var of Selbia</i></small>  
</div>

<p>The central theatre produced the wars most significant early shock. At the Battle of the Crossing, one of the largest engagements of all three wars, the coalition mounted a major assault on the Horad Isthmus and broke through. Ferik Jahrim of Mereskis, the beheri officer known as the "Blade of Dunes", saved the life of Meas Arsifan in the fighting, pulling the Masir from a position that would otherwise have been fatal. The victory at the Crossing belonged to Rezvyr zi Sribo: the first coalition commander in the history of all three wars to achieve military victory on Elbae homeland soil. The symbolic weight of that achievement was not lost on either side. The Sultanate recovered quickly enough at the Battle of Copper Tower, where Arsifan and Jahrim counter-attacked a Vihri van that had advanced too far, supported by local militias under the human archer Orectis "the Eagle", who killed Lord Tabis of Slobosi, one of Grevennas senior knights, in the engagement. But the Crossing had happened and the coalition had stood on Beheran soil and won.</p>

<h2>The Stalemate and the Battle of Thunders</h2>

<p>Following the Crossing and its immediate aftermath, both theatres entered a period of consolidation. The coalition retreated back over the Isthmus, regrouped, and prepared to apply pressure in the Vale of Rean. The Sultanate reinforced both its Isthmus positions and its Hainach frontier. For approximately three years no major engagement occurred on either front, a pause that reflected the genuine exhaustion of the war's opening campaigns and the strategic difficulty both sides faced in converting their respective advantages into decisive action.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Orectus.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Orectus the Eagle, Hero of Behera</i></small>  
</div>
<p>The stalemate broke at the Battle of Thunders, where the Sultanates plan to finally break the Danipol position took its most ambitious form yet. While Arsifan applied direct pressure on Danipol from the south, Ferik Maros al-Corum, a senior elven officer whose enthusiasm for magical solutions to military problems was his strongest conviction, led a naval force that landed on Danipols eastern cliffs and fought through the swamps surrounding the fortress, supported by Magi of Karajal and a fully invulnerable Golem that Jurahim al-Biad himself had constructed specifically for the campaign.</p>
<p> The combined pressure of the direct assault and the swamp approach forced Rezvyr into a tactical decision that remained controversial long after the war ended: he withdrew from Danipol rather than risk full entrapment of the consolidated armies. His defenders argued that the withdrawal was sound, a coherent force in the field was worth more than a besieged garrison and that the coalition subsequently demonstrated his wisdom. His critics argued he had abandoned the wars primary objective at the moment it was most threatened.</p>

<h2>Helena of Kontini and the Western Theatre</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Helena.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Helena of Kontini</i></small>  
</div>

<p>While Rezvyr and Arsifan conducted the wars central drama at the Isthmus and Danipol, the western theatre produced its most celebrated figure. Helena of Kontini was seventeen years old at the Battle of Belzena, where Parak led the united Hainach-Elbae forces against the Urbridge and Garmish coalition pressing into the Vale of Rean. She was, by the accounts of those who fought alongside her, an unassuming young Hainachi warrior at the dawn of the fighting emerging a hero at its dusk, her presence on the field inspiring the southern troops in a way that exceeded anything her rank or reputation would have predicted. At Belzena she killed Count Gregor Riedek of Melmbirth in personal combat. At the following Battle of the Maiden Fair, the campaigns conclusion, fought with the full weight of Urbridges knights now committed alongside Garmeley she killed Sir Richard of Rassel and wounded Duke Aras II of Garm himself, though not fatally. The coalition sounded the retreat. The Vale of Rean passed under full Elbae control. The Third War had a new name that soldiers on both sides spoke differently depending on which banner they were under.</p>

<h2>The Battle under the Stars and the Fall of Danipol</h2>

<p>The coalitions answer to its accumulated reverses was its most audacious operation of any of the three wars. At the Battle under the Stars, Rezvyrs forces launched a large scale assault that broke through the Sultanates lines and forced a retreat from Danipol. This alone would have been a significant achievement. What made it extraordinary was what Marfenna of Grevenna did simultaneously: leading a small force through a tunnel into the grevenni fortress itself, attacking from within at the precise moment the external assault struck the Sultanates camped forces to the south. The coordination required for this operation, fought at night, across multiple lines of approach, requiring precise timing between the tunnel force and the main assault, was a testament to both Rezvyr's planning and Marfenna's personal courage. Danipol fell yet again into coalition hands. The siege that followed was massive, but well supplied and defended, giving the coalition a strong position yet again.</p>

<h2>The Battle of Orzina and the End of Helena</h2>

<p>With Danipol besieged and the coalitions position in the central theatre at its strongest, the western front saw the wars final major engagement. Parak, older now and fighting a frontier war he had been conducting with considerable skill for nearly a decade, launched the Hainachi offensive at Orzina, threatening to pierce into the Garmish Heartlands. Helena of Kontini was the wa's living myth by this point, a figure whose name the Hainachi troops shouted and whose presence transformed the character of any engagement she joined. The charge at Orzina was inspired by that myth. It was also anticipated.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/William.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Sir William "the Staunch" of Merville</i></small>  
</div>

<p>Count Deydrik of Forrington had assumed command of the Garmish forces after his father Heyrims death in the Second War, and learned from every engagement since. At Orzina he baited the Hainachi charge deliberately, drawing the attack before springing the counter: Sir William "the Staunch" of Merville drove his Urbridge knights into the Hainach left flank like an anvil, crushing it before the charges momentum could carry to the coalition centre. The battle was a pyrrhic tactical coalition victory, the Hainachi forces were broken, but the cost in Garmish and Urbridge lives was severe enough that the result satisfied no one. Helena, seeing the banner failing, challenged William of Merville to single combat. He accepted. She died at his blade. The wars most celebrated figure died on a field her side had not won, at the hands of the coalitions most consistently effective western commander, in an act that neither party had sought. Deydrik of Forrington returned to Garmeley after the battle with no ceremony and no particular satisfaction. The House of Forringto's complicated relationship with Hainach, begun when his father had told Prince Agia to "let the cities burn," deepened another layer.</p>

<h2>The Siege of Danipol and the Invocation</h2>

<p>The Sultanates high command had been debating the use of magical intervention to break the Danipol siege for months. The scale of force required, and the unknowable consequences of deploying it in a confined theatre with active engagements ongoing, produced sustained disagreement within the Sultanate's military councils. Ferik Maros al-Corum, who had championed magical solutions throughout the war, favoured immediate deployment. Masir Meas Arsifan Elbae commanding the central theatre, counselled against it, and the deliberations produced no resolution. The decision was made instead to launch a direct assault: the two largest armies of all three wars assembled at Danipol and the battle began.</p>

<p>On the second day of the battle, Jurahim al-Biad completed his invocation.</p>

<p>He had been preparing it for weeks, working from his encampment on the far side of the Isthmus while the fighting continued around him. Sultan Arbalas I gave the order to abandon the invocation, the general staff had finally convinced him that a spell of this magnitude with active engagements on the very ground it would affect, had consequences that could not be predicted. Al-Biad acknowledged the command but continued nontheless. Whether he acted from the conviction that the wars outcome required it and that no one else would make the necessary decision, from an arrogance cultivated across three centuries of unmatched magical achievement, or from instructions passed to him by Ferik Maros al-Corum, whose known enthusiasm for magical measures and whose death in the invocations aftermath has made him the primary suspect in histories that prefer a conspiracy over individual egotism, has never been established. The motivations attributed to him have multiplied with the years and none has been definitively confirmed. What happened is not in dispute.</p>

<p>The Horad Isthmus ceased to exist. The waters of the Stormy Sea and the Pilvian Gulf connected through what the surviving world named the <a href="Strait of Horad" class="internal-link">Strait of Horad</a>. Everyone present at Danipol, coalition and Sultanate alike, died: Rezvyr zi Sribo, the finest commander the coalition had produced in all three wars; Marfenna of Grevenna, who had led the force that took the fortress; Sir Boris of Szulblood of Vihr; Maros al-Corum, the suspected architect of the invocation himself, killed by what he may have ordered; Orectis "the Eagle"; Ilare Morag the Drow Oracle. Thousands more on both sides who had no part in any of the decisions that led to this moment. Jurahim al-Biad perished in self-disintegration, the spell consuming the elf who had cast it at the moment of its completion. His epithet, Urul Horad in elvish "the one who swallowed the Horad." It was not intended as a compliment.</p>

<h2>Aftermath</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Arsifan.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Meas Arsifan Elbae, Masir of the Sultanate</i></small>  
</div>

<p>Meas Arsifan Elbae, who had not been present at Danipol when the invocation struck, was considered by nearly everyone in the Sultanate the clear successor to Sultan Arbalas I. He renounced all ambitions to the throne. "My loyal men were betrayed by their own banner," he told those who urged him to claim what seemed rightfully his, and he declined to explain further whether he meant al-Biad's disobedience, Maros al-Corum's suspected interference, or the Sultans own failure to enforce his order in time. He retired to the Almadi countryside, where he lived out the rest of his life in the company of his former mentor Ulrun Parak, who visited him regularly until Paraks own death. The friendship between the mentor who conquered Hainach and the student who refused a throne was the most dignified thing the Third War produced.</p>

<p>The <a href="Treaty of Danipol" class="internal-link">Treaty of Danipol</a>, signed in 815PA at the fortress whose name the war had made famous and whose ground the invocation had destroyed, was the first formal peace agreement between the Sultanate and any coalition in all three wars. Previous conflicts had simply ended, armies withdrew, fighting stopped, but no legal framework governed the cessation. Now the Sultanate relinquished its claim on Grevenna and formally ceded the destroyed Isthmus and the coalition recognized Hainachs Eyalet status. The <a href="Reanport" class="internal-link">Republic of Reanport</a>, whose maritime interests made the Straits future governance a matter of direct commercial importance, was granted the remains of the destroyed Isthmus and the minor islands that the invocations devastation had created, on the condition that it guarantee free and safe passage to vessels of all signatories. Reanport accepted. The commercial consequences of this arrangement proved, in the following decades, to be the invocations most enduring legacy: the Stormy Sea was no longer an inland sea but a connected waterway, and the ports that controlled its approaches, Reanport foremost among them, entered a golden age of mercantile prosperity that the war itself had done nothing to predict and everything to enable.</p>

<p>Sultan Arbalas I died in 844PA in circumstances his contemporaries described as disputed. He had reigned for sixty three years, surviving the war by nearly thirty, and the manner of his death whether natural, assassinated by those who blamed him for the catastrophe, or something more complicated, was never established to the satisfaction of those who investigated it. The <a href="Orenburg Pact" class="internal-link">Orenburg Pact</a>, signed in 817PA by the magical institutions of Ilrakhan in direct response to what al-Biad had done, attempted to ensure that nothing like the Invocation could happen again. Whether it succeeded is a question the Pact's signatories have continued debating across the century since.</p>
