<p>The Yellow War of 660PA to 668PA was an eight year conflict between the <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Lordship of Grevenna</a> and the <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Khatrym of Kherova</a>, fought over the contested lands of the Yellow Moor and the strategically vital port city of <a href="Kulum" class="internal-link">Kulum</a> on the Bay of Kulum. It is remembered in Grevenna as the war their armies won and their commanders could not finish, and in Kherova as the war that forged the Heriod Dynastys legitimacy from the furnace battle. Neither side achieved its strategic objective and the peace that ended it created an arrangement rather unsual, a Kherovan city sitting on Grevenni soil, that its consequences outlasted the war by two centuries.</p>

<h2>Origins</h2>
<p>The Yellow Moor had been a source of friction between Grevenna and Kherova for decades before open war broke out. The moor's sparse but strategically positioned communities, the trade routes running through the Opuzna delta toward the Pilvian Gulf, and the competing claims had produced a steady accumulation of border skirmishes, disputed levies and unresolved grievances that no diplomatic settlement had managed to permanently resolve. When a border skirmish in 660PA escalated beyond what either court could contain, the war that had been building for a generation arrived. What transformed a manageable escalation into a full commitment of forces was <a href="Tiblin VII" class="internal-link">Khat Tiblin VII</a>, newly consolidated on a throne he had claimed through clerical endorsement and careful politicking rather than military demonstration, and acutely aware that the splintered Kherovan noble houses needed a common cause before they found a reason to question his rule. He committed Kherova's full strength immediately, before any diplomatic channel could cool the situation. The war was his choice as much as its cause.</p>

<h2>Tark Fields and the Siege of Kulum</h2>

<p>The wars opening gave Grevenna every reason for confidence. At the Battle of Tark Fields in the northern Yellow Moor, the Grevenni force under Ahrim of Sera the Lord of Gagarin, a commander of considerable personal reputation within a martial aristocracy that produced capable soldiers with regularity, met the Kheri armies in open field and broke them decisively. Grevennas knightly tradition, its disciplined cavalry and its organized military culture proved exactly as formidable as its reputation suggested when given the terrain to express itself. The Kheri force at Tark Fields had no answer for that combination in open country, and the defeat opened the Yellow Moor to Grevenni advance. Among the figures who distinguished themselves at Tark Fields was Hannah of Krav, a knight known across the moor campaign as "Thawblade", whose personal combat record in the early conquests of the Yellow Moor became the wars most celebrated individual story on the Grevenni side. A devotee of Saint <a href="Yelena" class="internal-link">Yelena of Dreva</a> who had died at the Reanport Uprising less than forty years before the war and whose memory as a real person was still alive in Grevenna, Hannah killed several notable Kheri knights during the moors early fighting, among them Lord Darvit of Yevstatt, whose death in single combat against her became one of the wars most retold encounters. She survived the Yellow War entire, one of the few senior figures on either side to do so without wound or capture, and her subsequent career as a duelist of considerable renown made her the conflicts most enduring legacy on the Grevenni side. She is remembered long after the territorial settlement had been absorbed into the ordinary business of border politics.</p>
<p>Grand Lord Stefan I moved south with the main body of the army shortly after, establishing the siege of <a href="Kulum" class="internal-link">Kulum</a> that would define the wars entire remaining course. The citys position on the eponymous Bay of Kulum gave it commercial significance that made it Grevennas primary strategic objective, control of Kulum was control of the Pilvian Gulf approach, and Stefan committed the forces and the supply capacity of a man who expected the siege to be a matter of months. Kulums commander and ruler, Lord-Elect Faraz Davut, had other intentions.</p>

<h2>The Yevan Line and the Unbroken City</h2>

<p>The middle years of the Yellow War were defined by two things Grevenna could not break: a river and a city. Along the Yevan River, the same waterway whose banks had seen the foundational Kheri coalition victory against Rean five centuries earlier, the Kherovan defensive line held against repeated Grevenni attempts to cross into the heartland. Lord Elahd of Kairos and Castellan Galgin of Effez, whose castle at the lower Yevan anchored the southern defensive position, coordinated the rivers defence with the kind of methodical competence that terrain advantage and motivated defenders reliably produce. Tiblin VII fought alongside his commanders at multiple engagements along the line rather than directing from a distance, a visible statement of personal commitment from a ruler who had launched this war partly to demonstrate exactly that quality. Grevenna won the Yellow Moor but it could not cross the Yevan and the swamps was not the prize.</p>

<p>Nor was <a href="Kulum" class="internal-link">Kulum</a> falling. Faraz Davuts defence of the city across six years became the wars defining narrative, a commander whose name had been unremarkable before the siege and whose nickname "the Unbroken" was earned by keeping a city alive through circumstances that steadily worsened. In approximately 666PA the Grevenni navy defeated the majority of the Kheri oared galleys and sailing cogs in the Bay of Kulum, completing the encirclement that Stefans land forces had maintained for years. Kulum was now cut off by sea as well as land. No supply reached it. The city held anyway, for two more years, on whatever reserves Davut had accumulated and whatever his garrison could endure. It did not fall.</p>

<h2>The Slaughter of Yazbridge</h2>

<p>With the river line unbroken and Kulum refusing to yield, Ahrim of Sera devised a solution to the stalemate that was bold in conception and catastrophic in execution. Rather than continue battering the Yevan crossings where the defensive preparation was deepest, he assembled a substantial force in the northern moor and marched toward Kher itself, bypassing the southern riverlines strongest points and striking at the capital directly. The force he brought was formidable by any measure of the age, and its core was extraordinary: seven experienced Magi, a concentration of arcane power without precedent in the conflict and rare in any military context, whose combined capabilities Ahrim understood as the decisive edge that had been missing from every failed river crossing. He was not wrong about their power. He was wrong about <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Kherovas</a> preparation for it.</p>

<p>Tiblin VII met him at Yazbridge. The Kherovan Spellbreaker regiments were the anti magical elite of the Khatrym and deployed their arsenal at the engagements opening, including the infamous mirror shields whose reflective properties disrupted magical workings in ways that no amount of conventional military preparation could compensate for. The seven Magi, were isolated, neutralised and slain before the day's end. Every one of them. Ahrims army, deprived of its arcane core, was beaten back. The Slaughter of Yazbridge ended Grevenna offensive capacity in a single engagement. Ahrim of Sera retreated. In the days that followed, within a martial culture that understood personal defeat with the clarity and severity that Grevenna's noble tradition demanded, he ended his own life. Among the pilvian nobility his death was received with the divided response that his career warranted, mourned by those who understood him as a great commander undone by an enemy noone could have prepared him for, accepted as necessary by those for whom the defeat at Yazbridge meant only one adequate response.</p>

<h2>Peace of Kulum</h2>

<p>With Yazbridge having ended any prospect of Grevenni advance and the Yevan line having held through six years of sustained pressure, neither side possessed the capacity to deliver a decisive blow. Kulum still had not fallen, despite complete encirclement, despite two years without resupply, despite everything Stefan I had committed to its reduction; but Kherova equally could not push Grevenna back across the moor or relieve the siege from the landward side. The war had produced a map neither realm wanted and a military situation neither could escape. Peace talks began and concluded at Kulum itself in 668PA, concluding a negotiation at the walls of the city Stefan had spent eight years failing to take. The Grand Lord of Grevenna and Khat Tiblin VII of Kherova signed the settlement in person. Kulum was formally acknowledged as a Kherovan city under Kherovan governance. The Opuzna delta and the large Yellow Moor stretches that Grevenna had fought through and occupied were acknowledged as de jure Grevenni territory. The result was an arrangement without clean precedent with a Kherovan city on Grevenni soil, legally Kherovan in its governance and geographically foreign in its surroundings, whose internal logic depended on both parties continuing to accept it. For Tiblin VII it was the peace of a ruler who had held what mattered and ceded what was already lost. For Stefan I it was the peace of a commander who had won the moor and failed the siege. Faraz Davut the Unbroken walked out of Kulum eight years after the siege began, into a Kherovan city that was still Kherovan.</p>
