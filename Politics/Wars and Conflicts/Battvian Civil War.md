<p>The Battvian Civil War, fought between 792PA and 800PA, was the eight-year conflict between the <a href="Battvia" class="internal-link">Serene Realm of Battvia</a> and the coalition of eastern city-states that would emerge from it as the independent <a href="Paper Cities" class="internal-link">Paper Cities</a>. Its cause was constitutional and its consequences were catastrophic for Battvia, the Princes refusal to honour his obligations resulted in the permanent secession of Battvia's entire eastern coastal holdings in the Bay of Butera, its cities, its industries and its maritime identity severed from the realm in a single peace settlement. The Bay of Butera, Albesan's printing industry, Butera's Smoking Sea trade routes, the Excavators Guild's networks and the agricultural wealth of Fierra and Caria all passed from Battvian control in a single peace settlement that Prince Guido II "the Lame" signed in exhaustion and under internal pressure. He had started the war to marry who he wanted and ended it having lost the foundations of Battvian economic power, historians of Cevolia and beyond have not forgiven the man or shown sympathy for his actions in the slightest.</p>

<h2>The Great Privilege and its Violation</h2>

<p>The commercial cities of the Bay of Butera had lived under a specific constitutional arrangement with the Princes of Battvia since the reign of Prince Anselmo I, whose Great Privilege granted them significant autonomy and political influence over national affairs in exchange for increased taxation. This arrangement had made Battvia wealthy and the cities powerful in a productive equilibrium that required only the crowns continued willingness to honour its terms to reap the benefits of the globally dominant industries of the Bay. When Guido II declined to submit his marriage arrangements to the Grand Councils approval, choosing a local noble over the northern candidate the council had sanctioned, the cities understood immediately that a prince who would not uphold the Privilege over a marriage would not keep it over anything else that truly mattered to him.</p> 
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/greenlord.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Prince Guido II "the Lame" of Battiva</i></small>  
</div>
<p>The western cities rose in revolt. The <a href="Jeledo" class="internal-link">Duchy of Jeledo</a>, approached by representatives of the Buteran Guild to intervene, declined as Battvia controlled the vital northern trade routes through the Kortopi passes into Meadrath territory, the Treaty of Bassira was ratified by the Prince of Battvia and had normalized relations between the two realms, and most importantly Duc Ayar II Ucarraz had no interest in establishing the precedent that powerful cities could expect ducal military support against their feudal overlords. <a href="Turpador" class="internal-link">Turpador</a> was paralyzed by internal religious crisis. The war would be decided without either major duchys involvement.</p>

<h2>The Battle of Gialo and the Loss of the Sea</h2>

<p>Before a single major land engagement had been fought, Admiral David di Mezzi made the decision that already determined the wars strategic outcome. The Paper Fleet under his command attacked the Royal Fleet at anchor at Gialo and destroyed it entirely. Battvia had been the nominally superior naval power at the wars opening. It never recovered its position on the water for the duration of the conflict. Without a major fleet Battvia could not resupply coastal positions, could not threaten the island city of Butera directly or intercept the maritime supply lines that kept the coalitions mercenary armies funded. Navally or not, the royalists also could not prevent <a href="Mindano" class="internal-link">Mindano</a>, which sent substantial financial support and its renowned mercenary company the known as the Gorgeous Singers, from contributing to the coalition by land later. The war had been lost on the water before it was fought on land and the eight years that followed and showed Battvias inability to press forward deep into coalition territory would only reinforce this point.</p>

<h2>The First Invasion</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Flayer.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Cosimo di Ura "the Flayer", Conte di Molina</i></small>  
</div>

<p>The Royal Armys opening land campaign was led by the infamous Commander Cosimo di Ura, Conte di Molina, whose operational competence and personal cruelty would define the Battvian war effort in ways that Prince Guido cannot have intended but certainly didnt mind either. At the Battle of Fiarco, fought near the village of the same name a few miles from loyal Cefran, di Ura defeated the first coalition forces in the field and pressed the first invasion forward. What followed the battle did more damage to the Battvian cause than any engagement the coalition would achieve for years: di Uras treatment of the surviving soldiers was of a cruelty sufficient to earn him the epithet "the Flayer" from both sides of the conflict, and the news of it reached <a href="Mindano" class="internal-link">Mindano</a> and the Contea di Caria before the Royal Armys next dispatch did. Mindano committed the Gorgeous Singers and Conte Marco "the Honest" di Caria, pledged his forces to the coalition. Di Ura had won Fiarco and recruited two enemies in doing so.</p>

<p>The first invasion reached Fierra whose Contessa Giulia di Fierra had already declared for the coalition and the city came under siege. The combined coalition mercenary forces, the <a href="Gilded Men" class="internal-link">Gilded Men</a> under Marcello Diagonis, the Gorgeous Singers, the <a href="Scarlet Feather Band" class="internal-link">Scarlet Feather Band</a> under Guisseppe Averno, Don Fernad Dineli's Terragna warbands, and Seressa a Gnicci's Silver Company of Butera,  assembled in the Albesan Lowlands and marched to relieve it. Arno di Bellada commanding the royalist Knights of the Claw, performed with considerable distinction in the defensive fighting that followed, but individual heroics were not enough. When Contessa Giulia led her own forces out of the besieged city on horseback to join the relieving force from within, the siege broke and the initial invasion of the Albesan Lowlands had failed.</p>

<h2>The Rial River Stalemate</h2>

<p>Both armies encamped along the Rial River, the waterway running south through the Albesan Lowlands past Cefran and Fierra that had become the de facto boundary between the two sides, for the better part of two and a half years in  a grinding period of disputed skirmishes that produced no decisive engagement and consumed resources both sides could ill afford. The coalitions attempt to draw Jeledo into the conflict during this period, through renewed diplomatic pressure on Duc Ayar II Ucarraz, failed as completely as the first approach had. The river line held and the war went on slowly but surely.</p>

<h2>Posa, the White Bridge and the Sack of Fierra </h2>

<p>The second invasion struck in the north while the Paper Cities scrambled to respond in the south. Leandro Erival and his River Sons, alongside the Knights of the Claw and the main Royal Army under di Ura, overran the Band of the Scarlet Feather near Posa. The news sent Marcello Diagonis pressing south with the main Paper Army to exploit the moment and apply pressure on Cefran, the royalist city sitting on the Rial's eastern bank whose fall would have unravelled the entire Battvian position in the south. Margavo di Cef had anticipated the attempt and Giffero of the Marble Blades held the White Bridge of north of Cefran against Diagonis' advancing force, four dozen men against more than three hundred, for hours while the main engagement occurred at the cities gate further south,  until the position became untenable and Diagonis was forced to withdraw. Giffero earned his epithet "the Wall" at the White Bridge and Cefran did not fall.</p>

<p>The second invasions deeper thrust succeeded where the first had not. Fierra was taken after a relatively minor engagement as Contessa Giulia and a small force with the Scarlet Feather's remnants fought a rearguard action to cover the evacuation of civilians from her city and the withdrawal of Diagonis main army. She was captured and her city was sacked. Di Ura's forces repeated in Fierra what they had done to surviving soldiers at Fiarco, and the accounts of what was done to both the captured Contessa and the citys remaining inhabitants reached every corner of Cevolia before the autumn. The Princes war against his own people was being won in a manner that cost him the sympathy of parties that had remained neutral and the loyalty of some that had not.</p>

<h2>The Battle under the Olive Trees</h2>

<p>The largest battle of the war was fought by the full united coalition forces as every company and contingent of the Paper Cities and their allies met the Royal Army in a pitched engagement whose scale and duration produced casualties that neither side had fully prepared for. The coalitions right flank was failing when Don Fernad Dineli led his Terragna Outriders in a charge that broke through the Royal line entirely, his White Hare standard rising above the melee as the coalitions forces reformed around its momentum and the Royal Army began to disintegrate. It was not until after the Royal retreat that the rebellions commanders learned that Fernad had not survived the charge that had won the battle. The Don had died somewhere in the chaos of the breakthrough, his standard carried forward by men who did not yet know their leader was gone. The warbands and mercenary companies of <a href="Ligoggia" class="internal-link">Ligoggia</a> were promised land and recompense to continue fighting without him.</p>

<p>Margavo di Cef was captured attempting to cover the Royal Army' retreat. He was treated with the honors his conduct had earned, a dignity conspicuously absent from the coalition's own prisoners in Battvian hands.</p>

<h2>Treason at Posi and the Battle of Bereggio </h2>

<p>Under pressure from royalist nobles who found the imprisonment and treatment of the Contessa  di Fierra an embarrassment and a liability, Guido II agreed to exchange Margavo di Cef for Giulia di Fierra and the Contessa was freed. Around the same time the coalition hired the Sunderbeards  a fully dwarven mercenary company from the Kortopi Mountains under the brutal and renowned Qerengar Mors, whose arrival gave the coalition new hope for battles sure to come.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Arno.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Arno di Bellada, Hero of the Battvian Civil War</i></small>  
</div>
<p>Prince Guido II then announced his willingness to negotiate a peace settlement and invited the coalitions envoys to Posi for talks. Marco the Honest, Conte di Caria, travelled to Posi as part of the Paper Cities' delegation and was killed there in lower treason. The war would end when one side could no longer fight, not at a negotiating table. Using the confusion that followed the Treason at Posi, di Ura led the combined Royal forces across the Rial River for the third and final time, pressing into the Albesan Lowlands toward the village of Bereggio. The coalition met them there with everything remaining. Thousands died and the day's outcome would decided in no small margin by the dwarven Sunderbeards.</p>


<p>Arno di Bellada, the war's most celebrated Battvian legend, the man who had performed heroics at the first Battle of Fierra and held his Knights through every subsequent engagement, killed the ferocious Qerengar Mors in personal combat in no doubt the finest individual achievement of his career. The Sunderbeards swarmed him immediately after, his Knightly Guard broken around him, claiming the grim revenge that dwarven tradition demands for a fallen commander. Arno di Bellada died on the same ground mere minutes after slaying Mors and dreaming of victory, the Sunderbeards reformed and drove the Royal Army from the field.</p>

<p>The three sons of the loyal Margavo di Cef had ridden out in gleaming plate, the youngest a mere fifteen years old, in what those who watched understood to be an act of dynastic honour rather than military calculation. They were pulled from their horses and killed by Paper infantry and Margavo, who had given the Prince more loyalty than the Prince had given anyone, outlived all three of them a broken man whose principles had cost him everything.</p>

<h2>Aftermath</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Silvermerc.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Buteran Mercenary of the Silver Company during the Battvian Civil War</i></small>  
</div>

<p>The peace signed in 800PA recognized the independence of the <a href="Paper Cities" class="internal-link">Paper Cities</a>, primarily Albesan, Butera, Ligoggia, but also Fierra and Caria as a federal union. The map of what Battvia lost tells the story more plainly than any diplomatic language: its entire eastern coastal facade, the Bay of Butera and every city and industry along it, the Excavators Guilds archaeological trade networks, Albesans printing revenues, and the Smoking Sea maritime approaches that had made eastern Cevolia commercially viable for three centuries. Battvia had fought eight years to keep the coast and ended the war with the only harbour where its navy had been sunk as its primary maritime outlet. A Prince who had started the war to marry who he wanted and spit on the idea of justification before his subjects, had ended it having lost the economic foundations of everything Battvia was built on.</p>

<p>Guido II ruled for nine more years, regarded by subsequent historians as the worst Prince in Battvian history without serious competition. Cosimo di Ura survived a post war assassination attempt on his estate and retreated into the paranoid isolation of a man who had made too many enemies in too many places to feel safe anywhere. He was despised by royalists who blamed his cruelty for widening the coalition against them, and by the Paper Cities whose survivors remembered Fiarco, Fierra and Posi with a specificity that time has not diminished. Giulia di Fierra returned to a sacked city and rebuilt it, forever remembered as the "Plated Contessa". Only a few miles west of her, Margavo di Cef returned to Cefran without his sons, a loyalty that history may have noted but that had ended his line. The White Hare standard of Don Fernad Dineli hangs in the council chamber of Ligoggia, where it has hung since 800PA, and the chair beside it has never been formally reassigned. Over the coming decades men of integrity and honour like Dineli would become exceedingly rare in the Council Chambers and streets of Ligoggia.</p>
