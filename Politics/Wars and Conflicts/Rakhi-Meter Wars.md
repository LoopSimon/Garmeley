<p>The Beheran Campaigns, known in the records of the <a href="Elven Dynasties" class="internal-link">Meter Dynasty</a> as the Rakhi-Meter War, were a prolonged military conflict fought between the <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakhian Empire</a> and the Meter, the ruling elven dynasty of eastern Behera and the dominant power of ancient Behera, across twenty-one years from 210BA to 189BA. They represent the most significant direct confrontation between the two greatest powers of the ancient world.</p>

<p>The campaigns arose from the collision of two expanding spheres of influence in the contested lands south of the Stormy Sea, territory that had once belonged to the <a href="Addacia" class="internal-link">Addacian Empire</a> and following Addacia's final defeat and absorption by Rakh in 271BA had become a region where Rakhian administrative reach and Meter political ambition came into increasingly direct contact. The precise casus belli is not preserved in surviving sources, but the structural conditions were evident for decades before hostilities began: Rakh now shared a frontier with the Meter's sphere of influence, and neither power had any tradition of accepting limits at a frontier it had not chosen. The conflict opened in 210BA, at the accession of Jir-Meter I to the Beheran throne who would preside over the entirety of the war and outlive its conclusion by decades, reigning until 121BA.</p>

<p>Fighting was centred primarily along the southern shores of the Stormy Sea and across the former Addacian borderlands, a landscape of river crossings, fortified hill-towns, and the remnants of Addacian infrastructure that both sides found strategically valuable and neither could hold without contest. The war lasted two decades, making it one of the longest sustained conflicts in Rakhian history, though the surviving record of its individual engagements is thin. What is known is that it ended at the Battle of Alma-Nexis in 189BA in a settlement of arms, if not of ambitions, that left both empires in possession of roughly what they had held at the outset with a border at the Mesovo River.</p>

<p>The surviving record of the Rakhi-Meter Wars is almost entirely drawn from the Meter side. Rakhian accounts of the campaigns, if they were ever committed to writing, have not survived the destruction of 0PA and the subsequent loss of the empire's archives. What is known of the conflict from the Rakhian perspective is limited to the bare chronological framework preserved in documents such as the Scroll of Hapzut and the accounts of neighbouring peoples. The Meter Dynasty by contrast produced a body of court literature and scholarship during and after the war that represents the primary source base for modern understanding of the conflict.</p>

<p>The war's most enduring legacy is literary rather than territorial. <a href="Ptamon" class="internal-link">Ptamon Akbril</a>, the Beheran elven philosopher and scientist who spent over a century at the Great Library of Rahzalbis and served as personal tutor to both Jir-Meter IIand later Gil-Meter I — the two rulers who followed Jir-Meter I — composed his poem "Tears of Alma-Nexis" in the aftermath of the conflict. It is considered one of the primary surviving sources for the Meter perspective on the war and  a lament that captures something of the weight the battle carried for those who witnessed the campaigns from within the elven court. No equivalent Rakhian literary response is known to have survived, if one was ever composed.</p>
