They remain the only attempts by the Urien successor states to take back any of the lands lost in the <a href="Hobgoblin Invasion" class="internal-link">Hobgoblin Invasion</a>. In the first war the League drove the hobgoblins from the east bank of the Trinmo, took the country around Fort Garlin and retook Turv. In the second it won the river country as far as Merbridge. Neither war crossed the river in force, neither reached the heart of Ak-Saray, and in both the League's own quarrels ended the war before it could be carried further.</p> <h2>Background</h2> <p>When the Battle of Gobsfall Keep halted the Tirbi in 696PA, the clans that had crossed the Trinmo with Tirbo did not all go back over it. For a century and a half they held a strip of the east bank, from the country below the Bend down to the hills of Hetli, and Ak-Saray reached south to the lands above Zacin, where Greenstone Tower stood alone inside its border. <a href="Hobgiliath" class="internal-link">Hobgiliath</a>, the old Dursan, stood on the west bank facing the Urien lands across the river. The Order of Urbridge had been refounded in 736PA for the single purpose of ending this occupation, and had never been strong enough to attempt it alone.</p> <p>By 838PA the pieces had come together. Gadvin I, a much loved king, had taken the throne of Eldmar. The Order had grown into a power of heavy cavalry. The Church of Ur, whose Archbishop sat in Gelmsburg, had never accepted that its old cathedral lay in the hands of unbelievers. And the cities of the Trinvale Republics, rich on the trade of the Pass of Ogul-Vosko, were willing to lend a great sum for the war.</p> <h2>The Oath at Pearfawl</h2> <p>The League was founded in the spring of 841PA on Pearfawl Field near Leston, at the largest gathering of Urien lords and knights since the Invasion. It was a holy occasion as much as a military one. Archbishop Iselher I of Ur spoke before the host in what became known as Iselher's Address. He named the hobgoblins the Infestation, reminded the host of Archbishop Felix of Preston, who had died holding the walls of Dursan with the Prophet's Light, and called on the faithful of Ur to take back the cathedral city and the lands of the Gryphon Throne. The lords and knights then swore the Oath at Pearfawl before the Archbishop, to make no peace with Ak-Saray apart from one another and to hold what each host won. The Church has called the League's war a holy cause ever since.</p> <h2>The First War</h2>

<p>The war was fought in two parts. The main host, made up of the heavy cavalry and levies of Gelmsburg, the knights and bowmen of Eldmar under Gadvin I and the knights of the Order under its Lord-Protector, Gorlan II, marched from Pearfawl Field to Gobsfall Keep, which the Order held at the time, and fell on the hobgoblin holdings of the east bank. King Edwyn I of Gelmsburg raised the Gelmish host and never saw combat himself, and his nobles led it in his name. Gadvin I fought in the field, and later historians came to see him as the face of the war, but the League had no single commander. As in any host raised by oath, each faction and subfaction followed its own captains. A second column, mostly knights errant and adventuring companies, went down through the lands of the Trinvale Republics, which gave it passage, and came at the hobgoblin country from the south.</p> <p>The tribes were taken by surprise. The Great Boss was chosen in haste when news came of a vast host marching west, and the choice fell on Shattal of the Hara-Vel, who had to lead a people that had not made war together for a hundred and fifty years. In the north the Unogi held the country around the Bend, and in the south the Balaqi and the Darkeyes faced the second column. The hobgoblin holdings along the east bank were overrun, and the cruelties done to the settlers there, who were by no means all warriors, passed counting.</p>

<p>It drew out <a href="Hrened" class="internal-link">Hrened</a>, a practitioner of the <a href="The Malagul" class="internal-link">Malagul</a>, whose sect had refused to take any part in the war. He left the northern forests against its teaching and rallied the survivors of the river holdings behind him, because he still counted them his own people.</p>


<p>In the south the second column came up the river in 842PA and raised Fort Garlin as the League's forward post. Greenstone Tower, which had stood within hobgoblin lands since the Invasion, passed to the Urien side of the border with the advance. The greatest danger to the League was the hobgoblin mage whom the Urien called the Syrspeaker, and before the great battle he was killed by Alina of the Feather, an assassin. Alina was rumoured to be the bastard child of Count Lancel of Durtrees, but this was never confirmed.</p> 

 <div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Alina.webp" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  
<small><i>Alina of the Feather, Assassin of the League</i></small>

<p>The decisive battle was fought in 843PA at Rinstree Hill, a few miles south of Turv. It was a clear victory for the League and broke the host of Shattal. It was also the last great battle of the war, because Baron Arran of Nestlemine, the leader of the knights of Eldmar, died there. With him went the order of the camp. Claims and restitutions were fought over, and so was the honour of leading the van in the battles to come, and a great contingent of knights, from Eldmar and from Gelmsburg alike, left the host. The war was still a great success, but it was plain that the League could not profit from it further. Gelmsburg took Turv, the Trinmo became the border, and the Trinvale Republics waived the Valesloan to celebrate the victory.</p> <h2>The Interlude</h2> <p>For forty years the League did not march. The <a href="War of Horn and Fang" class="internal-link">War of Horn and Fang</a> drew the attention of the north between 851PA and 866PA, and the Dance of Husks of 854PA struck central and southern Ur hard. The quarrels of Rinstree were not forgotten, and the Gelmish and the Eldmari kept apart. The lands south of Fort Garlin filled with farmsteads and river settlements, Turv stayed Gelmish, and the fort was kept by veterans of the League.</p> <h2>The Second War</h2> <p>By the 880s the southern tribes had begun to raid the new farmsteads below Garlin, and the Order and Eldmar called the League to arms. The Republics again gave the host passage and supplies, but there was no second loan. The High-Bisop of <a href="Iztaw" class="internal-link">Iztaw</a> blessed the war this time rather than the Archbishop, with much less ceremony, and only after the host was already on the march. In Ak-Saray the tribes raised Atbar the Blind of the Krezuke as Great Boss.</p>

<p>The war opened in 883PA with an attack on Fort Garlin. The siege was broken at dawn by the heavy cavalry of the Order, and the saying of the Light of Urbridge is told of that charge. The leading figure of the League was King Folk II of Gelmsburg, a warrior king whom many said was gifted with magic. Men swore that he moved quicker than any man should and that in the heat of battle he grew to twice his size, so large that he could wield a longsword in each hand, and afterwards the Gelmish called him Greatfolk. Beside him fought the Paladin Vastilla of Vansel, a devout follower of Saint <a href="Yelena" class="internal-link">Yelena</a> who wielded a lucerne like her saint. She was said to stride slowly across the battlefield, and around her the hobgoblins fell like wheat to a scythe.</p> <p>The League advanced up the river and took the bridge-town of Merbridge in 886PA. By 888PA it stood within reach of the lake country and of Hobgiliath itself, and Atbar the Blind brought the whole strength of the tribes against it at Merbridge.</p>

<h2>The Battle of Merbridge</h2> <p>The battle went the League's way. Vastilla of Vansel cut through the Krezuke guard to Atbar the Blind and killed him, and for an hour it seemed that the road to Hobgiliath lay open. Then <a href="Zurgush" class="internal-link">Zurgush</a>, a Wyrdspeaker of nineteen who had held the office for less than two years, answered with a blight. His plague magic swept through the League's camps and supply trains within days. The mages of the colleges, bound by the <a href="Orenburg Pact" class="internal-link">Orenburg Pact</a>, could not take the field against him, and the Archmage of Greenstone Tower could do no more than tend the sick. The advance stopped where it stood, and King Folk II, who had fought at the head of his knights, was among the sick.</p> <p>The coalition fell apart quickly. The Eldmari accused the Gelmish of drawing off their host first and leaving the Eldmari wing to the sickness, the Gelmish answered in kind, and the knights went home in bands rather than as a host.</p>

<h2>Aftermath</h2> <p>No treaty ended the Second War. Ak-Saray has no government that could sign one, and the members of the League had no wish to be seen asking for terms. What the League held when it broke apart became the border, a line running south-west from the Trinmo at Merbridge to meet the line of the first war, which it follows to the mountains. It has not moved since.</p> <p>King Folk II died in 889PA of the sickness he had taken at Merbridge, and Klovis I took the crown of Gelmsburg. Not long afterwards the Order, which had lost too many knights to man Gobsfall Keep, handed it over to Gelmsburg. Fort Garlin and Merbridge remain the League's two river forts, held by the remnants of its veterans and their adventuring followers, supplied from Greenstone Tower and supported by the Trinvale Republics and by Gelmsburg.</p> <p>In 896PA Gelmsburg lost Turv to Gisvale in the <a href="War of 896" class="internal-link">War of 896</a>, and its eyes turned from the west. <a href="Urbridge" class="internal-link">Gorlan V of Urbridge</a> has twice called for a Third War in recent decades, and both calls failed, blocked by the rivalry of Gelmsburg and Eldmar. The League's planners have no answer for Zurgush, who is now fifty-eight and whose power has only grown, while the Pact keeps the League's own mages off the field. Since 923PA the Garmish Succession Crisis has drawn Eldmar and Urbridge south.</p> <h2>People of Interest</h2> <p>Gadvin I of House Lavellan<br> King in Eldmar, the face of the First War</p> <p>Iselher I<br> Archbishop of Ur, author of Iselher's Address</p> <p>Gorlan II of House Goodwind<br> Lord-Protector of Urbridge in the First War</p> <p>Edwyn I<br> King in Gelmsburg in the First War</p> <p>Baron Arran of Nestlemine<br> Leader of the knights of Eldmar, fell at Rinstree</p> <p>Alina of the Feather<br> Assassin, slayer of the Syrspeaker</p> <p>Folk II, called Greatfolk<br> King in Gelmsburg in the Second War</p> <p>Vastilla of Vansel<br> Paladin of Saint Yelena, slayer of Atbar the Blind</p> <p>Shattal of the Hara-Vel<br> Great Boss in the First War</p> <p>Atbar the Blind of the Krezuke<br> Great Boss in the Second War</p> <p><a href="Hrened" class="internal-link">Hrened</a><br> Malagul practitioner, leader of the dispossessed of the river holdings</p> <p><a href="Zurgush" class="internal-link">Zurgush</a>, the Hand of Blight<br> Wyrdspeaker of Ak-Saray</p>
