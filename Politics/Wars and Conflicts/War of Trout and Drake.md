<p>The War of Trout and Drake was a thirty-three year conflict fought between the Gismar loyalist coalition backed by the Kingdom of Korsfall and the ruling House von Medlan of <a href="Gisvale" class="internal-link">Gisvale</a>, lasting from 631PA until 664PA. Its conclusion saw the extinction of the Medlan line, the restoration of House von Gismar in the person of Liara I, and the immediate union of Gisvale and Korsfall into the unified Kingdom of <a href="Meadrath" class="internal-link">Meadrath</a>. It is remembered in Meadi history as the foundational war of the united kingdom, a conflict whose human cost was staggering and whose political consequences shaped the future of Meadrath.</p>

<h2>The Usurpation and the Long Exile (589PA – 631PA)</h2>

<p>The war's origins lie in the usurpation of the Gisvale throne by Diedrik of Medlan, Lord of Wormill, who in 589PA betrayed and supplanted Tibor II "the Feeble" of House von Gismar. Tibors children fled first to Weadland and were refused at the court of Schipwig, then found shelter at Korsfall, whose lords had no love for the Medlan upstart now consolidating power across the Trinmo. Among those children was Giselher, who would spend the better part of four decades transforming himself from a refugee prince into a credible claimant. His first attempt to reclaim Giselstatt came around 613PA, when Diedrik died and his son Ringil I, freshly crowned and married to a Urien princess, appeared momentarily exposed. </p>
<p>As Korsfalls commitment was still wavering and Giselhers coalition insufficient he failed miserably and the Medlan line proved more resilient than the loyalists had hoped. Giselher returned to Korsfall, regrouped, and spent the following eighteen years building the alliances and military resources that would make a second attempt decisive. In 631PA, with Gunther III von Kor firmly committed and the Lords of Medlan's own highland vassals increasingly restless under Ringil's rule, Giselher crossed the Trinmo in force. In those same early months, <a href="Galiatar Inkfingers" class="internal-link">Galiatar Inkfingers</a>, the former Court Magus of Gisvale and Rector of Korsfall College, who had long been an outspoken critic of the Medlan usurpation, was found dead in Korsfall in 635PA, his stomach ailment later suspected to be poison. No perpetrator was ever officially identified.</p>

<h2>The War (631PA – 660PA)</h2>

<p>The early years of the war favoured the loyalists, who advanced steadily through the southern Trinmo territories while Ringil consolidated his defenses around Wormill & Giselstatt and the northern river approaches. The war consisteed of  largely small scale engaments and skirmishes until  the <strong>Battle of Nuckheim</strong> in 641PA, fought on the southern slopes of the Trinwood where Giselher led his forces into what appeared to be a decisive engagement against a significant Medlan field army. The battle turned as  Giselher' banner fell in the fighting, and the loyalist forces  watching the standard of their prince collapse into the muck, broke and routed before the position was lost. Giselher was dead. His three sons  inherited the campaign, and none possessed their fathers personal legitimacy. </p>
<p>The war hardened into a grimmer and more attritional affair in the years that followed. The eldest, Ardol, died in a river crossing in 648PA. In 655PA, the coalition drove on Wormill, the city that had been Diedriks original seat of power, and at the Battle of Wormill the two remaining sons of Giselher, Gishelm and Karel, fell in the same engagement. It was Count Heinrich von Lot, military commander of the Korsfall forces, who held the shattered coalition together in the immediate aftermath and pressed the assault to its conclusion, taking Wormill within days. Only Liara, Giselher's daughter, remained of the Gismar line. <a href="Wulfrick" class="internal-link">Wulfrick the Elder</a>, Court Magus to Gunther III, had refused to participate in the conflict from its outset, spending the war years at Praum as advisor to the Count von Praum. He died of the Spring Fever in 661PA, three years before the union was formally proclaimed.</p>

<p>The final phase of the war was prosecuted in Liaras name and von Lots command, with Ringils strength visibly failing and his vassals abandoning him in growing numbers. Ringils most formidable remaining asset was the Count of Mussling, a battle hardened wizard of considerable power whose command of lightning and elemental force had blunted several loyalist advances in the south and made him the cornerstone of Medlans defensive strategy in the wars closing years. It was von Lot who, at the Battle of Summerlake in 659PA,  led a cavalry charge personally into the Count of Mussling's position, cutting through the wizard's guard and killing him in the melee. The battle itself was a decisive loyalist victory and effectively broke the last coherent Medlan field army. Ringil I died the following year without a credible successor, and Giselstatt opened its gates to Liara shortly after. The city that Tibor II had lost seventy years before finally returned to the house of Gismar.</p>

<h2>Aftermath (664PA – 682PA)</h2>

<p>In the autumn of 664PA Liara I von Gismar married Gunther III von Kor, and their firstborn son Gildher was crowned the first King of unified Meadrath. The human cost of the war were tens of thousands dead, the rich farmlands of the Gisvale devastated across three decades of campaigning and it would take a generation to recover from. In 682PA, Gildher I broke up the former Medlan heartlands into the new Duchies of <a href="Risfalt" class="internal-link">Risfalt</a> and <a href="Wargel" class="internal-link">Wargel</a>, granted to the Houses von Praum and von Buhr respectively in recognition of their loyalty during the war. The Medlan name, stripped of title and territory, faded from the political record within a generation.</p>
