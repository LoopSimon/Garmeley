<p>The Leveli-Shirin Wars were two wars fought along the Mondi River between the <a href="Twintail Imperium" class="internal-link">Twintail Imperium</a> and the Vocas a <a href="Levelix" class="internal-link">Levelix</a> during the reign of Imperator Natan X "Irontail". The First Leveli-Shirin War, from 399PA to 402PA, ended in a costly stalemate on the river line and led to the military reforms that created the great peasant armies for which the Natani Imperium became known. In the Second Leveli-Shirin War, from 413PA to 416PA, the Imperium crossed the Mondi, won the battles of the Rimagna Valley and the Hazen River, and took the Rimagna Valley and much of the southern Leveli Lowlands, fixing its new northern border on the Hazen River, where Fort Nutun was built.</p>  
  
<h2>Background</h2>  
<p>In the late 4th century PA the Vocas a Levelix, the realm of the Lower Leveli governed from Nastulia, extended its rule south across the Leveli Lowlands until it reached the Mondi River. The Mondi had long marked the northern edge of Shirin settlement, and the fertile lands on its southern bank around Tonot formed some of the most productive prefectures of the Imperium. Leveli raiding parties crossing the river fords, and the Leveli claim to the river crossings themselves, brought the two realms into open conflict within a few years of Natan X's accession in 395PA.</p>  
  
<h2>First Leveli-Shirin War (399PA - 402PA)</h2>  
<p>The first war was fought almost entirely along the line of the Mondi. The imperial legions of the time were small professional forces, sufficient to defeat any Leveli host that crossed to the southern bank but too few to hold the far bank against the combined levies of Levelix. For three years each side forced crossings that the other threw back, and the fighting around the river crossings, above all at the Tiachen Bridge, cost both realms heavily without moving the border. Among the Leveli the war produced a hero in Hinseou, an officer of lesser rank whose defence of the northern approaches to the Tiachen Bridge made his name across the Leveli Lowlands. The war ended in 402PA with a peace that restored the river as the border, leaving neither side with any gain.</p>  
  
<h2>The Natani Military Reforms</h2>  
<p>The stalemate convinced Natan X that the Imperium could not win a war beyond the Mondi with the forces it had. In the years of peace that followed he reorganised the imperial recruitment and supply offices on a scale the Imperium had not seen before. Every prefecture was required to raise a fixed quota of peasant infantry from its rice villages, to be drilled in the months between planting and harvest and called to the legions in time of war, while granaries along the Mondi at Tonot and Tiakait and a fleet of river barges were set aside to keep these armies supplied in the field. The reforms produced the great peasant armies that later became the defining image of the Natani Imperium, vast lowland hosts of lightly armed Shirin infantry whose strength lay in their numbers, their discipline in formation and the river supply network behind them.</p>  
  
<h2>Second Leveli-Shirin War (413PA - 416PA)</h2>  
<p>After eleven years of peace the Imperium went to war again in 413PA, this time on its own initiative. The new peasant legions crossed the Mondi at the Tiachen Bridge into the Rimagna Valley, the lowland basin on the northern bank of the Mondi between the Alev Swamp in the west and the Hazen River in the east, opposite Tonot. The Leveli forces were commanded by Hinseou, now a general, who had risen to the command of the southern armies of Levelix on the reputation he had won in the first war.</p>  
<p>The decisive engagements of the war were the Battle of the Rimagna Valley in 414PA, fought in the west of the valley, and the Battle of the Hazen River in 415PA, fought at the eastern edge of the valley where the Hazen flows south into the Mondi. Both were imperial victories won by the weight of the new peasant armies, and after the Hazen River the Imperium overran large parts of the southern Leveli Lowlands. In 416PA the southern armies of Levelix surrendered and withdrew from the Rimagna Valley. At the surrender Hinseou presented his Iril, a traditional Leveli blade capable of capturing and releasing spells, to Natan X as a token of the submission of his armies. The Iril was afterwards kept on display in the Palace of Salt in the city of Mihim, until it was stolen by an unknown thief in 909PA.</p>  
  
<h2>Aftermath</h2>  
<p>The Imperium fixed its new northern border on the Hazen River and in 417PA began the construction of Fort Nutun on a small island in the river. Over the following decades Nutun became the principal hub of the Imperium's commerce with the north, and a city grew up around the fortress on both banks of the Hazen. On the battlefield of the Rimagna Valley the Shirin raised the Statue of the Irontail, a massive monument to the victory and to the Imperator who had won it. The Imperium held the Rimagna Valley for more than four centuries, until the Rimagna War of 848PA, also known as the Invasion of Rimagna, in which the newly formed Trachez a <a href="Girdineux" class="internal-link">Girdineux</a>, itself a successor of Levelix, conquered the valley together with Nutun and large parts of the Alev Swamp. The recovery of these lands north of the Mondi remains a cause pressed by powerful voices at the imperial court.</p>  
<p>For Levelix the loss of the Rimagna Valley and the southern lowlands closed the road south, and the realm turned its ambitions east against <a href="Amurez" class="internal-link">Amurez</a>, whose central lands it conquered at the end of the 5th century. For the Imperium the wars marked the last great achievement of the High Natani Period. The peasant armies created by Natan X remained the backbone of the imperial legions for generations, but after his death in 422PA no Natani Imperator would again lead the Imperium to a comparable victory.</p>
