 <p>The League of the Gryphon is a great alliance between the Kingdoms of <a href="Gelmsburg" class="internal-link">Gelmsburg</a> and <a href="Eldmar" class="internal-link">Eldmar</a>, as well as the Order of <a href="Urbridge" class="internal-link">Urbridge</a>, named in honour of the Gryphon of the fallen Kingdom of <a href="Urland" class="internal-link">Urland</a>. Its primary goal is the destruction of the Hobgoblin Infestation and the Federation of <a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a> in western <a href="Ur" class="internal-link">Ur</a>, and the restoration of its western lands under <a href="Urien" class="internal-link">Urien</a> rule. During the <a href="Wars of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">Wars of the Gryphon</a> the League reconquered significant territories from the hobgoblins, but both times it was unable to press its advantages into the deeper lands west of the Trinmo. Beyond its common goal the League has suffered from rivalries and contested interests among its members, specifically Eldmar and Gelmsburg, and in recent decades the attempts of individual leaders to begin a Third War against the hobgoblins have failed for political reasons.</p> <h2>The Oath at Pearfawl</h2>

 <p>The League was founded in the spring of 841PA on Pearfawl Field near Leston, at a great gathering of the lords and knights of Gelmsburg, Eldmar and the Order, blessed by Archbishop Iselher I of Ur. In his address to the host, known as Iselher's Address, the Archbishop named the hobgoblins the Infestation and called on the faithful of Ur to take back the cathedral city of Dursan and the lands of the Gryphon Throne. The lords and knights swore the Oath at Pearfawl before him, to make no peace with Ak-Saray apart from one another and to hold what each host won. The oath had a religious character from the first, and the Church has spoken of the League's wars as a holy cause ever since. The first war was financed by the Valesloan of the Trinvale Republics, and it was fought under the banner of the Urland Gryphon.</p> 
 
 <h2>Structure and Leadership</h2> <p>The League has never had a standing council, a treasury or a permanent commander. It is an oath that was sworn in 841PA and renewed for each war, and between the wars it exists only as the oath itself, the river forts and the memory of its victories. Each war was fought by a host of factions and subfactions, each following its own commanders, much as a holy host is raised. Gadvin I of Eldmar came to be seen by historians as the face of the first war, and King Folk II of Gelmsburg was the leading figure of the second.</p> 
 
 <p>All three members claim the leadership, and none of them holds it. Gelmsburg fields the largest host and holds the seat of the Archbishop of Ur. Eldmar's king was the face of the first war, and the Order keeps the Gryphon banner of the old Knights of the Gryphon. Each of the three realms is sometimes counted the leader of the League by its own people, and the quarrel over which has the right has outlasted both of the wars.</p>  
 
 <p>Gelmsburg was the strongest of the three, with the largest standing host of any Urien state and the heavy Gim cavalry that carries it, its garrison holds Gobsfall Keep. Eldmar brings the levy of the Eld Valley, its knights and its renowned bowmen, and the crown joined the League in 841PA under Gadvin I, often considered the leader of the first war. The Order of Urbridge is far the smallest, but its heavy cavalry is known across Ur, and the League's cause was always the Order's own purpose. Its Lord-Protector, Gorlan V, has twice called for a Third War in recent decades.</p> <h2>Friends of the League</h2> <p>The Trinvale Republics are not formally members, but they supported the League from the beginning. They financed the first war with the Valesloan, let the League cross their lands, and have helped to support Fort Garlin and Merbridge ever since, although the Republics also trade openly with the southern hobgoblin tribes, which the Order cannot forgive. Greenstone Tower, a mage tower of the <a href="Orenburg Pact" class="internal-link">Orenburg Pact</a>, stands firmly with the League and supplies the garrison of Fort Garlin, though the Pact keeps its mages from the field. Many heroes of the <a href="Trifflands" class="internal-link">Trifflands</a> joined the League in the wars, but always as individuals and never as a decision of their country, and the Order sees the godless commune as a nuisance. The Church of <a href="Iztaw" class="internal-link">Iztaw</a> blessed both wars, the first with the full ceremony of the Archbishop and the second quietly and after the host was already on the march. In both wars the League also drew mercenary companies and adventuring bands.</p> <h2>The Forts</h2> <p>The League's holdings in the west are two river forts on the Trinmo. Fort Garlin, raised in 842PA, and Merbridge, taken in 886PA, are manned by remnants of the League's veterans and their adventuring followers, supplied from Greenstone Tower and supported most actively by the Trinvale Republics and by Gelmsburg. Gobsfall Keep, where the Invasion was halted in 696PA, was held by the Order until it handed the keep to Gelmsburg after the Second War, and it is now held by a Gelmish garrison.</p> <h2>Rivalries</h2> <p>The oldest quarrel of the League is that of Eldmar and Gelmsburg, which began with the <a href="90 Days War" class="internal-link">90 Days War</a> and which the two kingdoms carried into the League with them. The first war ended when the Eldmari knights lost their leader at Rinstree, and the quarrel in the camp over claims and the honour of the van sent a great contingent home from both realms. The second war ended in mutual accusations of desertion. Gelmsburg also holds a grievance against Gisvale for the loss of Turv in the <a href="War of 896" class="internal-link">War of 896</a>, and since then it has looked less to the west than to the Bend. The Order, for its part, has little love for the Trinvale Republics and the Free Lands of Triff.</p>  <p>The League is quiet in 927PA. Gorlan V of Urbridge has called twice for a Third War, and each time Gelmsburg and Eldmar have refused to follow a captain of the other. The League's planners also have no answer for <a href="Zurgush" class="internal-link">Zurgush</a>, the Wyrdspeaker of Ak-Saray, who stopped the Second War at nineteen and whose power has grown since, while the Orenburg Pact binds the mages of the colleges. The Garmish Succession Crisis has drawn Eldmar and Urbridge south, and Gelmsburg is undecided, watching the Eld Valley and Turv. Some chiefs of Ak-Saray have noticed, but none has found a following to act on it.</p> <h2>People of Interest</h2> <p>Gorlan V, Lord-Protector of Urbridge<br> Twice a caller for a Third War</p> <p>Klovis I, King in Gelmsburg<br> Successor of Folk II, leader of the Second War</p> <p>Moira III, Queen in Eldmar<br> Descendant of Gadvin I, face of the First War</p> <p>Iselher I<br> Archbishop of Ur, author of Iselher's Address</p> <p><a href="Zurgush" class="internal-link">Zurgush</a><br> Wyrdspeaker of Ak-Saray, the League's greatest concern</p>
