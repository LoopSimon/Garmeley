<p>The Drow, also called Under-Elves or Underland Elves by surface civilizations, are the elven peoples of the <a href="Underlands" class="internal-link">Underlands</a>, the vast network of cave systems and subterranean territories that runs beneath the surface of Inara. They share with the surface <a href="Elf" class="internal-link">High Elves</a> of <a href="Behera" class="internal-link">Behera</a> a common biological origin in the <a href="Nua" class="internal-link">Nua</a>, the ancient precursor peoples of the central beheran underland cave systems, and in the fundamental biology that unites all elven peoples: the Jawahr, the unique organ associated with their exceptional magical aptitude and their extended lifespans. Beyond this shared foundation, they have spent nearly eighteen thousand years adapting independently to an existence of perpetual darkness, building civilizations of extraordinary antiquity in the deep places of the world while the surface elves built theirs above. For most surface-dwellers, the Drow are most visible as the priestly class of the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae Sultanate</a> and the guardians of its Paragon Tombs under the institution of the <a href="Golden Dusk" class="internal-link">Golden Dusk</a>. This view really only captures the most accessible portion of a culture whose full depth reaches further back in time and further down in the earth than any surface realm may think.</p>

<h2>Biology and Appearance</h2>

<p>The Drow are physically recognizable as elven, the same elongated proportions, wire like hair, large eyes and extended ears, but millennia of underground adaptation have produced a distinctly different presentation. Their skin runs from dark grey through jet black to obsidian, with many individuals showing blue undertones within the dark palette that varies considerably between communities and lineages. Their eyes are even larger than those of their surface cousins, adapted to conditions of near total darkness, and extraordinarily sensitive to sunlight, which constitutes a genuine physical barrier to extended surface life. Eye colours tend toward the vivid and unusual: bright reds, white, blue, lilac, pink and silver are all common, producing an arresting appearance in individuals who rarely surface to display it. Hair tends strongly toward the pale end of the spectrum: stark white and pale yellow are most common, with silvery and copper tones present in some lineages. Uniquely among the Drow, the mouth tells its own story: their teeth may be white, black or purple, while the gums, tongue and inner throat run to shades of red, pink or purple. Almost no physical size difference exists between Drow male and female, a biological feature they share with their High Elf cousins.</p>

<p>The Drow share with High Elves the Jawahr, a small, bean shaped organ located behind the pelvis whose precise function remains poorly understood by surface scholarship, but which appears to be central both to elven magical aptitude and to their extended lifespans. Drow who have prolonged exposure to planar energy age as slowly as the oldest High Elf sages; most  age at the more standard elven rate of up to two hundred years. The same natural inclination toward planar magic that characterizes the High Elves is present in the Drow in equal measure, and the same theoretical connection to the Jawahr has been proposed to explain both. It is worth noting that the Drow possess this aptitude independently of any relationship to surface institutions or surface magical traditions, their own relationship to the arcane long predates any academic framework that the surface world has developed for it.</p>

<p>The exception to the standard Drow appearance is the Ithri: Drow of albino complexion, their skin dramatically paler than any typical Drow, and some of them bearing deep jet black hair rather than the characteristic whites and yellows of their kin. The Ithri are unusual enough in Drow communities to attract particular attention, and their significance varies between cultures.</p>

<p>In ornamentation, Drow culture has long favored jewelry incorporating Glintstone, a material native to the Underlands whose distinctive property is a yellow greenish luminescence produced when the stone is stationary, fading when it is moved. The practical and aesthetic applications have made Glintstone culturally significant across essentially all Drow communities regardless of their other differences.</p>

<h2>The Nua and the Cave Emergence</h2>

<p>The common precursors of both the Drow and the High Elves of the surface are the <a href="Nua" class="internal-link">Nua</a> — sometimes called the Nua-elves, the Nua-peoples, or simply the Ancients — who inhabited the central underland cave systems approximately thirty thousand years before the present. The Nua were largely replaced through unknown circumstances roughly twelve thousand years before the first recognizably elven cultures emerged from the eastern Beheran cave systems around 18,000BA, leaving behind the two branches that would become the Drow and the High Elves. What caused the Nua's disappearance — whether ecological, cataclysmic, or something more gradual — is not established, and what relationship the earliest proto-Drow and proto-surface-elves had to the Nua's end is unknown.</p>

<p>The emergence of elven cultures from the eastern Beheran cave systems in approximately 18,000BA marks the point at which the two branches that would become Drow and High Elves began their distinct trajectories. One group moved upward, adapting over millennia to surface existence in the Beheran desert and river systems. The other remained in the caves and tunnels below, entering what the Drow themselves call the <strong>Era of Migrations</strong> — a period of approximately seven thousand four hundred years during which Drow communities spread through the Beheran underlands and the northern underground passages, establishing the first large permanent settlements that would eventually become the foundation of their civilizations.</p>

<h2>History</h2>

<p>The history of the Drow is one of the longest continuous civilizational records in Inara, a history that surface scholarship can only partially reconstruct, and which Drow communities themselves preserve in oral and written traditions far older than any surviving surface text. Its earliest documented phase ends around 10,600BA, when the Era of Migrations concluded and the Drow had established substantial permanent settlements in the Beheran and northern underlands. From these settlements, the great civilization of Anazirt rose in the Beheran underlands around 4,033BA, followed within two centuries by the beginning of<a href="Tur Enzal" class="internal-link">Tur Enzal</a>: the longest lived Drow high civilization, whose cultural and architectural legacy defined Drow civilization for nearly three millennia.</p>

<p><a href="Tur Enzal" class="internal-link">Tur Enzal</a> (~3800-1090BA) was the great constant of Drow history across the Bronze and early Iron Ages, surviving the rise and fall of the surface world's earliest civilizations, enduring the Age of Thirst that destroyed <a href="Murajk" class="internal-link">Murajk</a> (the Drow imperial state that had grown within Anazirts sphere in approximately 3,500BA), and outlasting the surface world's Beheran Dark Ages entirely. Within Tur-Enzal, Drow civilization produced its greatest achievements, including the discovery of a means to share thoughts and emotions almost instantaneously through the use of ancient stones and innate magic, a form of collective communication that predated academic magical theory by centuries and whose mechanism surface scholars have often described as a rudimentary form of what would later be called artificery or alchemy. The same period saw the first joint Drow and High Elf institution in the <a href="Biadarkyr" class="internal-link">Tomb City of Biadarkyr</a> (~2370BA), built simultaneously in surface and underland form, which established the cooperative tradition around the Paragon Tombs that would eventually produce the Golden Dusk.</p>

<p>Tur Enzal's end came from catastrophe rather than decline: the Great Flood of approximately 1,090BA destroyed the civilization that had endured for nearly three millennia, sending refugees in multiple directions. Some emerged in the far northern arctic. the ancestors of the <a href="Aelf" class="internal-link">Aelfs</a> of <a href="Skemfing" class="internal-link">Skemfing</a>, who adapted to surface conditions through magical means over the following millennium and are now regarded by most observers as an entirely distinct people. Others contributed to the later blossoming of <a href="Tur Ajk" class="internal-link">Tur Ajk</a> (~1535BA), the great Drow empire that had been founded in Murajk's ruins as early as ~2420BA and that endured until the two Alparid conquests of 960BA and 890BA ended its independent existence, followed by the Alparid Dynasty's formal enslavement of the Beheran Drow population in 643BA, a subjugation that ended only with the Meteri Dynasty's liberation and elevation of the Drow priesthood in 276BA.</p>

<p>The most consequential rupture within Drow history was the  <a href="Vuron" class="internal-link">Vuron</a> Schism, a violent religious and political fracture within Tur Enzal's civil wars of 2,430-2,310BA, which produced the Vuron Zealots: a faction that fled east, attacked and burned the goblin Undercity of  <a href="Mitimgar" class="internal-link">Mitimgar</a> in the event known as the Day of Pain (2,245BA), and founded their own empire, <a href="Tur Vuron" class="internal-link">Tur Vuron</a>, in the northern Underlands. Tur Vuron collapsed for unknown reasons around 1,540BA, its surviving cities isolating themselves; the timgar goblins settled their account with the Vurons remnants in 662BA, when the warlord Opronik led the Vengeance of Mitimgar and annihilated the last Tur Vuron cities. The Drow Goblin enmity created by the Day of Pain is the single most significant political legacy of the Vuron Schism, shaping underland politics in ways still felt in 927PA.</p>

<p>The Birth of the End earthquake of approximately 240BA destroyed much of the western Ilrakhan underlands and with them the Drow communities that had established settlements near the Sundered Coast over the preceding nine centuries. Surviving Drow dispersed in multiple directions, some northward toward the <a href="Rogyn" class="internal-link">Rogyn Forest</a>, others southward to rebuild below the Smoking Sea. A significant group eventually established <a href="Tur Aman" class="internal-link">Tur Aman</a> in the central <a href="Nirydia" class="internal-link">niryd</a> Underlands (66PA), whose brief and violent encounter with the surface of northern Nirydia , known as the Slaughter of the First Night in 72PA, led the Drow there to seal themselves underground permanently, collapsing all known Nirydian underland entrances and completing the Great Seal of Tur Aman by 110PA. Tur Aman has had no confirmed surface contact since. Even goblins and dwarves avoid the approaches to its sealed borders.</p>

<h2>The Drow in the Modern World</h2>

<p>The Drow of 927PA exist across several distinct communities whose relationships to the surface world and to each other range from deeply integrated to entirely unknown. The most visible are those who live in large numbers beneath the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae Sultanate</a> in eastern Behera, those Drow who were liberated by the Meteri Dynasty, whose priesthood was elevated in the same moment, and have since become the institutional heart of the Sultanates religious and funerary life through the <a href="Golden Dusk" class="internal-link">Golden Dusk</a>. The Grand Hierophant of the Golden Dusk, the spiritual leader of all Beheran elves, Drow and surface alike, is currently Ramira Ued al-Raban, herself a Drow, residing at the Great Tomb of Paret in Biadarkyr. The drow High-Priest of <a href="Jiravid" class="internal-link">Jiravid</a>, Rivil al-Faet, holds a permanent seat on the Sultanates Dirhan council. The Golden Dusk operates a network of underground outposts, Brass Domes, across the Sultanate, staffed by Drow clerics and scholars who administer healthcare, funerary services and the Paragon Tombs with characteristic reluctance to ascend to the surface even for institutional purposes. Light sensitivity is not performance: it is a physical reality that shapes the entire character of their engagement with the above ground world.</p>

<p>Westward, in the underlands beneath <a href="Itharia" class="internal-link">western Itharia</a>, the nation of <a href="Kilvea" class="internal-link">Kilvea</a>, founded in 705PA with the assistance of the Spurshey people of <a href="Runsby" class="internal-link">Runsby</a>, formalized through the Pact of Hanley of 675PA — represents the newest Drow political entity and one of the few with an actively maintained relationship of mutual benefit with a surface human culture. The Spurshey alliance is an anomaly in Drow political history as most Drow engagement with the surface world is either ancient and institutional.</p>

<p>In the deep south of Behera, beneath the jungles of Kaito and the Yppori Islands of the Purple Sea, the <a href="Tur Filash" class="internal-link">Drow Realm of Tur Filash</a>, declared in 319PA in the Cave of Fire>, a chamber that lies deeper below the ocean floor than any other known inhabited space, represents the most remote and least understood of the known Drow communities. The Yppori made contact with Tur Filash in 514PA, and some limited knowledge has filtered upward through that connection, but the Cities of Fire remain essentially unknown to surface scholarship. Their proximity to the deep ocean territories of the <a href="Krestasi" class="internal-link">Krestasi</a> is the primary political concern of Tur Filash and these drow are more wary of what lives below them than of anything above.</p>

<p>Beyond these three major realms of the drow, and the sealed mystery of Tur Aman, Drow cities of unknown number and size persist in the deepest underlands, in spaces that surface exploration has not reached and that even Goblins and Dwarves consistently avoid. What those cities are, what they practice, and what relationship if any they maintain with the Drow communities that surface scholarship has documented is entirely unknown. The Drow are among the oldest civilized peoples in Inara. The portion of their world that the surface has seen is not large.</p>
