<p>Red, Grey, Blue and Green skinned seafarers, masters of sail and wind;</p>
<p>The humble and gentle Orcs, or "Qari" in their tongue, are of tall stature and imposing builds, they can reach ages of up to 70 years and commonly sprout fierce tusks from their lower jaws.</p>
<p>Most Orcs today can be found in the vicinity of the remnant of Qa, but a part of their people has made its home even further east, across the great ocean, on the island of Durennis, the land of Dawn.</p>
<h2>Names</h2>
<p>Qari is the name the Orcs give themselves. Most everyone else calls them Orcs, from the ancient Elvish "Orq", "of the seas", the name given to them by the elves of the <a href="Elven Dynasties" class="internal-link">Ancient Elven Dynasties</a>, who contested them for maritime hegemony.</p>
<p>Qahori is the name of the culture of the <a href="Qahori" class="internal-link">Federation of Qahor</a> and of the diaspora that descends from the exiles of Qa, and older records use it for all the Orcs of the island in the age of the <a href="Wars of Qahori Subjugation" class="internal-link">Wars of Qahori Subjugation</a>. <a href="Qalmyr" class="internal-link">Qalmyr</a> is the name of their faith and of nothing else, it is not the name of a people.</p>
<h2>Qa and the Diaspora</h2>
<p>The holy birthland of the Orcs is the island of Qa, now <a href="Il Haena" class="internal-link">Il-Haena</a>, where the Maqari Mountains in its centre are held to be the closest place to Qastor himself. Their loss to the Elbae Sultanate in the Wars of Qahori Subjugation, which the Orcs remember as the Destruction of Qa, scattered the Qari across the seas and made of them a travelling people, always yearning for something that could never be theirs again. Yet Qa was never emptied. About seven in ten of the people of Il-Haena are still Orcs, living as subjects of the Sultanate in a land that is both home and lost to them.</p>
<p>Wherever they live, the Orcs are rarely tied down to one place. They take extremely well to different climates and are found in the ports of the Niryd Sea, in <a href="Paqor" class="internal-link">Paqor</a>, in the <a href="Kareli Hierarchy" class="internal-link">Kareli Hierarchy</a>, in <a href="Roxeria" class="internal-link">Roxeria</a> and <a href="Kulum" class="internal-link">Kulum</a>, in the southern towns of <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Kherova</a> and in <a href="Raximan" class="internal-link">Raximan</a>.</p>
<h2>The Orcish Nations</h2>
<p>There are four Orcish states, and all four style their ruler Sandimar, the king-title of old Qa.</p>
<h3>The Qahori Federation</h3>
<p>The largest and the heart of the diaspora, a maritime federation of former chiefdoms on the Qahori Archipelago, north of Il-Haena, united under the House of Tihiok. See <a href="Qahori" class="internal-link">Qahori</a>.</p>
<h3>The Qarzi Elayat</h3>
<p>The autonomous kingdom of the Qarzi tribe in south-eastern Il-Haena, a loyal and prosperous subject of the Elbae Sultanate that kept its holy city of Benwaral and is hated by the rest of the diaspora for it. See <a href="Qarzi" class="internal-link">Qarzi</a>.</p>
<h3>Kamerzin</h3>
<p>The kingdom on the island of Durennis, in the far east of Nirydia, founded by those who left the Federation to return one day to Qa in conquest. See <a href="Kamerzin" class="internal-link">Kamerzin</a>.</p>
<h3>Abaleq</h3>
<p>The kingdom of the Fazaar Isles, which entrenched itself as a proper, sedentary Orcish kingdom in the 8th century after breaking away from the Federation in a religious schism. It is the only state of the <a href="Path of the Second Moon" class="internal-link">Path of the Second Moon</a>, which almost all other Orcs hold to be heresy. See <a href="Abaleq" class="internal-link">Abaleq</a>.</p>
<h2>Faith</h2>
<p>Almost all Orcs practice <a href="Qalmyr" class="internal-link">Qalmyr</a>, the dualistic faith of the two moons, Qastor and Zolra. The great majority follow the First Path, the orthodox path of Qastor, which knows no hierarchy beyond elders and shrine-keepers and takes a different shape in each community: the Enduring of the Federation, the Keepers of the Qarzi, the Quiet of Il-Haena and the Returners of Kamerzin.</p>
<p>Two other paths have branched from it. The <a href="Path of the Second Moon" class="internal-link">Path of the Second Moon</a> is followed by the Zolraii of Abaleq, who worship Zolra and embrace her tendencies, and is called the Path of the Damned by those who fear it. The <a href="Path of the Third Moon" class="internal-link">Path of the Third Moon</a> is a smaller sect, close to the original faith and not maligned at all, which holds that Qa is a paradise one must make for oneself.</p>
<h2>Bone-Craft</h2>
<p>Where other peoples work metal and stone, the Qari work bone. It is the great craft of their culture and the clearest mark of it: their shrines, their armour, their clothing and their weapons are made of the bones of the animals of Qa, above all of the <a href="Igulan" class="internal-link">Igulan</a> and the <a href="Ossur" class="internal-link">Ossur</a>, two animals native to Il-Haena whose bone is hard, light and takes a carving well. The Qari do not hunt either animal for sport. A hunt begins with a prayer and nothing of the animal is wasted, neither its meat, hide and sinew nor its bone.</p>
<p>The shrines and great halls of old Qa were framed in bone, its beams, arches and screens raised on stone footings. Little of this survives beyond the shrines of Benwaral and the ruins of Ensihr, but the forms of it are still used in the temples and halls the diaspora has built.</p>
<p>Armour is the greater martial craft of the Qari. Plates of carved bone laced together with cord over leather or heavy cloth make a light armour that does not rust in the salt air, which suits a people who live on the sea. The same bone is worn as clothing: cloaks and coats set with bone scales, belts, collars and the carved ornaments that mark a person's family, ship and faith. Sailors wear it at sea, the guards of every Orcish state wear it, and a Qari warrior is known more for what they wear than for what they carry. Weapons are fewer and simpler: spears and harpoons with bone heads, bone-hafted daggers and clubs, tools of the hunt and the sea first. To the Qalmyr, which counsels against the sword, armour is the honest craft of a people who would rather endure a blow than strike one.</p>
<p>Carving is both craft and prayer. Bone is cut with the moons and the Qinari, with waves and sea routes, and with the names of those who wore it. Navigators carve their charts in bone, and a family's lineage may be carved on a strip of it and worn.</p>
<p>After the Destruction of Qa, bone from Il-Haena became the most prized of all, both as a trade good and as an heirloom, a piece of Qa carried on the body. The Quiet and the Qarzi on the island kept the old forms of the craft alive, and in the diaspora pieces of old Qa bone are treasures handed from generation to generation.</p>
<h2>Seafaring</h2>
<p>Due to their origins and common practice as wandering sailors, Orcs are rarely tied down to one place, especially after the Destruction of the Lands of Qa in what is now known as Il-Haena at the hands of the Sultanate. They can be found sailing even the icy waters of the Torn Sea in the far north, where they are valued for their prowess and enjoy rich lifestyles in port cities like Sallesand or Albesan.</p>
<p>It was Qari sailors who gave the <a href="Krestasi" class="internal-link">Krestasi</a> their name, from the Orcish word "Krest", "crust", when they first met the crustacean peoples in the waters south-east of Il-Haena. Their merchant charts from <a href="Raximan" class="internal-link">Raximan</a> show sea routes and landmasses far to the south, and it is likely that the seafaring Orcs were the first of the peoples of Inara to find the continent of <a href="Sirenna" class="internal-link">Sirenna</a>.</p>
