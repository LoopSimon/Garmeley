<p>The Safir are a broad family of reptiloid humanoids native to the jungle regions of southern <a href="Behera" class="internal-link">Behera</a>, the subcontinents of Kaito to the east and Vinjira to the west of the Smaragd Sea and the most numerous and politically significant non human species in the southern parts of the Continent. The term itself is a generalization, covering four distinct colour groups whose geographic distribution, cultural character and biological appearance differ considerably, united by common descent from the <a href="Astafir" class="internal-link">Astafir</a> precursor genus and by the defining biological influence of <a href="Yari" class="internal-link">Yari</a> Ichor on their evolutionary history. Beheran human cultures know them as the Naga, a term carrying mythological weight in the <a href="Pravazik Pantheon" class="internal-link">Pravazik Pantheon</a>, where they are described as the children of the sea Yari Aleac, though the Safir themselves do not use this name. The dominant Safir civilization, the <a href="Pravazik" class="internal-link">Empire of Pravazik</a>, is one of the most populated and powerful political entities in the known world, its Pravi dominated imperial culture extending across both subcontinents in a claim of sovereignty over all Safir peoples that the other colour groups observe with varying degrees of compliance and indifference.</p>

<h2>The Astafir and the Gift of Atul</h2>

<p>The immediate precursors of the Safir were the <a href="Astafir" class="internal-link">Astafir</a>, fully serpentine sentient beings who inhabited southern Behera long before the first Safir emerged, organized into small communities, capable of basic culture and social structure, but fundamentally different in biology from the humanoid forms their descendants would eventually take. The relationship between Astafir and Safir is one of evolutionary succession, the Safir did not replace the Astafir through conquest or catastrophe but through the gradual biological advantages granted through outside change. That change, according to the oldest religious, historical and scientific traditions of the Safir, was the gift of Atul.</p>

<p><a href="Atul" class="internal-link">Atul</a> was, in this largely mythological telling, the first of the <a href="Yari" class="internal-link">Yari</a>, the demigod beings of southern Behera, to share his divine blood, his Ichor, with the Astafir. The gift occurred approximately 29,000 years before the present day, in a period so remote that no written record reaches it and all accounts are reconstructions from oral tradition, religious text and academic inference. Those Astafir who received Atuls Ichor began to change: over generations, the fully serpentine body of the Astafir genus gave way to forms with limbs, with hands capable of manipulation, with the upright or semi upright postures that define the morphological range of modern Safir. These changed lineages proved more adaptable, more numerous, more capable of the complex social organization that produces cities and empires, and over millennia they became the Safir, replacing the Astafir as the dominant genus of southern Behera not through war but through the simple arithmetic of biological advantage. Whether any of this is literally true, whether Atul existed, whether the gift was a deliberate act of divine will or something else entirely, whether the changes it supposedly triggered were supernatural or a natural evolutionary process that later tradition attributed to a convenient divine cause; is a question that Safir religious scholars, scientists and historians have argued about for centuries without approaching agreement. Atul is considered either a mythological figure, a genuine historical being whose actions have been impossibly distorted by time, or a dead Yari whose demise would have been undetectable across such a span of time.</p>

<h2>Biology and the Morphological Spectrum</h2>

<p>The most immediately striking feature of the Safir as a species is the range of physical forms they exhibit, a spectrum running from fully bipedal humanoid bodies, through forms with partially or fully serpentine lower halves, to individuals possessing additional limbs beyond the standard four, to forms so serpentine as to be nearly indistinguishable from the Astafir that preceded them. This variation is not random and is not primarily determined by colour group or regional origin. It is a consequence of generational Yari Ichor accumulation: lineages whose ancestors have received Yari blood across many generations tend toward more humanoid forms, while lineages with little or no Ichor exposure over their history retain the serpentine characteristics of the Astafir.</p>
<p>Colour group and morphology are entirely independent variables, a green Irud Safir may be fully bipedal or fully serpentine, and a dark Vekti Safir may have four functioning limbs or only a massive tail, depending entirely on their community's Yari history rather than their colouration. Among the Zoroi, the most aquatically adapted colour group, tail-dominant morphology is the most common form as many Zoroi possess only a large tail and arms rather than full legs, a body plan well-suited to life in the reef systems and river networks they inhabit but relatively unusual among the other three groups.</p>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Kash.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> 
<small><i>A Kash from Vinjira</i></small>
</div>
<p>Safir are egg laying rather than viviparous, clutches are small and carefully tended, and the communal protection of eggs is one of the more consistent cultural practices across all four colour groups regardless of other differences. They can interbreed across colour lines without difficulty, and mixed-colour individuals are not uncommon in communities where multiple groups live in proximity, typically displaying blended or patterned colouration that reflects both parental lineages in varying proportions. Colour itself is not a cultural construct or a social designation but a biological one, the four dominant colour groups represent ancient regional adaptations to different environments within southern Behera, present in the Astafir before Atul's gift and preserved through the Safir transition, each originally suited to the specific light conditions, vegetation and terrain of a different jungle ecosystem. The Pravi warm colours blend with the dappled interior jungle; the Vekti dark tones suit the deep shadow of cave systems and dense canopy floor; the Irud greens provide camouflage in the most light-saturated jungle undergrowth; the Zoroi blues adapt to coastal light and underwater environments.</p> 
<p>The rarest biological variation of all is the Kash, Safir of pure albino complexion, bearing no colour group's markings and no camouflage adaptation. The birth of a Kash is regarded across Safir culture as an omen of change and potential doom, and <a href="Arishi" class="internal-link">Yari</a>, the prime divine figure of the Pravazik Pantheon, has issued a standing demand that all Kash be killed at birth. Any Kash alive in the present day has survived by being born beyond the Empires's reach, hidden by their community, or protected by those willing to defy the most powerful divine authority in southern Behera, and is regarded by those who encounter them with a mixture of awe, superstition and profound unease.</p>

<h2>The Pravi</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Pravi2.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> 
<small><i>A Pravi Priestess from Niztapur</i></small>
</div>

<p>The Pravi are the most numerous and politically dominant of the four Safir colour groups,  warm-scaled in browns, yellows and reds, their elaborate scale patterns marking genetic heritage in ways that Pravi communities can read as readily as a written genealogy. They occupy the interior heartlands of both Kaito and Vinjira, the territories that provide the most productive agricultural and resource base in southern Behera, and their deep institutional history reflects the advantages of having held the most defensible and fertile ground for the longest time. The Pravi are, above all, builders. Builders of cities, institutions and of legal and administrative frameworks capable of sustaining complex civilization across generations. Where other Safir groups organize around kinship networks, religious communities or nomadic traditions, the Pravi organize around the state, and the state the eastern Pravi have built in the <a href="Pravazik" class="internal-link">Empire of Pravazik</a> is the largest single political entity in the known world. The claim that Pravazik rules all Safir peoples is not literally enforced across the full range of the species, but the claim that Pravi culture and Pravi institutions dominate almost every significant Safir political entity is harder to dispute. More tolerant than the Zoroi and Irud of dry heat and open terrain, the Pravi have expanded beyond the jungle into adjacent environments without the biological cost that such exposure imposes on their more climate specialized kin.</p>

<h2>The Vekti</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Vekti.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> 
<small><i>A Vekti Nomad of the southern Desert</i></small>
</div>

<p>The Vekti are the great wanderers of the Safir world, dark-scaled in greys, dark blues and blacks, adapted to the deep shadow environments and cave systems of the peripheral territories that the Pravi heartlands do not reach, and uniquely among the four colour groups genuinely tolerant of the dry heat of desert and semi-arid environments. This desert tolerance has made the Vekti the most widely distributed Safir group by geography: their communities are found not only in the margins of Kaito and Vinjira but across the wider Beheran continent in nomadic caravans that traverse the desert routes, in mountain and hill country far from the jungle, and in diaspora populations across the known world, in cities of <a href="Nirydia" class="internal-link">Nirydia</a> to the east and even occasionally in the northern reaches of <a href="Ilrakhan" class="internal-link">Ilrakhan</a>. Within the greater Pravi realms, Vekti are present but rarely comfortable and almost always occupy the lower tiers of urban social hierarchies, regarded with the particular combination of fascination, disgust and spite that settled cultures tend to extend to nomadic peoples who move through without staying. The Vekti have few territories they can claim as unambiguously their own. The greatest independent Vekti political entity is the tribe known as the Oro, whose independence from Pravi institutional dominance is a matter of considerable pride among Vekti peoples. Many Vekti follow the <a href="Singer Worship" class="internal-link">Faith of the Singer</a> a religion whose emphasis on movement and journey maps naturally onto a culture that has made perpetual travel its defining condition.</p>

<h2>The Irud</h2>

<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Irud2.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> 
<small><i>An Irud Hunter of Vinjira</i></small>
</div>

<p>The Irud are the deep jungle safiri, green-scaled and effectively camouflaged in the densest and most biologically rich environments that southern Behera produces, living in communities organized vertically as much as horizontally: in the root systems beneath the ground, in the hollow trunks and lower branches at ground level, and in the canopy above. The defining architectural feature of Irud civilization are the God Trees, the vast trees of the deep Beheran jungle, combining their enormous buttressed trunks with sprawling aerial root networks , reaching sizes that make individual specimens resemble geographic features more than plants. Irud communities are build around and within these structures, whose scale can accommodate hundreds of inhabitants across multiple vertical levels of settlement. The God Trees are regarded in the oldest Irud traditions as the desiccated husks of divine beings from an age before the Yari, the so-called old gods whose bodies became the forest, though whether this belief reflects any reality or is simply the mythological explanation a deeply religious people gave to extraordinarily large trees is a question that the Irud themselves do not consider particularly interesting.</p>

<p> The Yari religious framework has largely subsumed the older God Tree worship, reinterpreting the trees through the lens of Yari theology, and the Irud are the most fanatically and comprehensively Yari devoted of all four colour groups with all Irud communities organize their social and spiritual life around service to a Yari patron. Non-Yari religious traditions are rare in Irud territories. The deep jungle's isolation from the Smaragd Sea trade routes and from Pravi urban civilization means the Irud are the least known of the four groups to outside observers, and the Astafir remnant communities, the surviving fully serpentine populations that the dominant Safir regard with a mixture of shame and hostility, are most commonly found in the deep Irud territories, where the jungle provides sufficient isolation for them to persist uncontacted.</p>

<h2>The Zoroi</h2>
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Zoroi.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> 
<small><i>A Zoroid noble from the southern Seas</i></small>
</div>

<p>The Zoroi are the sea people of the Safir world, blue-scaled and amphibious, capable of breathing underwater as readily as in open air, their communities organized around the coastlines, reef systems, river networks and islands of the Smaragd Sea and the Beheran Ocean beyond. They are easily the finest navigators and seafarers the Safir produce, their knowledge of currents, reefs and weather systems in southern Beheran waters accumulated across generations of living in and on the sea rather than merely beside it, and their presence within the great Pravi coastal cities gives them a somewhat more integrated position in Pravazik's social hierarchy than the Vekti, though full social equality remains elusive. The Zoroi are also the colour group with the fewest humanoid characteristics despite being the most outward-facing and relatively cosmopolitan: the aquatic environment favours sinuous body plans over bipedal ones, and many Zoroi possess only a large tail and arms in place of the full complement of limbs that more terrestrial Safir exhibit. It is the Zoroi who represent the Safir worlds primary point of contact with civilizations beyond southern Behera, Geoli, Elbae and Qahori sailors who have reached the legendary port of Niztapur encountered Zoroi intermediaries long before they encountered Pravi imperial officials, and the Zoroi understanding of outside cultures, while incomplete, exceeds that of any other Safir group.</p>

<h2>The Yari and the Safir</h2>

<p>The relationship between the Safir and the <a href="Yari" class="internal-link">Yari</a> is the defining context of Safir civilization and the ultimate explanation for the morphological diversity that distinguishes modern Safir from their Astafir predecessors. The Yaris divine blood, the Ichor, has shaped Safir biology over thousands of generations, and the different forms that relationship takes across different Safir communities produce correspondingly different social, religious and political structures. Some Yari share their Ichor willingly with Safir followers, creating patron client relationships of dependency and empowerment in which the Safir community draws power and biological transformation from regular Ichor contact while the Yari receives devotion, service and a community organized around its divine presence. Others have been captured by their Safir servants and are bled ritualistically while effectively imprisoned, the worshipped entity now serving the worshippers. A smaller number have been killed, their Ichor absorbed entirely by the "Yarimanji", mortals who fully absorb a Yari's essence in an act that can be seen as the highest expression of religious aspiration or its most transgressive. And some Yari remain entirely isolationist. For a full account of the Yari themselves, their forms and their known individuals, see <a href="Yari" class="internal-link">Yari</a>.</p>
