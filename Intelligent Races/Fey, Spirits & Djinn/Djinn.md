<p>Djinn are beings of smokeless fire, found in the deserts of northern <a href="Behera" class="internal-link">Behera</a> and the <a href="Wasteland of Rakh" class="internal-link">wastelands of Rakh</a>. Their name comes from the ancient Beheri <i>Jiihn</i>, meaning "smokeless fire", and it describes them well: a djinn shows itself as a shimmer of heat and pale light that burns nothing and leaves no smoke. Djinn are famed above all for granting wishes, remaking a thing as it is asked of them through what scholars can only call their unique essence. They are just as famed for granting a wish as the wisher felt it rather than as they meant it, and the deserts are full of tales of men buried in their own gold.</p> <p>Djinn are not summoned as <a href="Daemon" class="internal-link">Daemons</a> are, but bound, and binding is an art of its own that shares little with casting or weaving. A binder shapes nothing themselves; their craft lies in holding a djinn to a vessel, a name or an oath, and in speaking a wish so exactly that it cannot be turned. The oldest binders are said to be the coldest, for a djinn bound long enough begins to grant even what its binder never says aloud.</p> <h2>True Djinn</h2> <p>The true djinn are truly ancient, said to be older than any record of Rakh, and are the undisputed lords of the Sea of Dunes, the great Beheran Deserts. Each is a power in its own right, and they are seldom met far from the burning lands near the girdle of the world. Some scholars tie their origins to the great <a href="Conflux" class="internal-link">Confluxes</a> of ages past, though none can say how. Many reckless fools have tried to barter with or bind a true djinn, and few lived to boast of it. Only a handful were ever recorded bound: by the ancient Palmunians, by the elves of the eastern deserts, by the cruel <a href="Yorgos" class="internal-link">Yorgosi</a> of the Vale of Yor, and by the old nomads of the deserts, the Inori and the Migoni.</p> <h2>Nurdjinn</h2> 
<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Nurdjinn.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">

<p>The Nurdjinn, or lesser djinn, are bound to the <a href="Wasteland of Rakh" class="internal-link">Wasteland of Rakh</a> and the <a href="Nurlight" class="internal-link">Nurlight</a> at its heart, and are not known from any time before the Eruption of Al-Akka. Weaker than the true djinn, they fade when carried too far from the wastes, and those who keep one close for too long are said to sicken as men sicken near the Nurlight itself. Two traditions of binding have grown up around them. In Kerlaen, binding has been practised since the fall of <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakh</a> and the end of the kingdom of Kerlaes as the art of the warlock, and its warlocks call their work the Calling. The nurbinders of the Lameidus, restless nomads and sorcerers descended from old Palmunia, call theirs the Pygmion, and treat their Nurdjinn as slaves to be drawn upon without mercy. Both now depend on a dwindling supply, for the Nurdjinn grow fewer each year in what has come to be called <a href="The Thinning" class="internal-link">the Thinning</a>, studied seriously only by the <a href="Children of Dust" class="internal-link">Children of Dust</a>. They are found in greatest numbers nearest the Red Sun, and binders of both traditions hold that all djinn, true and lesser, grow restless in the strong eras of the <a href="Conflux" class="internal-link">Conflux</a>.</p>
