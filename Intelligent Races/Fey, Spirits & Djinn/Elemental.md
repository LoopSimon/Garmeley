<p>Elementals are beings of living matter, fire, stone, water or storm that moves and acts with an awareness of its own, yet was never born of flesh. They are found in every corner of the world, feared in some lands and worshipped in others, and scholars count them among the oldest beings in existence: many hold that the first of them woke with the world itself, long before anything lived.</p> <h2>Nature</h2> <p>An elemental has no mind as mortals understand it. Those who have studied them note that they never hesitate and never doubt, but pursue their own nature without deviation; charms and threats find nothing in them to take hold of, and spirit-talkers report that they cast almost no presence beyond the <a href="The Veil" class="internal-link">Veil</a>. Each is bound to its substance. A stone elemental may endure for ages, while a fire elemental lives only as long as it has something to consume, and is never seen at rest.</p> <h2>Kinds of Elemental</h2> <p>The oldest surviving attempt to order the elements is the Key of <a href="Ptamon" class="internal-link">Ptamon</a>, which counts Fire, Wind, Water and Earth among its ten elements and joins them in a ring. Its fullest refinement came far to the south, in <a href="Kaito" class="internal-link">Kaito</a>, where the Yppori studied the elements like no other people, and above all the moments in which matter passes from one state into another. Their fascination with change would in time give rise to the faith of <a href="Sochor" class="internal-link">Sochor</a>, the Ashwalker between Inferno and Rebirth, which grew out of the older faith of <a href="Chenism" class="internal-link">Chenism</a>.</p> <p>In the 2nd century PA the channeller <a href="Thandar" class="internal-link">Ung Thandar</a> built on Ptamon's Key a model that maps every elemental state, set out in his Circle of Thandar. It holds four pure elements, Earth, Water, Wind and Fire, and six between them, each born where one element passes into another: magma between earth and water, storm between water and wind, lightning between wind and fire, frost between earth and wind, and two so rare that few have witnessed them, glass struck from stone by fire from the sky, and fire burning within water. Thandar held that the elementals of the between are the most violent of all, and his school, the Art of Change, is still taught across the lands of the Purple Sea.</p> <h2>The Mountain Gods</h2> <p>The dwarves and gnomes of the Underlands worship the <a href="Terem-Zal" class="internal-link">Terem-Zal</a> as their gods of stone, and the greatest dwarven cities are built in dead volcanoes where those gods are said to have fought in older days. The faithful hold their gods to be spirits, or something greater still. The southern canon, and a handful of northern scholars, hold instead that the Terem-Zal are elementals of earth of a might no other elemental approaches, a view the priesthoods consider close to heresy. Dwarven lore itself tells that the gods of stone shaped the ancestors of their people in the deep, from the <a href="Trog" class="internal-link">Trog</a> into the <a href="Dwarf" class="internal-link">dwarves</a> of today.</p> <p>Legends from <a href="Aisligos" class="internal-link">Aisligos</a> speak of an Age of Roil, when elementals as great as any mountain god warred with one another for untold ages and left the land unfit for life.</p> <h2>Bonded Elementals</h2> <p>Some elementals act in ways their nature cannot explain: they guard a shrine, keep a bargain, or linger by a grave. The yppori arcanist <a href="Szuan" class="internal-link">Tihir Szuan</a> explained these as elementals that a great will, a <a href="Greater Spirit" class="internal-link">spirit</a>, has attached itself to, the two living together as one being he called a Myan (see <a href="Myan-Bond" class="internal-link">Myan-Bond</a>).</p>
