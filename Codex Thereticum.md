Sing of Nissa, chieftain of the Roymer, in the days before maps were drawn.  
Iron was her will, but heavier still was the crown of her pride.  
She looked to the horizon not to greet the morning sun, but to count the shadows of thieves.  
For her hoard was vast, yet her heart was a cavern that silver could not fill.

To this hollow heart came Lempo, Father of all Moths.  
He brought no spears of bronze, for bronze meets bronze and shatters.  
He brought the silent flutter, the pale silk, the whispered half-truth she desired.  
He wrapped her eyes in a veil of wings and taught to her the Dance of Nissa.  
“Dance,” spoke the Feylord, “and see the hidden gold of the earth. Dance, and see the daggers in your brothers' hands.”

So Nissa danced to songs cruel and silent.
Through the moth-veil, kin became killers, and allies became ashes.  
She struck out to protect what was hers, and in her striking, tore her own house down.  
Blood watered the Roymer fields, and her clan was fractured on the altar of her paranoia.

From the deep earth came Kalvel, the wise Giant, friend to her father and mentor to her youth.  
He came to still her spinning feet, to shake the fey-madness from her brow.  
But through the fluttering deceit, Nissa saw no friend, only titan come to steal her gold.  
Her spear leapt. The giant fell.  
The earth groaned under the weight of murdered elder bones, it crumbled the last of Nissa’s halls.

The dust settled and with it moth-wings parted.  
Nissa looked upon her bloodied hands and the dying eyes of wise Kalvel.  
No curse did the giant speak, though his lifeblood soaked the soil.  
With a voice like grinding stones, he gave his final gift:  
“Illusion is a weed that feeds on the rich dirt of greed, little warrior.  
If you would tear the Moth from your mind, seek not the high peaks, but the deep root grasping tall.  
Seek the Crown of the World known only to the eldest druids of the lands.”

Nissa stripped the amber from her neck. She cast her silver into the river. She left her crown of iron in mud.  
Clad only in the heavy mantle of her grief, she walked into the deepest woods.  
Lempo followed, a swarm of whispers, promising resurrected halls and returned glory.  
He urged her to turn back, to claim her empty throne, his voice of silk and softest promise.  
But Nissa was emptied of greed, and the moths found no purchase on a hollowed heart.

For seven days and seven nights she walked, until the trees grew older than the names of gods.  
She found it at the center of the breathing earth what crowned the world of all. 
A sacred grove of ancient timber, where once the Aeldpriests first whispered to the leaves.  
Here, the magic of the land was pure, and the air was thick with ancient truth.  
The breath of the grove swept through the ancient boughs and tore the pale wings of Lempo’s deceit to shreds.  
Stripped of his shadows, the Father of Moths fell bare upon the moss.

In the Crown of the World, Nissa used no spear of ash, no blade of iron.  
She took the Feylord by the throat and bound him with sorrow, unbreakable and absolute.  
She threw him beneath the roots of the world, leaving her pride to rot beside him.

The Dancer returned to the valleys a woman without a clan, a queen without a crown, but she walked with clear eyes.  
And so it is sung to the children of Nostroya:  
When the Fey whisper lies of gold, leave your treasures in the mud, and seek the silent grove where truth takes root.
