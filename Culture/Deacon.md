<p>A Deacon is a person of trust chosen for his learning, and the word names two quite different offices: a rank of the Gethric clergy, and, among the Meadi and Weadi, a minister of the law who serves a noble lord.</p>  <p>The word comes from the Old Wermian "Dekian", which described a fast and thorough reader. It was taken up mainly by the <a href="Vihri" class="internal-link">Vihri</a> and by the <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethric</a> Church, which gave it the meaning best known across Inara. Other cultures, among them the Meadi and Weadi, took the word for an office of their own.</p> <h2>In the Church</h2> <p>In the Gethric clergy a Deacon is an ordained minister or father of the faith who works beside a bishop as his right hand. He is usually concerned with direct service, public outreach and the more direct protection of the faith within the ecclesiastical province. A bishop tends to have only one Deacon, chosen at his personal request, although smaller bishoprics, or those that work closely with an order of knights, may do without one and leave its duties to the Vicar. The ranks of the clergy are described on the page of the <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">Church of Iztaw</a>.</p> <h2>Among the Meadi and Weadi</h2> <p>Among the <a href="Meadi" class="internal-link">Meadi</a> and Weadi a Deacon is usually not a cleric at all, but a minister whom a noble lord, a lord castellan or a city council calls on for his expertise in a certain subject, and almost always that subject is the law. The office is often held only for a time. A Deacon is typically drawn from the nobles of the quill, the most educated and scholarly of the realm, but a liege lord wants more than learning, and the person chosen must also be politically loyal and trustworthy in his or her eyes. It is common procedure to call a Deacon when a nobleman dies in uncertain circumstances, when a large dispute over civil trade must be settled, or when a contract is to be read, and the word of a Deacon usually carries with it the implicit authority of the Duke or even the Monarch they serve.</p> <p>Most Deacons therefore handle the legal and more complicated business of a lord for as long as he needs them. In <a href="Giselstatt" class="internal-link">Giselstatt</a> the office is an exception, for the Deacon appointed by the Archduke of Gisvale is a permanent and more important officer who handles most civil and criminal matters of the city. In Zeeport, a free city, the Deacon is appointed by the Burgherrad and not by the Baron.</p> <p>The Crown has its own Deacon, the royal deacon, whom the <a href="Royal Congress" class="internal-link">Royal Congress</a> appoints. When a line of one of the great Houses of <a href="Meadrath" class="internal-link">Meadrath</a> ends, a royal deacon may be charged with finding the closest successor to its titles, although in other cases the nearest liege lord of the title decides whom to raise.</p> <p>The office is a distinctly Meadi institution of the law, and both of its meanings keep something of the old sense of the word, for the Deacon is the one who reads and knows.</p>
