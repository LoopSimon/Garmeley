<p>The Speculum Regni, the Mirror of the Realm, is the great codex of law of <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a>. It was compiled between 724 and 741PA by <a href="Radosław of Szievek" class="internal-link">Radosław of Szievek</a>, the Bishop of Szievek, on the joint commission of Ras <a href="Vormil I" class="internal-link">Vormil I</a> and the High-Bisop of Iztaw, and it gathers into one work the canon law of the <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">Church</a>, the rights of the crown, the customs of the Vadcy and the charters of the realm. It was the first time that the laws of Vihr were written down together, and it has been the common reference of every court in the realm ever since. It does not make the law of Vihr one law. It holds up a mirror to the many laws the realm already had, which is what its name was meant to say.</p>  
<h2>Background</h2>  
<p>The law of the old <a href="Vihrmen Federation" class="internal-link">Vihrmen Federation</a> was spoken and not written. Lords and elders judged by the Old Right, the custom of their people, feuds were ended with a blood price, and the most solemn oaths were sworn at the Ullkustone of Trydlew. The lords who submitted to Viago I kept that custom as the price of their loyalty. Ras Zervind I "the Eagle" reserved the Crown Pleas, the cases of blood, treason, coin and the highway, to the king and sent the <a href="Royal Judges of Vihr" class="internal-link">royal judges</a> to hear them, and the settlement of the Lords' Crisis in 384PA divided the rest of the justice of the realm between the Var, who judge their own lands, and the bishops' courts, which judge the clergy and the matters of faith, marriage, oaths and wills. Each court judged by its own law, and none of them had it in writing beside the others.</p>  
<p>By the early 8th century the realm had grown rich and crowded under the Ralpia, and the confusion had grown with it. Merchants trading across the Vadcy, lords disputing over inheritances and clerics quarrelling with lords over the reach of the bishops' courts found that nobody could say for certain what the law was. Vormil I, who had already sent his Kar-Zavr through the realm in the company of the Church, saw the codex as a work in the same spirit, one that would bind Crown and Church closer together, and the High-Bisop agreed to share in it.</p>  
<h2>Compilation</h2>  
<p>In 724PA the Ras and the High-Bisop jointly commissioned Radosław, the Bishop of Szievek and a master of its University, to compile the laws of the realm. Radosław took his method from Saint <a href="Palus" class="internal-link">Palus Aergestes</a> and the <a href="Wermian Accords" class="internal-link">Wermian Accords</a>. He did not write law himself, and he did not let the crown or the Church write it through him. Between 725 and 738PA he convened a congregation in every Vadcy, at which the Var, the Gri and the elders of the land declared their custom before him and his clerks, and he had their words written down as they spoke them. The lords were suspicious, for they had never let their prerogatives be put on parchment, and many feared that a written custom could be read against them. Radosław is said to have answered them that he held the mirror and did not paint the face.</p>  
<p>The work was done at Szievek, by Radosław and the clerks and masters of the University, and the Speculum Regni was completed in 741PA, a year before the death of Vormil I.</p>  
<h2>Contents</h2>  
<p>The Speculum is divided into four books. The Book of the Altar holds the canon law of the Church, drawn from the Institutiones Volboria and the decrees of the Conclaves, and sets out the courts of the bishops and the law of the clergy, of faith, of marriage, of oaths and of wills. The Book of the Crown holds the rights of the throne, the Crown Pleas, the settlement of 384PA and the duties of the realm under the Accords. The Book of the Lands holds the customs of the Vadcy, each written as its own lords declared it, so that the inheritance of a Gri in Rovgi and the blood price of a Sarloni in Breza stand in the same codex and say different things. Not every people spoke for itself. In Opuzna, and above all in its south, the Sarloni were spoken for by their Var, and their custom stands in the book as their lords declared it, while in Selbia, where the Sarloni have long lived on better terms with the crown and their Var, their elders came before the congregation and declared it themselves. The <a href="Wytlands" class="internal-link">Wytlands</a>, where no lord would answer for the land, held no congregation at all, and the blank they left in the Book of the Lands is one of the reasons the Wytlands are still counted lawless. The Book of Charters holds the charters of the crown, from the Charter of Pretko of 562PA to the grant of 716PA to the Table of Trazlov.</p>  
<h2>Use</h2>  
<p>The Speculum quickly became the reference of every court in Vihr, and copies were made for Zavlech, for Iztaw and for the hall of every Var. It did not bind the Var to anything they had not declared themselves, and in their own courts they have always held that their living custom stands above the words of the Book of the Lands. The royal judges and the Ministers of the Church, on the other hand, learned their law from it, and in their courts it came to have the force that the lords denied it in theirs.</p>  
<h3>The Pogroms</h3>  
<p>The clear procedure of the Book of the Altar proved its darkest worth after the Conclave of 763. When the <a href="Gethric Pogroms" class="internal-link">Gethric Pogroms</a> were ratified, the accused were tried in the bishops' courts under its chapters, and the Speculum made the work swift and orderly. Nidislaw the Keen, the youngest son of Vormil I and a student of Radosław, argued against the Pogroms with his teacher's book in his hand, and he is remembered for it, but he could not stop them. The Guest Charter of Szulbod of 778PA, which shut the bishops' courts out of that city, was in part an answer to what the Speculum had made possible.</p>  
<h3>The Jakubine Speculum</h3>  
<p>In 889PA Ras Jakub II "the Good" issued a revised edition, known as the Jakubine Speculum. It added a fifth book, the Statutes of Jakub, which founded the Royal Tribunal at Zavlech, seated a royal judge in the capital of every Vadcy and granted the Peasants' Plea, by which any free man or woman of the land may appeal the sentence of their lord to the royal judge. The <a href="Honoured Assembly" class="internal-link">Honoured Assembly</a> let it pass because the Book of the Lands and the courts of the Var were left untouched.</p>  
<h2>The Speculum Today</h2>  
<p>Radosław's original lies in the library of Szievek University, and the Jakubine Speculum is the edition read in every court of the realm. Royal judges and Ministers study it for years, and the lawyers of the Vadcy know their own Book of the Lands better than any other part of it. Reformers at court, the Kisras Waśko among them, say that Vihr will not be one realm until the Speculum is one law, and the Var answer that it was never meant to be.</p>
