<p>The Meadi peoples, together with their closely related Weadi cousins on the Gullsong Sea coast, are the inhabitants of <a href="Meadland" class="internal-link">Meadland</a>, named after the River Mead. In the tongue of the ancient tribes of the Age of the <a href="Auguri Dominion" class="internal-link">Auguri</a>, "Meadi" simply meant "Those who come from the west" or just "West", and "Weadi" in the same way "Men from the North". The Meadi make up one of the largest and most influential groupings of humans on Inara and are largely united under the Crown of <a href="Meadrath" class="internal-link">Meadrath</a> in the modern day.</p> <h2>The Meadi</h2> <p>The Meadi are less one people than a gradient, and the manners of Meadland change with the land. To the east, from Korsfall to the Bend of the Trinmo, speech and names have a harder sound and the noble houses are styled "von", like von Kor, von Praum and von Buhr. In the west, along the Mead, speech is softer and more courtly and the houses are styled "du", like du Jera, du Bregelle and du Chian. The Weadi beyond the Trinwood style themselves "van", as in Korman van Schipwig. These styles are customs and not laws, and houses on the borders between the regions keep whichever style their founder brought with him.</p> <h2>The Peoples</h2> <p>The Higher and Lower Meadi, together the Meadmer, live in the south and the north of western Meadland alongside the Mead itself, the Higher Meadi around <a href="Dirneux" class="internal-link">Dirneux</a> and the Lower Meadi in <a href="Morelle" class="internal-link">Morelle</a> and <a href="Mear" class="internal-link">Mear</a>. The Higher Meadi keep the memory of the Auguri closest and are known for their art and their old customs, while the Lower Meadi are a plain-spoken folk of farms and market towns, slow to change.</p> <p>The Rhilmer of central Meadland make up the largest culture group within the entire region. They are the people of the Trinmo and of the Crown lands around <a href="Korsfall" class="internal-link">Korsfall</a>, and the royal House and much of the court are Rhilmer. The Gismer of the Gisvale are named after the legendary hero <a href="Gismar" class="internal-link">Gismar</a>, who slew the <a href="Dragon" class="internal-link">Dragon Heximar</a> in elder days. They ruled their own kingdom in <a href="Gisvale" class="internal-link">Gisvale</a> until the Union of 664PA, and have not entirely forgotten it.</p> <p>The peaceful but largely isolationist Hundi live around the Whisperhills where the Trinmo finds its spring, and their dialect is still spoken at <a href="Zmalig" class="internal-link">Zmalig</a> on the northern coast. Last are the Greenfolk, the people of the Trinwood, the wild and ferocious inhabitants of its hills, still beholden to the ancient ways of the land.</p> <p>The Meadi are not alone in the west. In the far west of Ilrakhan live the Locni, who belong to the greater Othiri, Locni and Sertovi group of peoples and are no part of the Meadi. The Lower and Higher Meadi have traded, wed and fought with them since the days of the Auguri, most recently in the <a href="Meadi Othiri War" class="internal-link">Meadi-Othiri War</a>, but the Locni keep their own customs and their own tongue.</p> <h2>Language and Names</h2> <p>The language of the Crown, the courts and the Church is High Meadi, which grew out of the Old Meadi of the Auguri. Each people speaks it in its own way, and the dialects of the Gismer, the Lower Meadi and the Hundi differ from court speech in sound and in word.</p> <p>A Meadi name is usually built from a given name, one or two middle names handed down in the family, and the name of the house, as in Gisela Rianna Bring von Kor or Pavo Doerr von Alban. Commoners have no house and are known by a given name and a trade, a father or a place. The ruling houses return again and again to a small stock of names and number their bearers, so that Meadrath has had four queens named Gisela, and the people add epithets of their own, often only after a ruler's death, such as <a href="Torvald I" class="internal-link">Torvald the Tall</a>, <a href="Marlina I" class="internal-link">Marlina the White Lady</a> or Siegmar the last Tree. The full line can be found in the <a href="Rulers of the Meadi" class="internal-link">Rulers of the Meadi</a>.</p> <p>The Meadi give the great stars plain, hard names in High Meadi. <a href="Urden" class="internal-link">Urden</a> is Wirkstern, "Working-star", the red <a href="Karrod" class="internal-link">Karrod</a> is Rotbrand, "Red-Brand", <a href="Sarhen" class="internal-link">Sarhen</a> is Sonnwart, "Sun-warden", the North Star <a href="Malham" class="internal-link">Malham</a> is Polwacht, "Polewatch", the twin star <a href="Dyrrim" class="internal-link">Dyrrim</a> is Eidpaar, "Oath-Pair", and <a href="Tholmir" class="internal-link">Tholmir</a>, the guide of the southern seas, is Kielwacht, "Keelwatch". Their neighbours the Urien use older sounding names for the same stars, Aughral, Haldrath, Ernath, Nordhal, Brudrath and Suthal. Some of the lore around the stars is older than either tongue, for the Sages of the Auguri Dominion turned to Urden when they built their monopoly on magic, and the war leaders of the tribes watched Karrod.</p> <h2>Society</h2> <p>Meadi society in Meadrath is ordered into three estates, the Church, the nobility and the commons, who are all represented in the <a href="Royal Congress" class="internal-link">Royal Congress</a>. The crown passes to the eldest child regardless of sex, which has given the realm queens as well as kings. The great Houses marry into each other often, and many of these marriages are matrilineal, the heirs carrying the name of their mother.</p> <p>To keep the Houses from vanishing into each other, the Kingdom knows the institution of Congenitura, which is a cornerstone of the Union of Meadrath and of the understanding between the von Kor and the great Houses. An heir who inherits the throne gives up the primary title of their other parent, so that this title and its House pass to another child, or to a branch of the House, and keep their own name. The principle does not stop at the throne, for the Great Ducal Houses and their most powerful vassals practise it among themselves as well, while lesser titles may still pass to the eldest child. What passes to whom is set down in marriage contracts, the Scrolls of oath and blood, which the Church seals and which are among the most revered legal documents in Meadi culture. They name which child inherits what, and what is to happen in a childless marriage or one that brings only a single child, and the custom has trickled down in lesser forms to the other estates and the burghers. If a line ends, it ends, although in some cases a royal <a href="Deacon" class="internal-link">deacon</a>, appointed by the Royal Congress, is charged with finding the closest successor, and in others the nearest liege lord of the title decides whom to raise. Every monarch since the Union, beloved or hated, has upheld the principle, and it is often named as the reason that the high Houses of Meadrath are more cooperative and loyal to the Crown than the Var of <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a>.</p> <p>The Deacon is an office that the Meadi and Weadi lords have made their own. Where the Gethric Church means by the word a cleric at the right hand of a bishop, a Deacon in these lands is usually a minister whom a lord calls on for his expertise, almost always in the law, and often only for a time. Deacons are drawn from the nobles of the quill, the most learned of the realm, but a lord also wants someone politically loyal and trustworthy in his own eyes. A Deacon is called when a nobleman dies in uncertain circumstances, when a large dispute over civil trade must be settled, or when a contract is to be read, and the word of a Deacon usually carries the implicit authority of the Duke or even the Monarch they serve. The royal deacon is the Crown's own.</p> <p>In general the customs of Meadrath's lands are more egalitarian than those of most of Inara, but they are not the same everywhere. The Gismer are often seen as the most progressive of the Meadi, while the Higher Meadi usually come across as the most traditional, in their view of the sexes as in much else. In an agrarian society the countryside is also less progressive than the cities, and a village of the Trinwood or the Highlands will think differently from a guild hall of Giselstatt.</p> <h2>Faith and Custom</h2> <p>Today the Meadi are overwhelmingly followers of <a href="Trinicism" class="internal-link">Trinicism</a>, but traditions from the time before Gethricism survive all the same. The Green Week, a festival around midsummer, is one of them, and the old ways in general are stronger in the western lands than in the centre or the east. <a href="Augurism" class="internal-link">Augurism</a> lives on in small communities in the larger western cities, almost nothing is left of the <a href="Turvik Paganism" class="internal-link">Turvik</a> cults east of Korsfall, and various cults along the Trinmo still worship <a href="Melara" class="internal-link">Melara</a>, the Trinmother, as their patron and goddess. The Meadi have kept the Auguri reverence for scholars, physicians and mages, and learning is held in high regard from the court to the countryside.</p> <p>The Trinicist Churches of Meadland are decentralized and diverse. The bishops of Meadrath raise no tithe and live from donations and worldly business, and in Korsfall, which never officially converted, the kings took Gethric oaths only after their coronations.</p> <p>Festivals and holy days, celebrations and mournings differ between the peoples, between the regions and often between neighbouring towns and valleys. The Green Week is a quiet and courtly affair in Korsfall and a livelier one in the west, <a href="Giselstatt" class="internal-link">Giselstatt</a> opens the year with Goodmans March and thanks the harvest at First Harvest, and Union Day is kept in every town in its own way. The sky keeps the farmer's calendar. The year turns when Sarhen, the Sonnwart, first rises again before dawn after its weeks in the glare of the Sun, and when the red Rotbrand flares, lords and bishops alike speak of war and failed harvests.</p> <h2>Learning and Magic</h2> <p>Magic and learning have been extremely important to the Meadi since the days of the Augurs. The Sages built the Dominion on a monopoly of magic, and their rune stones and larger holy sites still stand across Meadland, among them the Stone of Burd in eastern Gisvale, raised around 69PA, on which the classic Auguri invocation is recorded (see <a href="Auguric Rite & Autonomous Spells" class="internal-link">Auguric Rite &amp; Autonomous Spells</a>). The Great Stones at Praum were torn down by Gethric zealots in the 6th century, but the reverence for scholars, physicians and mages outlasted the Dominion, and today it lives on in the nobles of the quill, in <a href="Korsfall College" class="internal-link">Korsfall College</a> and in the <a href="Wulfrick" class="internal-link">Wulfrick</a> Observatory.</p> <h2>Chivalric Culture</h2> <p>Around the 5th and 6th centuries a chivalric culture grew up among the knights and nobles of Meadland, and it still plays a major part in Meadi society today. It is a culture of vows and courtesy, of honour in word and deed, of heraldry, tournaments and loyalty to a lord, and it shapes how the nobility treat each other, their vassals and the Church. Its heroes are remembered in song, such as the Smaragd Knight, Count Adalbert von Lindsfall, whose deeds in the <a href="Meadi Othiri War" class="internal-link">Meadi-Othiri War</a> are still sung in Korsfall.</p> <h2>Among Themselves</h2> <p>Every people of Meadland has an opinion of the others. The Rhilmer are thought lordly, brutish and overly serious, and the Gismer arrogant and jovial. The Lower Meadi have an almost backwater reputation, as do the Hundi, who are said to go out on nights of the full moon to dance with gremlins and budnicks. The Higher Meadi are considered heathens by many, and artists and hedonists by others. These opinions are repeated more often with a smile than in earnest, but they colour how the Meadi deal with one another at court and in the market.</p> <h2>The Weadi</h2> <p>The Weadi, or Weadmer, are often seen as their own cultural group, largely because of their different albeit related language, their political independence from Meadrath and their more liberal and autonomous culture, beyond obvious historic differences and challenges. They count themselves among the Four Tribes of Wead, the Lotmer, Frijmer and Osmer, named after the Lot, the Frij and the Ostor, the three largest rivers dividing the flatlands of Weadland, and the Kranmer of the Moors, who are ruled by <a href="Lesmere" class="internal-link">Lesmere</a> as a Duchy under Meadrath. The first three are largely united within the loose political entity of the <a href="Blijgrat" class="internal-link">League of Blijgrat</a> in modern times. </p> <p>The Weadi live in the low and watery lands between the Trinwood and the sea, and they have shaped those lands with their own hands. With irrigation, ditches, dams and dikes they have drained fens, held back the tides and turned the rivers to their purposes, and their country is one of reclaimed fields, canals and windmills, with great hills here and there on which the oldest lords of the land once built their seats. The lords of old Weadland took the title of Highlord from these hills.</p> <p>The Lotmer of the north-west around <a href="Zeeport" class="internal-link">Zeeport</a> and the Lot, the Frijmer of the north-east around Hevn and the Frij, and the Osmer of the south-east around Schipwig and the Ostor differ in dialect and custom, but they share the Weadi tongue, which is related to High Meadi and easily told from it, and the custom of styling their houses "van". The Weadi are a people of burghers, traders and seafarers, proud of their liberty and wary of kings, and their cities are governed by councils, guilds and patrician families more than by lords. Most of them are Trinicists like the Meadi. For their older history see <a href="Weadland" class="internal-link">Weadland</a>.</p>
