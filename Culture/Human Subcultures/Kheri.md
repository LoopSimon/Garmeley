<p>The Kheri are the human peoples of the lands around Lake Kher, bound together by a shared language, the <a href="Kethricism" class="internal-link">kethric</a> faith, and a centuries old coexistence with the <a href="Dwarf" class="internal-link">dwarves</a> of Daban-Kol that has shaped their culture in ways that distinguish them from every other human people on the continent. Even though often treated as such, the Kheri are not a uniform people: the lake, the lowlands, the frontier and the coast have each produced distinct communities with their own characters..</p>

<h2>The High Kheri</h2>
<p>The High Kheri are the people of the lake shore itself, centred on the capital Kher and the communities that have grown along its western and northern banks across millennia. They are the political and cultural heartland of the <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Khatrym</a>, the group from which the ruling dynasty and the kethric Patriarchate both traditionally draw their identity. They are formal, proud, and deeply conscious of their role as the standard against which other Kheri measure themselves. The concentration of <a href="Dwarf" class="internal-link">Kheri dwarves</a> in and around Kher is higher than anywhere else in the lowland settlements, a consequence of centuries of commerce and intermarriage between the lake shore communities and the northern holds, and the High Kheri are the most thoroughly integrated of all the subcultures in their daily dealings with their dwarven neighbours.</p>

<h2>The Yevani</h2>
<p>The Yevani inhabit the western reaches of Kherova between the Yellow Moor and the border with <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Grevenna</a>, taking their name from the Yevan River where the coalition of Kheri city states first united in battle against the reani in 156PA. That foundational moment of Kheri history is a point of particular pride among the Yevani, who often call themselves as the people who began the process that eventually led to the unified Khatrym. They are the least urban of the Kheri subcultures, long accustomed to the swamps of the Yellow Moor.</p>

<h2>The Low Kheri</h2>
<p>The Low Kheri occupy the broad plains of central and southern Kherova between the lake and the Pilvian Gulf coast, the agricultural backbone of the Khatrym whose farmers and river communities feed the cities and supply the trade networks that connect Kherova to the wider world. Alongside the Yevani they are the least politically prominent, while their labour is essential and their contribution to Kherovan life fundamental, the great houses and the Patriarchate are all centred elsewhere, and the Low Kheri tend to relate to the institutions of the state as subjects rather than participants. Their kethric practice is characteristically private and devout, the internal vow tradition observed with particular strictness in communities where outward ceremony has always been less available.</p>

<h2>The Kulumites</h2>
<p>The Kulumites are the people of the southern coast around the Bay of Kulum, defined in the modern era almost entirely by the catastrophe of <a href="Ahrmiz Saceren" class="internal-link">Ahrmiz the Conjurer</a>'s rise and the destruction of old Kulum in the 9th century. The original Kulumite community was among the most commercially prosperous around the niryd Sea, controlling access to the Pilvian Gulf and the southern trade routes, and the loss of the city left a wound in Kulumite identity that New Kulum's rapid growth has not fully healed. Contemporary Kulumites carry a particular intensity in their hatred of arcane practice, even by Kherovan standards, born of a living cultural memory of what unchecked magical power did to their home. The Hunters of the Onyx Cult draw a disproportionate number of their most committed members from Kulumite families. Almost all Half-Orcs of the greater Kheri diaspora are Kulumites.</p>

<h2>The Ankheri</h2>
<p>The Ankheri are the people of the ancient eastern and southern lake shore around Kairos, whose city is believed to be one of the oldest continuously inhabited fortified settlements in the known world with founding traditions reaching back to the 13th century BA. This extraordinary antiquity gives the Ankheri a proud sense seniority, the root from which everything else grew, and their culture preserves traces of pre kethric spiritual practice more openly than most, the mountain villages south of the lake retaining forms of spirit worship that the Inquisition still attempts to eradicate. The Ankheri sit adjacent to the Daban-Kol approaches in the north and have long standing trade and cultural relationships with the dwarven communities of the northern holds, though their integration is of a different character than the High Kheri's and more traditional, rooted in specific craft and trade relationships rather than the cosmopolitan mixing of the capital.</p>

<h2>The Kurini</h2>
<p>The Kurini take their name from the Kurini Mountains that define their eastern horizon and are the frontier people of Kherova, occupying the contested ground between the lake basin and the Forbidden Lands beyond. A majority of <a href="Azuvke" class="internal-link">Azuvkes</a> population is of the kurini peoples. Fort Iblis and far Gisil are kurini strongholds, their castellans drawn from ancient noble houses that have garrisoned this frontier for generations. The Kurini are the among the most militarized of the Kheri subcultures, alongside the Ankheri, by necessity. Their land faces threats that the western communities know only through the reports of the <a href="Kherovan Inquisition" class="internal-link">Kherovan Inquisition</a>'s Tribunal of Nirydia, and their daily reality involves the management of dangers that the rest of Kherova prefers to regard as distant. They are less integrated with dwarven culture than the northern kheri, more insular, and regarded by the Inquisition as among the most reliable sources of recruits for its eastern frontier operations.</p>
