[[Ur]]
%%
<p>The people of Ur are, by rather lose definition, one of the largest cultural groups of humans on Inara, from the northern ports of Uhl to the delta of the Trinmo River at the Stormy Sea, they are largely united by a common lingual family and a complicated history of prospering kingdoms and broken crowns, nestled between the Kortopis in the West and the Trollbacks in the east. Similar to the Region of Ur itself, the Urien peoples derive their name after the Tribe of the Urna, roughly translating to River people, that used to reside in the west of the region now fully under the control of the Hobgoblins of <a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a>.</p>
<p>The Garmish people are the most culturally distinct of those under the greater urien Umbrella, owed to their long history of religious practices removed from old <a href="Turvik Paganism" class="internal-link">Turvik Paganism</a> and resistance to the gethric conversions of the Silver Age, they have long been united under the Riverkings of Garm and later the Duchy of <a href="Garmeley" class="internal-link">Garmeley</a> itself, always thought of as the rich jewel of Ur and one of the most culturally diverse stretches of land in Ilrakhan. The Ares people are found north of the garmish, around the Ares Highlands and the Eld Valley making up the primary populations of modern day <a href="Urbridge" class="internal-link">Urbridge</a> and <a href="Eldmar" class="internal-link">Eldmar</a>, while the old kerleans have been subsumed by Ares, Urna and Garmish influence over hundreds of years, their own sense of culture slowly fading in the Nied Valley, being replaced with the emergent Niedmen of the <a href="Trinvale" class="internal-link">Trinvale</a>, while true kerlean peoples can only be found in the southern wilderness. Lastly the north of Ur is made up of the Gim, centred around the Frogleaf Forest and the Mit and Gim Rivers, who are primarily found in <a href="Gelmsburg" class="internal-link">Turvik Paganism</a> as well as the Turvi peoples near the Bay of <a href="Uhl" class="internal-link">Uhl</a>.  </p>
<p>Urien culture is often associated with classical chivalry, the arts and a strong sense of independence. Theirs is a history of religious evolution but also one of unity, divide and rebirth nowhere better displayed than within the history of the lost Kingdom of <a href="Urland" class="internal-link">Urland</a> itself, that almost unified the entire Region and held it strong for hundreds of years. Cultural stereotypes within the region are strong, from the backwater reputation of the Ares and Kerleans, the humourless Turvi or the free-spirited and godless garmish peoples, strong lines are drawn between communities within Ur.</p>
