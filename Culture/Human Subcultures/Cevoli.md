<p>The Cevoli are the human peoples of the region of <a href="Cevolia" class="internal-link">Cevolia</a> and the western <a href="Sertova" class="internal-link">Sertovan</a> colony of <a href="Martella" class="internal-link">Martella</a>, a cultural family united by a common linguistic tradition, the legacy of the <a href="Cevolis" class="internal-link">Kingdom of Cevolis</a> and a shared faith in <a href="Trinicism" class="internal-link">Trinicism</a> that has expressed itself in the arts, architecture and intellectual life of the region more visibly and more extravagantly than in any other part of the world. From the printing houses of Albesan to the Velasangra Cathedral of Jideux, from the Duomo delle Lune in Molina to the fortress-monastery of Teggio, the Cevoli have built more in the name of their faith than they have perhaps believed in it, a distinction that the region's long tradition of underground Oresti revival, its secret philosophical societies and its celebrated poets have quietly insisted upon for centuries. The Patron Saint of all Cevolia is <a href="Luca" class="internal-link">Luca Cadente</a>.</p> <h2>The Levi</h2> <p>The Levi, the people of the Levo River valley and the Cevoli heartland, are the dominant cultural group of the region by population, political weight and self-perception. The cities of Molina, Battvia and Levoggo are among their cultural centres; the Cevoli Highlands and the broad agricultural plains of central Cevolia are their country. The Levi are the heirs of the kingdom that Lingolio built in 180PA, the people from whose ranks the House of Molina drew its kings across three centuries, and as such they have never entirely relinquished the assumption that their culture represents the standard against which other Cevoli variants are measured, a quality that the other inhabitants of the "Land of the Lions" find somewhere between mildly amusing and deeply irritating. Their Trinicism is practiced with the comfortable assurance of people who have lived surrounded by its art for so long that faith and aesthetic have become difficult to distinguish. The Levi produce the regions finest religious paintings and its most celebrated festival culture, and are capable of extraordinary religious devotion and serious religious hypocrisy within the same calendar year.</p> <h2>The Niardi</h2> <p>The Niardi take their name from an ancient tribe recorded in <a href="Orestis" class="internal-link">Oresti</a> sources as inhabiting the hills of eastern Cevolia long before the League's foundation, a people who left almost no record of their own but whose name the lives on. They are the oldest Cevoli in terms of continuous settlement, the group that has lived closest to the ruins of Orestis civilization for the longest time, and this proximity has given the Niardi a particular relationship with the ancient world that the western subgroups lack, part scholarly reverence, part folk tradition, and part the quiet persistence of old rites that Trinicist authorities have condemned but chosen not to examine too closely.</p> <h2>The Turpadori</h2> <p>The Turpadori are the people of the southwestern coast, the Servi Sea and the port approaches to the Smoking Sea, a maritime culture shaped by the outward-facing perspective of people who have always lived at the edge of the known world and looked beyond it. The Turpadori have produced more poets per capita than any other nations, a fact that the other cevoli acknowledge with gentle mockery, the Turpadori poet brooding over the infinite sea is a stock figure of Cevoli comedy. Their fatalism and melancholy are genuine, but rooted in a maritime culture that understands the Sea as something vast and indifferent to the human condition. The <a href="Cult of Vibattu" class="internal-link">Cult of Vibattu</a>, centred in the Diebri countryside north of Calmona finds its largest following here, for better or worse.</p> <h2>The Rialo</h2> <p>The Rialo are the people of the Rial River and the eastern Cevoli highlands and the original "Lionkings". The warlord tradition that eventually produced the conquering kings of <a href="Cevolis" class="internal-link">Cevolis</a> originated among the Rialo, whose forebears were the self proclaimed Lionlords of the eastern hills before the <a href="Orestis" class="internal-link">League of Orestis</a> halted their expansion south and drove them west. It was from exile in the central plains that their descendant tradition eventually turned around, absorbed the League's collapse and built the kingdom that bore the Lion across all of Cevolia. The Levi who live in the heartland those exiled warlords eventually dominated, tend to regard the Rialo as provincial, a condescension that the Rialo receive with a particularly annoyed patience. Their connection to the <a href="Cult of Tirea" class="internal-link">Cult of Tirea</a> is the strongest of any Cevoli subgroup, the Goddess of Harvest and the Wilds and the Tirean cult's ecstatic rites have persisted in Rialo highland communities through Gethric conversion, Trinicist dominance and Fellowship censure alike.</p> <h2>The Terragni</h2> <p>The Terragni are the people of the southern coast within the hills and dales, the city of <a href="Mindano" class="internal-link">Mindano</a>, the communities south of the Bay of Butera and along the lower coastal reaches toward the Deathcap Mire. They are the most culturally varied of the Cevoli subgroups, their coastal communities ranging from the ancient dignity of Mindano to the fishing villages and market towns of the Terragna littoral. The reputation that follows the Terragni across the rest of Cevolia is shaped almost entirely by <a href="Ligoggia" class="internal-link">Ligoggia</a>, whose criminal governance and liberal relationship with the concept of commercial ethics has given the entire southern coast a reputation for immorality and deceit that Mindanos Council of Contesti finds deeply offensive and that the farming communities of the Terragna interior find puzzling at best. The Terragni are largely simply people unaffected by the ongoings in the city of scoundrels, and most of them know not to worry about the slander of pompous northerners.</p> <h2>The Batturani</h2> <p>The Batturani are the people of the Bay of Butera in Albesan, Butera and the commercial communities of the bays northern shore. The printing houses, the Excavators Guild, the auction halls and the constant traffic of scholars, adventurers, merchants and opportunists from across the known world have produced a culture that is cosmopolitan, commercially fluent and genuinely indifferent to the question of where you come from as long as you can pay. The Batturani are not less Trinicist than other Cevoli  but their Trinicism coexists with a practical tolerance for other faiths, other peoples and other practices that the Levi find slightly vulgar and the Niardi find slightly alarming. The underground philosophical societies that have rediscovered Oresti rites and ancient mystery traditions for the educated elite are at their most elaborate and most fashionable in Albesans wealthy circles. The Batturani treat ancient religion as they treat everything else, as another commodity whose value lies in its rarity and its capacity to make an interesting evening.</p> <h2>The Marci</h2> <p>The Marci are the Cevoli of <a href="Martella" class="internal-link">Martella</a>, the people who followed the <a href="Cevolis" class="internal-link">old Kingdoms</a> western expansion through the <a href="Rogyn" class="internal-link">Forest of Rogyn</a> into <a href="Sertova" class="internal-link">Sertova</a> in the early 2nd and 3rd century PA and spent the following centuries developing in relative isolation from the mainland. They are recognizably Cevoli but the Forest of Rogyn as a physical and cultural boundary has given them a distinct character that mainland Cevoli register immediately upon meeting them. The Sertovan cultural influence, the dual practice of Trinicism alongside Nyrkalbinism, and their frontier traditions have made the Marci something that the other subgroups regard with a mixture of curiosity and mild condescension. They are interesting cousins who went west and came back speaking strangely. The Marci for their part tend to find the mainland Cevoli slightly insular and close-minded.</p>
