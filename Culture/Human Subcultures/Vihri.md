<p>The Vihri peoples are descendants of the old "Men of the Horn", the literal translation of Vihrmen and likely in reference to the Unicorns of the expanse. While all three groups share distinct lingual roots, they are usually broadly divided into the so called "Three old Families" of the Vihri, the Grachi and the Wermi peoples.</p>
<h2>The Vihri</h2>
<p>The largest of the Families, the Vihri or often called Vihrmen are those humans living in the rich lands east of the <a href="Trollbacks" class="internal-link">Trollback</a> mountains, and the vast majority of them live within the borders of the opulent and powerful <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Rascy zi Vihr</a>. They share the Vihri tongue and for the most part the Gethric faith, but they are not one people. Five peoples are counted among them: the High Vihri of the hills and moors of the west, the Low Vihri of the river plains, the Tloky of the Tlok Valley, the Wyt of the south and the Sarloni of the Expanse. The Low Vihri are by far the most numerous, followed by the High Vihri and the Sarloni, while the Tloky are fewer and the Wyt fewest of all. Each people speaks Vihri in its own way, but the court and the Church use High Vihri, which takes its name from the speech of the hill country, and Old Vihri, its older form, is still read at Mass.</p>  
<p>The High Vihri live in the Oathhills and on the moors west of the Tryd River, and they are administered for the most part in <a href="Rovgi" class="internal-link">Rovgi</a> and the inland parts of <a href="Torry" class="internal-link">Torry</a>. Their country was the old frontier with <a href="Wermia" class="internal-link">Wermia</a>, and its villages were built for defence long before there was a Vihr. In those frontier villages every household swore on the hilltop to hold its ground and to come when the horn of its neighbours sounded, and the Oathhills take their name from this hill oath, which the High Vihri still swear when they come of age or take up arms. They are mountaineers, great hunters and excellent archers, a hardy people even among the Vihri, famous for their stubbornness and their loyalty, and the Oathhills give Vihr more of its soldiers and knights than any other land. Most of them are devoutly Gethric, and the memory of the Incursions has left them with a deep distrust of wizards and of anything that comes from the Wermian side. For all their hardness they love their culture, and their summer dresses and their dances are known across the realm. They love to drink as well, and Szeni Schnapps is one of their most famous exports. Many of them have come down from the moors to work in <a href="Zavlech" class="internal-link">Zavlech</a>, where the Moor Quarter by the western gate is theirs.</p>  
<p>The Low Vihri are the greatest of the Vihri peoples and the most diverse. They live in the basin and farmland of the lower Tryd and Lehva rivers, where the crownlands around the capital of <a href="Zavlech" class="internal-link">Zavlech</a>, <a href="Iztaw" class="internal-link">Iztaw</a>, <a href="Szievek" class="internal-link">Szievek</a> and <a href="Trazlova" class="internal-link">Trazlova</a> rule, and the free cities of <a href="Pretko" class="internal-link">Pretko</a> and <a href="Szulbod" class="internal-link">Szulbod</a> are Low Vihri as well. They are the farmers of the open fields and the burghers, guildsmen and merchants of the river cities, most of the clergy and scholars of the realm come from among them, and both the House of Zav, the first royal house of Vihr, and the House of Ralpia that rules today are Low Vihri. Both High and Low Vihri fought in the Wermian Incursions under Viago of the Zav. Their speech is the closest to the High Vihri of the court, and the other peoples like to point out that the court tongue was named for the hills but is spoken best on the river. The Low Vihri tend to think of themselves simply as the Vihri, and of the rest of the realm as its provinces. Among the other peoples the Low Vihri of the coast and the rivers are stereotyped as arrogant, soft and pampered, while those living inland are associated with the classical "Vihri patriotism", a thinly veiled jab at the racist and ultratraditionalist tendencies found among them. They are known for their love of cheese, above all the Ippenvek and the Blue Tryd.</p>  
<p>The Tloky, a single one of whom is called a Tlok, live in the Tlok Valley in the west of the realm, under the mountains, and in the north-west of <a href="Selbia" class="internal-link">Selbia</a>, and their lands are administered in the Vadcy of <a href="Tloky" class="internal-link">Tloky</a>. Their capital Tlokyemy is among the oldest cities of the Vihrmen, and the Horn Lords of the <a href="Vihrmen Federation" class="internal-link">Vihrmen Federation</a> met there. The Tloky count themselves the heirs of that council, and they say that the realm was talked into being in their valley before the Zav fought it into being. The lords of the Tlok River were among the last to bow to the Zav, and were broken at the Battle of Tlok Valley in 246PA. The saints and heroes of the valley are warriors, foremost among them <a href="Tytus" class="internal-link">Tytus</a> the Unbound, who broke the undead host of Mendizaba there in 545PA with free dwarves fighting beside him. The Tloky have lived beside the dwarves of Xer-Kol for centuries, and many of them know some Dwarvish. The miners of the <a href="Silver Towns of Gleamig" class="internal-link">Silver Towns of Gleamig</a> are Tloky as well, and the Silver Charter of 591PA, which took the towns from the Var of Tloky, is still held in the valley as an injury to the whole people and not to the Var alone. Yet the Tloky are usually seen as the most jovial and hearty of the Vihri, a people who value good food, poetry and singing. "As lazy as a Tlok" is a common saying, and the Tlok ferryman asleep on his river ferry is a familiar image across the realm.</p>  
<p>The Wyt, sometimes called Wytmen, live in the south, in <a href="Opuzna" class="internal-link">Opuzna</a>, in the eastern hills of <a href="Levazci" class="internal-link">Levazci</a>, along the edge of the <a href="Ravenleaf Forest" class="internal-link">Ravenleaf Forest</a> and in the aptly named <a href="Wytlands" class="internal-link">Wytlands</a> to its north, their old country. They are the smallest of the Vihri peoples in number, though they are spread over a great deal of land. They cut timber and make charcoal along the forest, and they are known for their love of dance and for the Bolov, their stringed instrument. The old faith of <a href="Turvik Paganism" class="internal-link">Turv</a> has never quite left them, and among the Wyt it has blended with the Gethric faith, so that the saints are honoured much like the demigods of old, as children of the gods of the land. On the saints' days the Wyt dance at the edge of the forest in their honour, which Iztaw frowns upon and the Bishop of Opuzna looks away from. The rest of the realm sees them as old-fashioned, backwater and simpleminded people, while many Wyt, above all in the Wytlands, see the northern lords as the people who took their country. Their great pride is <a href="Zawis I" class="internal-link">Zawis I</a> Silverbeard, a Wyt lord of Nediv who became Ras of Vihr in 562PA.</p>  
<p>The Sarloni are the people of the Expanse, the great grasslands of the south where the unicorns still graze, and because the unicorns live among them the Sarloni claim to be the truest of the Men of the Horn. They make up most of the people of <a href="Breza" class="internal-link">Breza</a> and <a href="Selbia" class="internal-link">Selbia</a> and of the western plains of Levazci, and their old city of <a href="Trydlew" class="internal-link">Trydlew</a>, where the Ullkustone stands, is remembered as the oldest seat of all the Vihri. The golden fields of Breza are sometimes called the breadbasket of Vihr, and the Sarloni have a surprisingly good reputation as hardworking men. They are just as often associated with occult paganism, for little practical reason, since the vast majority of them are simply Gethric, though small communities, above all in the hills of Breza, have indeed kept to the old gods. They are known too for the ancient blood feuds within their communities and among their petty, landless nobles, some going back generations, and for being great lovers and dancers. Their most famous dance is the Sarzok, in which men and women dance in a circle, faster and faster, until only the last man whose legs have not given out is left standing.</p>  
<p>The Wyt and the Sarloni are distinct peoples, and they fought amongst themselves long before either of them fought the northern Vihri. Together they were the last to hold out against Viago I, until both were broken at the Battle of Glancz in 251PA. Most Sarloni of Vihr are ruled by lords who are not Sarloni themselves, such as the House of Dibrevi in Breza, and they have long been bitter about it. In 529PA a violent and bloody revolt of the people of the Expanse, Sarloni and Wyt alike, against their northern overlords in Breza, Opuzna and Selbia was put down, now known as the Sarloni Uprising. To the south-east live the southern Sarloni of <a href="Dalniz" class="internal-link">Dalniz</a>, the descendants of the Great Sarloni Migration that founded the Rascy zi <a href="Leok" class="internal-link">Leok</a>. The two were once one people, and both still speak Sarloni, a strong dialect of Vihri, but over the centuries the southern Sarloni have grown apart from those of the Expanse into a culture of their own, though the two still call each other cousins.</p>  
<p>Each of the Vihri peoples has its picture of the others. To the river folk the High Vihri are as stubborn as their sheep, and to the hill folk the Low Vihri are clerks and moneychangers. The Tloky are thought proud of their old council, the Wyt dancers and half heathens, and the Sarloni horse thieves and rebels. Yet all five agree that they are Vihri, and that the Grachi are not.</p>

<h2>The Grachi</h2>
<p>The Grachi are descendants of those vihri settlers that followed Fidor of Trazlov when he sailed the Iron Claw and steeped foot in the land of <a href="Nostroya" class="internal-link">Nostroya</a> in 124PA. The Grachi can easily be divided between those who found in the cities and towns of the nostroyan lowlands, organized in the <a href="Halgrach" class="internal-link">Rascy zi Halgrach</a> and later the <a href="Grachy" class="internal-link">Grachy Republic</a> and opposed to them the so called "Free people of the Hyll" or simply the Hyllmen, that reside in the passes and smaller communities of the Grachhyll. Albeit over centuries of urbanization and cultural development, the people of the lowlands have themselves grown somewhat apart between the eastern Grachi of the rural, wild and harsh east and the southern "Grachior" of the lands south of the Halgior River and the citizens of the Coastal ports and communities of the so called "old land".</p>
<h2>The Wermi</h2>
<p>The Wermi, a term derived from the legendary hero Wermaz, are the branch of the vihri with the oldest but also the most tragic documented history out of the three families. The <a href="Wermia" class="internal-link">Kingdom of Wermia</a>, before its fall and the two Incursions, was one of the oldest feudal realms in all of Ilrakhan, competing with the <a href="Auguri Dominion" class="internal-link">Auguri</a> and standing in exchange with <a href="Urien" class="internal-link">urien</a> and Dwarvish communities for centuries before the Vihrmen of the Expanse ever began to unite as tribal federations. In the modern day the Old Wermi are group of humans living under the administration of <a href="Nuv Werm" class="internal-link">Nuv Werm</a> and the <a href="Order of the Silver Wheel" class="internal-link">Order of the Silver Wheel</a> around the Neverrest Heights and east of the Weirwood. The Taarmen are often differentiated from these "Old" Wermi due to their live away from the organised administration of the eastern parts of Wermia, living primarily in and around the Wermian Moor and the Hagshorn. In recent times many of them have re-organized, largely due to investments from the City of <a href="Uhl" class="internal-link">Uhl</a>, in the Realm of <a href="Taar" class="internal-link">Taar</a> officially recognized by Ras Orian II of Vihr in 879PA. </p>
