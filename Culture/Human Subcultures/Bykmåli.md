<p>The Bykmåli are the native people of <a href="Trykkerog" class="internal-link">Trykkerog</a>, the large island north of the Torn Sea. They are commonly divided into four peoples, the Olbellinge of Melmers Bay, the Jueli of the Malksbay, the Skori of the western fjords and the Hevri of the northern wastes and mountains, who share one tongue in its many dialects and much of a common past, but who have grown far apart in their ways of life. A fifth people, the Nervei of the Giants Steps and the northern shores, live beside them and are often counted among the peoples of the island, although they are kin to the Sehvki of Nostroya rather than to the Bykmåli. The name Bykmåli literally means "inheritors of the land" in Bykmål.</p>
<p>In the lands south of the Torn Sea the Bykmåli are still often seen as a harsh and brutish people, a memory of their long history of raiding the southern coasts. The raids gave way to the empire building of the <a href="Baeling Empire" class="internal-link">Baeling Empire</a> and faded with the Gethric conversion that followed the voyages of Saint <a href="Melmer" class="internal-link">Melmer</a>, and the Bykmåli of today are farmers, fishers, sailors, traders and mercenaries as much as warriors. They are culturally closely tied to the <a href="Pehki" class="internal-link">Pehki</a> peoples of <a href="Nostroya" class="internal-link">Nostroya</a> across the Torn Sea, with whom they share many old traditions and most of their gods.</p>

<h2>Land and Livelihood</h2>
<p>The divisions between the Bykmåli owe more to the land than to blood. The mountains, swamps and fjords of the west and north have always limited farming, and among the Hevri and many of the Skori they fostered a society of independent chieftains and jarls ruling isolated valley, forest, swamp and mountain communities, each of which looked after itself. The flatter lands along the southern shores and great bays of central and eastern Trykkerog favoured a settled and growing farming culture instead. These lands took up the Gethric faith earlier, drew closer to the cultures of the south and gave rise to the strong crowns of <a href="Baeling" class="internal-link">Baeling</a> and <a href="Juelfluk" class="internal-link">Juelfluk</a>.</p>
<p>The influence of the lands beyond the Torn Sea fell on Trykkerog in the same pattern, each shore of the island taking after the shore that faces it. The great ports of the south, Trazlov, Uhl, Schipwig Hevn and <a href="Zeeport" class="internal-link">Zeeport</a>, became the models for the great cities of southern Trykkerog, <a href="Sallesand" class="internal-link">Sallesand</a>, <a href="Levangr" class="internal-link">Levangr</a>, Troese, <a href="Tversby" class="internal-link">Tversby</a> and Malksham. The Olbellinge have been deeply shaped by Urien merchants, the southern Jueli by the Vihri and the Pehki, and the southern Skori by the Weadi cities across the Bloody Strait, above all since the days of the Baeling Empire, which ruled lands on both sides of the sea.</p>

<h2>Peoples</h2>
<h3>Olbellinge</h3>
<p>The Olbellinge are the largest of the Bykmåli peoples. They live around Melmers Bay and in the farmland of Briland, and most of them are united under the crown of Baeling, whose House claims descent from the Seal Lords of Alsebyn. They were among the first to take up the Gethric faith, and their lands are the most thoroughly Gethric on the island. Long trade with Urien merchants has made them the most southern in manner of the Bykmåli, and their towns, Alsebyn, Rillberg, Kastock and Troese, are imposing by the standards of the island. Their dialect, Lower Bykmål, is the speech of the Baeling court and of the island's trade.</p>
<h3>Jueli</h3>
<p>The Jueli are the seafaring people of the Malksbay, the Giants Steps and the colony of <a href="Nilborg" class="internal-link">Nilborg</a> in northern Nirydia, and most of them live under the crown of Juelfluk. Their culture rests less on tribe than on region, and it is divided among the peoples of the Egillsbay, the Malksbay and the Synbay, the folk of the Ansrocks, the mountains that run south between the Malksbay and the Synbay, the even more isolated people of the Furkenheights and the people of the so called Green Lands, the forests of Vunrumur and the country below them down to Seldrland. Each region keeps its own networks of kin, trade and marriage, and each can be told apart by its pottery and the way it builds its houses. The southern Jueli show the strong influence of the Vihri and the Pehki, and all Jueli carry the sea law of the Malksbay wherever they sail.</p>
<h3>Skori</h3>
<p>The Skori are the people of the western fjords, of <a href="Vignasoya" class="internal-link">Vignasoya</a>, <a href="Rensköldsvik" class="internal-link">Rensköldsvik</a> and the <a href="Free Jarldoms" class="internal-link">Free Jarldoms</a>. They resisted the Gethric conversion and have kept the old faith of <a href="Forrvegur" class="internal-link">Forrvegur</a> more faithfully than any other Bykmåli, and the Olbellinge have long called them "wolfsmen", a name they have taken up with some pride. Their greatest achievement is their law. Long before the 5th century PA the Skori had developed a legal system of assemblies and lawspeakers that was strikingly democratic for its age, and it remains the heart of Skori life. The Skori of the south and those of the north are now almost two different peoples. The rural Skori of the north keep to their jarls, their thralls and their old gods, while the southern Skori of the Rensvik Bay and the Wolfsbay have been so deeply shaped by trade with the Weadi cities across the Bloody Strait that they are the most outward looking of all Bykmåli.</p>
<h3>Hevri</h3>
<p>The Hevri are the smallest and most scattered of the Bykmåli peoples. They live in isolated communities in the northern wastes, on the heights of Fagnirs Rest, in the Stormlands, in the valley of <a href="Hvirfill" class="internal-link">Hvirfill</a> and among the Vannsi of <a href="Zren" class="internal-link">Zren</a>, and they speak whichever dialect of Bykmål is spoken around them. Farming is hard in their lands, and they live by herding, hunting and trapping. Most Hevri keep the old gods, and the Hevri of Hvirfill share the valley and the veneration of its True Stones with the Firbolg.</p>

<h3>Nervei</h3>
<p>The Nervei are a people of the Giants Steps, the chain of rocky isles that leads east from Trykkerog towards the Waelajokull, and of the far north, where they live in extremely isolated fishing hamlets on the often frozen shores of the northern seas. They are not Bykmåli by descent or speech. Their tongue and customs are close to those of the Sehvki, the mountain tribes of the <a href="Pehki" class="internal-link">Pehki</a> in Nostroya, and the two peoples are thought to share a common origin, although how the Nervei came to the isles is not remembered by either. They live by fishing and by hunting seal and walrus, move their camps with the seasons and the ice, and cross the frozen land on sleds drawn by teams of dogs. They keep a faith of their own that stands closer to the <a href="Aeld Faith" class="internal-link">Aeld Faith</a> of the Pehki than to the Old Road of the Bykmåli.</p>
<p>Although the crown of <a href="Juelfluk" class="internal-link">Juelfluk</a> claims the Giants Steps, the Nervei have little to do with its lords and much to do with its sailors, who trade with them for furs, hides and ivory. They trade as readily with the Sehvki of the Waelajokull, with the Vannsi and humans of <a href="Zren" class="internal-link">Zren</a>, where they are always welcome, and even with the Aelfs of <a href="Skemfing" class="internal-link">Skemfing</a>, which few other peoples can claim.</p>
<h2>Language</h2>
<p>All Bykmåli speak Bykmål, a single tongue that has split into three main dialects. Upper Bykmål is spoken by the Skori and is the official language of Vignasoya and Rensköldsvik, Jueli Bykmål is spoken around the Malksbay and in Nilborg, and Lower Bykmål is the speech of the Olbellinge and of Baeling, Jallbreg and Grynborg. Speakers of the three can generally understand each other, although each finds the others' speech old fashioned, rough or affected.</p>

<h2>Society</h2>
<p>Bykmåli society has always been built on kin. A man of name, in the old saying, is a man who can call back to a named ancestor, and to be noble is above all to belong to a lineage whose founder is remembered. The custom reaches back to the clans and tribes of more than a thousand years ago, and although nobility today is bound up with the feudal service owed to the crowns, the great families of the island still carry the names of their founders, such as the Rensköld of the Rensvik Bay, the Roering of Skeppbirk and the Billstrom of Sallesand. Common folk are known by their own name and their father's or mother's, as in Nylldottr, daughter of Nyll, although lords of new standing often keep such names as well.</p>
<p>Among the Olbellinge and the Jueli the lords hold their lands from the crown and owe it service in war, and both Baeling and Juelfluk are ruled by strong monarchs. Among the Skori and the Hevri power lies with the jarls and chieftains of each fjord and valley, who rule with the consent of their free landholders. The Skori still keep thralls, a custom the Gethric realms of the island have long given up.</p>

<h2>Law and the Thing</h2>
<p>The Thing, the assembly of the free men of a district, is the oldest institution of the Bykmåli, and it survives above all among the Skori and the Hevri. At the Thing disputes are judged, laws are spoken and jarls are acknowledged, and its lawspeaker, who knows the law by heart and recites it before the assembly, is often as respected as the jarl himself. The greatest of these assemblies is the Great Thing of the Skori at Mannelik, which chooses the Korre of Vignasoya, and the Thing of the Rensvik Bay, the summer Thing of the Free Jarldoms on the Gotjebay and the Thing of the burghers of Levangr follow the same old form. Some scholars of the Bloody Strait have argued that the councils and assemblies of the Weadi cities owe as much to the example of the Skori as the southern Skori owe to the Weadi.</p>
<p>In the kingdoms of Baeling and Juelfluk the crown and its courts have long since taken the place of the Thing, which survives there only in the councils of villages and in old place names. Among the Jueli the sea law of the Malksbay, an ancient body of custom governing the shares of crews, salvage, wrecks and the rights of captains, carries as much weight as the law of the crown.</p>

<h2>The Sea</h2>
<p>The Bykmåli have always lived from the sea. Their ships once raided the coasts of the south so often that the Archdukes of Morelle founded the fortified port of Meadhavn in 399PA to hold them off, and the Baeling Empire was a realm held together by its fleets. Fishing, whaling and sealing still feed much of the island, and the Saltmother <a href="Nyllena" class="internal-link">Nyllena</a> is honoured by Gethric and old faith sailors alike. Bykmåli sailors fear few things at sea as much as the <a href="Unðimaðr" class="internal-link">Unðimaðr</a>, the being that waits beneath the keel. The Skori were the first to cross the North Tirean Ocean, reaching the Gerjodi Islands in 688PA, and the Jueli captains guard the knowledge of the ice of Henmars Passage as their greatest treasure.</p>
<p>Bykmåli fighting men have long served abroad. The mercenary companies of Rensköldsvik and the Free Jarldoms take service with princes in Meadland, Othirdon and beyond, and the warbands of Vignasoya sail to foreign wars as their forefathers once sailed to raid.</p>

<h2>Faith</h2>
<p>The great majority of the Bykmåli are Gethric. The faith is strongest in the coastal towns from Levangr to Juelfluk, the Archbishop of Trykkerog sits at Tversby, and Saint Melmer is the most important saint of all Bykmål peoples. In the west and the far north, and above all among the Skori and the Hevri, the old faith of <a href="Forrvegur" class="internal-link">Forrvegur</a> is still kept, and its sagas, chief among them the <a href="Fjorisjom Saga" class="internal-link">Fjorisjom Saga</a>, are told and loved by Gethric Bykmåli as well. Rensköldsvik, some towns of Vignasoya and Sallesand also have <a href="Trinicism" class="internal-link">Trinicist</a> minorities, brought by traders from Blijgrat and Meadrath.</p>

<h2>Bykmåli Abroad</h2>
<p>Bykmåli communities are found all around the Torn Sea and beyond it. The settlers of the Baeling age make up a substantial part of the people of <a href="Zmalig" class="internal-link">Zmalig</a> on the Bloody Strait, Olbellinge families still live in Zeeport, where the Skori remain little welcome, and the City of <a href="Jallbreg" class="internal-link">Jallbreg</a> on the Hagshorn was founded in 411PA by Bykmåli and Vihri merchants. Skori and Olbellinge sailors founded <a href="Grynborg" class="internal-link">Grynborg</a> on the Gerjodi Islands in 719PA, the Jueli have settled Nilborg across Henmars Passage, and Bykmåli traders can be met as far away as Mereskis on the Stormy Coast.</p>
