<p>The Gorati are a human people of eastern Nirydia whose homeland is the region of <a href="Gorat" class="internal-link">Gorat</a>, between the Limsul Peaks and the Gavivag Bay. They are known as a tall people, deeply religious and fiercely individualistic, among whom blood feuds and rivalries passed down through generations are common. With the exception of the Soki, nearly all Gorati are devout followers of <a href="Zuhurism" class="internal-link">Zuhurism</a>, and their high art of magic, the <a href="Veziva" class="internal-link">Veziva</a>, is among the oldest magical traditions of the continent. The Gorati were united only once, under the <a href="Zepria" class="internal-link">Zeprian Empire</a> from 491PA to 767PA, and today live in the Kingdoms of <a href="Plašato" class="internal-link">Plašato</a> and <a href="Amlija" class="internal-link">Amlija</a>, the Republic of <a href="Dišela" class="internal-link">Dišela</a>, the Duchy of <a href="Jateš" class="internal-link">Jateš</a>, the Principality of <a href="Ruginë" class="internal-link">Ruginë</a>, the city states of <a href="Sredit" class="internal-link">Sredit</a> and <a href="Bruša" class="internal-link">Bruša</a>, and in the southern lands of the <a href="Twintail Imperium" class="internal-link">Twintail Imperium</a>. They are divided into six peoples: the Zepri, the Buzinci, the Kraguvci, the Mustari, the Opmani and the Soki.</p>  
<h2>Peoples</h2>  
<h3>Zepri</h3>  
<p>The Zepri are the largest of the Gorati peoples and live mostly in Plašato, the most populous of the Gorati nations, where the old imperial capital of Plavigrad lies. As the people of the Zeprian Empire they are closely bound to royal and noble tradition and hold to the belief in Zuhur's lot, the place under the Sun Lord's gaze that is given to every person at birth and that each should follow. They are known as a militaristic people who raise large peasant armies, and as great builders, famed for their imposing architecture, their roads and works such as the aqueduct that carries water from Lake Višine to Plavigrad. The Zepri are also renowned songwriters and singers, who express worship, love and sorrow in ballads and choirs, and those among them gifted with magic are often associated with song. Their great houses, among them the Kupjin and the Marvi, have feuded with one another for generations under the oversight, and to the leisure, of the royal house. The Zepri regard themselves as the true rulers of all Gorat.</p>  
<h3>Buzinci</h3>  
<p>The Buzinci live mostly in Amlija, Dišela and Bruša, and take their name from the Buzin River, which runs from the Limsul Peaks through the Velvet Valleys to the ports of Amlija. They are a rich and open-minded trading people, often seen by others as arrogant and self-absorbed, and are known for their wines, the lords of the vineyards being called the winelords of the Buzin Vale. They are more tolerant towards the Bihra and the Mogyri than the other Gorati, and more drawn to the pursuit of the arcane and of science, yet remain deeply religious. Because Hastermon Temple lies in their lands, some Buzinci call themselves the first children of Zuhur.</p>  
<h3>Kraguvci</h3>  
<p>The Kraguvci take their name from the Kragu Valley and live almost entirely in Jateš. They regard themselves as the most refined of the Gorati and are famed for spirits distilled from wine and for their metalwork and crafts. Among them a sect of mages practises a form of the Veziva that binds magic and commands into metal, not unlike the golems of the west. The Kraguvci have been rivals of the Mustari to their north since ancient times, and mock them as rat lovers and tail lickers for their perceived submission to the Shirin of the Twintail Imperium.</p>  
<h3>Mustari</h3>  
<p>The Mustari take their name from the Mustar River, which divides the north of Gorat into the Gorati Highlands and the Mustar Peaks. Most of them live in Jateš, in the Free City of Sredit or in the southern lands of the Twintail Imperium. They are a jovial and festive people, known for their humour and their many regional feast days, and the carpets and rugs woven on the banks of the Mustar are traded throughout Nirydia. In turn they call the Kraguvci stonesmellers and kneelers for their perceived submission to the Zepri of Plašato, and the rivalry between the two peoples played its part in the secession of Sredit from Jateš in 880PA.</p>  
<h3>Opmani</h3>  
<p>The Opmani live in Ruginë, the Velvet Valleys and the southern Sunset Mountains. The Northern Opmani live mostly within Ruginë, which takes pride in never having been truly conquered, even in the days of the Zeprian Empire. The Southern Opmani live more isolated lives and are a frequent hazard to the caravans of the <a href="Velvet Caravan" class="internal-link">Velvet Road</a>. Theirs is a close and communal society built around family, and the Buzinci, whom the Opmani deeply hate, often regard them as lawless and uneducated. The hardiest of the Opmani communities in the southern peaks are among the few Gorati who have regular dealings with the Bihra of the Limsul Peaks.</p>  
<h3>Soki</h3>  
<p>The Soki are the wild people of the Limsul Peaks, living in the Soki Valley and around the shimmering lakes of the Kolrog Highlands. They are a tribal people and the least like any of the other Gorati, and many of them are <a href="Ral" class="internal-link">Ral</a> shifters said to take the forms of owls and falcons. Unlike the other Gorati, the Soki do not worship Pen Zuhur. They are sometimes associated with Ruginë, though they live largely outside the reach of any Gorati state.</p>  
<h2>Religion</h2>  
<p>Religion is of the utmost importance to the Gorati, who are among the most fervent followers of <a href="Zuhurism" class="internal-link">Zuhurism</a>, the monotheistic sun faith that arose among the Pagri across the Gavivag Bay. Hastermon Temple, one of the four great holy sites of the faith, lies in Amlija, where according to tradition the prophet Hastermon stayed awake for fourteen days and nights in the 4th century BA until he reached communion with Pen Zuhur, and his eyes opened as rays of light that blessed the hill forever. The priests and monks of Hastermon guard the site fiercely, and its highest-ranking members wear sun masks of brass and gold. Ritual blinding, practised by some members of the Pagri churches further north, is very uncommon among the Gorati.</p>  
<p>The Nyads of the Gorati Sea and the Gavivag Bay are held to be the daughters of the Sun Lord's dreams. To converse with a Nyad is a great honour, and the content of such a conversation may never be shared. Killing a Nyad or bringing misery upon one is a great taboo, and the curse of a Nyad appears in many folk tales as a moral punishment.</p>  
<h2>Magic</h2>  
<p>Among the Gorati magic is seen as a gift of Pen Zuhur, in particular among those born with supernatural abilities. Physical disfigurement, change or mutation, on the other hand, is taken as a sign of the god's disfavour, and many children born disfigured are left to die. The highest art of magic is the <a href="Veziva" class="internal-link">Veziva</a>, whose old oral traditions and circles predate the more scholarly study of magic of the present age, and which has since adapted to the cosmology of <a href="The Anak" class="internal-link">the Anak</a>. Its most important schools are the Five Towers of Rivno, in the capital of Plašato, and the Hall of Sunrise in Meveti in northern Amlija.</p>  
<p>Gorati mages favour magic that venerates Zuhur, above all the magic of fire, light and the sun, while flight, necromancy and spells of water, ice and cold are regarded as lesser arts. Divination is considered the highest gift among the Zuhuri priests and mages, but in Gorat it is almost entirely restricted to the shrines and temples of the Sunpriests. Mages are seen as ordained to serve, and while they commonly rise to high positions at courts and in the cities, they are expected to wield Zuhur's rays for the good of all Gorat. A noble heir who shows magical aptitude is traditionally disinherited and sent to a temple or a circle. The last ruler who wielded powerful magic, Radov the Silver, the last Emperor of Zepria, turned himself and nearly three dozen members of his family and court in the palace of Plavigrad into pure silver in a failed invocation of great magic, and he is remembered as a cruel tyrant and a warning against monarchs who wield magic.</p>  
<h2>Folklore</h2>  
<p>The great hero of the Gorati is Johin, who wielded the favour and light of Pen Zuhur and struck down the Nightbringer, a great beast, cutting off its wings of nightmare and dusk. He is usually depicted wielding a sword conjured from Zuhur's light, and his tales are thought to be one of the cultural foundations for the religious acceptance of magic among the Gorati. The Nightbringer is sometimes associated with the wingless dragon <a href="Noor-Rah" class="internal-link">Noor-Rah</a> of the Limsul Peaks, who is worshipped in <a href="Noorism" class="internal-link">Noorism</a>, honoured by the <a href="Karaluk" class="internal-link">Karaluk</a> as one of its divine beings within the greater faith of the <a href="Vas Ira" class="internal-link">Vas Ira</a>, and revered as the living god of the temple of <a href="Rha-Gis" class="internal-link">Rha-Gis</a>.</p>
