<p>The Yppori are the <a href="Human" class="internal-link">humans</a> of <a href="Tal" class="internal-link">Tal</a> and the Purple Sea, the people of the great realms that have risen and fallen in eastern <a href="Kaito" class="internal-link">Kaito</a> for two thousand years and more. They take their name from the <a href="Yppo" class="internal-link">Yppo</a>, the great running bird that they domesticated long ago, and nearly all of them follow <a href="Sochor" class="internal-link">Sochor</a>, the Ashen God of Judgement and Chance. The climate and geography of Kaito have given the Yppori darker skin, in shades that range from region to region, and they are not one people but several.</p> <h2>Peoples</h2> <p>The Talang live in the south east of the mainland, around the cities of Natein and Bangoy. They raised the greatest and the earliest of the cities of Tal, among them ancient Char, where Chagor took the Ivory Crown and which was razed in the sixth century PA, and the country takes its name from their language. West of the Talang, in the jungles where the Kingdom of <a href="Byamon" class="internal-link">Byamon</a> lies today, live the Al Ypp, the people of the Yppo Riders, whose name and that of the Yppo are one. Many Al Ypp also live in the jungles of western Kaito under the rule of the Safiri states.</p> <p>The Anpon live in the north of Tal, on the shores of the Kaitan Gulf. The name Rytoku is a generalisation of the island cultures of the Purple Sea, such as the people of <a href="Hanshi" class="internal-link">Hanshi</a> on the island of Ryto, seafarers whose homes lie among the straits and archipelagos east of the mainland.</p> <h2>Language and Names</h2> <p>Talang, the tongue of the earliest cities, is the official language of the Yppori states of the mainland and the language of law and learning, while the Al Ypp, the Anpon and the Rytoku keep their own speech beside it. An older form, ancient Yppori, survives in the liturgy of the <a href="Ashcult" class="internal-link">Ashcult</a> and in honorifics such as Athaw, "the greatest teacher", and Ngalas, "born of Fire". Yppori names are short, and a person of consequence often carries an epithet given by others, such as the Athaw, the Ngalas or the Hungering Wave.</p> <h2>Faith and Law</h2> <p>Few peoples let a single faith shape so much of daily life. Sochor is the god of judgement and chance, and the Yppori hold that Fate alone judges truly. In a dispute that neither law nor witnesses can settle, a coin is cast before a Judge of the faith, and an oath sworn on a coin is held to be the most binding there is. The Judges are the faith's wandering magistrates, Acolytes who have finished their pilgrimage, and a Judge of the Great Kiln is trusted in many places above the magistrate of a local lord. The two schools of the <a href="Great Kiln" class="internal-link">Great Kiln</a>, the Dogmatists and the Radicals, quarrel over how far Chance may overrule the written law, and their debate is followed far beyond the temple.</p> <p>Ash is part of everyday ritual. Sochor is the Ashwalker, the god who follows the Inferno of his brother Doi-tor and clears the ground for the Rebirth of his brother Mezhan, and the Yppori burn their dead and give the ash to the wind or the river so that it may be carried towards rebirth. The clerics rub ash on their skin, and the faithful mark their brows with it on days of judgement and at funerals.</p> <p>Before Sochor the Yppori followed Chenza, the Sunrider who rode the great elephant Parakar through the sky and carried the Sun, and the old faith lives on chiefly in the myth of Sochor, whose herald and father Chenza became (see <a href="Chenism" class="internal-link">Chenism</a>).</p> <h2>Learning and the Arts of Change</h2> <p>The Yppori study the <a href="Elemental" class="internal-link">elements</a> as no other people does, and above all the moments in which one state of matter passes into another. In the 2nd century PA <a href="Thandar" class="internal-link">Ung Thandar</a> set out the Circle of Thandar, with its four pure elements and the six that lie between them, and founded the Arts of Change, which are taught across the Purple Sea to this day. Centuries later Tihir <a href="Szuan" class="internal-link">Szuan</a>, raised among the Thandari Channelers of the island of Surma, added the <a href="Myan-Bond" class="internal-link">Myan-Bond</a>, the theory that a great will can live with an elemental as a single being. Scholars of the north have long called this faith dressed as scholarship, but in Tal it is simply what the learned know.</p> <h2>The Yppo and War</h2> <p>The Yppo is more than a beast of burden to the Yppori, for it gave them their name. The Yppo Riders of Byamon, the Al Ypp, are famed for their charges and their command of broken ground, and a rider is expected to know his bird as well as his sword. In the deep jungle the Yppo has few enemies, but breeders watch for the shadow of the <a href="Elik" class="internal-link">Elik</a>, which preys on the young.</p> <h2>The Sea and the World</h2> <p>The Yppori are also a people of the sea. The Purple Sea has joined their realms for as long as there have been realms, and its traders, sailors and pilgrims, many of them Rytoku, are found in the ports of the Smaragd Sea and the Niryd Sea. In 923PA the captain Armida Zheni sailed from Hanshi to find the New World of Sirenna, and the Yppori now count the southern continent among their ambitions. A few have made their names abroad. Cường Đức Lý left Kaito as a wanderer and rose to lead the Skyriders in <a href="Ikara" class="internal-link">Ikara</a>.</p> <h2>Realms and Memory</h2> <p>No single Yppori realm exists today, but the memory of one is strong. The <a href="Golden Empire" class="internal-link">Golden Empire</a> and the Purple Empire of <a href="Naigan" class="internal-link">Naigan</a> are remembered as the ages when Tal was whole, and older still are the <a href="Dynasties of Tal" class="internal-link">Dynasties of Tal</a>, whose Ivory Crown the rulers of Byamon hold today, and the Yppori Moons of Naigan's banner remain a common emblem. The states that followed each claim the inheritance in their own way: Byamon with its riders and its holy ground, <a href="Rikarma" class="internal-link">Rikarma</a> with the Head of Sochor on its banner, <a href="Soren Vin" class="internal-link">Soren Vin</a> with its Golden Elephant, the merchant city of <a href="Chezin" class="internal-link">Chezin</a>, and the Rytoku islanders of Hanshi, who sail farther than any of them.</p> <h2>Neighbours</h2> <p>The Yppori have long lived beside the <a href="Safir" class="internal-link">Safir</a>. Naigan conquered the Safiri cities of the western Antulun Bay, and in <a href="Koshtir" class="internal-link">Koshtir</a> Mirani Safir live under their own Great Sage within the borders of Tal. Beyond the Kaitan Gulf lies the Elbae Sultanate, whose <a href="Grand Ziflis Company" class="internal-link">Grand Ziflis Company</a> is the most important foreign trader in the Purple Sea, and beneath their land and sea the drow of <a href="Tur Filash" class="internal-link">Tur Filash</a> have known the Yppori since the early 6th century.</p>
