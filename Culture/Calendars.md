<p><i>Important Calendars of the known world</i></p> <h2>The Common Calendar</h2> <p>The Common Solar Calendar is accepted and used in the Subcontinent of Ilrakhan and dates its Year 0 to the Eruption of Al-Akka during the End times of the Rakhian Empire. It counts a Year as 400 Days, with 10 Months of 40 days every Year. The Common Calendar has no year 0 because the concept of zero was not well known in Ilrakhan when it was devised.</p> <h2>The Rakhian Calendar</h2> <p>The <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakhian</a> Calendar is no longer in wide use anywhere but remains relevant primarily in Academic Circles, as it is the key to every date in the records of Rakh. It is a calendar of <a href="Conflux" class="internal-link">Conflux</a> and not of the Sun or the moons. The Thaumaturges of Rakh could not count the many small Confluxes that <a href="Ulrike Metzen" class="internal-link">Ulrike Metzen</a> found later, but none of them could miss the crest of a strong era, the days on which the swell stood at its greatest and began to turn, and this crest the Rakhi called the Conflux. Each one began a new Year, and the Year is reckoned at 3104 Solar Days, the span between two Confluxes as it stands in the Alwazattar Tablets, created by <a href="Phiodales" class="internal-link">Phiodales</a> of Mizk in the 1st Century BA, from which most of our knowledge regarding the calendar stems. It dates its Year 0 to the founding of the Great City of Rakh in 881BA.</p> <p>The Rakhi believed that the Conflux returned on the day, and the search for the rule that would foretell it was the work of their Conflux Theory. The sky keeps no such rule. A cycle of the eras may run anywhere from eight to thirteen years, and the Conflux came early or late as the sky gave it, so that the 3104 days were the Rakhi's expectation and not the length of the Years they lived. The priests proclaimed each Conflux when their Thaumaturges felt it, and the levies, campaigns and great rites of the Empire were made to wait upon the proclamation (see <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakh</a>). When Rakh fell in 0PA the proclamations ended, and today a Rakhian date can be turned into a Common one only where the Tablets or another record fix it to a known day. Set against the tables of Ulrike Metzen, the Tablets show how long each cycle of their time really ran (see <a href="Conflux" class="internal-link">Conflux</a> and <a href="History of Astronomy" class="internal-link">History of Astronomy</a>).</p> <h2>The Elven Calendar</h2> <p>Also known as the Elbae Calendar, it is a fixed Calendar Dividing the Year into 400 days, consisting of 4 seasons of 90 days and an intercalary month of 40 days that are treated as outside the year proper. Current understanding of the earliest development of the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae</a> Calendar is unclear but likely relates to discoveries regarding the heliacal rising of the star <a href="Sarhen" class="internal-link">Sarhen</a> during the early first Jirahid Dynasty: the intercalary month matches the roughly forty days in which the star lies hidden in the Sun's glare, and the year proper begins when it is first seen again at dawn. Elven tradition ascribes those discoveries to the astronomer Merenkhar of Farzirash, now Jiravid (see <a href="History of Astronomy" class="internal-link">History of Astronomy</a>). The four seasons are equal by convention and not by the sky, whose halves of the year between the equinoxes run about 187 and 213 days (see <a href="The Sun" class="internal-link">The Sun</a>), a difference the elven astronomers had measured long before and the calendar never took up.</p> <p>It is Commonly used in lands on the Beheran Subcontinent.</p> <h2>The Alder Calendar</h2> <p>Usually called the Alder Calendar, this is a lunar calendar that bases each month on a full cycle of the Moon's phases, lunation, without regard for the solar year. Its current year 0 is dated to approximately 819PA. Notably the Calendar is "reset" during times of great importance by the Narzoi, while other cultures such as the pharang tend to keep to the "true calendar", that dates the year 0 to approximately 4720BA, making it the oldest kept calendar in the known world.</p> <p>The Alder Calendar observes Luna Major, whose phases run through a full cycle roughly every 39.5 days, as such the inaran calendars that date a month to exactly 40 days tend to drift away from the Alder Calendar. Many peoples also count the round of Luna Minor, the small moon that circles Luna Major, as three days, three, three, three and four, so that five rounds fill sixteen days, and the Zolraii of the <a href="Path of the Second Moon" class="internal-link">Path of the Second Moon</a> keep their feasts by it (see <a href="Luna Minor" class="internal-link">Luna Minor</a>).</p>
