<p>The Art of the Soulbond, often simply called the Soulbond, is a forbidden practice of <a href="Bihra Shamanism" class="internal-link">Bihra Shamanism</a> in which a shaman consumes the spiritual shadow and presence of a living being, draining its life force to sustain their own. Scholars count it among the most extreme forms of <a href="Weaving" class="internal-link">Weaving</a>. Its founding is attributed to Volys the Spiritdrinker, the shaman who united the <a href="Vol" class="internal-link">Empire of Vol</a> in the 5th century BA. According to myth Volys consumed the souls of captives and defeated opponents and through them lived for more than 250 years.</p>  
<p>The Soulbond was hunted throughout the many occupations of the southern lands by the <a href="Great Khaganates of the Steppe" class="internal-link">Khaganates of the Steppe</a>, whose khans held it to be antithetical to everything they believed in. Where the steppe Bihra sought to prove spiritual superiority through triumph of the physical, the Soulbond was seen as a perverse destruction of the physical through theft of the spiritual, and many of its practitioners were put to death. After his victory over the <a href="Luchun Khaganate" class="internal-link">Luchun Khaganate</a> at Verivale in 671PA, <a href="Gaden" class="internal-link">Gaden the Golden</a> reinforced the taboo throughout the southern protectorate he established, which broke away in the turmoil following his death in 743PA and became the independent <a href="Kareli Hierarchy" class="internal-link">Kareli Hierarchy</a> in 752PA, and the practice remains forbidden throughout the region to this day.</p>  
<p>Despite the taboo the Soulbond is still practised in secret.It survives above all in the cursed forest of the <a href="Blodrise Dark" class="internal-link">Blodrise Dark</a>, among the moor communities of the Citrine Swamp and in small occultist sects within the larger cities, although almost every major city of the region forbids it on pain of death.</p>
