<p>The Veziva is the high art of the <a href="Gorati" class="internal-link">Gorati</a>, the old mountain people of <a href="Nirydia" class="internal-link">Nirydia</a>, and in their own eyes the greatest expression of magic there is. Its practitioners, the Vezi, bind a version of reality they wish to be real into a vessel, and later release it onto the essence of the world around them, dragging, as they say, a new world on top of the old.</p> <h2>The Art</h2> <p>A working of the Veziva has two halves, the binding and the release. In the binding, the Vezi imagine the world as they would have it, a frozen pass in bloom, a wound unmade, a fortress fallen, and bind that image into a vessel. Most often the vessel is an urn, a bottle, a small container or an amphora; rarer Vezi bind into rings, weapons or clothing, and the rarest of all into their own bodies, a practice the Gorati regard with equal awe and dread. A binding may take days or whole seasons, for the Vezi hold that a vision must be complete before it is bound. In the release the vessel is opened, broken or spoken over, and its stored world falls upon the world around it.</p> <p>The outcome is said to rest on two things alone: the strength of the Vezi's imagination, and the will to inflict it upon the world. A great Vezi's world takes hold. A lesser one's takes hold only in part, leaving the land half one thing and half another. And a failed release is among the most feared sights in <a href="Gorat" class="internal-link">Gorat</a>, for the world, the Vezi say, does not take kindly to being overwritten, and sometimes overwrites the Vezi instead.</p> <h2>Under the Anak</h2> <p>The Gorati understand the Veziva within the greater teaching of <a href="The Anak" class="internal-link">the Anak</a>. In its terms, the Vezi reach into the untouched essence of the Lands Beyond, give a world that has never had time a vessel to rest in, and release it into the Lands of Time, where the null point grants it the time to become real. The devout among them, faithful of Pen-Zuhur (see <a href="Zuhurism" class="internal-link">Zuhurism</a>), speak of the release as letting the god's light fall upon a world that had waited in the dark.</p> <h2>The Vezi and their People</h2> <p>No art could suit the Gorati better. A fiercely individualistic people who prize expression and freedom above almost everything, they teach the Veziva not in schools but from master to apprentice, and every Vezi's world is held to be their own: to copy another's vision is shameful, and the vessels of famous Vezi are kept and spoken of as signed works. Many of the greatest bindings are of the mountains themselves, the harsh homeland as its people love it, and the Gorati say, half in jest, that the land answers no one so well as a Vezi who dreams of it.</p>
