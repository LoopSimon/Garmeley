<p>The Veil is the common name for whatever separates, depending on who is asked, the planes from one another, the material world from the spiritual, the living from the dead, or the waking mind from what lies beneath it. Few words are used so widely in arcane and religious life and agreed upon so little. Scholars, priests and common folk use it for several things at once, and whether these are one veil or many is itself disputed. It gives its name to the <a href="The Veilcurse" class="internal-link">Veilcurse</a>, the epithet of the gethric saint <a href="Tronos" class="internal-link">Tronos</a> and a holy forest of the north, and it sits at the centre of <a href="Acceleration Theory" class="internal-link">Acceleration Theory</a>, the most feared idea in modern arcane scholarship.</p> <h2>In Faith and Folk Belief</h2> <p>Long before the academies took up the word, the belief that something is drawn between the world and what lies beyond it was among the most common in the known world. Faiths that agree on little else describe it in a handful of recurring shapes: a wall, a cloth, a seal, a sleep. Scholars of religion have long noted the pattern, though they differ on whether it remembers something real or only reflects how mortals have always imagined their gods, near but out of reach.</p> <p>The oldest image is the wall. The tribes of central and eastern Sirenna who worship <a href="Darmiga" class="internal-link">Darmiga</a> call her Avatars "the goddess's fingers beyond the great wall", reaching through what the goddess herself does not cross. In <a href="Kalbinism" class="internal-link">Kalbinism</a>, when the Creator gave Saint Kalbin his vision in the Hour of Revelation, he is said to have "torn down the walls of heart and mind", a wall that stands within the believer rather than at the edge of the world, and the Mural theorists of Korsfall have not failed to notice the phrase. The Pagri priests of <a href="Zuhurism" class="internal-link">Zuhurism</a> choose cloth instead, sometimes portraying Pen-Zuhur obscured by a carpet of silk and shade, the god set apart from his worshippers until he emerges from the Sun in the final days.</p> <p>Elsewhere the boundary is a seal or a sleep. The <a href="Kindly Lords" class="internal-link">Kindly Lords</a> of Garmish and Reani worship are said to "walk among us in their domains, wrought in Seals of Earth and Water", present only where the land itself holds them. The Listeners of <a href="Singer Worship" class="internal-link">Singer Worship</a> believe that all the living lie in a slumber and collective dream from which the Singer is trying to wake them, and that whoever dies before waking is lost to the true world for ever.</p> <p>Most widespread of all is the Veil between the living and the dead. <a href="Tronos" class="internal-link">Saint Tronos of the Veil</a>, the urien arcanist who sought to commune with spirits and the dead, carries it in his epithet, and his writings on the soul were thought dangerous enough to be rescinded as the Apocryphae. Among the Pehki of the <a href="Aeldcult" class="internal-link">Aeldcult</a>, the holiest forest is called the Aeldveil, and it is still common to send elders ready for death out "into the veil" and to bury the fallen there. Across most faiths the Veil is something to be respected rather than studied, and those who go looking for its edges are rarely trusted.</p> <h2>In Arcane Scholarship</h2> <h4>A Veil between the Planes</h4> <p>To Accelerationists, the Veil lies between our plane and the planes beyond, held together only by the fabric of matter itself. Every Fluxbridge opened is said to corrode it, letting more energy through, raising more trueborn casters and corroding it faster still, until one day it gives way. In its hardline forms the idea drifts into talk of a Great Convergence or a Third Incursion.</p> <h4>A Veil between the Realms</h4> <p>Planar theorists have more often placed the Veil between the material and spiritual realms. <p>Planar theorists have more often placed the Veil between the material and spiritual realms. <a href="Zovac Harlini" class="internal-link">Zovac Harlini</a> described that boundary as a space of its own, through which energy ebbs and flows in both directions, rather than a wall, and in which the <a href="Dulmic Gate" class="internal-link">Dulmic Gates</a> open. Modan Krindor's <a href="Realmatic Nativism" class="internal-link">Realmatic Nativism</a> went further in its much maligned principle of Inverse Projection, holding that the division of the realms is itself a spell, cast without knowing by every living mind, and the largest ever wrought.</p> <h4>A Wall Within</h4> <p>The most recent view comes from <a href="Korsfall College" class="internal-link">Korsfall College</a>. Its <a href="Mural Theory" class="internal-link">Mural Theory</a>, drawn from years of studying Veilcurse patients, holds that the Veil is not between planes or realms at all but a wall inside each person's own mind, and that the Veilcurse is that wall breaking. Older observations lend it some weight: <a href="Avero" class="internal-link">Avero of the Eightfolds</a> recorded that leynodes fluctuate differently around trueborn casters than around learned ones, as though the Veil did not lie equally thin for everyone.</p>  <p>The secretive <a href="Scions of Markur" class="internal-link">Scions of Markur</a> are said to hold, after the prophecies of their founder <a href="Markur" class="internal-link">Saint Markur</a>, that only a thin membrane of divinity hangs between the world and apocalypse. Whether they mean the same Veil as the academies is unknown, as is nearly everything else about them.</p> <h2>The Veil Today</h2> <p>Whatever the Veil is, a small number of scholars now fear it is weakening. Foremost among them is <a href="Rigoran" class="internal-link">Boris Rigoran</a>, Archmage of Edessa Tower, who points to the rising aptitude recorded in parts of the Royal Aptitude Census of Vihr and to <a href="The Thinning" class="internal-link">the Thinning</a> of the Nurdjinn in the Wasteland of Rakh. His warnings have found little hearing at Korsfall College, and most of the academy still regards talk of a failing Veil as the province of doomsayers.</p>
