<p>A sigil, often called a seal or a ward, describes an autonomous spell with a single dedicated function that can vary in its complexity. The Term Sigil was first used by the Augurs of the old Dominion, but the practice can likely be traced back further with various arcanists arriving at its most broadest building blocks separately across history.</p>

<p>A Ward is usually describing a Sigil that protects an object or organism from something, and can take a numerous amount of forms, the classical alarm spell as often employed by wandering caravans via scrolls is a well known application of a temporary and simple ward, that sets a single base condition with a single base reaction if activated.</p>
<p>Seals tend to be of more complicated nature, often lock and barring enchantments take this form, preventing anyone without conditions set before to access an object, but seals can also be placed upon living creatures, a rare and often forbidden practice, that can take the shape of imprisoning spells, enslavement seals or even memory influencing enchantments.</p>


<p>There are thousands of theoretical or extremely obscure variants of this basic order of spells, some aim to weave highly complex conditions and prism structures for a single purpose, such as the occult <a href="Seal of Epios" class="internal-link">Seal of Epios</a>, others are applied broadly on other organisms, binding them to the will of an extraordinary Mage, like the Volanti Runes of <a href="Sihilixar" class="internal-link">Sihilixar</a>. Advancements and discoveries, such as the <a href="Parneu Field" class="internal-link">Parneu Field</a>, have let modern day academics make strides in the complexity and autonomous nature of the Sigil.</p>
