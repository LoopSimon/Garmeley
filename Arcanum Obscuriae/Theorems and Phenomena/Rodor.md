<p>The Rodor are magically gifted individuals among the <a href="Shirin" class="internal-link">Shirin</a> whose powers take on a personality of their own, one that mirrors and answers the person who wields them. Although outsiders often describe the phenomenon as a spell or a possessing spirit, it is not an autonomous magic but a true illness of the mind, akin to an extremely heightened form of schizophrenia, in which the gift and the voice that accompanies it become impossible to separate. Most Rodor eventually go mad. Within the <a href="Twintail Imperium" class="internal-link">Twintail Imperium</a> those in whom the condition is discovered are taken into the care of the <a href="Penitent Ward" class="internal-link">Penitent Ward</a> of the Nan of Rites, which houses, studies and watches over them for the rest of their lives.</p>  
  
<h2>Nature of the Condition</h2>  
<p>The condition can manifest at very different stages of a Shirin's life. Some Rodor show its first signs in childhood, others only in adulthood after years of apparently ordinary magical practice, and in some it surfaces suddenly after a long period in which the second personality was present but hidden. In every recorded case the personality that forms around the gift reflects its bearer, sharing their memories, their voice and their manner, but speaking, judging and wanting as if it were a separate person. Rodor are known to address this other self aloud, to argue with it and to defer to it, and as the condition advances the boundary between the two grows thinner until the bearer can no longer tell which of them is speaking.</p>  
<p>The powers of the Rodor are considered considerable, which is one reason the Imperium has never treated them simply as madmen. Scholars observing them under the Penitent Ward have recorded that the gift tends to grow stronger as the second personality grows more distinct, so that the most powerful Rodor are usually also those furthest gone.</p>  
  
<h2>In Lakalist Belief</h2>  
<p>In <a href="Lakalism" class="internal-link">Lakalist</a> teaching the Rodor are a gift of Lakal, described in the formula "two made one, one made in two". Their divided self is understood as a living image of the paired correspondence that runs through all of Lakal's creation, the same principle that the faith sees in the twinned tail of the imperial line. This reverence sits uneasily beside the reality of the condition, and the Imperium's answer to that tension has been to honour the Rodor while keeping them enclosed. Shirin who are found to be Rodor are "protected" in enclosed houses maintained by the Penitent Ward, where they are cared for, observed by scholars and kept from public life, an arrangement that the Nan of Rites presents as the safeguarding of a sacred gift and that critics, particularly in Zuhuri <a href="Vigzil" class="internal-link">Vigzil</a>, describe as imprisonment.</p>  
  
<h2>Tonzuen of Rezizer</h2>  
<p>Before the 7th century PA no single institution of the Imperium held authority over the Rodor. In 674PA the Rodor Tonzuen broke out and went on a killing spree in the great city of Rezizer, and the inquiries that followed found that the competing interventions of several government institutions, each claiming a part in his care or control, had brought about his outbreak. The Liandar was blamed at first and its alchemists persecuted by agents of the Imperial Secretary, until the investigations revealed the part the other offices had played. In response Imperator Yoroy III founded the Penitent Ward in 675PA and gave it sole jurisdiction over the Rodor.</p>  
  
<h2>Yoroy XV</h2>  
<p>The most famous Rodor in Shirin history is Imperator Yoroy XV, known as the Mad, who reigned from 912PA to 926PA. He had shown signs of instability as a prince, a secret the Imperial Palace guarded closely, and in the tenth year of his reign his condition broke into the open. He began giving orders to himself and speaking with his other self before the court, demanded that servants and courtiers address "both Imperators", and planned the construction of a second palace. In the culmination of his madness he attempted to divide his empress Lon-Zen into two, one for each of his halves, and had her cleaved in two before his eyes. By 925PA he was almost never seen outside his private quarters, and his death at the age of 39 in 926PA was received with relief. Rumours that he still lives, entombed somewhere within the palace, have circulated ever since the accession of his son Yoroy XVI. His reign was the first time a Rodor had sat on the throne, and it has made the Penitent Ward's watch over the condition a matter of the highest political sensitivity.</p>
