<p>The Great Index describes a thought experiment turned real policy, it is also known as the Absolute Name Theorem or the Form-Fix Hypothesis. This practice goes back over a millennia ago to the School of Tarsus in ancient <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakh</a>, where the mighty Thaumaturge Hissir is credited with its invention, the so called Hissiri columns were a real attempt to find a single true name for all emotions, concepts, objects and assumptions. </p>
<p>At its core the central assumption that is that if names fix form, a fully named world can’t unravel. The namers of the Index set out to register one true name for every river, hill and hearth.</p>
<p>Houndreds of years after the fall of Rakh, the <a href="cevoli" class="internal-link">cevoli</a> Arcanist Diago di Locci began actively sponsoring expeditions of the buteran guild to venture into Tarsus and find records of Hissirs findings and theorems. In 664PA, he published his treaty "The Great Index" to mixed success at first, however almost two decades after do Loccis own death, Prince Arno di Battvia, ruler of <a href="Battvia" class="internal-link">Battvia</a> instituted real policy to create the Great Index in his name. Arnos decree even went so far as outlawing false names with punishments of fines or chains attached and culminated in the infamous effort to invade the Forests of <a href="Rogyn" class="internal-link">Rogyn</a> and steal the <a href="Minruism" class="internal-link">Minruisim</a> Book of Names. Arnos death spelled the end for the royal registrar efforts and di Loccis Theory of a fully named world has not found much ground in modern arcane theory.</p>
