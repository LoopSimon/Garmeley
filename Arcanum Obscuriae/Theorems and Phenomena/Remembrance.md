<p>Remembrance is a religious concept derived from the old <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethric</a> Rite of Remembrance. Its origins lie in some of the earliest records of the White Book of Saint <a href="Razmyr" class="internal-link">Razmyr</a> and in musings that are attributed to him but are not part of the greater Gethric Canon. As an institution and magical thesis it is widely accepted within <a href="Razmyrism" class="internal-link">Razmyrism</a> but less popular in other clerical and academic communities. It is kept by the Remembrancers, who preserve the names of the dead and read them aloud without end.</p>
<h2>Origins</h2>
<p>Razmyr the Soul Shepherd, who represents Death and Knowledge within the Gethric Canon, compiled the writings and records of the earliest fathers of the Church into the White Book. He was an avid student of the soul and is held largely responsible for the adoption of the concept of the Homestead Beyond, and central themes of the faith such as the nature of the soul within this world and the next can be traced back to him. The old Rite of Remembrance dates back to some of the earliest records of the White Book, while the concept of Remembrance as the Remembrancers keep it also draws on musings of Razmyr that the Church never received into the Canon.</p>
<h2>The Remembrancers</h2>
<p>The Remembrancers keep the names of the dead on name-rolls and read them aloud without end, in the belief that this shelters the souls of those named. They guard their name-rolls like holy treasures, and the burning of a name-roll is the harshest sentence their order can pass. Necromancers, or at least those who aspire to the art, have repeatedly attempted to steal the name-rolls of the Remembrancers.</p>
<p>Some Remembrancers claim that the oldest halls of names have begun to open onto the spiritual realm as the urien tombs once did, the tombs in which the first <a href="Dulmic Gate" class="internal-link">Dulmic Gates</a> were observed. It is a claim their order has been careful not to repeat.</p>
