<p>The Veilcurse is the name given, within the terminology of <a href="Basic Magical Theory" class="internal-link">Basic Magical Theory</a>, to a phenomenon that befalls spellcasters who open a Fluxbridge and channel powerful planar energies without sufficient preparation. It is a loose and much argued term, gathering a range of afflictions that share one cause and one shape: the caster is overwhelmed in an instant, loses their own connection to the energies they were channelling, and does not come back whole. It takes its name from <a href="The Veil" class="internal-link">the Veil</a>, though which veil is meant depends, as always, on who is asked.</p> <p>The curse shows itself in mind and flesh at once. Survivors speak of perceiving far too much: sights and voices without source, memories that are not their own, the leypoints of the world laid bare. Many never regain a clear sense of where they end and the world begins, and mental fracture is the rule rather than the exception. The body follows the mind, its flesh mutating as though it had forgotten its proper shape, sometimes slowly over years and sometimes in the moment of casting. No cure is known, and the most any physician has offered is to slow its course.</p> <p>Every tradition of casting has its defences against it. The Auguri taught precise thought, clean restraint and the preservation of the Ego as the first duties of a caster, and most academies still drill their students in preparation before power. The lesson all of them share is that the curse rarely strikes those who know exactly who they are.</p>  <p>The first sustained study of the curse is credited to <a href="Merzigal" class="internal-link">Merzigal of Alahmid</a>, better known as the "Great Sculptor", whose research was largely left untouched when his reign ended in the latter half of the 1st century PA. Centuries later, in 587PA, the <a href="Kherovan Inquisition" class="internal-link">Kherovan Inquisition</a> destroyed Alahmid and salted its ruins, yet confiscated many of his tomes and manuscripts on the curse, which became the subject of further research.</p>  <p>The curse is studied most closely today at <a href="Korsfall College" class="internal-link">Korsfall College</a>, whose <a href="Mural Theory" class="internal-link">Mural Theory</a> grew from years of observing its victims. Mural theorists hold that the Veil is a wall within each mind and that the Veilcurse is simply that wall breaking. To them the cursed and the trueborn are two ends of one condition: a wall worn thin makes a trueborn, a wall torn down makes the cursed. The Wallwardens of the College's sanatorium, the House of Chimes, treat their patients with ritual routine and silence, and the officially banned practice of breaching, which thins a patient's wall on purpose in the hope of making a trueborn, is whispered to have filled more of its beds than it has emptied.</p> <p>A few scholars, <a href="Rigoran" class="internal-link">Boris Rigoran</a> among them, have begun to ask whether the curse is growing more common, and whether it is the first sign of a Veil that is weakening for everyone at once.</p>
