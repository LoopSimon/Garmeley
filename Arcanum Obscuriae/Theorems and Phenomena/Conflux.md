<p>Conflux describes a phenomenon during which extra-planar energies are especially potent and those who can tap into them feel a surge of power. The earliest known records of Conflux within the western Canon stem from transcriptions of rakhi Thaumaturges. The discovery of this phenomenon influenced rakhi society tremendously: its Calendar was built around it, and important decisions of military, administrative and religious significance were influenced by it, if not made entirely dependent on it.</p>
<p>The astronomer <a href="Ulrike Metzen" class="internal-link">Ulrike Metzen</a> observed and determined different intensities of Conflux, and correctly determined that the phenomenon was actually occurring many more times than previously thought, up to hundreds of times a month in certain time periods she called "strong eras" and dropping to less than one a week during what she dubbed "weak eras". Her research was largely theoretical until the discovery of the so-called <a href="Talnod-Swarn Gradient" class="internal-link">Talnod-Swarn Gradient</a> and the Turnometer made scientific cataloguing of minute conflux-dependent energies possible.</p>
<p>The eras follow one another in a cycle of roughly ten years, though no two cycles are alike, and a single cycle may run anywhere from eight to thirteen years. A strong era typically holds for three to four years before Conflux falls away quickly into a weak era of four to five years, from which it recovers only gradually. Ulrike noted that the strong eras coincide with the years in which <a href="The Sun" class="internal-link">the Sun</a> shows few spots, and the weak eras with the years in which its face is crowded with them. The turn in Conflux trails the turn in the Sun by half a year or more, which may be why the connection went unnoticed for so long. Djinnbinders have long held that sightings of djinn rise in the strong eras.</p>
<p>She further observed that the strong eras themselves alternate. One will be long and even, holding steady for four or five years, while the next is shorter and sharper, rising to a pronounced peak within about two years before it fades. The full pattern therefore only repeats every twenty years or so.</p>
<p>Rarely, the Sun falls quiet for decades at a time, and Conflux remains strong throughout. Such great strong eras last fifty to ninety years and occur only a few times in a millennium, and some of the most celebrated ages of thaumaturgy in the historical record may coincide with them.</p>
<p>Aside from her discoveries of the Strong and Weak Eras and the minor Confluxes, Ulrike also determined that various Conflux events as described in historical records of the last millennia were abnormal, calling these "Super Events", or "Super Conflux", estimating they would occur roughly once every 100 to 200 years. Super Events are felt with great force in any era, although those falling in a strong era are recorded as the most violent. Several coincide with the appearance of a "guest star", a new star that shone for weeks or months before fading, but many others do not. Ulrike cautioned that her estimate rests on records that are surely incomplete, and that the true frequency may be higher.</p>

<p>Far beyond the reach of Ulrike's tables, which hold barely a thousand years, lies a Super Conflux greater than any in the records: the <a href="Nawiunic Event" class="internal-link">Nawiunic Event</a>, some twenty-five thousand years ago, when the brightest guest star the Globus has ever carried outshone the full <a href="Luna Major" class="internal-link">Luna Major</a> for weeks. No record of it survives, and Ulrike has never written of it.</p>
