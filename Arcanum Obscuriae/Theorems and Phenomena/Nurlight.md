<p>The Nurlight, also called "The Nur", "the Red Sun", "the Rakhlight" or "Ziuvas Wrath", is a mysterious visual phenomenon above the crater of Al-Akka, the volcano sitting in the centre of the <a href="Wasteland of Rakh" class="internal-link">Wasteland of Rakh</a>. It is observed as a sphere of crimson red light radiating a strange glow and can be seen for many miles. Unaffected by day or night, it has been observed since the end of the so called "Burning Decades" for over 900 years and is associated with the Doom of the <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakhi civilization</a>.</p> <p>The many ventures and expeditions into the Wastes following the so called "Great Expedition" in the 6th century PA have usually steered far away from the Nurlight and kept to the outer ruins, but over the centuries more and more reports of expeditions venturing closer to the light have surfaced, reporting catastrophic effects on their members, among others strange mutations, a rapid weakness of the bones, hallucinations, blindness, skin burns, hair loss, cataracts, nausea, vomiting, failure of the body, skin change and deathly sickness. These gruesome effects seem to increase with continued exposure to the Nur and with one's proximity to it. The scholar and explorer Derdryk Trahava documented the progression and effects of the Nur on his party and himself in his treatise "Boons of the Red Sun", published posthumously, in which he also describes a lifelong series of symptoms in other explorers who had not ventured as far, and even risks of hereditary mutations in the children of the affected.</p> <p>Those who have watched it longest report that the Nurlight is not steady. Trahava was the first to set down that it brightens and dims over the course of hours, as though breathing, and later expeditions have confirmed the rhythm, though none can say what drives it.</p> <h2>Theories</h2> <p>Scholars have never agreed on what the Nurlight is. Followers of Akkalian Theory (see <a href="Basic Magical Theory" class="internal-link">Basic Magical Theory</a>) hold it to be the open mouth of the absolute Fluxbridge that ended Rakh, still drawing upon the planes; <a href="Larkomas" class="internal-link">Larkomas</a> of Ott, who died of the Nur's sickness, tied it to his Syntropic-Collapse Hypothesis of Al-Akka. More than one traveller from the north has compared its glow to the lights that hang over the polar skies on winter nights, though no one can say why such lights should burn above a desert.</p> <p>The Wasteland's lesser djinn, the Nurdjinn, are found in greatest numbers nearest the Red Sun, and the <a href="Djinn" class="internal-link">djinnbinders</a> of Kerlaen and the Lameidus have long treated the light as the source of their quarry. In recent decades the <a href="Children of Dust" class="internal-link">Children of Dust</a> have claimed that the Nurlight is slowly growing dimmer, and that its dimming keeps pace with <a href="The Thinning" class="internal-link">the Thinning</a> of the Nurdjinn. Few outside their circle have taken the claim seriously.</p> <p>The Nurlight is worshipped as a living God by the fringe "Cult of the Red Sun", with reports of "final pilgrimages" of members venturing as far towards their deity as their bodies permit.</p>
