<p>A Dulmic Gate is a point where the material and spiritual realms meet so closely that spiritual and material beings can pass between them, without the tremendous planar energy that crossing otherwise demands. The term comes from the ancient urien tomb structures in which such passages were first observed. Scholars at first took them for literal gates, for tunnels could be seen within them, but in modern use the word covers any realmatic access point into the space between the realms that <a href="Zovac Harlini" class="internal-link">Zovac Harlini</a> first described.</p>  <p>Harlini held that the gates are driven by Realmatic Exchange, the constant ebb and flow of energy between the realms, and passage through them is known to grow easier during a <a href="Conflux" class="internal-link">Conflux</a>. He thought highly enough of them to name the beginning of the world after them in <a href="Harlinis Theorem" class="internal-link">his Theorem</a>, calling the first act of perception the Dulmic Event. <a href="Realmatic Nativism" class="internal-link">Realmatic Nativism</a> counts the gates among its fixed points, anchors of the realms to particular places in the material world, and weavers cite them as proof that <a href="Weaving" class="internal-link">weaving</a> can be made as permanent as a Fluxbridge.</p> <h2>Kinds of Gate</h2> <h4>Old Gates</h4> <p>Most known gates are old and still: barrows, tombs and holy places that have been tended for generations. The urien tombs are the classic examples, and many scholars count the Aeldveil of the <a href="Aeldcult" class="internal-link">Aeldcult</a>, where the Pehki send their dying, among the largest gates known, though the druids have never allowed it to be studied. Nativists have long noted, without agreeing why, that the gates of abandoned tombs grow faint and close within a few generations of their last rites.</p> <h4>Wounds</h4> <p>Rarer and far more dangerous are the gates that seem to have been torn open rather than grown, always in the wake of great and terrible works of magic that a multitude of witnesses felt as the turning of their age. Realmatic theorists count the scars of <a href="The Incursion" class="internal-link">the Incursion</a> in Wermia among them, and sailors of the <a href="Strait of Horad" class="internal-link">Strait of Horad</a> tell of drowned soldiers walking the waves on still nights where the Isthmus stood before <a href="Jurahim al-Biad" class="internal-link">al-Biad's Invocation</a> sank it.</p> <h4>Made Gates</h4> <p>Whether a gate can be made at all remains disputed. Harlini's research into so called Dulmic Transistors, devices meant to open a gate where none existed, was never published in full, and it is whispered that his last experiments at Edessa Tower before his disappearance in 799PA were of this kind.</p>  <p>Gates are not only passages for the living. Spirits cross through them more easily than anywhere else, and the dead are said to be nearest there, which has made the old gates prizes for necromancers and places of pilgrimage for the grieving alike. Some <a href="Remembrance" class="internal-link">Remembrancers</a> claim the oldest halls of names have begun to open onto the spiritual realm as the urien tombs once did, a claim their order has been careful not to repeat.</p>
