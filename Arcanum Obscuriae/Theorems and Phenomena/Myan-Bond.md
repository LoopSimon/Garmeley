<p>The Myan-Bond is the theory, set out by the yppori arcanist <a href="Szuan" class="internal-link">Tihir Szuan</a> in the 7th century PA, that the great wills, the beings known elsewhere as <a href="Greater Spirit" class="internal-link">spirits</a>, can attach themselves to <a href="Elemental" class="internal-link">elementals</a> and live with them as a single being, which Szuan called a Myan. Together with the Circle of <a href="Thandar" class="internal-link">Thandar</a> it forms the core of the southern canon of the arcane, and it is taught in the lands of the Purple Sea to this day.</p>

<p>Szuan built on Thandar's Circle by adding what he called its third dimension. Thandar had described the elementals as standing beyond the curse of doubt, and Szuan argued that this was exactly what made the bond possible. An element has a body in the waking world but no will beyond its nature; a great will has purpose and feeling but no body of its own. Where the two meet, neither has to give anything up: the will gains a body without leaving what it guards, and the element gains a temper it never had. A river's will bonded to a water elemental, in Szuan's own example, is the river walking its banks.</p> <p>Szuan was careful to set the Myan apart from two things it resembles. It is not possession, for nothing is overpowered and the element remains itself. Nor is it a <a href="Fey" class="internal-link">Fey</a>, for a Fey has given up whatever it once belonged to in order to take a body, while a great will in a Myan keeps its bond to its river, mountain or people.</p>  <p>Northern scholars long dismissed the Myan-Bond as yppori faith dressed as scholarship, and it remains little taught outside <a href="Kaito" class="internal-link">Kaito</a> and the Purple Sea. Its defenders point to the elementals that act as their nature cannot explain, guarding a shrine, keeping a bargain or mourning at a grave, and to the many accounts of such beings in the south, where the bond is held to be far more common than anyone in the north has cared to look for.</p>
