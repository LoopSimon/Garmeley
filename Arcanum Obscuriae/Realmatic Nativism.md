<p>Nativism, within the field of planar theory not cultural politics, describes the idea of concepts and energies unique to or originating from a specific point in time and space. It is a relatively modern field of study that has developed alongside and build upon the discoveries of <a href="Modern Realmatic Theory" class="internal-link">Modern Realmatic Theory</a>, such as the provable existence of an <a href="Astral Realm" class="internal-link">Astral Realm</a>. The basics of Realmatic Nativism within the greater arcane academia were first compiled and structured into the well known "Principles of Nativism", as outlined below, by the Arcanist <a href="Krindor" class="internal-link">Modan Krindor</a> in the early 9th century.</p>
<p>One of the classical hypothesis of Nativism is, that the spiritual realm as we perceive it is a mirror world mapped on top or beneath the "real" world we perceive, also known as the material realm. To engage or interact with it in different locations or points in time yields vastly different results and its make up corresponds and influences specific points in time and space on the material realm as well. This is the first Principle of Nativism.</p>
<p>The sources or effects of spiritual energies, such as summoned by weavers like druids or shamans, can be traced back to not only specific entities or locations, but also concepts within culture and historic traditions. To tap into the same forces or summon the same powers from a place far away, instead of local correspondence points, yields weaker and weaker results. Therefore one can assume that to weave energies of the spiritual realm one must accept they are not drawing from a large and homogenous reservoir of powers, but instead on a myriad of specific and malleable emotional energies, to whom they may have vastly different relationships. This is the second Principle of Nativism.</p>
<p>There are points in time and space, where the spiritual energies are so readily available and potent they bleed over into the material realm, while there are others where "the third realm", usually referred to as the Astral Realm, takes on vastly different forms and shapes depending on their corresponding regions in the material realm, such as within the Niryd Plaines of the Bihra. There exist fix points that allow the traversal between the realms for those daring enough to do so, usually referred to as the Dulmic Gates, and these act as anchors to the Material Realm. However there exist also points in space within our own material world, such as great watery desert of the wider Tirean Ocean, where it was almost impossible for practiced Seers to draw upon spiritual energies, likely due to the fact little sentient life has existed there for millenia upon millenia. The idea that the spiritual and perhaps astral realm are almost aftereffects of communally lived experiences that take on an existence of their own, is known as the third principle of Nativism.</p>
<p>The fifth principle, usually not as accepted and a more occult and niche reading of theory, describes the idea that the realms of existence, sometimes called "the Division" are a perhaps unique phenomenon to our own world, that if one were to travel to distant places, across space and time and beyond the worlds of our own heavenly body, one may not find the same situation. This is concept is known as Geocentric Nativism.</p>
<p>Adjacent to it but much more accepted is the idea of Inverse Projection and Realmatic Force. To consider that it would take immense powers and energies to create and maintain constructs and subrealms such as these, that  the subconscious and real division of the realms is fundamentally no different than the largest and most efficient Spell ever wrought, tapping into the fundamental planar forces of the Formative and Ideal. This is a much maligned and arguably baseless theory, and many consider the conclusions of <a href="Vilkin" class="internal-link">Vilkins</a> Universal Theory to be more sensible, who posits that the planes of power and energy affect everything in existence, that the division of the material and spiritual is a consequence of one of the fundamental "grids", as Vilkin calls them, that care little about our own world and permeate all existence, this theory is also build upon and accepted by Vilkins famous teacher, <a href=" Carassa al-Kael " class="internal-link"> Carassa al-Kael</a> and her Triqueta Model. </p>
