<p>Currentbending is the engineering art of the dwarves of <a href="Tizin-Kol" class="internal-link">Tizin-Kol</a>, the craft of bending current through metal and making it push. Founded on the work of <a href="Mamus Rolim" class="internal-link">Mamus Rolim</a>, who published his findings in 807PA, and of the mineralist <a href="Magda Bazena Nib" class="internal-link">Magda Bazena Nib</a>, it has within a single century lit the tunnels, drained the shafts and bound together the holds of the Underlands, and given the world above its first glimpse of the <a href="Keyzal Workshop" class="internal-link">Keyzal</a> Osmiumthrowers.</p> <h2>Origins</h2> <p>Its two foundations were known long before anyone understood them. The smiths of Tizin-Kol's deepest forges, sunk near the strata where the gods of stone are said to rest, had for generations produced rare batches of a pale alloy that would not touch a lodestone, but hovered a finger's breadth above it. The faithful called it the Untouching, and took it for a sign of the gods' favour. In the mines, crews feared the crystals they called strikestone, which spat sparks when struck and had set off more than one firedamp blast.</p> <p>Testing the Untouching as a Symposar of the Mineralist Symposium, Mamus Rolim found that current passed through it without warming it or growing weaker, and that a current set running in a closed ring of it did not stop: his famous ring, kept beside a lodestone needle, held the needle turned for a whole season after its source was taken away. Rolim held that the deep forges give the metal a memory of the stone's weight, and that a metal which remembers the deep lets current pass unburdened. He dubbed what he had found the Current, named the alloy deepforged silver, and published in 807PA, at about the age of 47. His younger colleague in the Symposium, the mineralist Magda Bazena Nib, shared in the work, grew obsessed with strikestone and, in the decades that followed, learned to tame it: slowly compressed in iron frames, it holds its strain and looses it, at a hammer's blow, as a single great pulse of current, the current-ignition.</p> <h2>The Art in the Deep</h2> <p>The first uses were humble. Rolim's endless rings carried signals through the Inos mines in the 810s and 820s, and soon ore carts rode over lodestone tracks on runners of deepforged silver, floating without friction and making the Inos Mining Cartel rich. With Nib's strikestone came the rest. Storm-lamps lit the tunnels without burning a breath of air or touching off firedamp; breath-wheels drove fresh air into the deepest holds; current-pumps kept the shafts dry; and cold forges melted metal from within, with no flame and no fuel. By the turn of the 10th century, signal-lines carried word between holds days apart, the reservoir rings of Rolim's student Mex Harfal stored current for when it was needed, his everlocks sealed the vaults of the clans, and light-farms grew fungus and root crops far from any sun. Few in the Underlands now live a day without the art.</p> <h2>The Grey Years and the Crisis</h2> <p>From the mid 9th century the Keyzal Workshop turned the art toward arms. Its early Osmiumthrowers, which drove a slug down a pair of deepforged rails by current alone, were as dangerous to their makers as to anyone else: rails overheated and went grey, losing their deep memory for good; strikestone cracked and loosed its whole charge at once; and the first osmium slugs, heated in the throw, gave off a poison that clouded their gunners' eyes. Those were the grey years. It was over such uses that Rolim and Nib parted: Nib led the Symposium's Practicalists, who put the science first and had a hand in the first Osmiumthrower, while Rolim turned in his later years toward the ethics of the art.</p> <p>Osmium is holy to the dwarves of Tizin-Kol, and the spectacle of the gods' own metal cast at enemies, and of forges older than memory turned to engines, divided the Hold. Between 879 and 882PA unrest became open war between religious radicals and progressive engineers, the <a href="Crisis of the Untouched" class="internal-link">Crisis of the Untouched</a>, and it ended in 882PA with the assassination of Rolim, by then a peaceful and diplomatic voice on the engineers' side, whom the radicals called the Father of the Sin. The Council of Tizin-Kol answered in unity and crushed the radicals' cells, and in the years after, a moderate arm of the church that welcomed the art slowly took its place, among it a divine engineering of its own. Some still whisper that the hand which killed Rolim belonged to his own side, and made of him the martyr he became.</p> <h2>Law and Legacy</h2> <p>Nib grieved him deeply. As Chair of the Council of Tizin-Kol from 886 to 907PA she secularised the Hold and empowered the Symposium and the Workshops, yet, perhaps tempered by his death, she also bound the art in mandates, which her own student Brean Eduak would come to criticise harshly: deepforged silver may only be made in the deepest forges and in small quantities, and few currentbent arms have ever been allowed to reach the surface. Even so the Workshop's line went on, and in 923PA it brought forth its flagship, the <a href="Keyzal 21" class="internal-link">Keyzal 21</a>, the first thrower able to hold the current of a single ignition and spend it shot by shot.</p>
