<p>The Cilenax Principle is a theorem of magical theory holding that the energy a caster directs need not fully overcome the threshold of a working to accomplish it, but can slip through it, so that magic routinely achieves its effects with less energy than the change it causes should require. Its corollary, that no working is ever wholly certain and that the precision of a caster's prism governs its odds of success, has made it one of the cornerstones of modern casting theory. The name is <a href="Cyraclion" class="internal-link">cyracli</a>, and has never been replaced.</p> <h2>Origins</h2> <p>Who first described the Principle is unknown. It survives in the Codex Kynosis of <a href="Neyadastes" class="internal-link">Neyadastes</a>, around 710BA, where the Father of Alchemy records among the observations of earlier Kynus that Kynomeia sometimes succeeds with less energy than its Trygisis should demand, and gives the phenomenon the name by which it is still known. Like much of cyracli learning, whatever fuller understanding stood behind the passage was lost with the Fall of Cyraclion, and for centuries it was read as a curiosity of <a href="Alchemy" class="internal-link">Alchemy</a> rather than a law of magic.</p> <p>It was rediscovered by the Ottian <a href="Gnome" class="internal-link">gnome</a> <a href="Xarelius" class="internal-link">Xarelius</a> in the late 6th century, who, in modelling the efficiency of spells and artefacts, found that workings consistently cost less than their effects should demand, and that the saving grew with the precision of the prism without ever reaching certainty. Xarelius took up Neyadastes' term for what he had measured, and the Principle entered the classical teaching of <a href="Artificery" class="internal-link">Prosum</a>, where Ottian artificers still reckon an artefact's reliability by it.</p> <p><a href="Jevana" class="internal-link">Jevana of Leston</a> built much of her Leyprism Model upon the Principle. In The Ley, Intent and Perception and Empathetic Power she argued that the prism is not merely the shape a caster gives to energy but the means by which it slips through, so that the precision of perception, more than the quantity of energy, decides whether a working lands. Her treatises carried the Principle into elven, gnomish and human schooling alike.</p> <h2>Debate</h2> <p>Adherents of the <a href="Law of the Settled Ledger" class="internal-link">Law of the Settled Ledger</a> have long distrusted the Principle, holding that whatever a working seems to save at its threshold must be paid somewhere, and they look for the debt in residue. Others note that the Principle explains why no spell, however practised, is ever certain, and why even the greatest casters speak of luck. What the Principle is, rather than what it does, remains unknown.</p>
