<p>Leygrid Theory is the body of scholarship holding that the leypoints, the fixed points at which planar energies gather most strongly, are not scattered at random but lie upon a single regular grid, and in its fullest form that this grid underlies the whole universe rather than the Globus alone. Who or what laid the grid, and why, remains one of the great unanswered questions of the arcane.</p> <h2>The Leypoint Model</h2> <p>The theory begins with the elven Magus <a href="Kwarzimi" class="internal-link">Kwarzimi ar Corbaik</a>, Keeper of the Great Library of Rahzalbis in the 3rd century PA. Blind from birth, Kwarzimi had learned to perceive planar energies directly, and he set the places he perceived against the shadow measures and old records of his library. He found that the places of power stood at regular distances from one another, in a lattice that ran across the Globus. His Leypoint Model held them to be springs of power rising through the ground of a world at rest, as water rises in the wells of the desert.</p> <p>The model passed to his student <a href="Karajal" class="internal-link">Karajal</a>, who made it a cornerstone of the <a href="Karajal School of Insight" class="internal-link">School of Insight</a> he founded at Alparak in 331PA, and it came west in 610PA in the fourth volume of <a href="Galiatar Inkfingers" class="internal-link">Galiatar Inkfingers'</a> Rudimenta Targorae. Within a century it was common ground for elven and human scholars alike, and the leypoints became, as they remain, the favoured places for great workings.</p> 
<h2>From Points to Grid</h2> 
<p>Later scholarship slowly loosened the leypoints from the ground. In the 7th century <a href="Avero" class="internal-link">Avero of the Eightfolds</a> recorded that leynodes fluctuate differently around trueborn casters than around learned ones. Since <a href="Carassa al-Kael" class="internal-link">Carassa al-Kael's</a> Astral Space of 881PA it has been known that leypoints are found in the <a href="Astral Realm" class="internal-link">Astral Realm</a> as well as the material and spiritual, and that astral travellers find their journeys easiest between them.</p> 

<p>The decisive blow to the old model came with the Wheeling of <a href="Gahenna" class="internal-link">Gahenna</a>. Once the universities and magi towers of the west had accepted, from the 890s, that the Globus is not at rest but travels around the Sun, and the School of Insight followed about 916PA, the leypoints could no longer be springs in the ground of a still world. If the Globus is a traveller, the grid that holds its places must be larger than the world. Leygrid Theory in its modern form, that the whole universe lies upon one grid of nodes, took shape from this, and many read the grids of <a href="Vilkin" class="internal-link">Roizar Vilkin's</a> Universal Model as its most ambitious statement, though Vilkin himself has never said so.</p> 


 <p>No school agrees on who or what laid the grid. Gethric theologians call it the lattice of the Creator's design, Nativists insist it must be the work of perceiving minds, and a few speculative scholars hold that it is older than any mind at all. Nor has anyone explained why the Astral Realm should hold leypoints as surely as the material world does. Carassa al-Kael, who first found them there, is said to regard her work on the question as unfinished.</p>
