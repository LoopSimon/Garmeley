<p>Takuzt, known in the cities of the Kaitan Gulf as the Cuarteña and among the scholars of the Arts of Change as the Fundamental Theory, is the philosophy and faith of the hobgoblins of <a href="Tal" class="internal-link">Tal</a>, the Aït Xerra of the mountains and the <a href="Mudaxari" class="internal-link">Mudaxari</a> of the cities. Its name means "the Fourness" in Tal Gob. It holds that the world is made of four elements only, Earth, Water, Air and Fire, that each of them is pure and complete in itself, and that a person grows wise by learning to hold all four within the self without letting any of them mix. It is at once a school of elemental channelling, a moral teaching and a religion, and its followers draw no line between the three.</p>

<h2>Teachings</h2>
<p>Takuzt rejects the six transitory elements of the Circle of <a href="Thandar" class="internal-link">Thandar</a>. To its teachers, magma, storm, lightning, frost, ash and seafire are not elements at all but mixtures, "elements that have forgotten themselves", and the violence that Thandar himself ascribed to the elementals of the between is taken as proof that mixing corrupts. In the same way it rejects the <a href="Myan-Bond" class="internal-link">Myan-Bond</a> of <a href="Szuan" class="internal-link">Tihir Szuan</a> entirely. A will that lives inside an element is held to be a mixture of the worst kind, and the binding of elementals as servants or soldiers, as the channelers of <a href="Naigan" class="internal-link">Naigan</a> once did, is counted the gravest sin the teaching knows. A follower of Takuzt channels an element by discipline alone, and asks nothing of it and gives it nothing in return.</p>
<p>The four elements are known in Tal Gob as Akal, Earth, Aman, Water, Adhou, Air, and Tamsa, Fire. Each of them is held to carry a virtue and a quarry, the failing that the virtue hunts in those who practise it badly. Earth is the element of Constancy, and its quarry is Stubbornness. Water is the element of Mercy, the power to yield and to carry others, and its quarry is Drifting, which is faithlessness. Air is the element of Truthful Speech and of freedom, and its quarry is Recklessness and rumour. Fire is the element of Courage and of the will, and its quarry is Wrath. The teaching holds that every person carries all four, that most are ruled by one, and that a life well lived brings the other three to stand beside it without blending into it.</p>
<p>The teachings are gathered in the Four Sayings of Tazdirt the Unmixed, a short work in four parts said to have been written in the 750s PA. Commentaries on the Sayings make up most of the literature of the faith, and they are read aloud in the halls of the Aït Xerra and in the houses of the Mudaxari on the four quarter days of the year, each of which belongs to one element.</p>

<h2>Practice and the Exarchs</h2>
<p>Every student of Takuzt begins with a single element, usually the one that a teacher, or Amghar, judges to rule the student already, and works at it for many years until the element and its virtue are mastered and its quarry is held in check. Most practitioners never go further, and a lifetime spent in the perfection of one element is held to be honourable. The mark of maturity is the taking up of a second element, which must be learned beside the first and never blended with it: a practitioner of Water and Fire may raise a wave and a flame in the same breath, but must never make steam. Those who have mastered a second element are called exarchs, and they are the most respected teachers and leaders of the hobgoblins of Tal. An exarch of two opposed elements, Fire and Water or Earth and Air, is held in the highest regard of all, since the teaching counts the holding of opposites without mixture as the hardest discipline there is.</p>
<p>Exarchs of three elements are very rare, and only one practitioner, Tazdirt herself, is said to have held all four. No one has claimed to since, and the teachers of Takuzt are divided over whether anyone should try.</p>

<h2>History</h2>
<p>Takuzt traces itself to Tazdirt the Unmixed, a hobgoblin born to a family of Tirbi sellswords who had come south in the service of the Elbae Sultanate after the <a href="Hobgoblin Invasion" class="internal-link">Hobgoblin Invasion</a> of Urland. She served Naigan as a hired soldier in the middle of the 8th century PA, studied the Arts of Change beside the channelers of the Pillar of Earth, and saw the elementals that the empire bound and drove into battle against the Safiri cities of the Antulun Bay. She came to hold that the binding of the elementals was slavery, that the Myan-Bond was the same sin given a gentler name, and that both had grown out of the error of the between elements. She left the service of the empire, and the Four Sayings date from the years that followed.</p>
<p>Her teaching spread among the hobgoblin companies of Tal during the collapse of Naigan and the <a href="Period of the Bleeding Heron" class="internal-link">Period of the Bleeding Heron</a>, when many of them fought for the warlords and city states that rose from the empire. It became the faith of the hobgoblin state of <a href="Guadaxerra" class="internal-link">Guadaxerra</a> on its founding in 851PA, and the hobgoblins who settled in the cities of the gulf, in <a href="Yorgos" class="internal-link">Yorgos</a> and in <a href="Iz-Almadus" class="internal-link">Iz-Almadus</a> carried it with them.</p>

<h2>Relations with Other Faiths</h2>
<p>The followers of Takuzt still honour the <a href="Kaladi Mysticism" class="internal-link">Kaladi</a> of their ancestors, though the teaching forbids any belief that the dead may dwell in stone, water or flame. Tazdirt herself is honoured as a Kaladi in the halls of the Aït Xerra, although she taught that no one should be.</p>
<p>The relationship with the <a href="Ashcult" class="internal-link">Ashcult</a> of Sochor is uneasy. By denying the between elements, Takuzt denies that ash is an element, and the priests of the Ashcult, to whom ash has always been earth born of fire and the holy substance of their god, regard this as heresy. The Mudaxari of the Yppori kingdoms therefore keep their faith quietly, above all in <a href="Rikarma" class="internal-link">Rikarma</a>, where the rites of the Ashcult are practised most fervently. The surviving <a href="The Pillars of Myan" class="internal-link">Pillars of Myan</a> regard Takuzt as a crude and stubborn reading of Thandar, and the exarchs return the judgement in kind.</p>
