<p>Vishams Question is an academic topic within the realm of extra planar conjuration and specifically daemonic consultation that asks: "Why do Outsiders resemble form perceivable by us?" In reality it goes deeper than just answering the basic question and concerns itself mostly with the categorisation of the creatures from beyond, differences between <a href="Greater Spirit" class="internal-link">Spirits</a> & <a href="Daemon" class="internal-link">Demons</a> and more noticeably the lack there of, and the chronology of ritualistic summoning and Naming.</p>
<p>Visham was an avid Daemonologist himself and studied the ancient grimoires, tablets and papyri of long lost civilisations, going back as far as <a href="Alparaz" class="internal-link">Alparaz</a> himself to compare the visual appearance and observable behaviour of various summoned and, in most cases successfully, bound creatures from beyond our own plane. He noticed that not only did creatures successfully summoned by a channeller or many into our own world were almost always perceivable, they often took on shape and form resembling their surroundings and retained them when called upon again by the same bounded name in some cases many centuries later. Notably Visham observes the same properties concerning what scholars describe as "Daemons" affect the mysterious "Spirits" in the same ways and muses that the idea of a spiritual realm being scientifically at all different than a plane of energy is largely arbitrary and based on cultural and religious traditions instead of scientific research, coming to similar conclusions as the elven Mage <a href="Carassa Al-Kael" class="internal-link">Carassa Al-Kael</a> would almost 400 years later.</p>
<p>Due to the nature of the subject and the history of the discipline of conjuration and summoning itself, Vishams Question is often regarded as problematic and dangerous and thought of as a safe on in theory while encouraging practice, of course well outlawed within the rules and standards of the Accords.</p>
