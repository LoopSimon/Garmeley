<p>Harlinis Theorem describes the core thesis of the vihri scholar <a href="Zovac Harlini" class="internal-link">Zovac Harlini</a>, after whom Harlinic Space and the Harlinic Forces are also named. In his final work before his mysterious disappearance in 799PA, the largely obscure Treatise on the Fabric of Reality", Harlini considers the differences in classifications between elemental and artificial, divine and arcane, spiritual and material and other dualistic structures as proposed by various academics of the course of history. </p>
<p>Harlinis Theorem proposes the cosmos as a once singular instance of essence, bereft of divisions, reflections, time and the shadows of nuance, that existed as such for an eternity beyond measurement. Only the act of perception itself was able to begin our world as we know it, to perceive anything is to notice a change, to notice a change is to compare an object on a scale from then to now. Harlinis Theorem describes this "origin state" as the true reference state of all existence, the fix point or "Axis Mundi" upon which all perceived reality is structured. At this point he references the work of <a href="Larkomas" class="internal-link">Larkomas of Ott</a>, whose consideration on the Ideal and Concrete, in Larkomas worldview simply "planes of energy" akin to the traditional planes of chaotic, stagnant, positive and negative,  Harlini describes as "inspirational, brilliant yet cut short by the restrictions of an institutionalised mind". </p>
<p>In this Axis Mundi, no such divisions between two, three or hundreds of realms ever existed, nor does anything like the traditional beheri-ilrakhani planar worldview apply, rather a single tapestry of existence once connected all matter in the universe, undisturbed in its simplicity, before sentient observation wrought untold destruction upon it, ripping it apart, enriching it and forever altering it. This event, dubbed by Harlini as the "Dulmic Event" in reference to the Dulmic Gates that allow traversal from the so called "material" to the so called "spiritual" realm, forced the universe to exist in a constant state of harmonic energies pulling towards the Axis Mundi and conscious energies pulling away from it. </p>
<p>Harlinis Theorem was re-introduced to the broader academic world in the later works of <a href="Carassa al-Kael" class="internal-link">Carassa al-Kael,</a>, Harlinis own student, and her proposed <a href="Modern Realmatic Theory" class="internal-link">"Triquetra Model"</a>. The school of thought that rejects the more simple planar or also often called "romantic" view on cosmology, that describes the commonly taught <a href="Basic Magical Theory" class="internal-link">basics of Magical Theory</a>, is sometimes called the Harlinic School or the post-romantic school of Arcane Academia.</p>
