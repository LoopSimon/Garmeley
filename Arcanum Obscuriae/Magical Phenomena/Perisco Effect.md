<p>The Perisco Effect, named after the 8th century wizard and physician <a href="Artor Perisco" class="internal-link">Artor Perisco</a>, describes a correlation between overexposure to magic and the human body. Perisco conducted multiple experiments over the cause of decades that observed and measured gradual and immediate exposure of individuals, both healthy and sick as well as young and old, to various amounts of energy summoned in physically harmless ways, using a spell named Periscos Light that summoned a heatless illumination the around a certain object or person.</p>
<p>Perisco found that young individuals exposed to a gradual and constant amount of planar energy were less likely to fall victim to disease and many other common sicknesses of the body, or have a higher chance at a full recovery if so. On the other hand he noted an increased risk in infertility, frailty of bones and weakness of the mind in some candidates that he had exposed for decades to the spell. Immediate overexposure to individuals that had never stood in contact with magical energies were found to be more likely to recover from physical trauma as well, such as injuries sustained in battle, but also developed a high chance of physical mutation, blindness, sudden hair loss and the disease known as cancer, Perisco likened this to the <a href="The Veilcurse" class="internal-link">Veilcurse</a> and its effects on ill prepared channelers.</p>
<p>These findings to Perisco explained a longer average lifespan of many long practicing magicians, although critics quickly remarked on the exclusivity of the arcane arts from a financial perspective and the powerful positions in society an advanced life long mage would likely find himself in. Access to expensive and elusive healing magic, good hygiene and food, as well as all other pleasantries afforded to those with coin and standing already created a situation that rendered many broad conclusions of Periscos studies vague and questionable. </p>
