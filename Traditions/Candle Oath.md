<p>The Candle Oath is the Gethric rite of judicial combat. Two parties to a dispute swear to the truth of their cause on holy ground and over a lit candle, and fight, in person or by champions, until one of them yields or dies or the candle burns down. The faithful hold that heaven will not let a false oath stand, and that a man who swears falsely over the candle severs his soul's road to the Homestead Beyond whether he wins or loses. The rite is older than any of the Gethric kingdoms and is practised widely across the Gethric world, in the High Gethric lands above all, but it has been forbidden in <a href="Meadrath" class="internal-link">Meadrath</a> since the reforms of Queen Gisela II. In <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a> it has given rise to the <a href="Flickersworn" class="internal-link">Flickersworn</a>, the champions whom the great houses keep to fight it for them.</p>  
<h2>Origins</h2>  
<p>The rite grew from the commonest act of Gethric devotion, the lighting of a candle for the Prophet, and from the saying of the first years of the faith that the flame of Gethric would find a candle in every home. If the flame was in every home, it was said, it would also be present at every quarrel, and a dispute sworn before it would be judged by it. The first Candle Oath is told in the Pandecta of the White Book, in the tale of the Two Millers, in which two brothers who could not agree on the inheritance of their father's mill swore before a priest over a candle and fought, and the brother who had lied was struck down before the candle had burned a finger's width. The tale is placed in the first or second century after the Prophet, and the custom was common in the Gethric lands long before the Church set down any rule for it.</p>  
<p>The Church regulated the rite in the centuries that followed. It required that the oath be sworn on holy ground and before a priest, fixed the words of the oath, and came to require a candle of a set measure, so that no party could win by the choice of a short candle or a long one.</p>  
<h2>The Rite</h2>  
<p>A Candle Oath is sworn in a churchyard, a cathedral close, a shrine or any other holy ground, before a priest who lights the candle and hears the oaths. Both parties swear to the truth of their cause in the fixed words of the rite, and their champions, if they have them, swear that they fight in good faith for a cause they believe true. Witnesses are needed. Where the <a href="Candleguard" class="internal-link">Candleguard</a> is present it keeps the ground and stands witness, though it never fights, and where no officials could be found the chronicles tell of disputes settled before any witness at hand, in some reports as far as an innocent child.</p>  
<p>The fight begins when the candle is lit. A party whose champion yields or dies has lost, and their cause is held false before heaven and before the court that granted the oath. If the candle burns down before either side has fallen or yielded, the accused is cleared, for it is held that heaven would not condemn them and has not. The rite favours the accused, and the accuser who swears it must be certain of the cause and of the champion.</p>  
<h2>The Candle</h2>  
<p>The candle of the oath is cast from consecrated wax to a set measure called a span, which burns for about an hour. The best and the most trusted oath candles come from the chandlers of <a href="Mesziev" class="internal-link">Mesziev</a> in Selbia, whose wax is prized across the Gethric lands and burns longer and steadier than common tallow, and a span of Mesziev wax is the measure by which the length of every Candle Oath is judged. Courts and churches that grant oaths often keep a store of them, and a party who suspects the candle may demand one from a sealed Mesziev box.</p>  
<h2>Across the Gethric World</h2>  
<p>In <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a> the Candle Oath belongs to the courts of the Var and to the <a href="Honoured Assembly" class="internal-link">Honoured Assembly</a>, which may grant it in the quarrels of the Var themselves. Because it is sworn on holy ground, every oath needs the leave of the bishop in whose diocese it is fought, and the Church has a hand in every duel of the realm. The <a href="Royal Judges of Vihr" class="internal-link">royal judges</a> will not grant it in the Crown Pleas. The nobility of Vihr still settles its gravest disputes by it, and most great houses keep champions, the Flickersworn, to fight it for them.</p>  
<p>In <a href="Meadrath" class="internal-link">Meadrath</a> the oath was abolished with the other trials by ordeal under the reforms of Queen <a href="Gisela II" class="internal-link">Gisela II</a>, who reigned from 704PA for sixty-eight years, for her new royal courts would not leave their judgements to the candle. After the Great Schism of 764PA the Trinicist bishops of the realm confirmed the ban and called the oath a tempting of heaven, and the crown had no wish to see the quarrels of its nobles placed in the hands of Iztaw's priests. Meadi lords who wish to settle a dispute by arms have been known to cross into Gethric lands to swear it.</p>  
<h2>Famous Oaths</h2>  
<p>The most famous champion of the oath in the old tales is the Wermian knight <a href="Dazhek Winghelm" class="internal-link">Dazhek Winghelm</a>, who is said to have won more than thirty duels in the 5th century and to have twice beaten Vinitia of Gardhill. Around the same time Ras Boris I "the Pious", in his later years, demanded that only chaste men fight Candle Oaths before the crown, in keeping with his Ideal of Virtue. The court mocked the demand as the "virgin candles", and it was quietly dropped after his death.</p>  
<p>The oath remembered as the Long Candle was fought in the 8th century, when Moskomir zi Tryd accused the Gri of Ahmsk and stood as his own champion against Calina of Kisnau, the Pilvian champion of the Gri. The two fought until the candle went out, the Gri was cleared, and Moskomir died of his wounds the next day.</p>  
<p>The most notorious Candle Oath of the present age was fought in 922PA between the houses of Selbia and Opuzna. Yeliza zi Sribo, the sister of Var Provim of Selbia, accused Var Ilia of Opuzna of having raped her at the Poets Fest of Tienov earlier that year. Her younger brother <a href="Beladoz the Black" class="internal-link">Beladoz the Black</a> stood as her champion against <a href="Carguez di Mindano" class="internal-link">Carguez di Mindano</a>, the Flickersworn of Opuzna, and was cut down and bled to death before the candle was half burned. Few in Vihr believe the verdict, and the oath has left a bitter feud between the two houses.</p>
