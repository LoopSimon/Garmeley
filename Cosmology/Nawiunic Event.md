<p>The Nawiunic Event was the death of a star. About 25,000 years ago, some three hundred light years from the <a href="Globus" class="internal-link">Globus</a>, a great star burst, and for weeks the new light hung in the sky brighter than the full <a href="Luna Major" class="internal-link">Luna Major</a>, bright enough to be seen by day. Its light and its sharpest radiation reached the world first, and the particles it flung out followed over the next centuries. With it came the greatest Super Conflux in the history of the Globus. The star was far enough away to spare the world the great dying that a nearer one would have brought, and near enough to leave a mark. Over most of the world the mark was slight. In one place it was deep. No one remembers the Event, and almost no one knows that it happened.</p>

<h2>The Star</h2>
<p>The new star faded over a year or two and was gone, leaving nothing that a bare eye could see. Everyone who looked up saw it, and no memory of it has come down from them, unless the Nawiu is such a memory (see below). The Super Conflux that came with the flash was greater than any in the records (see <a href="Conflux" class="internal-link">Conflux</a>). The Super Confluxes of the records come from stars bursting thousands of light years away, so far off that the star is faint and often unseen, and they give only a swell of planar energy. This one burst close, and its swell was like nothing since.</p>

<h2>The Rain from the Sky</h2>
<p>Over most of the Globus the radiation was only a modest rise. The thick air took most of the force, and the thin high layer that guards the world from the sharp light of the Sun lost a few parts in a hundred at most. Auroras were seen far from the poles for years. The magnetic field of the world, which turns aside most of what falls from the sky, was then in a weak phase and at its weakest for a few centuries, and it let more through than it does now (see <a href="Globus" class="internal-link">Globus</a>). The particles kept arriving for centuries and held the radiation a little raised for centuries and perhaps longer. Nothing died of it. No people, beast or plant was lost, and over most of the world no one noticed.</p>

<h2>The Caves of Eastern Behera</h2>
<p>Beneath eastern Behera the field has long been weaker than anywhere else on the Globus, a broad region where more of what falls from the sky reaches the ground, and in the centuries of the Event it was weaker still. The shallow caves beneath it, close enough to the surface to give only partial cover, were the most irradiated place on the Globus. They held <a href="Lizion" class="internal-link">Lizion</a>, a stone found nowhere else that is itself partly radioactive and that holds radon in its cavities, and the air of the caves was heavy with it. Lizion cannot have made the weak region, which comes from the core of the world, but it gathers and channels planar energy, and in the Super Conflux the caves were a natural amplifier, holding the surge and giving it back as nowhere else on the Globus.</p>
<p>The people of the shallow caves were Drow. They had lived for ages in raised radiation, and bore it far better than any surface people could have. The great cities of the deep Drow lay far below under much rock and were spared it. About 7,000 years after the flash, around 18,000BA, the first elven cultures came up out of the shallow caves of eastern Behera (see <a href="Elf" class="internal-link">Elf</a> and <a href="Drow" class="internal-link">Drow</a>). The Jawahr of an elf is larger and more responsive to planar energy than that of a Drow (see <a href="Jawahr" class="internal-link">Jawahr</a>). How the people of those caves came to be elves, and whether the Event had a hand in it, is a question that Karajal has kept for itself.</p>

<h2>The Remnant</h2>
<p>What remains of the star is a vast ring of faintly glowing gas, spread over many tens of degrees of sky, far too dim for any eye and found only through the greatest telescopes. It has no name, and no astronomer has tied it to any event. At its heart there may lie a pulsar, the dead core of the star, spinning many times a second and slowing a little every year, and if so the slowing itself would date the star to about 25,000 years ago. The young remnant also kept the planar energies of the world slightly raised for some thousands of years afterwards.</p>

<h2>Traces</h2>
<p>The world kept what the sky has lost. A thin layer of a rare iron, made only in dying stars, lies in the sea floor of that age. The ice at the poles and the growths of the caves hold a surge of the atoms that cosmic rays make, and the weak phase of the magnetic field is written into cooled lava and the mud of lakes. The best record is Lizion itself, which grows in thin layers and keeps in each the planar and radiation level of its age. Since the <a href="Talnod-Swarn Gradient" class="internal-link">Talnod-Swarn Gradient</a> made planar energies measurable, readings of Lizion with the Turnometer have shown that the energies of the world stood raised for thousands of years after the Event, a finding so recent that few have heard it and fewer will say it aloud.</p>

<h2>The Nawiu</h2>
<p>The elves keep one tale that may be the memory of the Event. The Nawiu, among the oldest tales in the world, speaks of a gift from the outer gods that raised those who dared to question existence as it is into a greater light. It has been told for so many thousands of years that it has changed beyond recognition, and no two tellings agree. In the oldest forms the gift is a light that comes into the dark. In later ones it is a god, then a Paragon, then a crown. The name Nawiunic is made from it, and is used at Karajal and nowhere else.</p>

<h2>Karajal</h2>
<p>Within the closed halls of the <a href="Karajal School of Insight" class="internal-link">Karajal School of Insight</a> the question of the Event, the caves and the fate of their people has been asked since the first years of the School. It is asked quietly, for the answer touches the Nawiu, the Paragons, and the standing of the elves in the eyes of their gods (see <a href="Elven Paragonworship" class="internal-link">Elven Paragonworship</a>). To settle it the scholars would need the Paragons, whose Jawahrs are the oldest to survive intact and are charged with energy far beyond any living elf, and the Paragons may not be studied. Arouf Dimelis al-Dezer, the present Ruby Eye of Karajal and counted among the School's radicals, has asked leave to examine them under an academic lens. The request has been refused, as yet.</p>
