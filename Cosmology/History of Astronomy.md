<p>The history of astronomy in the west is the history of three old schools, the elven, the Cyracli and the Rakhi, that watched the same sky for thousands of years, kept their records in their own ways and met only late. It is the story of how the motions of the Sun, the moons, the stars and the other celestial bodies came to be understood, and it leaves aside the study of the planes, which belongs to the arcane theorists and has a history of its own.</p>

<h2>What Is Settled</h2>
<p>By 927PA the scholars of every school agree on a good deal. The <a href="Globus" class="internal-link">Globus</a> is a sphere. <a href="Luna Major" class="internal-link">Luna Major</a> is the nearer of the lights, and <a href="The Sun" class="internal-link">the Sun</a> is very much the larger and the farther. <a href="Luna Minor" class="internal-link">Luna Minor</a> circles Luna Major and not the Globus itself. The Sun swells and shrinks by a tenth through the year, and the two halves of the year between the equinoxes are unequal, the one of about 187 days and the other of about 213. What is not settled is whether the Globus stands still, as the old Cosmology of <a href="Ralzig" class="internal-link">Ralzig</a> holds, or travels around the Sun, as <a href="Gahenna" class="internal-link">Gahenna</a> held, and the dispute is not likely to end soon.</p>

<h2>The Elven Records</h2>
<p>The elves watched longest. The priest-astronomers of the Tomb Cities kept the oldest unbroken records of the sky in the world, and their chief concern was <a href="Sarhen" class="internal-link">Sarhen</a>, the brightest star, which is lost in the glare of the Sun for some forty days of every year. About 1800BA Merenkhar of Farzirash, now Jiravid, fixed its return to the day, and so fixed the year of 400 days and the forty hidden days that still shape the Elven Calendar (see <a href="Calendars" class="internal-link">Calendars</a>). About 900BA, under the Alparid Dynasty, Nasr ibn Hadad of Alparak measured the shadows of the temple pillars through the year and found that the disc of the Sun grows and shrinks and that the halves of the year are unequal. The elves made no theory of it. They wrote it down. Under the Meter Dynasty, Sitamun al-Razhalbi gathered the records of the Tomb Cities, of Alparak and of the Meter priests into the Razhalbis Tables at the Great Library of Razhalbis, the largest body of dated observations in the world.</p>

<h2>The Cyracli Models</h2>
<p>The Cyclopi were the great makers of models. They watched from high towers, and in <a href="Starworship" class="internal-link">Starworship</a> they made the sky a matter of faith. About 700BA Meleandros of Hecvos measured the angle between the Sun and Luna Major at the half moon and found it so near a right angle that no instrument could tell them apart, from which he reckoned that the Sun lay very many times farther than the moon and was very much the greater. <a href="Kasthemis" class="internal-link">Kasthemis</a> of Lorqis measured the Globus itself, its girth and its tilt. <a href="Tripparch" class="internal-link">Tripparch</a> of Nyleikos made the Diolagma, the first catalogue of the stars, found the precession of the Globus, and drew the first exact models of the Moons and the Sun, which Starworship enshrined. Tesimene of Lorqis, who kept the eclipses at the Great Library of Lorqis in the 6th century BA, left the Record of Eclipses, in which the shadow of the Globus on the Moon is always round and the Sun is never wholly covered but always leaves a ring.</p>
<p>The Cyracli knew that the Sun was far the greater. But the stars did not shift through the year, and the numbers worked best with a Globus at rest in the middle, so they set the Moons and the Sun on circles about it, and their tables were good.</p>

<h2>The Rakhi Diaries</h2>
<p>The Rakhi were counters. The priests of <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakh</a> kept a nightly diary of the sky, begun by Nabu-Sharrim of Alwazattar about 700BA, and about 440BA Iltani of Entusa found that eclipses of the Moon return in a cycle that can be counted and foretold. Neither cared much what the sky was made of. After the Fall of Cyraclion in 370BA, Cyracli books and a few Cyracli masters came to Rakh, and <a href="Shimun" class="internal-link">Shimun</a> of Hantepe set the models against the diaries and made the Night Tables about 322BA, the first tables that looked forward as well as back. Some two and a half centuries later <a href="Phiodales" class="internal-link">Phiodales</a> of Mizk, whose Conflux Theory is among the oldest research of old Rakh, worked in the same tradition of dated record (see <a href="Conflux" class="internal-link">Conflux</a>).</p>

<h2>The Classical Cosmology</h2>
<p>When Rakh fell in 0PA it left its tables behind in the Urien provinces, and it was there, in the first centuries of the Gethric Church, that the old models were joined in one. Ralzig, an Urien Minister of the Church, wrote the Ordered Heavens about 328PA, and Ralzig's Cosmology became the classical account of the sky in the west: a Globus at rest in the centre, the lights carried round it on clear spheres, and the Homestead Beyond outside them all. It suited the teaching of the Church and it was good at foretelling. For close to six centuries nobody of weight doubted it.</p>

<h2>The Wheeling</h2>
<p>There was one who did. Gahenna of Jiravid, a Beheran woman working among the temple records of the old Tomb City, wrote On the Wheeling of the Globus about 497PA. She held that the Sun is too great to be carried round by the Globus, and that the Globus, with Luna Major and Luna Minor, travels around the Sun once in 400 days, as the other celestial bodies of the heavens do. She built her case on the observations of the elves and the Cyracli and led a small school that the followers of Ralzig called the Wheelers. She had no proof, and her book was forgotten.</p>

<h2>The Long Neglect</h2>
<p>A copy of the Wheeling went west among the elven works that <a href="Galiatar Inkfingers" class="internal-link">Galiatar Inkfingers</a> translated into the Rudimenta Targorae, and it lay unread at <a href="Korsfall College" class="internal-link">Korsfall</a>. The College, founded in 556PA, grew under Galiatar and his student Wulfrick the Elder into the centre of human astronomy and arcane study, and it taught the Ordered Heavens like every other school. The Wulfrick Observatorium was raised there in the elder's memory, and its great telescope, the largest instrument of its kind in the west, was built later. Telescopes are costly, few and crude, and the thick air blurs and dims what they show. Meanwhile the tables of Ralzig drifted from the sky by a day in a few generations, and each generation of astronomers set them right and went on.</p>

<h2>The Return of the Wheeling</h2>
<p>The return of the Broken Star about 845PA was the first to be watched with instruments, and its path would not fit the clear spheres of Ralzig's heavens (see <a href="The Tis" class="internal-link">The Tis</a>). In the 890s the astronomers of <a href="Reanport" class="internal-link">Reanport</a>, under <a href="Korman van Schipwig" class="internal-link">Korman van Schipwig</a>, found the treatise and set it against their own records and those of <a href="Szievek" class="internal-link">Szievek</a>, where the <a href="Eye of Kasimir" class="internal-link">Eye of Kasimir</a>, completed in 899PA, was giving the longest and sharpest run of naked eye observations yet made. The Wheeling kept the Sun and the moons where the sky had them. The Ordered Heavens did not. Reanport began to teach Gahenna beside Ralzig about 897PA, the University of Szievek followed, and in the next years the universities of the west and many of the magi towers, among them Patwin Tower and Edessa Tower, came over. About 916PA the <a href="Karajal School of Insight" class="internal-link">Karajal School of Insight</a>, whose city of Alparak had kept the shadow measures on which Gahenna built, accepted it too. At Korsfall <a href="Ulrike Metzen" class="internal-link">Ulrike Metzen</a>, the Chair of Astronomy, is quoted by both sides. Gahenna is now counted among the greatest of the old astronomers, redeemed by those who once let her book lie.</p>

<h2>The Question in 927PA</h2>
<p>The Wheeling is accepted in the universities, the magi towers and at Karajal, but it is not proven. The strongest argument against it stands as it did in Gahenna's day: if the Globus travels around the Sun, the near stars should shift against the far ones through the year, and no one has ever seen them shift. Her answer, that the stars are too far, is still only an answer. Between the two accounts stands a compromise that keeps the Globus still, sets the Sun circling the Globus and the other celestial bodies circling the Sun, and it is still held by serious astronomers. Starworship keeps the models of Tripparch as doctrine and has made no ruling. The Church of Iztaw has made none either, though its traditionalists look on the new account warily, and its schools still teach Ralzig. The next return of the Broken Star is expected about 1105PA, and astronomers on both sides expect it to teach them more than any lifetime of tables.</p>
