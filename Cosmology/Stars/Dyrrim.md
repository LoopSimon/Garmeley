<div class="wiki-infobox"> <h4 class="wiki-header">Dyrrim</h4> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Constellation</strong> <span>The Knot</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Appearance</strong> <span>A gold and a blue star,<br>close enough to look like one</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Cyclopi Deity</strong> <span>Samiral, God of Oaths,<br>Pacts and Kinship</span> </div> <hr class="wiki-hr"> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Qahori</strong> <span>Kasqari<br>"the Sworn Two"</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Beheran Elvish</strong> <span>Teshun<br>"the Wedding Lamp"</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>High Meadi</strong> <span>Eidpaar<br>"Oath-Pair"</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Urien</strong> <span>Brudrath<br>"the Brother-Pair"</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Gelfir</strong> <span>Laikaru<br>"the Consonant Pair"</span> </div> </div> <p>Dyrrim is the heart of the Knot-Constellation, and is really two stars, one gold and one blue, set so close together that most people see only a single light. Those who can tell the two apart are said to have the keenest eyes, and in many cultures the ability to split the pair is a test of eyesight.</p> <p>Across the world it is the star of promises. Oaths, marriages and treaties are sworn under it, and a pledge made under Dyrrim is held to bind twice, once for each star.</p> <h2>In Qalmyr</h2> <p>To the Qari of the <a href="Qalmyr" class="internal-link">Qalmyr</a> it is Kasqari, "the Sworn Two", two <a href="Qinari" class="internal-link">Qinari</a> born of one flame who refused to part. It is the sky's answer to the <a href="Ikval" class="internal-link">Ikval</a>, a pair that stayed together where Qastor and Zolra did not. Qahori weddings and the oaths of their trading houses are sworn under it.</p> <h2>In Starworship</h2> <p><a href="Starworship" class="internal-link">Starworship</a> counts it among the lesser powers as Samiral, God of Oaths, Pacts and Kinship. The treaties of the League of Cyraclion were sworn under it.</p> <h2>Other Names</h2> <p>The Elves call it Teshun, "the Wedding Lamp", and in High Meadi it is Eidpaar, "Oath-Pair". The Urien call it Brudrath, "the Brother-Pair". In Gelfir it is Laikaru, "the Consonant Pair", and the <a href="Lakalism" class="internal-link">Lakalist</a> faith of the <a href="Twintail Imperium" class="internal-link">Twintail Imperium</a> sees in it the visible proof of divine consonance and symmetry, a reading that sits comfortably with the rare twinned tail of the imperial line.</p>
