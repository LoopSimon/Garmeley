<div class="wiki-infobox"> <h4 class="wiki-header">Sarhen</h4> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Constellation</strong> <span>The Sickle</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Appearance</strong> <span>Brilliant white, the brightest star<br>in the night sky</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Cyclopi Deity</strong> <span>Vessal, God of Time,<br>Seasons and Harvest</span> </div> <hr class="wiki-hr"> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Qahori</strong> <span>Hasteriq<br>"the One Before the Sun"</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Beheran Elvish</strong> <span>Aluen<br>"the Opener"</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>High Meadi</strong> <span>Sonnwart<br>"Sun-warden"</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Urien</strong> <span>Ernath<br>"the Reaper's Lamp"</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Kheri</strong> <span>Nehuru<br>"the Year-Giver"</span> </div> </div> <p>Sarhen is the brightest star of the Sickle-Constellation and the brightest star in the whole night sky, a brilliant white light that no other star rivals. For roughly forty days every year it is lost in the glare of the Sun, and then it returns, appearing low in the east just before dawn. This heliacal rising is the most widely observed event in the sky. From the farms of Garmeley to the cities of the Beheran Subcontinent it is the sign that the year has turned: the winter gales are over, the seas open and the ground can be worked.</p> <h2>The Elbae Calendar</h2> <p>The earliest development of the Elbae <a href="Calendars" class="internal-link">Calendar</a> is poorly understood, but it likely traces back to observations of Sarhen's heliacal rising during the early first Jirahid Dynasty. The Elves call the star Aluen, "the Opener". The calendar's four seasons of ninety days are followed by an intercalary month of forty days that is treated as outside the year proper, which matches the weeks in which Aluen lies hidden in the Sun. The year begins again on the morning it is first seen.</p> <h2>In Qalmyr</h2> <p>To the Qari of the <a href="Qalmyr" class="internal-link">Qalmyr</a> it is Hasteriq, "the One Before the Sun", the <a href="Qinari" class="internal-link">Qinari</a> who walks ahead of Teriq. During its hidden weeks it is said to stand within Teriq's light, and its return at dawn marks the end of Zolra's winter: the storm season breaks and the Qahori put to sea again. The Qahori mark the turn of their year on that morning.</p> <h2>In Starworship</h2> <p>In <a href="Starworship" class="internal-link">Starworship</a> Sarhen is Vessal, God of Time, Seasons and Harvest. He is a lesser power beside <a href="Malham" class="internal-link">Xanring</a> and <a href="Urden" class="internal-link">Shoyal</a>, but the calendar of the League of Cyraclion was kept by his rising.</p> <h2>Among Humans</h2> <p>The Meadi know it as Sonnwart, "Sun-warden", and the Urien as Ernath, "the Reaper's Lamp". The oldest recorded name for it is in Kheri, the oldest human language, where it is Nehuru, "the Year-Giver".</p>
