<div class="wiki-infobox"> <h4 class="wiki-header">Luna Major</h4> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Orbits</strong> <span><a href="Globus" class="internal-link">Globus</a></span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Distance</strong> <span>About 588,000 km</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Orbital Period</strong> <span>About 36 days</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Phase Cycle</strong> <span>39.5 days</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Diameter</strong> <span>About 3,470 km</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Satellite</strong> <span><a href="Luna Minor" class="internal-link">Luna Minor</a></span> </div> <hr class="wiki-hr"> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Qari</strong> <span><a href="Qastor" class="internal-link">Qastor</a><br>"the Pale Father"</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Elves</strong> <span>Milin</span> </div> </div> <p>Luna Major is the great moon of the <a href="Globus" class="internal-link">Globus</a>, the larger and brighter of the two moons that the peoples of the world name. It is about 3,470 km across, a little over a fifth of the width of the Globus, and circles it at an average distance of about 588,000 km. One orbit takes about 36 days, but the Globus moves on around the Sun in the meantime, so the phases take longer, and the moon runs from full to full in 39.5 days, the common lunar month. The Alder Calendar is built on this cycle (see <a href="Calendars" class="internal-link">Calendars</a>).</p> <h2>The Near Face</h2> <p>Luna Major turns once for every orbit, and so it always shows the same face to the Globus. No one on the planet has seen the other side.</p> <p>The face that never turns away carries a scar. About 120,000 years ago a great asteroid struck the near side of the moon and left a crater more than 100 km across, with bright rays running far out over the surface. At this distance the crater itself is too small to be seen as a circle, but the rays show as a bright spot with streaks across the moon's face, always in the same place. Rays of this kind stay bright for a very long time, and the scar still looks fresh. The debris blown out of the crater gathered into <a href="Luna Minor" class="internal-link">Luna Minor</a>.</p> <h2>Eclipses and Tides</h2> <p>Seen from the Globus, Luna Major is smaller than the Sun, about two thirds of its width. When it crosses the Sun's face it cannot cover it completely and leaves a bright ring, so the world knows annular eclipses only and no total eclipse of the Sun. When the moon itself passes into the shadow of the Globus the eclipse is total.</p> <p>The moon is far away, and its tides are modest. The pull of the Sun on the seas is somewhat stronger than its own, and the highest tides come around the new and the full moon, about every twenty days.</p> <h2>In Qalmyr</h2> <p>To the Orcs of the <a href="Qalmyr" class="internal-link">Qalmyr</a> faith the moon is <a href="Qastor" class="internal-link">Qastor</a>, the Pale Father, and he is male. They read the scar on his face as the wound where <a href="Zolra" class="internal-link">Zolra</a> was torn from him in the <a href="Ikval" class="internal-link">Ikval</a>, and the small bright moon that circles him as the one thing he cast away that can never leave him. The elves call the moon Milin.</p>
