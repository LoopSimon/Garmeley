<p>The Sun is the star at the centre of the solar system of the <a href="Globus" class="internal-link">Globus</a> and the source of nearly all the light and warmth that the world receives. It is a G-type star that holds almost all of the mass of its system, and it formed about 4.6 billion years ago, some fifty million years before the Globus and a hundred million years before <a href="Luna Major" class="internal-link">Luna Major</a>.</p>

<h2>In the Sky</h2>
<p>The Globus circles the Sun once in 400 days at an average distance of about 159 million km, on a slightly stretched path that carries it from about 151 million km at its nearest to about 168 million km at its farthest. From there the Sun is about half a degree wide, and the sunlight that reaches the world is weaker than it might be, so that the thick air of the Globus has to do much of the work of keeping the world warm. At present the world is nearest to the Sun in the southern summer, which is why the south has short, fierce summers and long cold winters. The Sun itself turns once in about 25 days at its equator.</p>
<p>Luna Major is smaller than the Sun in the sky, about two thirds of its width, and so it can never cover it. The world knows annular eclipses only, in which a bright ring is left around the dark moon. The Sun also hides the stars that stand near it. The brightest of them, <a href="Sarhen" class="internal-link">Sarhen</a>, is lost in the Sun's glare for about forty days of every year, and its return at dawn marks the turn of the year, while the North Star <a href="Malham" class="internal-link">Malham</a> is never lost.</p>

<h2>In Culture</h2>
<p>To the Orcs of the <a href="Qalmyr" class="internal-link">Qalmyr</a> faith the Sun is Teriq, the giver of life and the nearest and greatest of the <a href="Qinari" class="internal-link">Qinari</a>, and the elves call it Var. The Orcish word Niryd, the Land of the Sun, gave <a href="Nirydia" class="internal-link">Nirydia</a> its name.</p>
<p>Among many peoples the Sun is more than a star. The Zuhurists of Nirydia face it in prayer and are called Sun Worshippers, or Yellow Eyes, for they believe it to be the home of Zuhur (see <a href="Zuhurism" class="internal-link">Zuhurism</a>). The <a href="Vas Ira" class="internal-link">Vas Ira</a> of the Bihra picture the creator Haizu in a similar fashion, as a sun god who dwells in Shila, the Palace of the Sun. The Pravazik know <a href="Gianor" class="internal-link">Gianor</a>, Who Crafts the Light, as the Yari of Truth, Illumination and the Sun, and yet place the moons above him, since the moons can be seen by night and by day (see <a href="Arishi" class="internal-link">Arishi</a>). The early Urien told of Ziuva, God of the Sky and the Sun (see <a href="Turvik Paganism" class="internal-link">Turvik Paganism</a>), the Arevi centre their faith on the Spirit of the Sun (see <a href="Arevi Shamanism" class="internal-link">Arevi Shamanism</a>), and the Rakhians told of Chimar the Sun Hunter (see <a href="Rakhian Mythology" class="internal-link">Rakhian Mythology</a>).</p>
<p>The oldest roots of <a href="Ilonism" class="internal-link">Ilonism</a> are likely a tradition built on the alignment of the two moons with the Sun, the three great bodies of the observable sky, and signs of sun worship in the Asef and Hessef reach back thousands of years. The Cyclopi of <a href="Cyraclion" class="internal-link">Cyraclion</a> bore an Eyed Sun in their arms.</p>
