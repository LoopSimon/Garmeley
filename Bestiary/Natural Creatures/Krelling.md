<p>The Krelling is a small nocturnal rodent found across southern <a href="Ilrakhan" class="internal-link">Ilrakhan</a>, northern <a href="Behera" class="internal-link">Behera</a>, western <a href="Nirydia" class="internal-link">Nirydia</a> and the island of <a href="Il Haena" class="internal-link">Il-Haena</a>, from which it is generally believed to originate. In appearance it resembles a fat, pale blue-grey mouse with a  rounded body, tiny limbs, a single large dark eye, and a surface of faint scale like bumps across its back giving it a texture that distinguishes it from ordinary rodents upon close inspection. Its most immediately striking feature is its tail: enormously long relative to the body, whip like, prehensile, ending in a small flower shaped or claw like tip, capable of coiling around objects with surprising strength for an animal of its size. The Krelling is not a remarkable creature by any conventional measure of danger or beauty. What makes it remarkable is what the tip of that tail does when it makes contact with another living thing.</p> <p>The Krelling conducts what western scholars call "planar energy" through its body in a manner that naturalists have not fully explained to their collective satisfaction, the prevailing theory involves the tail's unusual tip as the primary organ for this channeling, with the energy discharged on contact producing in larger animals, including humans, a sensation of profound drowsiness or gentle sedation. The mechanism almost certainly evolved as a hunting adaptation, allowing the Krelling to subdue insects and small prey that it would otherwise be unable to overpower. The discovery that the same touch produces a pleasant, opiate-like response in humans and other large mammals has had the predictable consequence: Krellings are kept as pets across their range, with some owners allowing the tail tip contact deliberately and repeatedly for recreational purposes. Scholars of planar energy find this mildly interesting. Physicians find it mildly concerning. The Krellings themselves appear entirely indifferent to either opinion, going about their nocturnal business with the equanimity of animals that have accidentally become useful to people much larger than themselves.</p>
