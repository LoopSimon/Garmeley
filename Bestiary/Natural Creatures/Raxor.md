<p>The Raxor, known in <a href="Meadland" class="internal-link">meadi</a> folklore and chivalric tradition as "The Questing Beast", is the most rarely encountered and least understood large predator in Ilrakhan. Its body is that of a spotted great cat, golden yellow with dark rosettes, powerful and fast, the size of a large leopard or small lion. From the shoulders rises a long serpentine neck covered in grey-blue scales rather than fur, terminating in a fully reptilian head, it is fanged, forked-tongued, yellow-eyed, the scales hard and iridescent in certain lights. A whipping serpentine tail completes the hybrid, and the overall impression in the rare documented sightings is of something heraldic made flesh, a creature that looks as though it was designed rather than evolved. It has been encountered in the ancient forests of Meadland, the deep woodland of Ur, the highland passes of Vihr,as far east as the Valleys of Othirdon or the Peaks of Sertova and the moorland of Wermia, never in sufficient numbers to establish a population, always alone, always briefly. <a href="Hubert of Tanverley" class="internal-link">Hubert of Tanverley</a> spent three years attempting to document a confirmed sighting and produced instead a chapter he titled "On the Raxor, or What I Believe I Saw at the Edge of the Weirwood in the Autumn of My Forty-Second Year," which scholars consider his most honest work.</p> 

<p>The Raxor is intelligent, this much is established by the consistency with which encounters unfold. It does not behave as prey animals do when observed, nor as territorial predators do when their space is invaded. Those who have found themselves in the presence of a Raxor describe being studied rather than threatened, assessed rather than hunted, and then watching it depart in whatever direction it chooses, at whatever pace it considers appropriate, with no acknowledgment. In Meadi knightly culture the pursuit of the Raxor, the Questing Beast of the old stories, is one of the great individual undertakings, a lifelong endeavour undertaken by knights of particular temperament who believe that the creature can be followed, that following it leads somewhere meaningful, and that what it means to finally stand in its presence and have it choose to remain is something that cannot be explained to those who have not experienced it. Whether the Raxor is aware of those who pursue it and makes its own decisions about the encounter is always somewhat in doubt.</p>
