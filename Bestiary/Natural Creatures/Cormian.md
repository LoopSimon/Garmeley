<p>The Cormian is a bipedal predator of the mountain ranges and adjacent wetlands of central Ilrakhan, found along the Kortopi and Trollback ranges and in the boggy ground at their feet, a creature whose specialization for the areas between land and standing water has produced something that appears almost like several different animals assembled without sufficient care. The body is roughly human sized and upright, covered in dark greenish brown shaggy fur or feathering that blends effectively with the reed beds and murky shallows it favours. The head is long and narrow, dominated by an elongated toothed bill reminiscent of a gharial's snout but covered in bare scabrous skin rather than scales, the teeth visible and numerous. The forelimbs terminate in widely splayed reptilian claws suited to wet ground, and a long dragging tail provides balance in the soft mud where the Cormian spends most of its time.</p> 

<p>The Cormians bite is widely understood to carry infection, whether this is a deliberate biological adaptation or simply the consequence of spending its time in stagnant water and carrion is debated by scholars who have not agreed on an answer yet. Communities in the Kortopi foothills treat a Cormian bite as a medical emergency requiring immediate and thorough treatment, a precaution that experience has validated often enough to become standard practice. The creature is not aggressive in the manner of territorial predators and it will retreat from a determined human approach without fighting, but it will take anything that comes within reach without warning. Its famous patience in waiting for that opportunity has resulted in a Cormian being the last thing seen by a sufficient number of people washing clothes or collecting water at dawn that the mountain communities of Ur and Vihr maintain specific folk customs around the safe use of isolated pools and slow streams. It appears in several Vihri bestiaries from the 6th and 7th centuries, usually described with an accuracy suggesting the illustrators had encountered one personally and survived the experience only narrowly.</p>
