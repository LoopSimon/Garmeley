<p>The Gryphon is a large predatory creature found throughout the mountain ranges of <a href="Ur" class="internal-link">Ur</a> and the surrounding highlands, the Kortopi to the west the Trollback Mountains to the east, and the untamed hill country within the region itself. With the body and hindquarters of a lion, the head, wings and forelegs of a great eagle, and a temperament that has made it the most feared animal in the hills of Ur since before recorded history, the Gryphon occupies the apex of its ecosystem absolutely, as the famous urien saying goes "nothing hunts a Gryphon".</p> 
<p>The Gryphon hunts everything else, and what it does not hunt it takes from those who did, a bonded pair of Gryphons will drive wolves, bears and big cats alike from their kills with the casual authority of animals that have never needed to develop a sense of caution. <a href="Hubert of Tanverley" class="internal-link">Hubert of Tanverley</a>, who observed a bonded pair in the Trollback foothills from what he described as "a distance I would not recommend reducing" during the research for his third volume, noted that the sound of their wings in a shallow dive was audible from nearly half a mile.</p>

<p>Gryphons are pair bonded for life, mated pairs holding and defending their territories together against all competitors including other Gryphons, territorial disputes between pairs are among the most spectacular and violent encounters the highlands of Ur produce, though they are as established pairs have usually settled boundaries over years of mutual acknowledgment. They hunt large game primarily, mountain goats, deer, wild horses in the lowland approaches, but are equally willing to descend on livestock and humanoids who venture into their range without sufficient awareness of what shares the heights with them. The knights of the <a href="Urbridge" class="internal-link">Order of Urbridge</a> maintain a tradition of riding out to attempt the taming of a Gryphon, in honour of the old <a href="Knights of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">Gryphon riders</a> said to have resisted the Rakhian conquest from their backs. The tradition persists in 927PA, but so does the overwhelming likelihood that it ends in the knights death. The few who survive the attempt without taming the animal are considered fortunate.</p>
