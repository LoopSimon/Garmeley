<p>The Barlon is encountered in the deep caverns and tunnel networks of the <a href="Underlands" class="internal-link">Underlands</a> a predator of the subterranean dark that hunts <a href="Goblinoid" class="internal-link">goblins</a>, <a href="Dwarf" class="internal-link">dwarves</a> and whatever else moves through the passages it has claimed as territory. Its surface appearance, on the rare occasions it emerges into mountain forests or moorland above ground, produces in most observers an immediate and visceral reaction that precedes any rational assessment of the danger. The Barlon stands roughly at human height, bipedal, its body entirely obscured by dense black and dark-purple fur that extends outward in all directions and makes it impossible to judge what shape lies beneath. Two large ridged horns of deep red-orange rise from the top of the mass. A wide triangular mouth, pale skinned, set low and ringed with recurved teeth faces forward. Its arms are bare skinned, extremely long, hanging almost to the ground and end in multi fingered clawed hands, its feet are similarly bare and clawed.</p> <p>Communities that live in proximity to Barlon territory, dwarven communities in the Kortopi and <a href="Trollbacks" class="internal-link">Trollback</a> ranges primarily, maintain a body of folk wisdom about them that varies considerably in its reliability. The most persistent claim, passed through Underlands communities as well as surface folklore, recurring in the stories of the <a href="Bykmåli" class="internal-link">Bykmåli</a> and other northern peoples, who have encountered ranging individuals, is that the Barlon moves faster in the presence of fear. That a fleeing, panicking target covers the same ground considerably more slowly than one that manages not to respond to the creature with terror, and that calm or indifference somehow diminishes the Barlons effectiveness as a predator. Whether this reflects a genuine biological mechanism by which the Barlon perceives and responds to physiological stress in its prey, or whether frightened prey simply make worse decisions and perceive their pursuer as faster than it is, nobody knows for certain. The experimental verification of this claim has not attracted volunteers.</p>
