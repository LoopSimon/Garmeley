<p>The Owlbear is a large predatory creature found throughout the woodlands and forested hill country of central Ilrakhan and beyond, most commonly encountered in <a href="Ur" class="internal-link">Ur</a> and <a href="Othirdon" class="internal-link">Othirdon</a>, where its territorial range overlaps with both settled farmland and the deep forest that the region's more isolated communities depend upon. The body is that of a bear, massive and low slung, covered in feather like fur of mottled brown and tawny that provides effective camouflage in the dappled light of forest undergrowth. The head is unmistakably that of a great horned owl with tufted ear like projections above the brow, and a sharp curved beak capable of considerable force. The forelimbs terminate in talons rather than ordinary bear claws, curved and substantial, and it is these that account for the majority of injuries inflicted on those unfortunate enough to encounter one at close range. <a href="Hubert of Tanverley" class="internal-link">Hubert of Tanverley</a> dedicated eleven pages of his second volume to the Owlbear, more than any other single creature, which his editors took as enthusiasm and his readers eventually came to understand as a warning.</p> 

<p>Owlbears are solitary and profoundly territorial, their ranges covering several square miles of forest that they patrol with the methodical consistency of animals that have learned their ground thoroughly and resent the presence of anything larger than a rabbit on it. They are primarily nocturnal hunters, their owl eyes giving them a decisive advantage in conditions that reduce human and elven night vision to near uselessness, though they will hunt at any hour if hungry or provoked. The creature's call, a sound that those who have heard it describe variously as a shriek, a roar and something that has no adequate comparison, carries considerable distance through forest and is used both to mark territory and to disorient prey. Settlements in the othiri woodland country take Owlbear presence seriously, maintaining watch schedules and keeping livestock in fortified enclosures during the months when territorial males range furthest. The creature appears in several Urien folk traditions as an emblem of the forests indifference to human concerns, present in woodcut illustrations from at least the 4th century PA, usually in the act of demonstrating that indifference to someone who should have been more careful.</p>
