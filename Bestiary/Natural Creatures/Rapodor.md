<p>The Rapodor is a winged fox native to the open moorland, grassland and heath of northern <a href="Ur" class="internal-link">Ur</a> and <a href="Wermia" class="internal-link">Wermia</a>, where its combination of ground hunting and aerial capability makes it one of the more adaptable small predators of the region. In appearance it resembles a common red fox closely enough that at distance or in poor light the distinction is easily missed. The difference becomes apparent when the Rapodor moves: a pair of feathered wings, brown and grey barred like a hawks, emerge from the shoulders and carry the animal into the air with a speed that startles observers expecting a fox. The forepaws have partially transformed into taloned gripping feet capable of seizing and holding prey considerably larger than those of an ordinary fox, and it is these that make the Rapodor genuinely dangerous despite its modest size. A creature roughly the weight of a large rabbit that can approach at ground level, launch itself airborne without warning, and strike with talons before its target has registered the change in attack vector is a more serious hazard than its appearance suggests.</p> 

<p>Rapodors hunt in the manner of a fox supplemented by occasional aerial strikes, coursing through grassland and heath in pursuit of small mammals, then using their wings to run down faster prey or to take birds from below in a strike that most avian prey never anticipates. They are common enough in Wermia that rural communities regard them as a nuisance rather than a danger, their primary impact being on poultry rather than people, and Wermian folk art frequently depicts them in the characteristic mid pounce posture over field mice and rabbits. In Ur they are less abundant but more feared. Wermian hunters have occasionally attempted to train Rapodors as hunting companions in the manner of falconry, the animals intelligence is sufficient for basic conditioning and it will work alongside a patient handler, but the combination of fox temperament and raptor instincts makes reliable domestication rare enough that trained specimens command considerable prices.</p>
