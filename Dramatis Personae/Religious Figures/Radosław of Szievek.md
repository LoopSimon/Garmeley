<p>Radosław of Szievek, called the Lawgiver, was Bishop of <a href="Szievek" class="internal-link">Szievek</a> and a master of its University, and the compiler of the <a href="Speculum Regni" class="internal-link">Speculum Regni</a>, the great codex of the laws of <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a>. Commissioned in 724PA by Ras <a href="Vormil I" class="internal-link">Vormil I</a> and the High-Bisop of Iztaw, he spent seventeen years gathering the canon law of the Church, the rights of the crown, the customs of the Vadcy and the charters of the realm into one work, and he completed it in 741PA. He is remembered as the man who first wrote down the laws of Vihr, and as the teacher of Nidislaw the Keen.</p>   
<h2>Early Life</h2>   
<p>Radosław was born in Szievek in 681PA, the son of a notary of the city, and grew up among the deeds, wills and contracts of his father's trade. He studied at the University and entered the Church, completed the Tregesia and was ordained a Minister, with the right to practise clerical law. He sat for years as a judge in the bishop's court of Szievek, where he became known for his patience and for his memory, and it was said that he could quote the custom of any Vadcy on any question put to him, though none of it was written anywhere.</p>   
<p>In 715PA he was raised to the Bishopric of Szievek, and he kept his chair at the University beside it.</p>   
<h2>The Speculum Regni</h2>   
<p>In 724PA Vormil I and the High-Bisop jointly commissioned Radosław to compile the laws of the realm. He took his method from Saint <a href="Palus" class="internal-link">Palus Aergestes</a>, the father of the <a href="Wermian Accords" class="internal-link">Wermian Accords</a>, who had brought the rulers of Ilrakhan to agree on a single document by letting each of them speak. Radosław would not write law himself, nor let the crown or the Church write it through him. Between 725 and 738PA he travelled from Vadcy to Vadcy and convened a congregation in each, at which the Var, the Gri and the elders of the land declared their custom before him, and his clerks wrote their words down as they were spoken.</p>   
<p>The lords distrusted him at first, for they had never let their prerogatives be put on parchment and feared that a written custom could be read against them. Radosław is said to have answered them that he held the mirror and did not paint the face, and the codex took its name from the saying. He was patient with the proud and stubborn with the powerful, and by the end of the congregations even the Var who had refused him at first had sent their elders to speak.</p>   
<p>The work was done at Szievek, by Radosław and the clerks and masters of the University, and the Speculum Regni was completed in 741PA, a year before the death of the Ras who had commissioned it.</p>   
<h2>Teaching</h2>   
<p>Radosław taught at the University for most of his life, and many of the Ministers and royal judges of the next generation learned their law from him. His most famous student was Nidislaw the Keen, the youngest son of Vormil I, who followed him into the Church and later became a renowned educator at Szievek himself.</p>   
<h2>Death and Legacy</h2>   
<p>Radosław died at Szievek in 752PA, at the age of seventy-one, and was buried in the cathedral of the city. He did not live to see what his codex would be used for. In 763PA, when the <a href="Gethric Pogroms" class="internal-link">Gethric Pogroms</a> were ratified, the bishops' courts tried the accused under the canon book of the Speculum, and its clear procedure made the persecution swift and orderly. Nidislaw argued against the Pogroms with his teacher's book in his hand.</p>   
<p>Radosław's reputation has survived it. The Vihri call him the Lawgiver, though he gave no law of his own, and his Speculum, in the revised edition of Jakub II, is still read in every court of the realm. His original manuscript lies in the library of Szievek University.</p>
