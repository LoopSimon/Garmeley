<p>Adzul I was the <a href="Kethricism" class="internal-link">Archbishop of Kherova</a> and subsequently its first Patriarch, the theologian and scholar whose intellectual work produced the Doctrine of Dualism and whose institutional courage drove the Kethric Declaration of 471PA that established Kethricism as a fully independent denomination. Born in 399PA in Erbil, he entered the Kherovan clergy as a young man and rose through its ranks during the decades of mounting tension that followed the canonisation of Tronos at the Conclave of 410PA. The controversy over Tronos was not merely a political grievance but the catalyst that compelled him to articulate formally what the Incursion had always implied. Over the following decades he assembled the theological framework that became the Doctrine of Dualism, convening the First Council of Opuzna in 458PA to debate and codify it. The Doctrines central argument, that divine power and arcane power were categorically different in origin and spiritual consequence, the former an expression of personal conviction exerted outward and the latter a self indulgent corruption of the soul that drew on forces external to the self, was his in its systematic form. An idea built from the accumulated cultural history of a people who had known what arcane excess produced long before the Gethric mission arrived among them.</p> <p>When <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">High-Bisop Taromir I</a> rejected the Doctrine without qualification, Adzul convened the Second Council of Volazc in 471PA and led the assembled bishops in issuing the Kethric Declaration. The Declaration transformed him from Archbishop of Kherova to first Patriarch of Kherova in the same act, and he governed the newly independent Kethric church for eight more years before his death in 479PA, long enough to see the institution he had founded take root, not long enough to see whether it would endure across generations. It has endured for 456 years. Within Kethric tradition Adzul I is venerated with an intensity that approaches and in many communities exceeds the veneration afforded to the canonical Saints. A movement in the 7th century sought his formal canonisation, and the theological case was not easily dismissed. The effort foundered on the same political reality that Adzul himself had navigated: canonisation is a Gethric institution, and the only bishop ever elevated to sainthood within it was <a href="Volboris" class="internal-link">Volboris</a> himself. To canonise Adzul would have required either accepting Iztaw's authority to do so, which the Kethric Declaration had explicitly rejected, or the Kherovan Patriarchate claiming a power it had never asserted, at a moment when relations between the two traditions had grown from hostile to just cautious. The movement came to nothing. Adzul I remains uncanonised and irreplaceable, a figure whose memory the Kethric church carries with the kind of devotion that formal sainthood would, in a sense, diminish.</p>
