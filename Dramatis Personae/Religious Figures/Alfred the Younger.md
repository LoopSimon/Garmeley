<p>
The Bishop of Szivek during the later 9th century, Alfred Hinri, better known as Alfred the Younger, was a very progressive bishop for gethric standards and made efforts to connect the clerical communities of Vihr with the arcane scholars of the west. His works on the Chronology of Study regarding the Arcane and Divine are internationally respected and used for entry level education into magical theory.</p>
<p>Alfred was born in Brov, on the coast of the Torn Sea north of Iztaw and west of Travlov into moderate wealth and rose through the <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">Clergy</a> at a moderate pace, showing interests in wider academia at a young age. His appointment to the Bishopric of Szievek, one of the most important Seats in the Clergy not on in Vihr but in all of Ilrakhan, occurred in 882 at the age of 41 under great controversy. Not only was he a little young for some of the more traditionalist ministers of the clergy, but a reputation around his scholarly interests and ties to western bishops, trinicists thought leaders and meadi institutions such as <a href="Korsfall College" class="internal-link">Korsfall College</a> had already well developed.</p>
<p>Alfred, known as Alfred the Younger as he came up under the tutelage of Bishop Alfred Zoitok "the Elder" of Tlokyemy, would hold the Seat for 27 years and in these nearly three decades make strides in connecting the east and west on matters of the theological through matters of the arcane. He was the instructor of infamous Mage and later tried criminal Olena Nargu, held many lectures at Szievek University and even visited Hevn, Uhl and Korsfall on numerous occasions. His trilogy of entry level books known as "A History of Magic" is widely regarded as a great chronology of how mortals have perceived and understood the supernatural throughout the ages, even referencing works from outside the classical "western canon" such as the nirydian <a href="The Anak" class="internal-link">Anak</a>.</p>
