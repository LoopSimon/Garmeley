	<img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Kisras.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;">  </div> <p>Waśko zi Ralpia is the Kisras of Vihr, the eldest son and heir of <a href="Vormil III" class="internal-link">Ras Vormil III</a> and Gri of Lovno. Since his father withdrew to the greater business of the realm he has also held the rule of <a href="Zavlech" class="internal-link">Zavlech</a>. He is thirty-three, unmarried and childless, and by his father's own judgement the most capable of the Ras's children by a long way. He is the leading voice at court for a smaller role for the Church in government, for better schools, and for a reform of the Honoured Assembly.</p>  <p>Waśko was born about 894PA in Zavlech, the first child of Vormil III and Gras Maroza zi Wogrov, the sister of the Var of <a href="Rovgi" class="internal-link">Rovgi</a>. That makes the lord of the Oathhills his uncle, and the Bishop of Wogrov Cathedral, who is by tradition the Archbishop of Vihr, a prelate of his mother's own country. He was raised at court and schooled at Zavlech Academy and at the University of Szievek, and from his youth he was known as a serious and watchful boy who would rather read than ride. His mother died in 915PA in giving birth to his youngest sister Anissa, when he was twenty-one, and his father did not marry again.</p> <h3>The Kisras</h3> <p>As heir, Waśko holds the castle of Lovno, a day's ride north of Zavlech, and the title Gri that goes with it. He seldom stays there. Ras Vormil, who is old and occupied, has left the rule of the capital to him, and he governs it from the palace on the lake. Zavlech gives him the means to attempt what he wants. The Academy is on his doorstep, the Towers of Open Skies of the Kar-Zavr stand beside his palace, and the Sekretar's offices are a short walk away.</p> <h3>Reforms</h3> <p>Waśko has three aims. The first is the schools. He wants Zavlech Academy raised toward the standing of the University of Szievek, and the clerkships of the court opened to scholars of any birth. The second is the Church. He would have clerics out of the royal chancery, the tax rolls and the royal courts, and leave to the Church its spiritual office and its own ground. He is a sincere Gethric and has no wish to break with Iztaw. The third is the crown itself. He wants to centralise power somewhat and to reform the <a href="Honoured Assembly" class="internal-link">Honoured Assembly</a>, but how far he would go has never been said, and the lords of the Vadcy have reason to guess at the worst.</p> <h3>Court and Family</h3> <p>The Sekretar, <a href="Tadeuz Pilsud" class="internal-link">Tadeuz Pilsud</a>, is thought to be his strongest friend at court, though few understand what Pilsud himself wants. Waśko's dealings with the High-Bisop Orthel II are polite and cold. With his brother <a href="Jakub Ralpia" class="internal-link">Jakub</a> there is a rift of temperament. Waśko is cautious, Jakub impulsive, and Jakub's part in the Garmish war has done nothing to heal it. The Ras and his heir are estranged, but not broken, and Vormil knows well that Waśko is the better king.</p> <p>His sisters bring their own business. Yelizaveta is being sent to marry the son and heir of Ildan IV, the Ras of <a href="Dalniz" class="internal-link">Dalniz</a>, a match which says something for her political sense and also, awkwardly, for how long her brother has put off his own marriage. Their relationship is unclear. Anissa is twelve, quiet, born when her father was already old, and she loves her eldest brother.</p> <h3>Character</h3> <p>Waśko is grave, learned and cool of manner. He is liked by scholars, and by the merchants and burghers who have risen since the Wolfsplague, who see in him a king who would hear them. The old nobility and the clergy mistrust him. He is no soldier, and the tournaments have never been his place. Rumours about his private life have followed him for years, about whom he prefers and what he does with his evenings, and he has never confirmed or denied any of them. The court talks the more of it because he has not married.</p> <p>Jakub stands second in line, and the unresolved matter of Waśko's marriage is the one weakness his enemies can count on. Whether he will win the lords before he comes to the throne, or lose them, is the question that the court asks behind its hands.</p>
