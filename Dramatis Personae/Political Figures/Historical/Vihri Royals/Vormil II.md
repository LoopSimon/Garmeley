<p>Vormil II zi Ralpia, called Axebreaker, was Ras of <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a> from 822 to 849PA. Before he was king he governed the realm for his brother Viago VII in all but name, and he came to the throne with a reputation for competence. He spent it on the <a href="Brothers War" class="internal-link">Brothers War</a> against the Kingdom of <a href="Halgrach" class="internal-link">Halgrach</a>, which he began in 828PA and in which he lost an eye and nearly his life at the Battle under the Red Cloud. The war ended in 835PA in a white peace that Vihr counted as a humiliation, and the name Axebreaker, which he had taken with pride, became a joke at his expense. He is remembered badly in Vihr to this day, even by those who are otherwise inclined to be fair to him.</p>  
<h2>Early Life</h2>  
<p>Vormil was born in Zavlech in 790PA, the younger son of Ras <a href="Miskomir IV" class="internal-link">Miskomir IV</a> and Queen Jadwiga of House Tryn. He grew up in the years of his father's sewers and bathhouses and of the <a href="Third War of Elbae Aggression" class="internal-link">Third War of Elbae Aggression</a>, and as a young man he took after his mother's martial kin more than after his careful father. Around 811PA he married Agnieszka zi Brov, the sister of Gri Mezekil zi Brov, a captain of the royal fleet, and their eldest son Jerziv was born the following year.</p>  
<h2>Brother of the King</h2>  
<p>Miskomir IV died in 813PA, and Vormil's elder brother became Ras as Viago VII. Viago reigned for nine years and is not much remembered. He saw the Third War to its end and the Treaty of Danipol signed in 815PA, the first formal peace with the Sultanate, for which the Gethric hardliners called him "Weak Viago", though the name never caught on beyond them. He took a great many women besides his wife but never fathered a child, legitimate or otherwise, and it was rumoured that he could not. In his last two years he suffered terribly from the gout and grew angry and, some said, callous.</p>  
<p>For most of Viago's reign it was Vormil who ruled the realm in his name. He received the envoys and wrote the letters, sat with the councillors and the treasurers, and dealt with the Var of the <a href="Honoured Assembly" class="internal-link">Honoured Assembly</a> on his brother's behalf. When Viago died childless in 822PA, worn down by the gout and a failing of the kidneys that the physicians of the Medicae could not treat, the crown passed to Vormil without dispute. He was thirty-two.</p>  
<h2>Reign</h2>  
<h3>The Road to War</h3>  
<p>Vihr had always thought of Halgrach as its long lost little brother. The Grachi were the descendants of Vihri settlers, and their kingdom had grown out of the eastern colonies of Vihr. Under the later kings of the House of Stir, however, Halgrach had pressed ever harder on the merchants of the <a href="Trazlov Consortium" class="internal-link">Trazlov Consortium</a> and on the other traders of Vihr, and a great supply of Graccum, a precious and newly found substance, had been discovered in the Grachhyll that divided the two realms. Vormil, who had spent nine years making the best of his brother's peace, wanted a victory of his own. In 828PA he went to war.</p>  
<p>The arms of Halgrach showed a great axe aflame, and at the outset of the war Vormil swore to break it, and took the name Axebreaker. He wore it proudly. He rode to war in a suit of golden armour made for him by the dwarven smiths of <a href="Xer-Kol" class="internal-link">Xer-Kol</a>, under his own green version of the royal arms, and many who saw him go said that he looked like a saint marching out to slay the demons of the Incursions. He limped home cursed and mocked.</p>  
<h3>The Brothers War</h3>  
<p>The war began well. The royal fleet under his brother-in-law Mezekil zi Brov, supported by the ships of the Consortium, took Fidograd and burned much of the Grachi fleet, and the Vihri armies laid siege to Istrov. But Ras <a href="Oleksandr IV" class="internal-link">Oleksandr IV</a> of Halgrach was a far better soldier than Vormil, and in 830PA he struck back. Vormil commanded in person at the Battle under the Red Cloud, near the Hyllpass, and was struck down by a magical warhammer. He lost an eye, and the wound carried a curse that no physician could close. From then on a priest of the Church who was capable of divine magic attended him day and night to hold the curse in check.</p>  
<p>The king never took the field again. Command passed to Idrov zi Sribo, the Var of <a href="Selbia" class="internal-link">Selbia</a>, who won the last great Vihri victory of the war at Kanyak Keep in 831PA, but the Vihri were pushed back over the following years across the whole of the Iron Claw. The war was saved for Vihr only by the collapse of Halgrach from within, when Oleksandr died at Gorling Vale in 834PA and the Grachi made themselves a republic. Their generals sued for peace, and in 835PA the war ended in a white peace. Vihr gained nothing, and Tuskwatch at the end of the Hyllpass stayed in Grachi hands. For the full account, see <a href="Brothers War" class="internal-link">Brothers War</a>.</p>  
<p>Halgrach was gone, and in a sense the axe had been broken. But no one in Vihr believed that Vormil had broken it, and the name Axebreaker, said of a half-blind king who had lost the war he started, took on a mocking tone almost at once.</p>  
<h3>Later Years</h3>  
<p>Vormil reigned for fourteen more years and grew bitter in them. He kept to Zavlech and rarely showed himself, his cursed wound always tended, and the lords of the Honoured Assembly, who had paid for the war in men and silver, grew bolder with him than they had been with any king in a century. He took it ill when Gasinik, the mage of Halgrach whose divinations had guided the ambushes of Vihri supply caravans in the Grachhyll, was questioned after the war and found not guilty of breaking the <a href="Orenburg Pact" class="internal-link">Orenburg Pact</a>. When the Archbishop of Ur called the faithful to the <a href="League of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">League of the Gryphon</a> in 841PA, Vihr stayed away, to the embarrassment of many at the Gethric courts.</p>  
<h2>Death</h2>  
<p>The curse in the king's wound was never broken, only held. In 849PA his physicians and priests resolved to try a new and experimental treatment, burning the curse out of the wound with holy fire. Vormil agreed to it, and it killed him. He was fifty-nine, and his son succeeded him as Jerziv II.</p>  
<h2>Legacy</h2>  
<p>Vihr never forgot the shame of the Brothers War, and Vormil II is remembered badly. In common speech an "axebreaker" is a man who boasts of what he cannot do. His defenders, few as they are, point to the years in which he governed well for his brother, and to the fact that he paid for his war with his eye and in the end with his life. In <a href="Grachy" class="internal-link">Grachy</a>, where the war is called the Great Southern War, he is remembered mainly as the king who could not beat Oleksandr.</p>
