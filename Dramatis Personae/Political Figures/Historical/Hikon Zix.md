	<img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Hikan.png" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"></div> <p>Hikon Zix was a gnomish advisor at the court of King Thormace of <a href="Eldmar" class="internal-link">Eldmar</a>, and the man whom the victors of the Lavellan Coup blamed for the weakness of the last Hornsley king. He is remembered in the valley as the sorcerer who held a king's mind in his jewels, and by those who doubt the tale as the scapegoat of a coup. Where he came from is not known.</p>  <p>Zix spent about eleven years at the Hornsley court, where he enjoyed wealth and power. He made the king and many of the courtiers great gifts, and Thormace came to rely on him in everything, until the Council of the Vale was summoned less and less and the counts and barons saw more of the gnome than they did of the king. Little of his own words is recorded.</p>  <p>When <a href="Moira I" class="internal-link">Moira of Lavellan</a> took the crown in 822PA, her party gave it out that Zix had controlled the feeble mind of the king with powerful enchanted jewels. Under the <a href="Wermian Accords" class="internal-link">Wermian Accords</a>, which forbid any spell or charm that influences or alters another's mind, this was a grave charge, and the one man who might have answered it was already silenced.</p>  <p>Zix was apprehended in the first night of the coup, and his lips were sewn shut the moment he was taken. No trial is recorded. Following the coronation of Moira as Queen in Eldmar, he was quartered by four horses before the court. The jewels that he had given away were gathered up and smashed, though there are those who say that some were never found.</p> <h2>Doubts</h2> <p>Many have asked since whether any of the jewels were enchanted at all. Gnomes were few at the Urien courts, and the old suspicion of their folk gave the tale of the enchanted jewels a belief that a tale of a counsellor of the king's own kind might not have found. Those who doubt it hold that Thormace was only a very weak king who had put his trust in a clever and generous counsellor, and that the Lavellans, who needed a villain for a coup, made one of the gnome. The question can no longer be settled, since the only witness had his lips sewn shut before he could be asked.</p>
