<p>Zervind II zi Zav was Ras of <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a> from 375PA until his death in 388PA, and is remembered primarily as the ruler under whose reign the Lords Crisis of 377PA and the establishment of the Honoured Assembly in 384PA permanently altered the political makeup of the Rascy. The epithet Kneelgood, nowhere recorded during his lifetime and first appearing in the satirical literature of the generation that followed him, reflects a contemptuous summary of a king who had knelt to the Church when he should have stood and then knelt to the lords when they gave him no other choice. That this reading hides a more complicated personal history into a single unflattering image did not prevent it from becoming the dominant one. His reign produced one of the most consequential institutional settlements in Vihri history, and he emerged from it with less authority than any Ras before him.</p>

<h3>Early Life and Accession</h3>

<p>Zervind was born in 329PA as the second son of Kisras Danizk zi Zav, the crown prince and eldest son of Ras Jasper II. His childhood was shaped early by loss as Danizk died in 343PA during the Doves Plague, the epidemic of pulmonary sickness that ravaged the ports of the Torn Sea and the northern Vihri coast across that decade, leaving Zervind and his elder brother fatherless at fourteen and consigning both to the long shadow of their grandfather's reign. Jasper II "the Old", whose rule was the longest in the Zav Dynastys history, survived the plague and his son both. The old Ruler passed away only in 351PA, at which point Zervinds elder brother ascended as Viago IV. The reign that followed was generally well regarded, Viago IV was beloved by his people and his court, but the Ras produced no legitimate children, and a depression that deepened across his later years left him disinclined to address the question of succession with any urgency. When Viago IV died in 375PA, the throne passed to Zervind as the last viable Zav male, a man of forty-six who had spent his adult life as a secondary figure in his brother's court and who arrived at the kingship without the political formation, military reputation, or personal authority that the position demanded.</p>

<h3>Conflict with Iztaw and the Lords</h3>

<p>Zervind IIs susceptibility to the influence of High-Bisop Morzin I was apparent within the first year of his reign. Morzin was a figure of considerable intelligence and institutional ambition who had spent years cultivating his position within the Clergy and who recognized in the new Ras a man whose need for guidance was greater than political self determinism. Correspondence routed through the clerical office, appointments made on Morzins counsel, and the Candleguards presence in the capital normalized as a sign of royal piety rather than ecclesiastical encroachment. By 377PA the arrangement had become public reality, and sufficiently intolerable to the great lords of the Vadcy, that the armies of the eight ancient lordships mobilized and marched on the capital. The Candleguard stood down. Zervind was released. The negotiations that followed produced the formal recognition of the Vadcy and the consultative framework that became the Honoured Assembly in 384PA, a settlement whose terms the Ras accepted because he had no meaningful alternative and which left him, in the words of one contemporary observer, "a man who wore the crown of Viago Firespear but could not lift it". He ruled for four more years in the diminished constitutional space the crisis had created, the Honoured Assembly conducting the business of the realm with a confidence that increased in inverse proportion to the kings own, and died in Zavlech in 388PA, reportedly without having spoken publicly of the events of 377PA in the eleven years that followed them.</p>
