	<img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Moira.png" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> </div> <p>Moira I, known before her crowning as Moira of Lavellan and later on veiled in whispers as Moira the Usurper, was the Countess of Lavellan, a county of the Eld Valley, and the first Queen in <a href="Eldmar" class="internal-link">Eldmar</a> of House Lavellan. In 822PA she took the crown from King Thormace of House Hornsley in the Lavellan Coup, and she held it for sixteen years. She is remembered in the valley as a powerful and ruthless woman, and by her own house as the founder of the dynasty that has ruled the kingdom ever since.</p> <h2>Countess of Lavellan</h2> <p>Lavellan is a county in the Eld Valley, and its countess sat in the Council of the Vale among the lords whom the Charter of the Vale protected. The chroniclers speak of Moira as the king's cousin, but if the blood of Lavellan and Hornsley met at all, it met much further back than that of cousins. She was a woman of great power, ruthless and above all ambitious, and she had a son, Gadvin, who would be her heir. Her husband was dead by the time of the coup and is not named in the chronicles.</p>  <p>By 822PA the crown of Eldmar sat on the head of Thormace of Hornsley, the last king of his house, and by every account he was a very weak king. For about eleven years he had relied on a gnomish advisor, <a href="Hikon Zix" class="internal-link">Hikon Zix</a>, who enjoyed wealth and power at his court and who made the king and many courtiers great gifts. As Thormace leaned more on his counsellor, the Council of the Vale was summoned less and less, and the counts and barons found their voice in the affairs of the realm shrinking. It was in this weakness that Moira saw her chance.</p> <h2>The Lavellan Coup</h2> <p>In the autumn of 822PA Moira struck. The Lavellan Coup was a bloody affair. The royal hall at Eldmar was taken during the autumn court, and a great part of the court elite was killed without trial, many of them hanged from the rafters of the hall. Thormace's queen and his elder children died in the first night. The king himself was taken alive and was kept confined, and he died the winter after, officially of a fever. Hikon Zix was apprehended at once, and his lips were sewn shut the moment he was taken, so that he could neither work a spell nor answer the charge. After the coronation of Moira as Queen in Eldmar, he was quartered by four horses.</p> <p>The story that the victors put about was that the feeble mind of the king had been held by the gnome with powerful enchanted jewels. The tale served Moira well. The <a href="Wermian Accords" class="internal-link">Wermian Accords</a> forbid any spell or charm that influences or alters another's mind, so the accusation cast Zix as a criminal against the Accords as well as against the king. To the lords who recalled that the Charter promised trial by one's peers, Moira answered that the dead had forfeited its protection as thralls of the gnome. Most of the counts and barons of the Council of the Vale sided with her or stood aside.</p> <p>It has been doubted ever since whether the jewels were ever enchanted. Many in the valley and beyond have held that the tale was manufactured upon the old distrust of the <a href="Gnome" class="internal-link">gnomish folk</a>, and that Thormace was only a very weak king, and Moira a very powerful subject who needed a villain for her crime. The jewels among the gnome's gifts were gathered up at his quartering and smashed, though some say not all of them were.</p>  <p>A legate of the <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">Church of Iztaw</a> came to Eldmar only when everything had been done, and he accepted the Lavellan account of the enchanted jewels after the fact. The formal blessing of Iztaw upon the new queen was issued only a year or two afterward. The Lavellans have been counted lawful Queens and Kings in Eldmar ever since.</p>  <p>The commonfolk cared surprisingly little about the coup at first. The harvests, however, failed in the years that followed, and the blame fell quickly on the new queen. "This wouldn't have happened under old King Thormace" became a common saying in the villages.</p> <h2>Prince Brittlehorn</h2> <p>Rumours that remnants of House Hornsley had survived the coup and had been smuggled away never died out. Around 830PA, a boy who came to be called Prince Brittlehorn rose in popularity as the true heir to the throne, heralded by some small communities as the prince returned for better times. The story told of him was that he was the youngest son of Thormace, a child of six at the time of the coup, hidden in the hill villages by loyal servants. His real name is not recorded.</p> <p>Moira invited him to her court. The boy was only fourteen years old. She mocked him before the assembled lords and made him perform as a jester, and then she had him thrown into a pit of dogs, saying: "A king can command his subjects, Brittlehorn. If these hounds bow to you, maybe so will I." The boy did not survive the ordeal. Whether he was truly a son of Thormace was never settled, and the Lavellans have always held him an impostor. The tale is told in the ballads of the valley. No Hornsley claimant has risen since, and the rumour that some of the line live still, in the hills or abroad, has never died.</p> <h2>Death and Legacy</h2> <p>Moira I died in 838PA of age and illness, and no foul play was ever proven. Her son Gadvin I took the throne, and unlike his mother he became a much beloved monarch. He reaffirmed the Charter of the Vale and, in 841PA, brought Eldmar into the <a href="League of the Gryphon" class="internal-link">League of the Gryphon</a>. The crown has stayed with her line since. It passed from Gadvin to his daughter Moira II and from her to Moira III, her great-granddaughter, who rules today. The Lavellans are often called the House of Eldmar, but they are House Lavellan, and the memory of how they came to the throne is part of the story of the kingdom.</p>
