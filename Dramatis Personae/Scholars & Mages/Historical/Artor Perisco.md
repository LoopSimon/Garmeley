<p>Artor Perisco da Mezar was a cevoli Physician and Arcanist during the 8th century, most popular for his controversial Experiments conducted on live human and inhuman bodies, his study of human Anatomy and development of the so called <a href="Perisco Effect" class="internal-link">"Perisco Effect"</a> of sustained exposure of planar energy on living organisms. While controversial during his time he has since been almost universally hailed for his strides in medical research, his treatise "De corporis divinita" challenged earlier anatomical teaches, arguing that established procedures and understanding of the human body at this point was based on observation of other mammals, not human bodies. The book includes a detailed series of explanations and drawings of the anatomical parts of the human bodies dissected and observed by Perisco. Perisco was essentially banished from his position as Court Magus of Luves, and travelled to the city of Ravajo in Firocco for permission to dissect victims of the gallows without fear of persecution, rumours of his dissection of criminals convicted to death are unsubstantiated. Perisco' work marked a new era in the study of anatomy and its relation to medicine and under him, anatomy became an actual discipline. His further studies on the human body would later be compiled in treaties known as the "De Humina Corporis Fabrica", as well as his more obscure work "Magica Organica" in which he described the so called "Perisco Effect" as mentioned above. Artor Perisco is often referred to as the founder of modern human anatomy.</p>
<p>Artor was born as the 3rd son to the Conde da Mezar, Casco da Mezar, and lived most of his life in the lands of <a href="Turpador" class="internal-link">Turpador</a> and later moved to <a href="Firocco" class="internal-link">Firocco</a> for political reasons as mentioned above. He returned to the Turpadori Capital of Vilgao in 799PA upon an invite of the Duc and was later killed during the Vilgao riots of 804PA at the age of 67.</p>
