<p>Shimun of Mizk was a Rakhi astronomer who joined the old night records of <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakh</a> to the models of the Cyracli schools, and so made the first tables of the sky that could be used to look forward as well as back. His Night Tables, compiled about 322BA, were copied in Rakh for three centuries and outlived the Empire (see <a href="History of Astronomy" class="internal-link">History of Astronomy</a>).</p>

<h2>Life</h2>
<p>Shimun was born in Mizk in 361BA, nine years after the Fall of <a href="Cyraclion" class="internal-link">Cyraclion</a>. Among the scholars who fled the ruin of the League was Eurymenes of Nyleikos, the last master of the school of <a href="Tripparch" class="internal-link">Tripparch</a>, who came to Rakh with a cart of tablets and took Shimun, then a boy in the record house of Mizk, for his pupil. Eurymenes died in 333BA. Shimun kept the diaries of Mizk for the rest of his life and lived through the Paragma of 298BA, when the Priesthood of Rakh rose against the Fireking and was divided. He died in Mizk in 289BA.</p>

<h2>The Night Tables</h2>
<p>The Rakhi had kept diaries of the night sky for centuries, entry upon entry, but without a theory. The <a href="Cyclopi" class="internal-link">Cyclopi</a> of Cyraclion had a theory but their records ended with their cities. Shimun's work was to set the one against the other. He tested the models of Tripparch against more than three hundred years of nights and corrected them where the sky had gone another way, and from the corrected models he drew up tables that gave the places of the Sun and of both moons for years ahead, and the nights on which the Moon would pass into the shadow of the Globus. They were the first such tables that were right within a night or two for the length of a lifetime.</p>
<p>Setting the Diolagma of Tripparch beside the diaries, he confirmed the slow shift of the stars' places that Tripparch had found and gave its rate more closely than the Cyracli had. He also entered the returns of the Broken Star, from the Cyracli tables and from the diaries, and found that the years between them were not the same, but wandered by some decades. He was the first to say so (see <a href="The Tis" class="internal-link">The Tis</a>). The diaries of his own years record a guest star that shone for some weeks (see <a href="Conflux" class="internal-link">Conflux</a>).</p>

<h2>His View of the Sky</h2>
<p>Shimun kept the Globus at the centre, as the Cyracli models did, and wrote nothing of what the Sun was or why it moved. He held that the task of an astronomer was to say what the sky would do, and left it to others to say what it was.</p>

<h2>After Him</h2>
<p>The Night Tables were copied in Mizk and in Rakh and used by the priests of the Empire for the calendar and for the eclipses. Some two and a half centuries later <a href="Phiodales" class="internal-link">Phiodales</a>, also of Mizk, whose Conflux Theory is among the oldest research of old Rakh, worked in the same tradition of dated record. When Rakh fell in 0PA its copies of the Tables were left behind in the <a href="Urien" class="internal-link">Urien</a> provinces of Garm and Ares with the rest of the administration, and it was there, three centuries on, that <a href="Ralzig" class="internal-link">Ralzig</a> found them. A few had also come by trade and war into the libraries of the Beheran elves, and so to <a href="Gahenna" class="internal-link">Gahenna</a>.</p>
