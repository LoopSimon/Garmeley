<p>Ptamon Akbil was an elven scientist, historian and philosopher of the 3rd, 2nd and 1st century BA during the rule of the Meter <a href="Elven Dynasties" class="internal-link">Dynasty</a> in ancient Behera. He taught and worked at the Great Library of Razhalbis for over 170 years and was personal tutor to Jir-Meter II and Gir-Meter I who would both ascend to power during the later years of the Meter. His various contributions are largely preserved and his musings in the poem "Tears of Alma-Nexis" is considered one of the primary sources for the great war between ancient <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakh</a> and the Meter during the 3rd century BA. </p>
<p>He studied magic and its effect in our world for centuries, categorizing different ways to conjure it and manipulate it and carefully documenting those people and objects channeling it, eventually determining various broad realms of source and effect of energies from beyond our world that would mimic phenomena from within our own when invoked. The result of his work was the creation of "Ptamons Key" a universal rite modelled after the Philosophy of the Zarsasha with which one could focus forces from beyond and project his will onto them. Ptamons Key is widely considered one of the first approximations of a Prism Evocation by modern day historians, and served as the bedrock for elven and later human Channelling for hundreds of years. Elements within the Rite hint at an awareness of Planar relativism within the model as well, theories that would later give room to the Axis models of the Age of Silver & Steel and even modern day Universal Models and Triquetra Theory.</p>
<p>Many details about Ptamons personal life are unknown beyond the personal connections he had to Jir-Meter II and later Gir-Meter I, but mentions of his death precede the collapse of the Meter dynasty and the Fall of Rakh.</p>
