<p>Neyadastes, often called the "Father of Alchemy" in modern times, was a cyracli Kynus and is widely regarded as the first true master of Kynosis, coining terms and theorizing the ground rules of Kynosis, the cyracli name for the modern day discipline of <a href="Alchemy" class="internal-link">Alchemy</a>, that are still practiced to this day. His works in compiling and distilling the works of many practitioners of the craft culminated in the Codex Kynosis that are usually dated to around 710BA and of which only excerpts remain in wide circulations outside of the Halls of the <a href="Lorqis" class="internal-link">Library of Lorqis</a>. Neyadastes was a contemporary and friend of the polymath and philosopher <a href="Kasthemis" class="internal-link">Kasthemis</a> with whom he is said to have stood in long exchange. He was the first of the Kynus, a row of important alchemists in the ancient cyracli traditions that would culminate and end with <a href="Pixos" class="internal-link">Pixos</a> and the Fall of Cyraclion during the 4th century BA.</p>
