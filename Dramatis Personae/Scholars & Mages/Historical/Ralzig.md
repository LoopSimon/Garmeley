<p>Ralzig was an Urien Minister of the <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethric</a> Church and the author of the Ordered Heavens, the book that gave the west its classical picture of the sky. In Ralzig's Cosmology the Globus stands still at the centre of all things and everything else turns about it. For close to six centuries it was the account of the heavens taught in the schools of the west, and in the schools of the Church it is taught still. Its classical form has since been set aside by the astronomers of the great schools (see <a href="History of Astronomy" class="internal-link">History of Astronomy</a>).</p>

<h2>Life</h2>
<p>Ralzig was born in <a href="Ur" class="internal-link">Ur</a> in 289PA, within fifty years of the founding of the Church, and was schooled in its houses of learning. He made his pilgrimage to <a href="Iztaw" class="internal-link">Iztaw</a>, where he was ordained and given the right to practise clerical law as a Minister (see <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">Church of Iztaw</a>), and was then given a parish in Ur, where he kept the sky from the tower of its church for the better part of forty years. The Ordered Heavens was written about 328PA, in the years that the Broken Star hung in the sky. He was called to teach at Iztaw in his last years and died there in 357PA.</p>

<h2>The Cosmology</h2>
<p>The Ordered Heavens holds that the Globus rests at the centre of the world and does not move. About it, in order of nearness, stand the clear spheres that carry the lights. The first carries Luna Major, which bears Luna Minor on a small wheel of its own. The next carries the Sun. Beyond them lies the sphere of the fixed stars, which turns once a day and so makes the day, and beyond the last sphere lies the Homestead Beyond, where the Prophets dwell, set outside the heavens as the place from which they are ordered. Each sphere turns in a circle and nothing passes through them. The circle of the Sun is set a little to one side of the Globus, so that the Sun draws nearer in one season than another and grows and shrinks by a tenth through the year, and so that the two halves of the year come out unequal.</p>
<p>Ralzig took his numbers from the Cyracli models of <a href="Tripparch" class="internal-link">Tripparch</a> and the Rakhi Night Tables of <a href="Shimun" class="internal-link">Shimun</a>, which he found in the old archives of the Urien provinces, where the Rakhi administration had left them, and he joined them into one system. His own contribution was the order, and the tables that followed from it, which gave the places of the Sun and both moons and the days of the eclipses to within a day or two for decades together.</p>

<h2>Why It Held</h2>
<p>The Cosmology agreed with what anyone could see. The sky turns about the Globus, the Globus feels no motion, and the stars show no shift through the year. The one body that plainly circles another, Luna Minor about Luna Major, was a wheel upon a wheel exactly as the Ordered Heavens drew it. It agreed with the Church as well. The Church had taught since 254PA that the world was made for man, and loved order and rank, and a Globus at the heart of the heavens with the Homestead Beyond at their rim fitted both (see <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethricism</a>).</p>

<h2>The Broken Star</h2>
<p>The one real difficulty was the comets of the Tis, which crossed the spheres. Ralzig placed them in the high air beneath Luna Major, as burning vapours that rose and fell with the seasons of the air, and he dismissed the Cyracli claim that they returned on a cycle (see <a href="The Tis" class="internal-link">The Tis</a> and <a href="Starworship" class="internal-link">Starworship</a>). The answer sufficed for six centuries, because no one alive had seen the Broken Star twice and the next return was always generations off.</p>

<h2>Decline</h2>
<p>The Ordered Heavens did not fail all at once. Its tables drifted from the sky by a day in a few generations, and each generation of astronomers set them right and went on. The return of the Broken Star about 845PA was the first to be watched with instruments, and its path would not fit the spheres. The long records of Reanport and of Szievek showed the Sun and the moons wandering from the places the Ordered Heavens gave them by whole days. In 927PA no astronomer of the great schools holds the Cosmology with its clear spheres, though a part of it lives on in the compromise that keeps the Globus still.</p>
