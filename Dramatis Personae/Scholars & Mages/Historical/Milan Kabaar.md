<p>Milan Kabaar was an elven arcanist and summoner of the 6th century PA, best known for her study of the <a href="Masakh" class="internal-link">Masakh</a>, the curse that befalls casters who overreach while calling upon entities beyond themselves. A summoner herself, she approached the curse not as a physician or a priest but as one of those it threatened, and her work remains the foundation of everything written on it since.</p>  
<h2>On the Three Curses</h2>  
<p>Kabaar gathered the scattered folklore of the Masakh and set it beside the oldest written accounts of the curse, found in the manuscripts of the Alparid tradition of conjuration. Above all she worked from transcriptions of the Vyr-Ench Scrolls, the Book of Promise, in which Pharaoh <a href="Alparaz" class="internal-link">Alparaz IV</a> recorded his decades of conversation with the bound <a href="Daemon" class="internal-link">daemon</a> Grahul. How she came by them is not known, for the scrolls' keepers have never been free with them.</p>  
<p>From these sources she set out the three curses by which the Masakh are still known: the deformed, the possessed, and the rare few who master the thing they called and carry it within them. She held that the three are one curse ending three ways, and that it falls when a summoner's call reaches further than they meant and is answered from <a href="The Abyss" class="internal-link">the Abyss</a>. Her warnings to fellow summoners were blunt: preparation before power, and never to call on anything while tired, afraid or in pain.</p>  
<h2>Fate</h2>  
<p>Kabaar did not heed her own warnings, or could not. Late in her life she became Masakh herself, and what became of her afterwards is unknown. The accounts that survive disagree as neatly as her own three curses: some say she was seen in her last years deformed beyond recognition, some that she spoke in a voice that was not hers before she vanished, and some that she walked away from the casting whole and was simply never seen again.</p>
