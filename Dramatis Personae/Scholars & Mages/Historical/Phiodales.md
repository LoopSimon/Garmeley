<div class="wiki-infobox"> <h4 class="wiki-header">Phiodales of Mizk</h4> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Occupation</strong> <span>Rakhi Thaumaturge</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Culture</strong> <span>rakhi <a href="Human" class="internal-link">Human</a></span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Born</strong> <span>early 1st century BA</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Died</strong> <span>likely 1BA, Alwazzatar</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Cause of Death</strong> <span>The Eruption of Al-Akka,<br>unknown Age</span> </div> <hr class="wiki-hr"> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Field of Research</strong> <span>Ancient <a href="Conflux" class="internal-link">Conflux</a> Theory</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Known Teachers</strong> <span>N/A</span> </div> <div class="wiki-row"> <strong>Known Students</strong> <span>N/A</span> </div> <hr class="wiki-hr"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Phiodales.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> </div> <p>Phiodales of Mizk was a Rakhi Thaumaturge of the last generation of the Empire of <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakh</a> and the author of the Alwazattar Tablets, the most complete primary document of the empire that survives. His Conflux Theory is among the oldest research of old Rakh, and the Rakhi reckoned the Years of their calendar by the span he set down (see <a href="Calendars" class="internal-link">Calendars</a>).</p>  <p>Little is known of Phiodales beyond what the Tablets say of him. He was born in the early years of the 1st century BA and came from Mizk, and he rose to the rank of Thaumaturge, the title by which the Tablets name him. It is unclear whether that title was borne by every priest of Rakh or only by those who had true gifts (see <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakh</a>). He worked in Alwazattar, a city on the slopes of the Cloudscratchers in the south of the Empire (see <a href="Wasteland of Rakh" class="internal-link">Wasteland of Rakh</a>), and inscribed the Tablets there about 33BA, a generation before the end. He is thought to have died in Alwazattar in 1BA, in the Eruption of Al-Akka.</p> 
 <p>The Tablets were recovered from the ruins of Alwazattar by the expeditions of the <a href="Excavators Guild of Butera" class="internal-link">Excavators Guild of Butera</a> and are kept under the Guild's control. They give a partial view of the administration, the religion and the learning of the late Empire, and they are the voice of one man at one moment, written when Rakh seemed to be permanent. Besides the matters of the Empire they hold Phiodales's own account of the planes, in which the <a href="Spiritual Realm" class="internal-link">Spiritual Realm</a> lies beneath the <a href="Material Realm" class="internal-link">Material Realm</a> as a mirror-world where great beings of thought and will reside.</p> <h2>Conflux Theory</h2> <p>The Thaumaturges of Rakh felt the planar energies rise and fall as an ebbing and flowing in the power they could draw, and Phiodales sought the rule behind it. He held that the crest of the Conflux, the days on which the swell stood at its greatest and began to turn, returned at a fixed interval, and the Tablets set that interval at 3104 Solar Days. The Rakhi began a new Year at each crest, the priests proclaimed it when their Thaumaturges felt it, and the levies, campaigns and great rites of the Empire were made to wait upon the proclamation.</p> <p>The rule did not hold. The eras of Conflux run in cycles of eight to thirteen years, and no two are alike (see <a href="Conflux" class="internal-link">Conflux</a>). The span of 3104 days is a little under eight years, shorter than the shortest cycle <a href="Ulrike Metzen" class="internal-link">Ulrike Metzen</a> has found, and the Tablets seem to give one short cycle of Phiodales's own years as if it were the rule. They are still of use. Set against Metzen's tables, the Tablets show how long each cycle of their time really ran.</p>  <p>Phiodales worked in the same tradition of dated record that <a href="Shimun" class="internal-link">Shimun</a> of Hantepe had joined to the models of the Cyracli nearly three centuries before (see <a href="History of Astronomy" class="internal-link">History of Astronomy</a>). When Rakh fell in 1BA the proclamation of the crests ended with it, and the Tablets and the rule they carry passed to the scholars.</p>
