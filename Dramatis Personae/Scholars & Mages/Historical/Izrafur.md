<p>Izrafur Missiam ar Kurum, sometimes referred to as Izrafur of Mereskis was a 7th and 8th century elven Scholar and Arcanist that practiced in the Elbae Sultanate and rose to the position of Sapphire Eye within the <a href="Karajal School of Insight" class="internal-link">Karajal School of Insight</a>.</p>
<p> He is most famous for creating the so called 4-Axis Model, he theorized that planar energies were directed by Intent, in the form of what he dubbed "Planar Force". He noticed singular Planes accessing multiple planar forces and how different Planes would also access the same Force, as well as a clear distinguishment between stronger and weaker Planar Force and Intent when channelled into different Realms via Fluxbridge. Izrafur would go on to further classify the Planar Forces as theorized by him as Harmony & Consciousness, Light & Dark, Life & Death and Syntropy & Entropy, mapping them on his new Model. For centuries this would become the pre-eminent cosmic understanding of Planar Relativity and <a href="Vilkin" class="internal-link">Vilkins</a> universal model fully build upon the 4-Axis Model as well as the less popular Hierarchical Model as theorized by <a href="Rugior" class="internal-link">Rugior</a>.</p> 
<p>Many of Izrafurs own observations and findings were based upon the works of gnomish Scholar <a href="Larkomas" class="internal-link">Larkomas of Ott</a>, whom he lauded as a "star on the academic firmament" many times. In general Izrafur was extremely unusual and an outlier for his open mind and show of respect towards the ilrakhani Schools and academics, especially in an age when the rise of the human and gnomish Schools had caused much resentment in the older elven traditions. Carassa al-Kael, his most famed student, would credit Izrafur for her own attitude towards non elven theorists and academia as well.</p>
<p>Izrafur was a contemporary and colleague of <a href="Jurahim al-Biad" class="internal-link">Jurahim al-Biad</a> although the latter was many decades his senior. He vehemently opposed al-Biad, at that time the Ruby Eye, in the High Chamber of Karajal on his views on magical applications in warfare and diplomacy, a stance that has been controversial long after both elves deaths and was largely vindicated following the destruction of Jurahims Invocation at Horad.</p>
