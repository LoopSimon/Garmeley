<p>Muglig Ruv was a gnomish scholar of Ott and the most influential member of the Nabat, the "School of Dust", whose work transformed <a href="Black Powder" class="internal-link">black powder</a> from a Shirin signalling curiosity into a reliable propellant. Trained in the Prosum tradition of <a href="Artificery" class="internal-link">Artificery</a>, he became one of the leading voices of the sect of Ottian scholars who turned away from magical research to study the substance, and he is regarded within the Nabat as the founder of its methods. The refinement process, the grained powder and the proving standards that bear his name made possible the first Ottian gun, completed by his students in 803PA, nine years before his death.</p>  

<h2>Early Life and Training</h2>  
<p>Ruv was born in Ott in 698PA and educated in the Ottian Schools, where he studied Prosum with a particular interest in Tiogum, the maintenance of artefacts. His early writings were concerned with the failure of artefacts under heat and pressure, and it was through this work that he first encountered black powder, which had reached Ott along the eastern trade routes and was then used by a small circle of Prosum scholars as a source of controlled force in their experiments.</p>  

<h2>Work with the Nabat</h2>  
<p>Ruv's first and most important contribution was a method for the purification of saltpetre, the substance that gives black powder its force. Imported powder and its ingredients were notoriously inconsistent, and Ruv developed a process of repeated leaching and crystallization, assisted by alchemical reagents, that produced saltpetre of a purity previously unknown in Ilrakhan. The resulting material, known in Ott as Ruv salt, remains the basis of Ottian powder production.</p>  
<p>His second contribution was the graining of powder. Loose powder tended to separate into its ingredients during transport and burned unevenly, and Ruv found that pressing the wet mixture into cakes and breaking these into grains of a fixed size produced a powder that kept its proportions, absorbed less moisture and burned faster and more predictably. Ruv's grain made black powder suitable for use in a barrel for the first time.</p>  
<p>Ruv also introduced the systematic testing of powder through a small proving mortar, which measured the force of a fixed charge by the distance it threw a weighted ball. The Ruv proof gave the Nabat a common standard by which batches could be compared, and his tables of charges and proportions became the foundation of Ottian ballistics.</p>  
<p>Beyond his technical work, Ruv was instrumental in defining the Nabat as a school in its own right. He argued that black powder was a material rather than an artefact and therefore fell outside the grading of the Ottian Codex published by <a href="The Ottian Syndicate" class="internal-link">The Ottian Syndicate</a> in 769PA. The acceptance of this argument allowed the Nabat to develop outside the Syndicate's protocols, a freedom that would later make the spread of black powder so difficult for Ott to contain.</p>  

<h2>Death and Legacy</h2>  
<p>Ruv did not build the first Ottian gun himself, but the prototype of 803PA was the work of his students and relied on his grained powder and his proving tables. He continued his experiments into old age and died in 812PA, at the age of 114, in an explosion in the Nabat workshops. Within the Nabat he is remembered as the Father of Dust, and his writings remain the first texts studied by its members.</p>
