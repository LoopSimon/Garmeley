<p>Gahenna of Jiravid was a Beheran astronomer and the first known to set down that the Globus is not at rest but travels around the Sun. Her book, On the Wheeling of the Globus, was written about 497PA and was ignored in her lifetime and for almost four centuries afterwards. It is now counted among the great books of the sky, and she among the great astronomers, and she is remembered chiefly for being right before anyone could show it (see <a href="History of Astronomy" class="internal-link">History of Astronomy</a>).</p>

<h2>Life</h2>
<p>Gahenna was born in <a href="Jiravid" class="internal-link">Jiravid</a> in 452PA, in the capital of the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae</a> Sultanate and the old Tomb City of Farzirash, which had kept the records of the sky for longer than any city in the west. Little is known of her family. She had no patron that history names and seems to have lived by her teaching. She gathered a small circle of students, never more than a dozen, whom the schools that followed <a href="Ralzig" class="internal-link">Ralzig</a> called the Wheelers, and not kindly. The circle did not outlast her by a generation. She died in Jiravid in 528PA.</p>

<h2>Her Sources</h2>
<p>Gahenna made no observations of consequence. She worked from the observations of others, and she had them to hand. The elven records of the city and of the Great Library of Rahzalbis gave her the shadow measures of Nasr ibn Hadad and the Rahzalbis Scrolls of Sitamun. The Cyracli books of Lorqis gave her the Diolagma of <a href="Tripparch" class="internal-link">Tripparch</a> and the half moon measure of Meleandros of Hecvos, with the measure of the Globus itself by <a href="Kasthemis" class="internal-link">Kasthemis</a>. A few copies of the Night Tables of <a href="Shimun" class="internal-link">Shimun</a> had come into the libraries of the Sultanate as well. What none of these men had done was to read the numbers together.</p>

<h2>The Wheeling</h2>
<p>On the Wheeling holds that the Sun is so great beside the Globus that it cannot be carried round by it, and that the Globus travels around the Sun once in 400 days and turns once upon itself each day, which is why the stars seem to turn. <a href="Luna Major" class="internal-link">Luna Major</a> circles the Globus as it goes, bearing <a href="Luna Minor" class="internal-link">Luna Minor</a>, and the other celestial bodies of the heavens circle the Sun as the Globus does. The path of the Globus is not set at the centre of the Sun but a little to one side of it, so that the Globus comes nearer to the Sun in one season than in another. The one setting answered what the elven priests had measured, that the Sun grows and shrinks by a tenth through the year and that the two halves of the year between the equinoxes are unequal, the one of about 187 days and the other of about 213.</p>

<h2>Her Arguments</h2>
<p>She argued first from the size of the Sun. If the Sun is many times the Globus, as the Cyracli measures show, she asked why the great body should circle the small. She argued second from the sky itself, which already shows one body circling another and itself carried round a third. Luna Minor circles Luna Major, and Luna Major circles the Globus, so that a body may be the centre of one motion and a traveller in another, and she saw no reason why the Globus should not be one more such traveller. She argued third from the sea, whose high water keeps the hours of the Sun more than those of the moon (see <a href="The Sun" class="internal-link">The Sun</a>), and took it for a sign that the Sun commands the Globus.</p>
<p>She knew the strongest argument against her. If the Globus travels from one side of its path to the other in half a year, the near stars should shift against the far ones, and no one had ever seen them shift. Her answer was that the stars are so far beyond the Sun that the whole path of the Globus is a point to them. Her opponents answered that this would make the heavens empty beyond reason, a waste that no maker would permit. Neither side could settle it in her day, and neither can in 927PA.</p>

<h2>Afterwards</h2>
<p>Her book was copied in Jiravid for some generations and then dropped from sight. A copy went west among the elven works that <a href="Galiatar Inkfingers" class="internal-link">Galiatar Inkfingers</a> translated into the Rudimenta Targorae, and it lay unread at <a href="Korsfall College" class="internal-link">Korsfall</a> for nearly three centuries. It was read again at <a href="Reanport" class="internal-link">Reanport</a> in the 890s, in the time of <a href="Korman van Schipwig" class="internal-link">Korman van Schipwig</a>, whose astronomers set her Globus against the long records of Reanport and Szievek and found that it kept the Sun and the moons where the sky had them, and that the Ordered Heavens did not. Within a few years the universities of the west taught the Wheeling beside Ralzig's Cosmology, the magi towers of Patwin and Edessa took it up, and the <a href="Karajal School of Insight" class="internal-link">Karajal School of Insight</a> followed, in the city of Alparak where the shadow measures on which she had built were made. Her Wheeling also undid the Leypoint Model of <a href="Kwarzimi" class="internal-link">Kwarzimi</a>, who had read the leypoints as springs in a Globus at rest (see <a href="Leygrid Theory" class="internal-link">Leygrid Theory</a>).</p>
