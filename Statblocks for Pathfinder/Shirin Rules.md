# Shirin (Pathfinder 1e race rules)

The <a href="Shirin" class="internal-link">Shirin</a> are rodent-like humanoids of central Nirydia and the founding people of the <a href="Twintail Imperium" class="internal-link">Twintail Imperium</a>. Nearly all living Shirin descend from two ancestral lineages, the Valfir and the Gelfir, which have interbred in the Mondi Delta since the Bronze Age. A Shirin character uses the shared traits below and then takes one lineage: Valfir, Gelfir or Mixed. Pure Valfir and Gelfir are rare today and mostly come from isolated communities, so most Shirin player characters should be Mixed.

## Shared Shirin traits

All Shirin have the following racial traits, whatever their lineage.

- **Type:** Shirin are humanoids with the shirin subtype.
- **Size:** Shirin are Medium creatures and have no bonuses or penalties due to their size.
- **Speed:** Shirin have a base speed of 30 feet.
- **Low-Light Vision:** Shirin can see twice as far as humans in conditions of dim light.
- **Prehensile Tail:** A Shirin's tail is strong and as long as their body. As a swift action, a Shirin can use it to draw a small, stowed object carried on their person. The tail cannot wield weapons or carry heavy objects.
- **Tailsense:** A Shirin's tail gives them an exact sense of their body's position in space, a holdover from ancestors who moved through lightless caves. Shirin can move at full speed while blinded or in darkness without needing an Acrobatics check, and take no penalty from being unable to see on Acrobatics and Climb checks.
- **Symmetry Sense:** Shirin are unusually sensitive to symmetry, and notice at once when a pattern is broken. They gain a +2 racial bonus on Perception checks to notice unusual stonework, hidden doors, traps and structural flaws, and on Knowledge (engineering) checks to judge whether a structure is sound. A Shirin who passes within 10 feet of such a flaw or concealed feature can make a check to notice it whether or not they are actively looking.
- **Lowland Lungs:** Shirin lungs process thin air poorly. Above 1,600 feet (about 500 metres), a Shirin becomes fatigued after any strenuous activity, such as a combat, running or a forced march, and stays fatigued until they rest for 1 hour. Above 3,300 feet (about 1,000 metres), a Shirin is treated as if at a high peak and suffers altitude sickness. Shirin cannot become acclimated to high altitude by normal means.
- **Languages:** Shirin begin play speaking Gelfir. Shirin with high Intelligence scores can choose any bonus languages of the player's choice.

## Valfir

The Valfir are the larger and older of the two lineages. They are heavy and robust, with dark, coarse fur, a long snout and large forward-set incisors. They are the look most outsiders mean when they say "Ratfolk", and the older delta farming and river communities trace back to them.

- **Ability Score Modifiers:** +2 Strength, +2 Dexterity, −2 Charisma. Valfir are strong and hardy, but their appearance draws the most prejudice from outsiders.
- **Gnawing Incisors:** Valfir have a natural bite attack that deals 1d4 points of damage. It is a secondary natural attack, made at the Valfir's full base attack bonus −5, when used alongside manufactured weapons.
- **Anchoring Tail:** A Valfir braces themselves with their heavy tail. They gain a +4 racial bonus to CMD against bull rush and trip attempts while standing on the ground.
- **Wide Gaze:** A Valfir's eyes sit far to the sides of the skull and give them a very wide field of view. Opponents flanking a Valfir gain only a +1 bonus on attack rolls instead of +2. In exchange, the Valfir's poor depth perception gives them an additional −1 penalty on ranged attack rolls beyond the first range increment.

## Gelfir

The Gelfir are the smaller and younger lineage, emerging about two thousand years after the Valfir. They are light and slender, with soft, pale fur, a round, short-snouted face and large upright ears. Their eyes sit further forward than those of the Valfir. The scribal and mathematical traditions that became the foundations of Lakalism grew out of Gelfir scholarly communities.

- **Ability Score Modifiers:** +2 Dexterity, +2 Intelligence, −2 Strength. Gelfir are quick and sharp minded, but slight.
- **Keen Ears:** Gelfir gain a +2 racial bonus on Perception checks based on hearing. They can also identify the direction a sound came from with a successful DC 10 Perception check.
- **Reckoner:** Gelfir gain a +2 racial bonus on Knowledge (engineering) and Knowledge (religion) checks, and both are always class skills for them.
- **Nimble Tail:** A Gelfir uses their light tail for balance. They gain a +2 racial bonus on Acrobatics and Climb checks.
- **Forward Gaze:** The forward-set eyes of the Gelfir give them better depth perception than other Shirin. They ignore the first −2 penalty for attacking at range beyond the first range increment.

## Mixed

Most Shirin alive today are of thoroughly mixed ancestry. They usually look broadly Valfir in fur and face, but often keep the large Gelfir ears and a lighter build. Siblings can differ a great deal in which lineage shows through.

- **Ability Score Modifiers:** +2 to one ability score of the player's choice.
- **Mixed Heritage:** A Mixed Shirin chooses two racial traits from the Valfir and Gelfir lists above, other than ability score modifiers. They can take both from one lineage or one from each.

## Age, height and weight

Shirin mature a little faster than humans and live slightly shorter lives. Lineage makes no difference to age, but it does to build. Male and female Shirin use the same table.

| Adulthood | Intuitive | Self-taught | Trained |

| Middle age | Old | Venerable | Maximum age |

## Alternate racial trait

- **Twintailed:** Very rarely, a Shirin's tail bones split to form two fully working tails. Lakalists see this as the clearest physical sign of Lakal's symmetrical grace, and it is best known as the mark of the Twintail imperial line. A twintailed Shirin can use Prehensile Tail twice per round, and gains a +2 racial bonus on Diplomacy and Intimidate checks against followers of Lakalism. This trait replaces Tailsense. It should only be taken with the GM's permission, and the character's story should account for how a twintailed Shirin outside the imperial family is treated.
