<p>The Crisis of the Untouched was a period of civil unrest and open conflict within the dwarven Hold of <a href="Tizin-Kol" class="internal-link">Tizin-Kol</a> between 879 and 882PA, fought between religious radicals and the progressive engineers of <a href="Currentbending" class="internal-link">currentbending</a>. It ended with the assassination of <a href="Mamus Rolim" class="internal-link">Mamus Rolim</a>, and its aftermath remade the Hold.</p> <h2>Causes</h2> <p>For generations the deep forges had produced the Untouching, the pale alloy that will not touch a lodestone, and the faithful had taken it for a sign of the favour of the gods of stone. When Rolim's discovery turned it into deepforged silver, the stuff of signal-lines, lamps and engines, many in the Hold welcomed the change. Others saw sacrilege in it: holy forges turned to the service of machines, and holy osmium, the densest and most enduring of metals, cast at enemies from the throwers of the <a href="Keyzal Workshop" class="internal-link">Keyzal Workshop</a>. The grey years, which cost many lives in burst strikestone and blinded gunners, gave the traditionalists their proof that the art was cursed, and their cry, that the Untouching be left untouched, gave the Crisis its name.</p> <h2>The Crisis</h2> <p>What began as dispute within the Symposium and the temples hardened into politics, and from 879PA into violence. Radical cells struck at workshops and forge-halls and sent signal-lines grey, leaving whole districts dark and deaf, while the new wave of engineers armed themselves in turn. The Council was divided for much of the conflict. Throughout it Mamus Rolim, long the most moderate voice of the art and by then turned toward its ethics, acted as a peaceful and diplomatic figure on the engineers' side, seeking a settlement between the faithful and the Symposium.</p> <p>In 882PA he was slain by a radicalised religious terrorist, a blade in the dark for the man the radicals called the Father of the Sin.</p> <h2>Aftermath</h2> <p>The murder achieved the opposite of its purpose. The Council of Tizin-Kol answered in rare unity and crushed the radical cells, and strict religious reforms followed. In the years after, a moderate arm of the church that welcomed the art slowly took the place of the old traditionalists, among it a true divine engineering that holds the work of the forge and the current to be worship in itself. <a href="Magda Bazena Nib" class="internal-link">Magda Bazena Nib</a>, Rolim's old colleague and rival, rose to lead the new Tizin-Kol as Chair of the Council from 886PA, securing the secularism of the Hold and the power of the Symposium and the Workshops, but also binding the art in the security mandates that govern it to this day.</p>  <p>It has long been whispered that Rolim's death was not the work of the radicals alone, but was arranged by members of his own faction, or by those who later reformed the Hold, to turn a moderate and respected scholar into a martyr for their cause. Nothing of the kind has ever been proven. If it is true, whoever conceived it was remarkably successful.</p>
