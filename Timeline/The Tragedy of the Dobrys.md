<p>The days of the summer of 927PA in the lands of eastern <a href="Grachy" class="internal-link">Grachy</a> speak of a small and inconsequential tragedy amidst the farming village of Varny. The death of an unborn child and the end of a family spell sadness and sorrow, but the survival of husband & wife brings with it the wind of hope.</p>
<p>The Village of Varny is a relatively small community on the Road between <a href="Kharnov" class="internal-link">Kharnov</a> and <a href="Izhevk" class="internal-link">Izhevk</a> in eastern Grachy, where the hundreds of farmsteads, millers, sheep hoarders and other men who live of and for the land come to trade, drink and rest on their way home or to larger markets. In May of 927PA, the <a href="Bykmåli" class="internal-link">bykmåli</a> Occultist <a href="Bersi Hurtfing" class="internal-link">Bersi Hurtfing</a> arrived here on his journey northwards to Kharnov, his goal to investigate the mysterious sickness that had taken a hold of the city in recent weeks. When Bersi, not always the consummate professional his betters would have him to be, saw the suffering of a local family and heard the villagers pleas for help, he could not help himself but offer his best to the locals, for better or worse.</p>

<p>He found the local Beekeepers, Yvor Dobry, his wife Sabrina and his father Pietr, taken by strange malice. Imprisoned in restless sleep they seemed to have fallen to a fever that no medical education could explain, at least none that Bersi had enjoyed. Sabrina had sustained wounds that kept opening, losing much blood in the process, while the old Pietr had been lacerated and fought not only with whatever had befallen the three but his own age as well. None of them showed any signs of recovery or wake and Bersi did his best to care for them while pondering the cause for their ailment. After a day of care and stay Hurtfing, who was told by the local woodsman Vilhelm about the Dobrys horrific ride into the village two nights prior on a blood soaked and morbidly wounded horse, decided to investigate the Dobrys Homestead himself. At their apiary, as soon as he entered the grounds many Bees took up form in strange and wilful shapes, chasing and attacking the ill-prepared and shocked norseman back into the village. While Bersi had no knowledge of what exactly caused their troubles, it was clear that supernatural forces had nested itself at the Dobry Apiary and cursed the family for unknown reasons.</p>
<p>It was on the third day of his stay that the village turned against the foreigner. No better were the Dobrys under Bersis care, albeit theyd likely already died where it not for him, strange were his customs and way of speaking and he made for a good target for their fears and frustrations with the world and their predicament. Led by Vilhelm the youths of the village assembled in an angry mod and took Hurtfing out on the streets, some with pitchforks and axe ready to blame him for the ill of the Beekeepers and find some sense of closure. Where it not for the unexpected arrival of travellers from north, it may have come to an end for the elderly Bersi on that day in Varny. Into the village rode Hildegard von Giswald, a meadi knight of the Iron Tower, clad in gleaming plate putting herself between the villagers and Bersi, backed up by a flamboyant noble from southern Garmeley named Jan von Kolgade, his rapier looking cold and dangerous to the worried locals. Near them the statue of the imposing Havu, a strange looking man from the forests themselves, and in the distance the ever watchful archer, a vihri by the name of Danusz that kept his distance and observed, as not every situation always needed a reaction. Their arrival and interference was enough to make the locals back off and bring Bersi into safe hands, Vilhelm who led the mob was quick to change course and appeal to the foreigners good hearts with the story about the ailing Beekeepers.</p>
