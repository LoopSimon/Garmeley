<p>The Poem of the Bad Omen describes the daring and tragic venture of the <a href="The Bad Omen" class="internal-link">Bad Omen</a> into the turvik ruins of the Howling Henge and later the drow ruins of Azagon, on the tracks of the <a href="The Golden Ring" class="internal-link">Golden Ring</a>, a lost group of heroes.</p>

Gathered at the Drunken Mice to hear the tale twins told, 
To track the missing Golden Ring, adventurers of old.

Left hearth and struck the trail, where frost begins to bite, 
Deep into Wormwood shade, away from warmth and light.

But shadows moved among old stones a greedy, cruel horde,
With goblin tooth and hobgoblin might, they met the heroes sword.

Our misfits fought and struck them down, clearing the holy ground, 
And there beneath the moved rock, the opened path was found.

The Golden Ring had gone before theyd breached the heavy stone, 
So down the party followed them, into the dark unknown.

Until the dry and silent air grew damp and freezing cold, 
Green fires lit a sunken lake, a wonder to behold.


They paused to rest upon the shore, to catch a fleeting breath, 
But from the webs the morlocks crept, to drag them to their death.

With slithering leeches at their side, a pale and hungry swarm, 
The misfits drew their blades again to meet the coming storm.

Through brutal clash and splashing blood, our heroes stood their ground, 
And left the pale beasts slain and broken, scatterd all around.

But victory demanded toll, for as the shadows cleared, 
Their trusty mounts lay dead and cold, exactly as they feared.

Below the glass a ghostly light began to pulse and glow, 
Illuminating shapes that slithered far below.


Freezing waters churned and foamed beneath a wooden keel, 
As massive shadows awoke to claim their meal.

Glowing arrows pierced the black and caught the monsters' gaze, 
Bought our heroes safe escape across the watery maze.

A sodden journal left behind, the Golden Ring’s despair, 
Of madness, rot, and broken trust that drove them to the lair.

Beyond the camp, the caverns changed to halls of elder stone, 
An ancient empire of the Drow, forgotten and alone.

Tall statues stood in silent watch, draped heavy in the white, 
A suffocating spider's web that swallowed up the light.

They braved the spiders silken thread and found the crimson crest, 
Where noble Filip lay in peace, forever laid to rest.

A touch of blood, a sudden flash, waking world gave way, 
And dragged them to a nightmare realm where shifting shadows play.

The Shrike came swooping from the void with talons sharp and long, 
While hollow Famine stalked the mist to starve the brave and strong.

A phantom horde of Hobgoblins struck down with brutal might, 
As every hero's deepest dread took shape within the night.

Poor Maiwynn watched her brother fall, sweet Jorji in the dirt, 
Cruel illusion born of fear to paralyze and hurt.

They broke the spell and won the day, but paid a bitter cost... 
For Sturla with his trusty bow was tragically lost

With Sturla gone and spirits low, they marched through web and stone, 
To face the darkest depths of all: the ancient spider's throne. 

And there beneath the violet light, the Golden Ring they found, 
Their broken minds and silken tombs held captive underground. 

Upon that seat sat Queen Azag, a husk of dust and bone, 
Who sought to pull the Anchor's power and make the world her own. 

She channelled magic from the void to rise up from the dead, 
And called the massive Matriarch to fill their souls with dread. 

The darkened spiders crashed ahead to tear them all apart, 
But Orlatz threw his heavy axe and struck them near the heart! 

He chugged his potions in the fray, a frantic, deadly dance, 
While Harold parried fate itself to give his friends a chance. 

Then Jorji raised his holy blade, a Paladin so bright, 
And burned a hundred spiders down with the Creators might! 

While Maiwynn marched into the void and claimed the Anchors spark, 
To rip her stolen soul right back from out the cosmic dark. 

She turned the ancient Queen to ash and broke the magic chain, 
And split the fated, twin born bond that both might live again! 

But with the anchor ripped away, the vault began to crack, 
Tur Agazon was caving in, there was no turning back.
