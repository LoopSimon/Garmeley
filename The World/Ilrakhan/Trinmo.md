<p>The Trinmo is the largest river on <a href="Inara" class="internal-link">Inara</a> and the great artery of western <a href="Ilrakhan" class="internal-link">Ilrakhan</a>. It rises in the Whisperhills of <a href="Meadland" class="internal-link">Meadland</a>, runs south-east through the heart of the Meadi lands, bends south at Trinfurt and crosses the whole of <a href="Ur" class="internal-link">Ur</a> and <a href="Garmeley" class="internal-link">Garmeley</a>, where it feeds Lake Forr, before it ends in a wide delta on the Stormy Sea. Kingdoms, cities and faiths have grown along its banks for as long as there are records, and more than one war has been fought for its crossings.</p>
<h2>Course</h2>
<p>The river begins as a spring among the Whisperhills, the home of the Hundi, and reaches Korsfall as a broad stream. Below the capital it winds through the Meadi Lowlands past Anweide and Rahn, along the stretch called Melaras Gift, and passes Wormill, where the town of Pelling lies on its banks, at the foot of the Trinwood. Beyond Wormill the river reaches the falls of Erlmeet, where the Erl joins it and where <a href="Gisvale" class="internal-link">Gisvale</a> begins, and flows on past Giselstatt, the old seat of the Kings of Gisvale. East of the city it widens into Lake Tran and swings south at the Bend of the River, where Trinfurt, Barbridge and Turv, the Bendburgs, keep its banks and the Silentree Forest covers the western shore.</p>
<p>Below the Bend the Trinmo leaves Meadland and for much of its length forms a frontier in Ur. The <a href="Hobgoblin Invasion" class="internal-link">Hobgoblin Invasion</a> of 690PA to 696PA was halted at the river, and everything west of it was lost, among it Dursan, which sat at the confluence of the river and was renamed Hobgiliath by the conquerors, so that the Urien lands in the east are still divided from the hobgoblins of <a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a> in the west. Further south the river carries the trade of the <a href="Trinvale" class="internal-link">Trinvale Republics</a>, whose cities, <a href="Niedrest" class="internal-link">Niedrest</a> among them, were founded along its southern course.</p>
<p>The trade on the river has always been worth fighting over. In the <a href="Treaty of Uhl" class="internal-link">Treaty of Uhl</a> of 866PA both <a href="Meadrath" class="internal-link">Meadrath</a> and Vihr bound themselves to protect the trade routes along the Trinmo bend, and both kept the right to trade along the river and at the Uhl Toll.</p>
<h2>Melara and the Name</h2>
<p>The Meadi know the spirit of the river as <a href="Melara" class="internal-link">Melara</a>, the Trinmother, whose hair is said to be the flowing waters. In their tales she is either the river itself or its mother, and her name and that of Mera, the old goddess of rivers of the <a href="Turvik Paganism" class="internal-link">Turvik</a> peoples, are one name seen from two sides, though the tales do not agree on which is the older. The Turvik faith itself is held to have been born in the lands of the Trinmo Bank, and the Tribe of Turv lived at the Bend. The name of the river, which lives on in the Trinwood, in Trinfurt and in the Trinvale, is traditionally derived from Trin, the <a href="Greater Spirit" class="internal-link">Greater Spirit</a> whose name the Augur Ralfantis wove into the teachings of <a href="Augurism" class="internal-link">Augurism</a>.</p>
