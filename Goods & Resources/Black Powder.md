<p>Black powder, known in its homeland by the High Gelfir name Huling, is an explosive substance first produced by Shirin alchemists in Bardrab, the capital of the <a href="Twintail Imperium" class="internal-link">Twintail Imperium</a>, in 456PA. Long neglected in Nirydia and used there chiefly for signal fireworks aboard ships, it reached the gnomish realm of <a href="Ott" class="internal-link">Ott</a> in the 8th century by way of the eastern sea trade. There a group of Ottian scholars known as the Nabat refined it into a propellant and produced the first Ottian gun in 803PA. Black powder weapons first appeared on a surface battlefield during the <a href="Vorellan War" class="internal-link">Vorellan Wars</a> and remain rare in most surface realms in the early 10th century, although their manufacture is spreading quickly from Ott and <a href="Xer-Kol" class="internal-link">Xer-Kol</a> in the 920s.</p>  

<h2>Origins in the Twintail Imperium</h2>  
<p>Black powder was discovered in 456PA by practitioners of Lian, the Shirin art of <a href="Alchemy" class="internal-link">Alchemy</a>, working in Bardrab during the Late Natani Decline. The tradition these alchemists belonged to would only be formalized three centuries later as the imperial institution of the <a href="Liandar" class="internal-link">Liandar</a>, and the discovery appears in later Liandar histories as one of the achievements of their predecessors. The alchemists named the substance Huling. </p>  
<p>Although its properties were recognized, the invention was largely set aside by the imperial court. Historians attribute this to the cultural and religious climate of the period. Lakali clergy regarded the violent and unpredictable burst of the substance as dissonant, a force without balance or symmetry that sat poorly with the principle of divine consonance on which imperial legitimacy rested. The late Natani administration, paralysed by factional ministers and preoccupied with the decline of the dynasty, offered neither the patronage nor the coherent direction that might have turned the discovery to any practical end. Huling instead found a practical home at sea, where it was used for fireworks and signalling aboard ships in the Niryd Sea and the Gavivag Ocean, and it was through this maritime use that knowledge of the substance began to travel beyond the Imperium.</p>  

<h2>Transmission to Ilrakhan</h2>  
<p>Traders and ship captains working the lanes of the <a href="Ziflis Trade" class="internal-link">Ziflis Trade</a> carried Huling and the knowledge of its preparation westward over the following centuries, at first as a curiosity and a tool of naval signalling rather than as a weapon. By the early to mid 8th century the substance had reached Ott in the <a href="Kortopis" class="internal-link">Kortopi Mountains</a>, where it came to the attention of scholars of Prosum, the Ottian study of <a href="Artificery" class="internal-link">Artificery</a>.</p>  

<h2>The Nabat of Ott</h2>  
<p>A sect of Prosum scholars began experimenting with the substance in the 8th century, giving it the name black powder. Their interest was unusual within the Prosum and within gnomish scholarship more broadly, both of which had long been oriented almost entirely toward magic and artefacts. The group grew into the Nabat, the "School of Dust" in Ottian Gnomish, which embraced non magical research in a manner closer to the traditions of the gnomes' dwarven cousins than to those of the Prosum. Led by scholars such as Muglig Ruv, the Nabat developed new methods of refining and producing black powder, and in 803PA they produced the first prototype of the Ottian gun. The first Ottian rifles followed in the 870s.</p>  
<p>Despite the Nabat's emphasis on non magical research, Ottian black powder has remained closely tied to Artificery. Its production commonly relies on alchemical refinement, and Ottian firearms are usually fired by means of artefact igniters.</p>  

<h2>Spread in the 10th century</h2>  
<p>As with other advanced discoveries of the Underlands and the Kortopi holds, such as Currentbending in <a href="Tizin-Kol" class="internal-link">Tizin-Kol</a>, Ott sought to keep the secret of black powder under its control. Unlike those discoveries, however, the methods of refining powder and building firearms proved difficult to contain. Much of the metalwork and construction for Ottian firearms was carried out in collaboration with engineers of Xer-Kol, the long standing partner and rival of Ott, and the hold had begun refining and manufacturing black powder of its own by the mid 910s. Xer-Koli production deliberately avoids both alchemy and artefact work as far as possible, owing in part to the aversion of many dwarves to magic. Through its trade arm, the <a href="Xerental" class="internal-link">Xerental</a>, Xer-Kol has since become one of the leading innovators in firearms, and its largest customer is the Kingdom of <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a>, reached directly through the Xer-Kol tunnel.</p>  
<p>While <a href="The Ottian Syndicate" class="internal-link">The Ottian Syndicate</a> has long kept a firm hold on the grading, repair and creation of artefacts, the more decentralized and unorthodox Nabat proved far harder to control, and the commercial instincts of Ottian gnomes worked against any lasting secrecy. Ottian explosives were used by the dwarves of Ogul-Vosko to collapse tunnels during the <a href="Granite Crusade" class="internal-link">Granite Crusade</a>, most notably at the Battle of Modim Cave in 916PA, and in the Vorellan Wars the Ottian mercenary company known as the Copperheads brought gnomish mortars and flintlock rifles to the Battle of Cefran, the first use of black powder weapons on a surface battlefield. In the 920s the knowledge of refining and handling powder has begun to spread more widely, driven in particular by the <a href="Garmish Succession Crisis" class="internal-link">Garmish Civil War</a>, in which many of the parties seek firearms and cannons for use in sieges. Guns and cannons nevertheless remain extremely rare across most of the surface world.</p>  
<p>In the east, reports of the Ottian refinements have travelled back along the trade routes to Nirydia, where the use of black powder as a weapon is slowly spreading in the Twintail Imperium and in the human lands of Gorat to its south. A small number of vessels in the Niryd Sea are reported to carry early cannons.</p>  

<h2>Reception</h2>  
<p>Many of the feudal realms of Ilrakhan regard black powder with considerable concern, as a weapon capable of piercing plate and breaking formations threatens to upset the established balance of the battlefield. Religious responses have varied. The <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">Church of Iztaw</a> has recently proclaimed the use of firearms in the cause of the Candleflame to be just and right, and conservative elements within the Kethric and High Gethric denominations have welcomed firearms as a means to combat the dangerous and mysterious forces of magic. In the west and in the <a href="Trinicism" class="internal-link">Trinic</a> realms, a generally more progressive outlook has met the new weapons with greater curiosity, though for most surface realms black powder remains a very recent development whose consequences are still uncertain.</p>
