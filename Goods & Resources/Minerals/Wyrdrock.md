<p>Wyrdrock is a very rare ore of the western and southern <a href="Underlands" class="internal-link">underlands</a>, a dark and uncanny stone that those who know how to treat it turn into metal of extraordinary toughness. It is also called Gobrock. The name Wyrdrock speaks of how strange it has always seemed to those who find it, while the Urien and the dwarves use Gobrock, half in mockery, because only <a href="Goblinoid" class="internal-link">goblins</a> and <a href="Hobgoblin" class="internal-link">hobgoblins</a> have ever learned to work it. It is most famously used by the goblins of <a href="Mitimgar" class="internal-link">Mitimgar</a> and the hobgoblins of <a href="Ak-Saray" class="internal-link">Ak-Saray</a> in west Urland, and the secret of its treatment is kept by a select few lines of masters and students. The dwarves of <a href="Ogul-Vosko" class="internal-link">Ogul-Vosko</a> and the gnomes of <a href="Ott" class="internal-link">Ott</a> have long desired to learn it.</p> <h2>Properties</h2> <p>In the seam Wyrdrock is a dull, dark grey stone with a matte surface, and it is fragile. Properly treated it becomes something entirely different. It is non-magnetic, so that a lodestone passes over it unmoved, and it is both hard and tough, so that a blow that would shatter steel only dents it. It hardens under use, so that an edge that is struck, ground or worn grows harder instead of duller, and files and chisels of ordinary steel barely scratch it. For all this it is surprisingly light in the hand, lighter and sharper than any forged steel, and its blades are said to part the air with an edge that never dulls. It is easily told from <a href="Mithril" class="internal-link">mithril</a>, which has a silvery shine that Wyrdrock lacks.</p> <h2>Working the Ore</h2> <p>The whole craft rests on a single moment. The ore must be brought to a precise heat and quenched at once, and only a piece quenched to perfection takes on the toughness of Wyrdrock, while one heated or cooled wrongly turns brittle and shatters. A piece quenched the proper way is effectively immutable. It cannot be reforged or reshaped, so that every object of Wyrdrock is finished once and for all.</p> <p>The craft comes from the ancient sects of Mitimgar and the legendary Rock Keepers, a tradition of smiths and artists that has endured the fall of the sects and centuries of history. The hobgoblins have produced few such artisans themselves, but some of their great heroes were awarded weapons of Wyrdrock in exchange for their service and for the protection of the goblins and the various Rock Keepers. Beyond Mitimgar the secret has begun to fade, for the hobgoblins have very limited access to the ore itself. The tradition of the Wyrdrock smiths has become an oral one and is losing its hold on the modern day, and the last known object forged by the hobgoblin masters of Ak-Saray was the so called Forlorn Shield, made of pure Wyrdrock in 782PA.</p>  <p>Wyrdrock is found only in the western and southern underlands, and nowhere in quantity. Its seams often lie near those of <a href="Mithril" class="internal-link">mithril</a>, and the two must be mined very differently. Mithril is sturdy without any treatment and can be mined safely, but Wyrdrock is not, and miners who treat the two alike all too often crush or shatter the Wyrdrock before it can be used. Mithril itself is generally very rare and incredibly expensive, but it is the more common of the two across the world, in the deepest mines of the underlands.</p>  <p>Wyrdrock is made into weapons and armour, above all for champions and warlords, and into heavy tools for breaking rock. Pieces seldom leave the hands of their makers, and the best known outside Mitimgar is the Relic Sword of <a href="Korgaz" class="internal-link">Korgaz</a> the Blade of Noon, a plain, unornamented blade that never lost its edge. The goblins of Mitimgar use it to great effect in war. The Dridil, the "invincibles", are an elite guard of Mitimgar clad in full Wyrdrock armour and armed with Wyrdrock glaives, and they are among the most feared and infamous warriors of the underlands.</p>  <p>The dwarves of Ogul-Vosko, masterful metalworkers themselves, and the gnomes of Ott, the foremost artificers in the world, have long desired the secret of Wyrdrock and have not learned it. The gnomes have tried to buy it, to steal it and to study captured pieces, but a piece quenched the right way cannot be unmade and shows little of how it came to be. For Ogul-Vosko Wyrdrock is yet another reason for the <a href="Granite Crusade" class="internal-link">Granite Crusade</a> against Mitimgar, which began in 862PA at the call of the High Priest of Ogul-Makar, and in that war the goblins of Mitimgar have used it to great effect.</p>
