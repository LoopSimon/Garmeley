<p>The Vas Ira describes the many religious and spiritual traditions originally of the Bihra or "Kobolds" that are unified in various agreed upon principles such as the concept of Tahan, the universal observance of the Saspi and the strife to seek Jitang. The greater Vas Ira derives itself from ancient practices of so called Bihra Shamanism, and many of the Yova, the Great Teachers, are said to have risen up from these traditions as well. Many cultures beyond just the Bihra, such as the chunri Humans, practice the Vas Ira. The major denominations within the greater umbrella of the Vas Ira are Noorism, Bigronism, Rayadan and Iratism.</p>
<h4>Tahan</h4>
<p>The Tahan is a key concept in almost all traditions of the Vas Ira, describing a divine law and order in motion. Tahan derives from the old Lhibari "Tah" meaning "Flow" and views the world as a single stream of experiences and perspectives. An important concept within the Tahan is the acknowledgement of different and warring perspectives in the same flow of the cosmos. Usually the Tahan is simply viewed as the law that sustains being and dictates moral obligation: from the cosmic Order of the Gods to ones duties to society itself.</p>
<h4>Saspi</h4>
<p>Within the Vas Ira, Saspi refers to the most important rituals of worship and meditation observed by all its practitioners, usually done to strive for Jitang by reflecting the world around oneself into oneself and viewing ones own experiences and emotions as a story with an already decided beginning and ending to experience. The Saspi also includes various other traditions depending on the regional or personal denomination within the greater Vas Ira.</p>
<h4>Jitang</h4>
<p>Jitang is a term in the Vas Ira traditions describing various forms of emancipation, liberation, or release. In its traditional doctrine sense, it refers to freedom from the cycle of remembering and forgetting within the greater Tahan. In its psychological sense, Jitang requires self realization, self actualization, and self knowledge.</p>
<h2>Major Deities within the Vas Ira</h2>
<p>Within the cosmos as understood by the Vas Ira, it is important to acknowledge that, across all of the major four denominations and generally all of the minor ones, the divinity of the Gods themselves is never questioned. But as most of the Yova encourage the followers of the Vas Ira to devote themselves to a single God especially, often including swearing to fight the followers, forces, or sometimes even manifestations of their divine rivals themselves, worship of the various gods takes at times greatly varying shape and form. </p>
<h5>Haizu</h5>
<p>Haizu is the god of Water and Fire and the creator deity of the Universal Cosmos, often portrayed in a similar fashion to Pen-Zuhur as a Sun God, they reside within Shila, the Palace of the Sun and are worshipped at the 20th day of the First Sequence during the Haizum Festival While acknowledged as the creator of existence by almost all forms of the Vas Ira, they are rarely worshipped as the supreme god in any bihra or non bihra tradition.</p>
<h5>Raym</h5>
<p>Raym is the supreme being of the cosmos according to the Rayadan, he is the last child of the universe and the collective experience of all that has ever been. He manifests in various incarnations across the myth and is associated closely with Survival, Family and Love. The Pragala is the great mythos of Gasch, the first Incarnation of Raym, and often considered one of the greatest surviving works of literature in the world.</p>
<h5>Maduga</h5>
<p>One of the three principle powers within Iratism, Maduga is the great Protector of the Universe and a warring deity associated with Strength and Dance, she is the wife of Inpat and usually represented as action.</p>
<h5>Inpat</h5>
<p>Inpat, Husband to Maduga, is one of the three principle powers within Iratism representing consideration. Inpat is associated with Law and Hierarchies, both on a societal context of worldly rule but also in the greater question of the Tahan as an inward guiding principle.</p>
<h5>Irat</h5>
<p>Irat is the greatest of the three principle powers within Iratism, the child of Inpat & Maduga. He is widely revered, more specifically, as the remover of obstacles and bringer of good luck as well as the patron of arts and sciences, and the god of logic, intellect, and wisdom.</p>
<h5>The Divine Beasts</h5>
<p>Noor-Rah, the Wingless Dragongod of the Limsul Peaks; Pihal-biim, the Lord of Dreams; Mupat of the Deathwind; Ginyaka the Mother and Eshgal the great Sage are all greater Gods within the Vas Ira, and considered the so called "divine Beasts", born as children of Haizu within his Dreams.  </p>
