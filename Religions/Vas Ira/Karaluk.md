<p>The Karaluk, from the Lihbare for "United Greats", is a school of the <a href="Vas Ira" class="internal-link">Vas Ira</a> practised by the <a href="Bihra" class="internal-link">Bihra</a> of the southern Nirydian coast, most of all in the <a href="Kareli Hierarchy" class="internal-link">Kareli Hierarchy</a>, <a href="Paqor" class="internal-link">Paqor</a> and the <a href="Ganoa Valley" class="internal-link">Ganoa Valley</a>.. Its followers worship the Divine Beasts of the Vas Ira, Noor-Rah, Pihal-biim, Mupat of the Deathwind, Ginyaka the Mother and Eshgal the Sage, through idols kept in temples and household shrines, and honour them together as the Greats born from the dreams of Haizu rather than raising a single one of them above the others. Which of the Greats is most favoured differs from region to region and often from city to city, so that a temple in one port may be built around a single Beast while the next is shared by all five.</p>  
<p>The Karaluk developed among the coastal Bihra in the centuries after the fall of the <a href="Vol" class="internal-link">Empire of Vol</a> and became the faith of the Bihra realms of <a href="Ekkam" class="internal-link">Ekkam</a> and <a href="Az" class="internal-link">Az</a>. Unlike <a href="Noorism" class="internal-link">Noorism</a>, which reveres Noor-Rah as the supreme being of the cosmos and is practised almost only in the valleys of the Limsul Peaks, the Karaluk honours the Wingless Dragon as one Great among five, worshipped through its images by people who have never seen it.</p>  
<h4>Regional Favour</h4>  
<p>Eshgal the Sage is the most widely favoured Great in Aznyr and the towns along the Velvet Road, where scribes, merchants and teachers keep his idol in their houses and counting rooms. Mupat of the Deathwind is favoured in Relloril, the old capital of Ekkam, and on the islands of the Citrine Sea, where sailors make offerings to him before the storm season, as well as in <a href="Majir" class="internal-link">Majir</a>, where the poisonous fumes of the Parabol Domes are commonly called his breath. Ginyaka the Mother is the patron of <a href="Jink" class="internal-link">Jink</a> and of the farming and fishing villages along the shores of Lake Ganoa. Pihal-biim, the Lord of Dreams, is most honoured in Halad, the former Uzra Vol, and in the Citrine Swamp, while Noor-Rah is chiefly worshipped in the inland towns closest to the Limsul Peaks, through which pilgrims travel towards <a href="Rha-Gis" class="internal-link">Rha-Gis</a>. The temples of Paqor most often house all five Greats together.</p>  
<h4>The Unborn Great</h4>  
<p>A sect of the Karaluk centred in the Ganoa Valley and the Citrine Swamp counts the One of the <a href="Javalim" class="internal-link">Javalim</a> as a sixth Great. Where the Javalim teach that the One sacrificed himself at the Pillar on Mount Javal so that all life could come to be, the sect believes that he is being born anew within the dreams of Haizu and that his birth as the sixth Great is yet to come. Its followers therefore hold Pihal-biim in particular reverence as the keeper of the dream in which the One is growing. Although the sect stands outside the faith of <a href="Roxeria" class="internal-link">Roxeria</a>, its members are among the few foreigners permitted to make pilgrimage to Mount Javal.</p>
