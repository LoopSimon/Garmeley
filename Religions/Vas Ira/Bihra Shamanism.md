%%[[Bihra]]%%

Bihra or "Kobold" Shamanistic beliefs and practices vary between the different cultural denominations of the Nirydian Bihra, notably changing drastically between the mountain tribes of the north, the southern Khaganates or the cities of the eastern Bihra.

Across most cultures however, Shamans and people filling similar roles to those performed by shamans among the Bihra, such as fortune-telling, magical rites (primarily weather based) and finding lost objects, are leaders and magical authorities within their communities.

Shamans in Bihra Culture usually tend to be born with physical anomalies such as a surplus of arms , legs or eyes, or even to greater extend secondary heads, the famous Rhoka, a character of the [[Kherova|Kherovan]] Myth Ralond and Reana, is said to have possessed 3 heads, likely not an exaggerated detail.

Soul-Dualism within Bihra Shamanism:

A main element of Bihra Shamanism is the belief that a person has two or even more types of souls, in many cases, one of the souls is associated with body functions, usually referred to as the "base soul", whereas others could leave the body itself, usually referred to as "higher souls".
An important characteristic of a shamans "higher soul" is its ability to undertake a spirit journey.

In the terms of Nirydian and Ilrakhi scholarship, the magic of Bihra shamans is a form of [[Weaving]], and most Bihra remain averse to arcane and other non spiritual magicks, preferring to rely on their long standing and largely oral shamanic traditions.

An element of western Bihra Shamanism is the belief of a shared soul, usually with an animal companion such as the Salamander Mounts of the Bihra Khaganates within the Nirydian Steppe. The most infamous corruption of Bihra soul-dualism is the forbidden [[Soulbond|Art of the Soulbond]], attributed to Volys the Spiritdrinker of [[Vol]], in which a shaman consumes the souls of the living to prolong their own life.

Among the Steppe Bihra the many shamanic traditions are bound together by a set of shared principles, the strongest of them being the triumph of the physical, the belief that spiritual superiority is proven through strength, mutation and victory. It was this principle that led the Khans of the [[Great Khaganates of the Steppe]] to hunt the practitioners of the [[Soulbond|Art of the Soulbond]], whose theft of the spirit they saw as a perverse destruction of the physical.


An elevated form of Bihra Shamanism, is the Dragonworship in [[Rha-Gis]], where the eternal being Noor-Rah has been accepted as the final form of all life.
