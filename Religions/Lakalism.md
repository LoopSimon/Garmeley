A monotheistic Faith that believes in the god Lakal and divine symmetry, it is the state religion of the [[Twintail Imperium]] of central Nirydia.

<p> Lakalism honours symmetry and consonance within nature and culture, as expressed within their four aspects of Lakal: holy symbols that represent the forces through which their god works his wonders on the realm of mortals.
Mathematics is a holy calling for many of the faith, and divine symbols can often express themselves as geometrical shapes or fractals.</p> 

<p> The Practice of worshipping anatomical symmetry is commonplace within the faith, mutations such as the name-giving twinned tail of the Royal Line are seen as holy omens, while the mutilation of one half of the body to create dissonance is seen as a punishment often worse than death, examples include the infamous practice of the Emperors of the Twinned-Tail to remove the eyes or ears of their political enemies.</p> 

<h2>History</h2> <h5>Origins within Goarism (120 BA - 60 BA)</h5> <p>Lakalism did not emerge as a sudden rupture from the religious traditions that preceded it but as a gradual crystallization of tendencies already latent within the <a href="Goarism" class="internal-link">Goara</a> faith that had governed Shirin spiritual life since the Age of Cities. The earliest recognizable articulations of what would become Lakalist theology appear in the scholarly literature of the Mondi Delta cities around 120BA, during the middle period of the <a href="Greater Aatar" class="internal-link">Greater Aatar</a>. In this earliest form, Lakalism was less an independent religion than a philosophical position within Goarism itself, an argument that the cyclical natural harmony the Goara tradition described was most purely expressed in the mathematical structures of bilateral symmetry, and that Goarism as institutionally practiced had become disconnected from this fundamental principle through centuries of accommodation to political power.</p> <p>The initial carriers of this tendency were concentrated among the scribal and scholarly classes of the delta cities, particularly those associated with the mathematical and astronomical traditions of the major temple complexes. The observation that symmetrical structures recurred with extraordinary consistency across natural phenomena, in the branching of river tributaries, in the physical forms of living creatures, in the geometric properties of crystalline minerals, was not new. What the earliest Lakalic thinkers contributed was the theological interpretation of this pattern as evidence of a single divine will expressed through consonance, rather than the Goaric understanding of natural harmony as an impersonal cyclical principle. The god Lakal, in these earliest formulations, was not yet the fully developed theological figure of later Lakalism but an emerging conceptual framework for understanding divine intentionality in the structure of the world.</p> <p>The Goara priesthood's initial response to these developments was dismissive rather than hostile. The mathematical and symmetry focused tendencies of the early Lakalic scholars appeared, from the perspective of the established religious hierarchy, as an interesting but peripheral intellectual fashion unlikely to challenge the institutional dominance of the Goaric temple system. This assessment would prove significantly mistaken.</p> <h5>Growth and the Aatar Conflict (60 BA - 83 PA)</h5> <p>The transformation of Lakalism from a scholarly tendency into a mass movement with genuine political consequences began approximately 60BA, driven by a convergence of material crisis and institutional failure that the established Goaric order proved unable to address. A sustained period of agricultural difficulty across the delta and river plain produced widespread hardship that the Greater Aatar's granary redistribution system, stretched by decades of population growth, could not fully absorb. The Goaric priesthood's response, intensifying ritual practice and attributing the hardship to collective spiritual failure, satisfied neither the scholarly class that had been engaging with Lakalic arguments for two generations nor the urban poor whose suffering required material rather than theological remedy.</p> <p>Lakalism offered something qualitatively different from the Goaric establishment's response. Its claim that the present disorder was the consequence of fundamental misalignment between the Aatar's institutionalized religious practice and the true principles of divine symmetry simultaneously explained the crisis, identified its cause in the failings of existing authority, and implied a program of reform that required not more of the same ritual intensification but a genuine transformation of political and religious order. The movement spread rapidly through the urban centres of the delta and river plain across the first century BA, finding its most enthusiastic reception among the literate administrative class, scribes, surveyors, lawyers, and junior temple officials, whose professional formation in mathematical and accounting disciplines made the Lakalic emphasis on mathematical holiness immediately legible as a validation of their own expertise and social role.</p> <p>The coexistence of Goarism and Lakalism during the final century of Aatar rule was tense and increasingly unmanageable. The two traditions competed openly for the loyalty of the Aatar's provincial administrators, military commanders, and the ruling families of the major cities. Goaric temple institutions that had dominated Shirin spiritual life for centuries found themselves challenged by Lakalic communities that offered alternative ritual frameworks, alternative legitimizing narratives for political authority, and alternative networks of literate administrative expertise that provincial rulers could draw upon without dependence on the established Goaric hierarchy. The Aatar court's attempts to manage this competition through selective suppression and accommodation satisfied neither party and produced only the escalating factional instability that ultimately destroyed the empire. By the formal dissolution of the Greater Aatar in 83PA, the Goaric tradition had effectively lost its capacity to function as the dominant unifying religious framework of Shirin civilization, surviving primarily in the ritual practices of rural communities and the conservative wings of a few urban temple institutions.</p> <h5>Dominance and the Twintail (83 PA - 175 PA)</h5> <p>The Era of Four Kingdoms that followed the Aatars collapse was the period in which Lakalism completed its displacement of Goarism as the dominant Shirin religious framework. By approximately 100PA, Lakalism was the effective religious identity of the major urban centers of the Mondi Delta and river plain, its theological vocabulary the shared language of political legitimacy, legal reasoning, and scholarly discourse across all four kingdoms. The Goaric tradition persisted but in a fundamentally diminished form, a faith of rural margins, aging temple institutions, and conservative aristocratic families whose identities were bound up with the pre-Lakalic order.</p> <p>The competition among the four kingdoms, Bardrab, Tiakait, Prasarp, and Tonot, was therefore simultaneously a political and a theological contest. The Lakalic establishment, by this period institutionally mature enough to function as an independent political force, distributed its patronage among the competing rulers based on their theological credentials and the degree to which their courts embodied Lakalic administrative and legal principles. Rulers who presented themselves as defenders of Lakalist orthodoxy accessed priestly legitimacy, scribal expertise, and the organizational networks of the temple complexes. Those who hedged their religious commitments found themselves structurally disadvantaged in an environment where Lakalic institutional support had become the single most important non-military asset available to any aspiring unifier.</p> <p>It was within this context that Natan I of Bardrab rose to found the <a href="Twintail Imperium" class="internal-link">Twintail Imperium</a> in 175PA. His adoption of Lakalism not merely as a personal faith but as the explicit ideological foundation of the new imperial order, embedded in the institutional design of the administration, expressed in the structure of legal codes, and articulated in the regnal name he assumed upon accession, represented the definitive conclusion of the two century process by which a tendency within Goarism had become the state religion of the largest Shirin political entity ever assembled. The Timit Codex, completed under Yoroy II in 640PA, represents Lakalism's fullest expression as a jurisprudential system the point at which the faith's principles of symmetrical consonance were translated into a comprehensive and self-consistent framework of imperial law.</p> <p>The most significant challenge Lakalism has faced as the state religion of the Twintail Imperium was not the lingering Goaric tradition, which it had effectively displaced by the time the Imperium was founded, but the rise of <a href="Zuhurism" class="internal-link">Zuhurism</a> as an alternative universal faith with an explicit ideological critique of Lakalist political theology. The <a href="Vigzil" class="internal-link">Vigzil</a> exile communitys adoption of Zuhurism following the Khulki Rebellion of 612PA-627PA established a permanent Shirin state defined by religious opposition to Lakalism, and the broader Zuhuri presence across Nirydia ensures that Lakalisms claim to universal validity continues to face organized ideological contestation.</p>
