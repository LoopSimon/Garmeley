<p>Star worship, sometimes referred to as Astral Cultism, is the veneration of stars by the faithful of <a href="Cyraclion" class="internal-link">Cyraclion</a>.</p>
<p>The observation and veneration of celestial bodies likely always played a larger role in the ancient Cyraclian culture, even before the development of a dominant theology within the League itself.</p>
<h2>The Two Strongest Forces</h2>
<p>The Star <a href="Malham" class="internal-link">Malham</a> is worshipped as the Deity Xanring and associated with cosmic order and governance, and is viewed as a symbol of earthly power and hierarchical power.</p>
<p>The Star <a href="Urden" class="internal-link">Urden</a> is worshipped as the Deity Shoyal and associated with the elements and magic.</p>
<p>The ability to align the celestial forces of Malham and Urden is seen as essential to the legitimacy of the First Seer and the Oracle.</p>
<h2>The Lesser Powers</h2>
<p>Beside the two strongest forces stand four lesser powers.</p>
<p><a href="Sarhen" class="internal-link">Sarhen</a>, the brightest star in the sky, is worshipped as Vessal, God of Time, Seasons and Harvest, and the calendar of the League was kept by his rising.</p>
<p><a href="Tholmir" class="internal-link">Tholmir</a>, the pale gold star of the far south, is worshipped as Kuraz, the Wayfinder, God of Journeys and Distance.</p>
<p><a href="Karrod" class="internal-link">Karrod</a>, the red star that flares without warning, is worshipped as Morgren, God of War, Fate and Ruin.</p>
<p><a href="Dyrrim" class="internal-link">Dyrrim</a>, the twin star, is worshipped as Samiral, God of Oaths, Pacts and Kinship, and the treaties of the League were sworn under it.</p>

<h2>The Broken Star</h2>
<p>Beside the fixed stars the Cyclopi kept the record of one that moves. The Broken Star, the family of comets of the <a href="The Tis" class="internal-link">Tis</a>, comes back together every few centuries and crosses the sky for some years, and its returns were entered in the temple tables of Cyraclion for generations. It has no god and no place among the six powers, for it is not a light that stays, but the League read each return as a sign that the heavens were shifting, and <a href="Tripparch" class="internal-link">Tripparch</a> is said to have been the first to see that the returns were one cycle. The meteors that fall each year from the same quarter of the sky are held to be its dust.</p>

<h2>Names</h2>
<p>The names the Cyclopi gave these stars remain the names by which they are known in the wider world, even where other peoples revere them differently. Each star's page lists the names used by the other cultures. The Qari, who know many of the same stars as the <a href="Qinari" class="internal-link">Qinari</a> of Qalmyr, have names of their own for them.</p>
