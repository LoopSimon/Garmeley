<p>Saint Tytus the Unboundrepresents Fate, Time and Ruins within the Gethric Canon, his favoured weapon is the Gladius. Tytus was born a vihri human in 514PA in the town of Pinopki, near Trydlew, and died at the age of 35 in 549PA, slain by his own sister. He was canonised at the Conclave of 555PA. He is venerated by soldiers, seers and those who mourn lost things, and his image is among the most common in Vihri roadside shrines. His primary place of worship is the Cathedral of Will in Tlokyemy, built on the site where he assembled his forces before marching on Xurugam.</p>

<p>Tytus entered the Gethric clergy young and rose to the rank of Minister by his early twenties, unremarkable in his piety if not in his intelligence, until visions struck him without warning in 538PA, two years after the signing of the <a href="Wermian Accords" class="internal-link">Wermian Accords</a>. The Churchs account holds that the Prophet appeared to him directly, charging him with what was coming. Tytus himself never confirmed this, recording only that he had seen things he had no language to describe. He subsequently renounced his clerical rank, took up a gladius he claimed had been left for him, a blade later observed to corrode and ruin the flesh of enemies and close the wounds of allies at its bearer's touch, and began walking north toward the <a href="Xer-Kol" class="internal-link">Xer-Kol</a> mountains. Alone and without armour, sustained according to witnesses by a visible divine radiance that turned blades and arrows from his body.</p>

<p>The Dwarven hold of Xer-Kol had by this time fallen almost entirely under the dominion of Mendizaba, a dwarven necromancer who had achieved lichdom within her mountain stronghold of Xurugam and was raising the <a href="Unliving" class="internal-link">dead</a> of the Tlok Valley in skeletal legions. Tytus arrived at the Tloky settlements ahead of any Church authority and began organizing a combined force of local Vihri and dwarves still free of Mendizabas influence. He led this force against her skeletal host across three engagements in the Tlok Valley in 545PA, breaking the undead army, then entered Xurugam alone. He emerged three days later, carrying Mendizabas phylactery, which he destroyed before witnesses. The Kherovan Patriarchate subsequently cited the Xurugam campaign as among the most direct demonstrations of what unchecked necromantic power could achieve. Tytus had proven the prophetic wisdom of the Accords in blood.</p>

<p>He did not live long after. Tytus had sworn an oath, in the precise Gethric formulation that cannot be unsaid, never to divine the future of his sister Yezofia, who had asked this of him when they were but orphans surviving together in Pinopki. She murdered him in 549PA, seeking to claim the gladius and the prophetic gifts she believed could be taken from him. Neither transferred. Yezofia fled south to the Yellow Moor, where she was eventually hunted down and tried as a witch by the <a href="Kherovan Inquisition" class="internal-link">Kherovan Inquisition</a> three decades later. The Church canonised Tytus six years after his death, during the turbulence of the Vihri War of Succession when a reminder of sacred continuity was as useful as it was sincere. His gladius, known as the Corrodent, has not been located since his death and is considered one of the most sought after holy relics of the faith.</p>
