<p>Saint Luca Cadente represents Love, the Moon and the Stars within the Gethric Canon, his favoured weapon is the Halberd. Luca was born a Cevoli human in 531PA in the city of Cefran and died at the age of 58 in 589PA, he was canonised at the Conclave of 592PA under High-Bisop Servann II. He is the Patron Saint of <a href="Cevolis" class="internal-link">Cevolis</a> and her successor states and is beloved across all three major denominations, his primary place of worship is the Duomo delle Lune in the city of Molina, containing the tallest Tower of any known building in Ilrakhan.</p>

<p>A poet of the streets and squares of Cefran in eastern <a href="Cevolia" class="internal-link">Cevolia</a>, he grew into a figure of considerable local renown before his divine gifts became apparent in a development his contemporaries would later describe as the natural consequence of a man who had spent his entire life speaking absolute truth about the human heart. His poetry concerned itself obsessively with the two moons of the night sky, which he understood as the twin faces of love: one constant and full, the other perpetually turning away. These were not decorative metaphors for Luca but sincere theological positions, albeit the <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">Church of Iztaw</a> would only tangentially recognize in them an official contribution to Gethric cosmology. Many of Lucas poems, like his famous "Pallida Unione" have survived until the present day, largely due to his later canonisation as a Saint.</p>

<p>The deed for which Luca is most celebrated was the resolution of the crisis of Parsa in 581PA. The <a href="Daemon" class="internal-link">Rakshasha</a> known as the Bane of Parsa had established itself in the Cevoli borderlands adjacent to the Dwarven holds of <a href="Ogul-Vosko" class="internal-link">Ogul-Vosko</a>, manipulating locals through illusion and false counsel until men and dwarf were on the verge of open war over a series of provocations that neither side had actually initiated. Luca traveled to Parsa alone, without arms or escort, carrying only his instruments and his writing. The precise account of what transpired over the three days he spent in the Rakshasas court varies considerably between sources, but all versions agree that the creature was bested entirely through language, exposed through a sequence of poetic challenges it could not resist answering without revealing its own deceptions. The Beast was bound by the terms of a bargain whose precise conditions Luca had embedded within an extended verse before the Rakshasa had understood what it was agreeing to. He emerged with an magical script that nullified every false claim the creature had made to both parties, walked into the Dwarven hall and the Cevoli war camp in succession and read it aloud, and the war that had been days from beginning simply did not happen. The war ready <a href="Fellows of the Dancing Flame" class="internal-link">Fellows of the Dancing Flame</a> hailed him as a hero of Cevolia despite his having never raised a weapon, a contradiction that Luca himself reportedly found amusing.</p>

<p>Lucas is claimed by all three major denominations without meaningful dispute, a rarity among Saints canonised close enough to the Great Schism to have their memory coloured by it. <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">High Gethricism</a> venerates him for the rigour and personal conviction with which he upheld his oaths and the miraculous effectiveness of his faith in confronting evil directly. Trinicist communities, particularly in <a href="Cevolia" class="internal-link">Cevolia</a>, claim him as a forerunner of their own values such as the primacy of individual character over institutional authority, the power of the common person's unmediated conviction. The <a href="Kethricism" class="internal-link">Kherovan Patriarchate</a> accepts him without controversy, finding in his binding of the Rakshasa through a sworn compact an expression of Law and Truth that sits comfortably within Kethric theological priorities. His feast day, celebrated at the autumn equinox when both moons are visible on the same night, is one of the most widely observed in Cevolia regardless of denomination.</p>
