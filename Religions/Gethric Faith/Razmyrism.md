<p>Razmyrism, also known as the Razmyrist Sect, is a <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethric</a> religious movement that views the Soul Shepherd <a href="Razmyr" class="internal-link">Razmyr</a> as the one true Prophet and questions the role of <a href="Gethric" class="internal-link">Gethric</a> in the <a href="The Incursion" class="internal-link">Incursion</a>. Many regard it as vaguely heretical. The Razmyrists are a controversial group that largely keeps to itself and has been repeatedly persecuted. They are found in parts of <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Grevenna</a> and <a href="Dalniz" class="internal-link">Dalniz</a>, in the Gethric communities of <a href="Il Haena" class="internal-link">Il-Haena</a> and in parts of <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Hainach</a> and southern <a href="Ur" class="internal-link">Ur</a>, usually in very small and isolated communities. The best known institution of the movement is the monastic order of the <a href="Broken Fingers" class="internal-link">Broken Fingers</a>.</p>
<h2>Beliefs and Practice</h2>
<p>Razmyrists revere Razmyr, the scholar and companion of Gethric who compiled the writings and records of the earliest fathers of the Church into the White Book, as the true saviour of the faith. They keep a version of the White Book of their own, known as the Codex Razmyra. The movement has its own priests as well, although outside the Broken Fingers they often act as underground sects and cults.</p>
<p>Razmyrism is largely inspired by the Practice of <a href="Qalmyr" class="internal-link">Qalmyr</a>, the moon faith of the orcs, whose holy land is Il-Haena. Its ideas reached the movement through the Gethric communities of northern Il-Haena, in cities such as Ras Ain and Mohog. Razmyrists believe the Prophet Razmyr to have been the child of Qastor, the Pale Father, and imagine him as a kind of reincarnation who drove back the forces of Zolra, the Ruinous Mother, during what other Gethrics call the Incursion.</p>
<p>Despite these differences, Razmyrists observe almost all of the Gethric rites properly, and in their practice they are far more closely aligned with High Gethricism than with <a href="Trinicism" class="internal-link">Trinicism</a>. The concept of <a href="Remembrance" class="internal-link">Remembrance</a>, derived from the old Rite of Remembrance and from musings attributed to Razmyr that are not part of the greater Gethric Canon, is widely accepted within the movement, although it is less popular in other clerical and academic communities.</p>
<h2>History</h2>
<p>The ideas of Razmyrism are believed to date back to the founding days of the Church, but the movement only gained wider popularity in the early 8th century. In 713PA the Var Gizimor zi <a href="Selbia" class="internal-link">Selbia</a> had all practising Razmyrists in his lands rounded up, peaceful as they were, and his knights mutilated their hands by trampling them under the hooves of their horses. The monks of the island of Ardol in southern Grevenna, an order founded in the 5th century in devotion to the teachings of Razmyr that had become one of the strongholds of the movement, later adopted the name of the Broken Fingers as a mark of pride.</p>
<p>In the 8th and 9th centuries the Razmyrists of <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Kherova</a> and <a href="Azuvke" class="internal-link">Azuvke</a>, the core lands of <a href="Kethricism" class="internal-link">Kethricism</a>, suffered frequent attacks by the <a href="Kherovan Inquisition" class="internal-link">Kherovan Inquisition</a>. They were carried out primarily by the Tribunal of the Marble Script, which is charged with religious peace and the persecution of heretics within Kherova, and by the Keepers of the Pale Truth, the internal police of the Inquisition and the clergy. The attacks have since subsided, as has Razmyrist activity in the Kethric lands.</p>
<p>Although Razmyrists have been persecuted on many occasions, the <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">Church of Iztaw</a> largely tolerates the Broken Fingers, as the order has never acted against the Church in any observable way. Razmyrists remain a small minority in most places. The monks of Ardol work as missionaries in heathen lands, in Il-Haena, on the Niryd Sea, in the <a href="Elbae" class="internal-link">Elbae</a> Sultanate, in Hainach and even in southern <a href="Tal" class="internal-link">Tal</a>, and have done much to spread the faith.</p>
