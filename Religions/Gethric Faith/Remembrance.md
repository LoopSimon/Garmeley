<p>Remembrance is a religious concept derived from the old <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethric</a> Rite of Remembrance. Its origins liein some of the earliest records of the <a href="White Book" class="internal-link">White Book</a> of Saint <a href="Razmyr" class="internal-link">Razmyr</a> and in musings that are attributed to him but are not part of the greater Gethric Liturgy. As an institution and magical thesis it is widely accepted within <a href="Razmyrism" class="internal-link">Razmyrism</a> but less popular in other clerical and academic communities. It is kept by the Remembrancers, an order of practitioners from across the Gethric denominations who preserve the names of the dead and read them aloud without end.</p>
<h2>Origins</h2>
<p>Razmyr the Soul Shepherd, who represents Death and Knowledge within the Gethric Canon, wrote the White Book, the core of which is the words and prophecies of the Prophet. He was an avid student of the soul and is held largely responsible for the adoption of the concept of the Homestead Beyond, and central themes of the faith such as the nature of the soul within this world and the next can be traced back to him. The old Rite of Remembrance dates back to some of the earliest records of the White Book, while the concept of Remembrance as the Remembrancers keep it also draws on musings of Razmyr that the Church never received into the Liturgy. The Sedesarium, the part of the White Book in which Razmyr tells of his visions of the Prophet, ends with the ideals of remembrance.</p>
<h2>The Remembrancers</h2>
<p>The Remembrancers are drawn from across the Gethric denominations and see themselves called to a higher task than worldly proselytising. Among the seats of the order are Wizto Castle in <a href="Szievek" class="internal-link">Szievek</a> in <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a> and the Cavern of Solemn Memory in <a href="Weadland" class="internal-link">Weadland</a>, in the territories of the cities of the <a href="Blijgrat" class="internal-link">Blijgrat League</a>.</p>
<p>The Remembrancers keep the names of the dead on name-rolls and read them aloud without end, in the belief that this shelters the souls of those named. They guard their name-rolls like holy treasures. The harshest sentence their order can pass is the burning of the condemned's own name-roll, so that no one will remember them. Necromancers, or at least those who aspire to the art, have repeatedly attempted to steal the name-rolls of the Remembrancers.</p>
<p>Some Remembrancers claim that the oldest halls of names have begun to open onto the spiritual realm as the urien tombs once did, the tombs in which the first <a href="Dulmic Gate" class="internal-link">Dulmic Gates</a> were observed. It is a claim their order has been careful not to repeat.</p>
