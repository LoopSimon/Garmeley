<p>Diarism is a <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethric</a> religious movement active in western <a href="Meadrath" class="internal-link">Meadrath</a>, in <a href="Sertova" class="internal-link">Sertova</a> and <a href="Othirdon" class="internal-link">Othirdon</a> and in the colonies of the new world, named after its founder, the theologian <a href="Perrin Diar" class="internal-link">Perrin Diar</a>. Its core tenet is the unification of <a href="Kalbinism" class="internal-link">Kalbinist</a> and Gethric belief, and it holds that <a href="Gethric" class="internal-link">Gethric</a> himself was the only true incarnation of the Creator. The movement was born with Diar's address at the University of <a href="Jera" class="internal-link">Jera</a> in 894PA and is still a young denomination that has been part of clerical discourse for barely three decades. It is a heresy to some and the true faith to others, and the city of Ballina, where Diar lives under the protection of its lord, has become its unofficial centre.</p>
<h2>Doctrine</h2>
<p>Diarism elevates Gethric above all others in his singularity. It rejects the divinity of the later prophets of classical Kalbinism as well as that of the traditional Gethric Saints, and holds Gethric to have been the one true incarnation of the Creator whom the Kalbinists worship. A less discussed aspect of the movement is its incorporation of older regional traditions of the worship of <a href="Augurism" class="internal-link">Augur Ralfantis</a>, whose cult survives in northern and western Meadrath and in <a href="Cimfon" class="internal-link">Cimfon</a>. The figure of "Gethric the Creator and Saviour" that Diarists revere in many ways resembles the Augur more than either the Creator of Kalbinism or the Prophet of Gethricism. Others connect much of this imagery to classical depictions of Saint <a href="Markur" class="internal-link">Markur</a> instead, and rumours that Diar is himself a member of the <a href="Scions of Markur" class="internal-link">Scions of Markur</a> persist.
<p>Diarists seek a shared meaning in the two faiths in the eyes of a single Creator. Many regard Saint Kalbin as a figure analogous to <a href="Razmyr" class="internal-link">Razmyr</a>, a worldly prophet who shared the divine will and message of Gethric, the Creator, among the children of the earth. Diarists hold the whole <a href="White Book" class="internal-link">White Book</a> to be scripture but give less weight to the Institutiones of Volboris and the later tales of the Pandecta, and Diar's On the One Flame reads the Vita flammae as the life of the Creator made flesh. Diar also teaches progressive views on the relationship between lord and serf and on the ties of the faithful to earthly wealth, and his teachings on equality and worldly wealth are among the best known parts of the movement.</p>
<h2>Worship</h2>
<p>Diarists celebrate both the Gethric Day of the Prophet and the Kalbinist Hour of Revelation on the 20th of October, the day on which Kalbin is held to have heard the will of the Creator. The Day of the Prophet, held at the end of summer, is called the Creator's Light among Diarists, who keep it as a whole week of rest. During that week little to no earthly work should be done, and rest, prayer and introspection should come first.</p>
<p>The movement is spread by its preachers, who have co-opted local churches and traditions of <a href="Panrity" class="internal-link">Panrity</a>, the dominant branch of Kalbinism in Othirdon, in a wave of conversions. Organised Diarist worship is not looked upon kindly in Meadland and has been outlawed by various Meadi lords, unlike across the mountains in the valley of Ballina.</p>
<h2>History</h2>
<p>Perrin Diar, born Perrin Ulrink in 864PA, addressed the scholars of the University of Jera in the Archduchy of <a href="Dirneux" class="internal-link">Dirneux</a> in western Meadrath in 894PA and spoke on the nature of divinity and worship. The address is held to mark the official birth of Diarism. Soon afterwards Perrin took the name Diar, meaning "servant of truth" in the old Locni tongue, and the movement came to be named after it. By 901PA Diarism was spreading with much welcome in western Meadland and Weadland and in central Othirdon, and Diar gained a massive following in the feudal dominions on both sides of the Ardfall Pass.</p>
<p>The movement long remained on the fringe of the faith until 912PA, when Diar was ambushed by a group of armed assassins, reportedly knights on horseback, in the village of Rubak in southern Cimfon. His assailants called him a "Stain on the Creator's Visage" and tried to take him prisoner in order to burn him alive, but his followers brought him to safety. The attack marked a turning point in the reactions to Diarism. In the same year Treffyn Effel, the Lord of Ballina, invited Diar to his city and offered him his protection and guardianship, and Diar has lived as a guest in the halls of Castle Ballina ever since.</p>
<h2>Reception</h2>
<p>Neither the <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">Church of Iztaw</a> nor any <a href="Trinicism" class="internal-link">Trinicist</a> bishop has yet formally condemned Diarism, but movements against it have formed within the Trinicist bishoprics, and the Bishop of <a href="Morelle" class="internal-link">Morelle</a> is a firm opponent of the movement. Worldly rulers have on the whole proven more inclined towards Diarism than the clergy, although many have come to hate Diar, not least for his views on lord and serf and on earthly wealth.</p>
<p>In Jera, where the movement was born, the Bishop of Jera, Berac Letrouse, an old friend of Diar and counted among the great living theologians of the Trinicist church, is surprisingly open to its ideals. Archduke <a href="Jean du Jera" class="internal-link">Jean du Jera</a>, himself a Trinicist with little love for the movement, has never moved against the university.</p>
<h2>Distribution</h2>
<p>In Meadland, Diarism is present in parts of western Meadrath, above all in Dirneux. West of the mountains it has spread in Sertova and Othirdon, the two sister regions, above all in the valley of Ballina, around the Visian Sea, in the Sertovi kingdom of <a href="Ravland" class="internal-link">Ravland</a> and on the island of <a href="Droil" class="internal-link">Droil</a>.</p>
<p>Diarism has also crossed the ocean to the colonies of <a href="Gethricism in the New World" class="internal-link">Aisligos</a>, where it is a very popular movement among the settlers and traders and is less regulated than in the old world. It is the primary faith of <a href="Ulbreek" class="internal-link">Ulbreek</a>, a free port off the southern coast of <a href="Peadyr" class="internal-link">Peadyr</a> founded by the Meadi nobleman <a href="Marrin Andil" class="internal-link">Marrin Andil</a> in 913PA, and one of the primary faiths of the Ravlander colony of <a href="New Fryd" class="internal-link">New Fryd</a> and of the free port of <a href="Vaggerport" class="internal-link">Vaggerport</a>. The clergy of the movement are few and little established in Ulbreek, where slave auctions are held openly as part of the <a href="Narzoi Slavetrade" class="internal-link">Narzoi Slavetrade</a>, and its settlers read the teachings of Perrin Diar on equality and worldly wealth as meant for themselves and their betters, and not for slaves.</p>
