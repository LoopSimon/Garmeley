<p>Kethricism is the dominant faith of the <a href="Kherova" class="internal-link">Khatrym of Kherova</a> and the <a href="Azuvke" class="internal-link">Principality of Azuvke</a>, and one of the three major denominations of the Gethric faith alongside High Gethricism and Trinicism. It is headed by the Patriarch of Kherova, currently Viktrik I, whose seat is in the city of Kher. The Patriarch of Azuvke leads a separate but closely aligned Kethric see. Unlike the Church of Iztaw, whose authority extends across much of the known world, and Trinicism, which has spread through Meadland, Urland and Cevolia, Kethricism is almost entirely regional as its geography is defined by the Kheri peoples, their dwarven neighbours, and the communities that have grown from centuries of their shared life. What it lacks in geographic reach it possesses in theological depth and cultural rootedness: Kethricism is not a faith imposed on the Kheri but one that grew from within them, shaped by their history, their traumas, and their particular understanding of what the divine asks of a person.</p>

<h2>Origins, The Kethric Character Before the Schism</h2>

<p>The Kheri peoples converted to Gethricism in 284PA under Khat Tiblin II, less than half a century after the faiths founding. The Kheri arrived at the faith carrying two centuries of accumulated horror at what arcane power could do. The shadow of <a href="Merzigal" class="internal-link">Merzigal of Alahmid</a>, the northern Witch-King whose life extending sacrifices and daemon consultation had tormented the lake basin communities for generations, was still living memory when the first Gethric missionaries arrived. The story of the <a href="The Incursion" class="internal-link">Wermian Incursion</a> was not, to the Kheri, a distant theological parable. It was the answer to a question they had already asked and answered in their own history: what does unchecked arcane power produce? The demon slaying Gethric latched onto this inheritance immediately and naturally. What in Iztaw was understood as the Prophets greatest spiritual achievement was understood in Kher as the most dramatic possible confirmation of what the Kheri already knew.</p>

<p>Alongside this, the centuries of human and dwarven symbiosis around Lake Kher had produced a cultural emphasis with no strong equivalent elsewhere in the Gethric world. Craftsmanship, the making of enduring things with skill and conviction, was understood as a spiritual act in itself, a way of answering the deepest Kethric question: "what do you leave behind as testament to your life?" This question, which shapes Kethric devotion as profoundly as any formal doctrine, emerged from the Kheri habit of measuring a life not by its public declarations but by its lasting works. The private character of Kethric oaths reflects the same orientation, where a Vihri Gethric might swear publicly and wear their conviction as a social identity, a Kethrics oath is internal, specific and concrete, a commitment to a defined goal or conviction kept within the self and rarely shared. To reveal ones innermost vow is considered at best unusual and at worst a kind of spiritual immodesty: the vow is between the person and the Prophet, not between the person and their community.</p>

<h2>Tronos Canonisation and the First Council of Opuzna</h2>

<p>The Conclave of 410PA, at which the Church of Iztaw canonised both <a href="Melmer" class="internal-link">Melmer of Uhl</a> and <a href="Tronos" class="internal-link">Tronos of the Veil</a>, was received very differently in Kher than anywhere else in the Gethric world. Melmers canonisation was uncontroversial. Tronos, an Urien arcanist whose research into conjuration and necromancy had contributed directly to the body of knowledge the Wermian Crimson Circle had drawn on was however a problematic case. In Kherova he represented precisely the category of spiritual corruption that the Kheri understood the Incursion to have demonstrated. That Iztaw had canonised him without Kherovan consent or consultation, over the explicit objections of the Kherovan clergy, was an institutional provocation as much as a theological one. The drift between Kher and Iztaw that had been building since the late 3rd century became, in the years after 410PA, a formal intellectual project. Kherovan scholars and bishops began systematically articulating their previously cultural preferences, a theology of the divide between divine and arcane power that could stand as a coherent doctrinal position rather than a regional prejudice. Under formal leadership of <a href="Adzul I" class="internal-link">Archbishop Adzul I</a> of Kherova, the First Council of Opuzna convened in 458PA to debate and codify this position. The result was the Doctrine of Dualism: a formal declaration that divine power and arcane power were not academic classifications on a spectrum but categorically different in origin and spiritual consequence. Divine power, the ability to exert one's will and conviction outward upon the world, had its source in the strength of the person's own faith and spiritual integrity. Arcane power was something else entirely: a self-indulgent corruption of the soul, a harnessing of forces external to the self that ultimately manipulated and corroded its user. The Incursion was not a cautionary tale about scale. It was a demonstration of what the arcane was.</p>

<p>The implications of the Doctrine were immediately radical. Tronos was not merely controversial but, under this framing, a man whose entire spiritual legacy was the corruption the Doctrine described. His canonisation was heretical. More troubling still, the Doctrine cast doubt on the image of <a href="Razmyr" class="internal-link">Razmyr</a> himself, a founding Saint who had compiled the White Book and was understood, in the classical Gethric tradition, to have possessed and exercised extraordinary spiritual gifts that did not map cleanly onto the dualistic framework the Doctrine demanded. To question Razmyr was to question the foundations of the shared faith. The Council of Opuzna understood this and proceeded anyway.</p>

<h2>The Second Council of Volazc and the Kethric Declaration</h2>

<p>Archbishop Adzul I presented the findings of the Council of Opuzna to High-Bisop Taromir I of Iztaw. Taromi's response was unambiguous: the Doctrine of Dualism was irreconcilable with classical Gethric liturgy, with the canonical tradition established by the founding Saints, and with the theological authority of the Church of Iztaw to define the boundaries of orthodox belief. He rejected it entirely. This reaction was not a surprise to Adzul I, who had spent the years since Opuzna preparing for it. The Second Council of Volazc convened in 471PA in the smaller town south of Opuzna on the Kherovan border where the assembled bishops chose to meet, deliberately outside the largest cities and away from any symbolic pressure, and voted on the question that had been building for sixty years. The vote was not close.</p> 
<p>The Council issued the so called Kethric Declaration: the formal declaration of independence from the Church of Iztaw, the establishment of the Kherovan Patriarchate as a fully autonomous institution, and the elevation of Adzul I as its first Patriarch of Kherova. The denomination they now led was called Kethricism, the faith of Kethric, the name the Kheri had always used for the Prophet,  and it acknowledged the shared Gethric foundation while asserting the absolute authority of the Kherovan Patriarchate over its own theological development. The Church of Iztaw, recognizing that it had neither the geographic reach nor the military capacity to reverse a decision made hundreds of miles to the south east by a people who had never been fully under its control, accepted the split with relative peace. High-Bisop Taromir I never withdrew his theological objection, but he did not pursue it beyond words. The Kherovan Patriarchate was granted a status the Church of Iztaw described as "similar honorific standing" to an Archbishopric, acknowledged but not recognized, respected but not integrated.</p>

<h2>The Doctrine of Dualism and Kethric Practice</h2>

<p>The Doctrine of Dualism remains the theological core of Kethricism in 927PA as clearly as it was in 471PA. Its practical consequences are visible in every dimension of Kethric life. The <a href="Kherovan Inquisition" class="internal-link">Kherovan Inquisition</a>, founded in 585PA, is the Doctrines institutional enforcement arm, its Tribunal of the Clear Soul tasked specifically with the identification and suppression of arcane practitioners within Kherova, its Spellbreaker Guard providing a military anti-magic capability that has proven decisive in every major conflict the Khatrym has fought. The rejection of Saints <a href="Tronos" class="internal-link">Tronos</a> and <a href="Markur" class="internal-link">Markur</a>, the latter canonised in 691PA for his free use of arcane magic in service of the Gethric mission, reflects the Doctrine applied consistently across centuries. The <a href="Wermian Accords" class="internal-link">Wermian Accords</a> of 536PA, signed in response to the devastation wrought by unchecked magical power, are understood in Kherova as an outside acknowledgment that the Doctrine identified a real danger but as inadequate: a minimum standard that does not approach the theological seriousness the problem deserves.</p>

<p>The apparent paradox of a faith defined by its anti-magic doctrine producing a sanctioned form of magical practice is resolved by Kethricisms most distinctive theological contribution: the dwarven tradition of Runebinding. Championed by <a href="Vazena" class="internal-link">Vazena of Kairos</a>, the Kheri dwarf Saint whose Codex Vazenis unified natural, spiritual and dwarven philosophy rooted in ancient rites that predate the Gethric conversion. Runebinding is understood within Kethric doctrine as wholly divine rather than arcane. Where arcane power draws on external forces that corrupt and manipulate the practitioner, Runebinding is the practitioner's own conviction and skill made manifest in physical form, the will and faith of the craftsman inscribed into the work itself.It is, under the Doctrines own terms, not magic at all but a form of prayer expressed through making. Vazenas theological work in the Codex Vazenis provides the doctrinal justification for this distinction, and her status as Patron Saint of Kherova gives Runebinding an institutional protection no Inquisitor has ever seriously challenged. The tradition is practiced primarily by dwarven craftspeople of Daban-Kol and the High Kheri communities, and the works it produces, weapons, tools, architectural elements, are among the most prized objects in Kherovan life.</p>

<h2>Saints, Vows and the Question of Legacy</h2>

<p>Kethric veneration of the Saints follows the shared Gethric pattern in its broad outlines and diverges in its particulars. The Patron Saint of Kherova is <a href="Vazena" class="internal-link">Vazena of Kairos</a>, the first non human Saint, a Mistspire Dwarf from Kherovas own oldest city, whose canonisation in 495PA was resisted by the Iztaw clergy and whose theological legacy is most fully realized within the tradition that separated from them. In popular practice, however, <a href="Ralond" class="internal-link">Ralond the Scaleslayer</a> is arguably the more beloved figure, a Kheri nobleman whose pilgrimage into the Nirydian wastes was already mythologized in pre Kethric Kheri folklore long before the Gethric mission arrived. His silver Rhoka Vulgslasher, speaks to the Kethric emphasis on hidden truth, the conviction that what is real is what endures, regardless of what is declared. The Saints Tronos and Markur are not acknowledged. <a href="Luca" class="internal-link">Luca Cadente</a>, whose defeat of the Rakshasa of Parsa through language and oath rather than force aligns well with Kethric values, is accepted without controversy.</p>

<p>Where High Gethric oaths are often public, ceremonial and broadly framed, expressions of identity and allegiance worn visibly, Kethric oaths are private, specific and goal oriented. A Kethric swears to accomplish a defined thing or to maintain a defined conviction regarding a specific matter, and does so within themselves, rarely if ever sharing the content with others. The oath is not a social performance but an internal commitment whose fulfilment is measured between the person and the Prophet alone. This practice, combined with the emphasis on legacy and lasting work, produces a devotional culture that is quiet, durable and difficult to observe from outside which is in part, why outsiders have so consistently underestimated its depth.</p>

<h2>Modern Kethricism</h2>

<p>Kethricism in 927PA is internally stable and externally embattled. The Kherovan Patriarchate under Viktrik I, himself a dwarf and therefore a living embodiment of the integration his tradition champions, maintains full theological authority over Kherovan religious life and a complex relationship with the secular power of the Khat and the institutional power of the Kherovan Inquisition. The Patriarch of Azuvke leads a sister see that separated from the Kherovan Patriarchate in the centuries following the original schism, further fragmenting the original Archbishopric of Kherova into two fully independent Kethric institutions that remain theologically aligned without being institutionally unified. The Niedrest Incident of 923PA, in which the entire Kherovan Inquisition delegation to southern Urland was assassinated, has created the most significant diplomatic crisis in recent Kethric institutional history, as the Treaty of Kulums authority the Inquisition relied on for international operations is being withdrawn by western nations one by one. Viktrik I and the Patriarchate navigate this crisis from an institution that has survived far worse, and whose deepest conviction, that the things which endure are the things that matter, is not easily shaken by political reversal.</p>
