<p>Trinicism is the newest and least centralised of the three major denominations of the <a href="Gethricism" class="internal-link">Gethric</a> faith. It was founded at the Conclave at Trinfurt in 764PA, which brought about the <a href="The Great Schism" class="internal-link">Great Schism</a>. The denomination was largely a response of the western bishops of Cevolia and Meadland to the extreme authority and control of the <a href="Church of Iztaw" class="internal-link">Iztaw</a> clergy and to the beginning of its anti-elven <a href="Gethric Pogroms" class="internal-link">Pogroms</a>. It served as a valve through which the emerging western realms took control away from what they perceived as a Vihri state church, and allowed their own people to seek the afterlife in their own way.</p>
<p>With only regional authorities and a far more progressive outlook, Trinicism may have produced fewer miracle workers and divine leaders, but it is often heralded as a faith for truly everyone, one that celebrates merit and equality over right of birth. Trinicist vows often express an attempt to change or reflect on oneself or one's surroundings, one's ambitions and dreams.</p>
<h2>History</h2>
<p>The cause of Trinfurt was actively supported by many worldly rulers who saw the clergy as a threat to their own hegemony, among them Queen <a href="Gisela II" class="internal-link">Gisela the Good</a> of Meadrath. When the Order of the Silver Wheel marched to dissolve the Conclave by force, it was met by local heroes and by the armed forces of the Meadi queen and failed. The western churches broke with Iztaw, and Gisela II joined the Trinic cause and purged the Meadi clergy of those who stood with Iztaw.</p>
<h2>Doctrine</h2>
<p>Trinicists traditionally hold the Scriptures of the White Book to be the only divinely inspired text, the only presently available source of divinely revealed knowledge, and the only infallible source of Gethric doctrine. To most modern Trinicists the idea of a High-Bisop representing the Prophet on this plane is nonsensical. To emulate the Saints and follow the teachings of the Prophet, the first step is to acknowledge their humanity and the strength and conviction they drew from it. In Trinicist doctrine <a href="Gethric" class="internal-link">Gethric</a> was a man enlightened through circumstance and character, not a divine hero ordained at birth and realised when the moment came.</p>
<h2>Organisation</h2>
<p>The highest office of the Trinicist Church is that of the Bishop. There is no clear hierarchy between individual Bishops and no greater regional authority. The old Archbishoprics of Cevolia, Meadland, Weadland and others are still held by the Bishops of Jiedeux, Bellhome and Schipwig respectively, but these hold no effective dominion over the other Bishops within their titular sees.</p>
<p>The difference between a Vihri or Urien ecclesiastical province and a Trinicist Bishopric shows clearly in the limits the worldly rulers set on the new church. Trinicist Bishops raise no religious tithe and finance themselves through donations and worldly business. No Bishop may raise a clerical militia of his own, as the <a href="Candleguard" class="internal-link">Candleguard</a> of the old Church does, and clerical legal authority is generally restricted to cases on church grounds.</p>
<p>The place of women within the clergy has changed little from their restricted position in the old Church, and women are still not permitted to rise high within the individual clerical dominions. This may yet be challenged by missionary work in <a href="Othirdon" class="internal-link">Othirdon</a>, a region classically more egalitarian that has seen matriarchal structures in society many times across its history.</p>
<h2>Saints</h2>
<p>Trinicists accept most Saints, with the notable exception of <a href="Andre" class="internal-link">Andre of Trydlew</a>, who is seen as a symbol of the corruption and false convictions of Iztaw. <a href="Markur" class="internal-link">Saint Markur</a> and <a href="Yahrwick" class="internal-link">Saint Yahrwick</a> are likely the most popular Saints within the Trinicist community as a whole, in so far as such a statement can be made.</p>
<h2>Trinicism outside Cevolia, Meadland and Weadland</h2>
<p>Urien Trinicism is a matter of political and clerical dispute. While the majority of the Ur region practises High Gethricism, a significant portion of the population of <a href="Uhl" class="internal-link">Uhl</a>, <a href="Trinvale" class="internal-link">Trinvale</a> and <a href="Gelmsburg" class="internal-link">Gelmsburg</a> prefers Trinicist rites and actively rejects the authority of Gethric Bishops.</p>
<p>Conversion and missionary work in <a href="Othirdon" class="internal-link">Othirdon</a> has been spreading steadily, encouraged by many Meadi monarchs who have sought to "open" the western lands and see religious conversion as a first step towards integration and cultural and economic conquest. The largest Trinicist community in Othirdon lies in the eastern Lissvalley and the city of Varlys, where a Trinicist Bishop resides in his own church.</p>
<p>Attempts to convert the people of western Trykkerog, the citizens of ancient Vignasoya, have met with little response, as much of their modern cultural tradition and recent history is defined by the country's resistance to the Gethric conversions of <a href="Melmer" class="internal-link">Melmer</a>, which saw most of the Bykmål people exchange their old gods for the Gethric flame.</p>
<h2>Faith and its Contradictions in Cevolia</h2>
<p>Trinicism dominates the spiritual life of <a href="Cevolia" class="internal-link">Cevolia</a> more completely than perhaps any other region of Ilrakhan, in the quantity and quality of its artistic expression if not always in the sincerity of its practice. The cathedrals, frescoes, statuary and devotional poetry that the Cevoli have produced in the name of their faith represent the most sustained cultural investment in Trinicist aesthetics anywhere in the world. <a href="Luca" class="internal-link">Luca Cadente</a>, poet, diviner and Saint, embodies the specifically Cevoli understanding that faith and art are not separate endeavours but the same endeavour approached from different angles. His shrine, the Duomo delle Lune in Molina, is the tallest tower in Ilrakhan and the most visited pilgrimage site in Cevolia.</p>
<p>Beneath this surface runs a persistent counter-tradition whose roots predate Gethricism and whose character is distinctly Cevoli. The old <a href="Oresti Pantheon" class="internal-link">Oresti Pantheon</a> never entirely died. The <a href="Cult of Tirea" class="internal-link">Cult of Tirea</a> survived Rakhian occupation and Gethric conversion alike, its ecstatic rites and Thaliae priesthood continuing in various forms across the Rialo and Niardi highlands. In the cities, the <a href="Nightleaf" class="internal-link">Nightleaf</a> assassin ring worships Gizana, the Oresti goddess of sleep. In the private salons and scholarly circles of Albesan and Molina, wealthy patrons and educated humanists have rediscovered ancient Oresti rites and pre-Gethric mysteries for themselves. They treat the old religion as a philosophical inheritance that the official faith suppressed rather than replaced, and gather in semi-secret societies whose practices mirror the ancient mystery traditions with the self-conscious enthusiasm of people who believe they are recovering something lost. Trinicist authorities sometimes tolerate these circles as a harmless eccentricity of the rich, and sometimes outlaw them when they become too visible or too influential.</p>
<p>The <a href="Fellows of the Dancing Flame" class="internal-link">Fellows of the Dancing Flame</a> present a religious paradox of their own. Founded as a hardline military order to enforce Gethric conversion by force, they have over the centuries moved their institutional allegiance from High Gethricism to Trinicism without deepening either commitment, and have become more interested in political power than in doctrinal purity. Their current alliance with <a href="Stefano Maldini" class="internal-link">Bishop Maldini</a> and the <a href="Luminists" class="internal-link">Luminists</a> is less a theological position than a political calculation, which is arguably the most Cevoli thing about them.</p>
