<p>The Ikval, the "great split", is the founding event of <a href="Qalmyr" class="internal-link">Qalmyr</a>, the moment the single universal being the Qari know as Qastor cast away all that was destructive within itself and in doing so became two. It is told as the story of the beginning of the world and as an explanation of the struggle every Qari believes is fought within their own soul.</p>  
<h2>The Story</h2>  
<p>Before the Ikval there was only Qastor, the sum of all good and evil, physical and spiritual, arcane and divine, who made the world and the <a href="Qinari" class="internal-link">Qinari</a> that shine above it. In the Ikval he cast away his destructive tendency, his evil thoughts and his lesser desires. What he cast away took shape as <a href="Zolra" class="internal-link">Zolra</a>, the Great Serpent, and the two came to hang in the sky as the moons of the world. Qastor was freed of all imperfection and evil, but stripped of his nigh-omnipotent power of creation. The Qinari who stood nearest the casting were struck down into the dark and became the great beasts of the depths, the first victims of the split.</p>  
<h2>Readings</h2>  
<p>The three paths of the faith read the Ikval differently. To the First Path it was an act of grief and love: Qastor made himself good by giving up his power, and the Qari honour that sacrifice by casting out what is base within themselves, not by making war on Zolra. To the <a href="Path of the Second Moon" class="internal-link">Second Path</a> it was an amputation that left the maker unable to act, the power to make and unmake having gone with what was cast away, so that the world has answered to Zolra ever since. To the <a href="Path of the Third Moon" class="internal-link">Third Path</a> the Ikval is neither a sacrifice nor a defeat but a wound that neither moon can close, for Qastor can no longer create and Zolra would only consume, and what is left to mend it is the work of mortals.</p>
