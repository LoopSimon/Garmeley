<p>Qalmyr, also known as Orcish Moon Worship, is the dualistic faith of the Qari, the people most of the world calls <a href="Orc" class="internal-link">Orcs</a> after the ancient elvish <i>Orq</i>, "of the seas". It is centred on the two moons of the sky, Qastor, <a href="Luna Major" class="internal-link">Luna Major</a>, and Zolra, <a href="Luna Minor" class="internal-link">Luna Minor</a>, which circles him, and it is practised in one form or another by almost every Qari alive, from the harbours of the <a href="Qahori" class="internal-link">Qahori Federation</a> to the quiet towns of <a href="Il Haena" class="internal-link">Il-Haena</a> and the far shores of Durennis. Over the centuries the faith has divided into three paths, the First Path of Qastor, the <a href="Path of the Second Moon" class="internal-link">Path of the Second Moon</a> and the <a href="Path of the Third Moon" class="internal-link">Path of the Third Moon</a>, but all three tell the same story of how the world was split and of what the Qari owe to it.</p> <h2>Cosmology</h2> <p>To the Qari, Qastor was once the single universal and transcendental being, the sum of all good and evil in the world, physical and spiritual, arcane and divine. In the <a href="Ikval" class="internal-link">Ikval</a>, the great split, he cast away from himself every destructive tendency, evil thought and lesser desire, and what he cast away became Zolra. Qastor remained freed of all imperfection and evil, but stripped of his nigh-omnipotent power of creation.</p> <p>Qastor, the Pale Father, is exalted as the all-knowing and benevolent god of Prosperity and the supreme being of the universe. He is associated with the summer, clear skies and warmth, and his larger appearance in the skies is seen not only as a sign of his greatness but of his true love for his creation.</p> <p>Zolra, the Ruinous Mother, also known as the Great Serpent, is the antithesis of Qastor, less a sister than a dark mirror to the creator god. She tempts mortals down a path of ignorance, greed, base desires and egoism, and is associated with the winter, the storm and the cold. To those who follow Qastor, her small light, bound to his side and never able to leave it, is a sign of her lesser power and her inability to pass his protection.</p> <p>Beneath the two moons the Qari recognize the <a href="Qinari" class="internal-link">Qinari</a>, divine beings who share some of Qastor's traits and appear as stars and constellations. The North Star <a href="Malham" class="internal-link">Malham</a>, by which every sailor steers, is known as "the child of destiny" and is associated with hope and innocence, while the Sun, Teriq, is the giver of life. The great beasts of the depths are often seen as the fallen Qinari, the first victims of the Ikval and the greatest insult to the greatness of Qastor. Each year, on the nights when stars fall, the Qari say that the Fallen are seen again (see <a href="Qinari" class="internal-link">Qinari</a> and <a href="The Tis" class="internal-link">The Tis</a>).</p> <p>Among the best known are <a href="Malham" class="internal-link">Qisvahl</a>, the Child of Destiny, <a href="Sarhen" class="internal-link">Hasteriq</a>, the One Before the Sun, <a href="Tholmir" class="internal-link">Mahzir</a>, the Warden, <a href="Karrod" class="internal-link">Zolqir</a>, the Listener, and <a href="Dyrrim" class="internal-link">Kasqari</a>, the Sworn Two.</p> <h2>Teachings</h2> <p>In the Teachings of Qalmyr, good fortune comes to those who do righteous deeds for their own sake and not for material reward, while those who commit evil align themselves with the foul ways of Zolra and her path of darkness. The struggle between Qastor and Zolra thus does not take place in any physical manner, but in the soul and life of every Qalmyri. Pacifism has always been the central principle of the First Path, for to unite and take up arms is to listen to Zolra's whispers and betray Qastor.</p> <h2>The Holy Land</h2> <p>The island of Qa, now Il-Haena, is the holy birthland of the Qari, and the Maqari Mountains in its centre are the place closest to Qastor himself. Two cities were held holy above all, Ensihr, nearest the sacred summit, and Benwaral in the southeast. Ensihr fell in the war and was never rebuilt, but its ruins are visited by pilgrims from across the sea. Benwaral survived intact in the hands of the Qarzi who guard it, and is for that reason a place both sacred and defiled.</p> 
<p>The Destruction of Qa, the elven conquest of 413 to 457PA described in the <a href="Wars of Qahori Subjugation" class="internal-link">Wars of Qahori Subjugation</a>, is remembered as both a material loss and a great sin. The Qari had been seafarers since long before the elves came to their shores, but after the Destruction their wandering was read within the faith as mourning, the restlessness of a people forever yearning for something that could never be theirs again. In their own minds they had betrayed the central principle of pacifism and been punished by the universe, for the faith had counselled against organized resistance, and to unite and take up arms against the elves was to listen to Zolra's whispers. The Destruction thus drove a wedge between the tribes in a religious as well as a political sense.</p> 
<h2>The Three Paths</h2> <p>Though Qalmyr is almost universal among the Qari, it is no longer a single practice and has no single church. The great majority follow the First Path, the path of Qastor, which holds to the teachings above. It has no hierarchy beyond its elders and the shrine-keepers of Ensihr and Benwaral, and it is practised in regional traditions that have drifted apart since the Destruction.</p> <p>The Enduring are the Qari of the Qahori Federation, for whom faith is important but not all-consuming, a people content with their lives on their islands who carry their mourning as habit rather than as cause.</p> <p>The Keepers are the Qarzi, who see their decision in the Wars as the only way the old ways in the holy land could have continued uninterrupted. They guard Benwaral and have not been forgiven by their cousins.</p> <p>The Quiet are the Qari who stayed on Il-Haena, roughly seven in ten of the island's people. They live pleasant lives under Sultanate rule and have no appetite for religious revolt. The insurgent groups that exist, largely among the young, are unsuccessful and quarrel among themselves, and their own people do not support them, for violence against a non-violent opponent is against all that Qalmyr stands for.</p> <p>The Returners are the Qari of Kamerzin, who settled in Durennis, the Land of Dawn, and swore to return one day in conquest. Over the generations their quest has become more and more of a farce, and they hate the Second Path as bitterly as any.</p> <p>The Path of the Second Moon is the faith of the Zolraii, who worship Zolra and believe she won the struggle when the elves took Qa. It arose in 788PA, is held to be absolute heresy by all other Qari, and has only one state, the kingdom of <a href="Abaleq" class="internal-link">Abaleq</a>.</p> <p>The Path of the Third Moon is a smaller sect that arose as a distinct denomination in the 870s. Close to the original faith and not maligned, it teaches that Qa is a paradise one must make for oneself. Its followers live among the First Path in the same towns, and many of the Quiet are both.</p> 

<h2>The Faith and the World</h2> <p>The Elbae Sultanate, which holds Il-Haena and trades with anyone, has never cared for orcish philosophy, and the faith is practised openly in every land the Sultanate governs. Qalmyr communities are found from the Federation and Il-Haena to Durennis, where the Qari of Kamerzin live beside Bihra and Sunborn, as in the city of Raximan, and in the northern ports of Sallesand and Albesan.</p>
