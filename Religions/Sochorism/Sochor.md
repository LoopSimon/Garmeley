<div class="wiki-infobox" style="overflow: hidden; display: flex; flex-direction: column;"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LoopSimon/Garmeley/refs/heads/main/Media/Sochor.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover; display: block; flex-grow: 1;"> <small><i>Sochor the Ashwalker, God of Tal</i></small> </div> <p>Sochor is the Ashen God of Judgement and Chance, mythical Son of <a href="Chenism" class="internal-link">Chenza</a>, and brother to Doi-tor and Mezhan. Sochor worship is mostly spread by the followers of his main temple, the <a href="Great Kiln" class="internal-link">Great Kiln</a> in the eastern Jungles of <a href="Kaito" class="internal-link">Kaito</a>, who travel throughout the world either as Acolytes on personal Pilgrimages or later on as wandering Judges. Sochorism is the primary religion in Kaito and the national faith of <a href="Byamon" class="internal-link">Byamon</a>, <a href="Rikarma" class="internal-link">Rikarma</a>, <a href="Chezin" class="internal-link">Chezin</a>, <a href="Soren Vin" class="internal-link">Soren Vin</a> and <a href="Hanshi" class="internal-link">Hanshi</a>, as well as the <a href="Naigan" class="internal-link">Great Empire of Naigan</a> before them.</p> <p>As a Deity of Law Sochor embodies this specifically in the aspect of absolute impartial Fate, his holy symbol and Sign of Worship is the Coin, representing unbiased Judgement. Usually depicted as a tall male figure of pale Ashen skin with dark hair and in robes holding a coin in his hand, when not portrayed as an individual on a painting or statue, he can commonly be seen as the central middle sibling between his older Brother Doi-tor and his younger Brother Mezhan.</p>  <p>In the mythos Sochor is the middle of the three Sons of Chenza. Chenza is the Sunrider of the old <a href="Yppori" class="internal-link">Yppori</a> faith (see <a href="Chenism" class="internal-link">Chenism</a>), and the myth of Sochor has taken him into itself: in the tradition of the Ashcult he was always the herald who foretold the coming of Sochor, and he carried the Sun across the sky only to show his son the way in life. Doi-tor, the eldest, is the destructive Inferno, and Mezhan, the youngest, is the Rebirth and Rejuvenation that follows it.</p> <p>Sochors role within the 3 Sons of Chenza in the Mythos is also that of the Ashwalker, following his brother Doi-tors destructive Inferno and preceding and clearing the ground for his brother Mezhans Rebirth and Rejuvenation. As such Ash is culturally intertwined with Sochorism, especially in Byamon itself, his clerics often rub Ash on their skin and those gifted with divine powers are said to be able to summon ashen Wonders and Miracles.</p> <h2>The Prophet and the Ngalas</h2> <p>Sochorists hold that Sochor lived once as a man, the Prophet, who was born in 253PA. In 275PA the myth of his life ends: he ascended and became a god, and his followers began the Holy Work, which was later known as the Golden Rule.</p> <p>The Prophet is counted as the first Ngalas, "born of Fire" in ancient Yppori, and the faith teaches that he is reborn again and again. In 281PA the <a href="Ashcult" class="internal-link">Ashcult</a> recognised the three year old Chusfun as his reincarnation, declared him Ngalas and raised him to the office of Osafar, the high priest who sits in the Great Kiln, and with him the Ashcult became an institution. Two Ngalas followed Chusfun, Juzoko and Nugen, and the fourth of the cycle is the present Osafar, <a href="Hien-Ro" class="internal-link">Hien-Ro</a>. A Ngalas is marked by the fire within: he seems unable to suffer injury, glows from within and radiates a heat as if he were burning alive. The two offices are not the same, for not every Osafar has been a Ngalas, but every Ngalas has been made Osafar.</p>  <p>The Coin is the Sign of Worship of Sochor and also the instrument of his priests. The ceremonial coin of the Ashcult is a disc of metal with one face blackened and the other left bronze, the two answers that Fate may give, and it is through the Coin that the priests are said to channel the powers of the god. When a priest calls on Sochor, a thin line of golden light is said to cut across the Coin as the god answers.</p> <h2>Doctrine and Practice</h2> <p>The faithful hold that Fate alone judges truly. In a dispute that neither law nor witnesses can settle, a Judge of the faith casts the Coin, and an oath sworn on a coin is held to be the most binding there is. A follower who wishes to serve goes first as an Acolyte on a personal pilgrimage, and those who complete it may be ordained as Judges, who wander the world to hear disputes and give judgement in the name of Sochor.</p> <p>The dead are burned and their ashes given to the wind or the river in the name of the Ashwalker, that they may be carried towards rebirth, and on days of judgement and at funerals the faithful mark their brows with ash.</p>  <p>The Great Kiln is the centre and oldest temple of Sochorism and home to many theological debates on his teachings. In the 9th century, two main Schools of Thought have developed within the Politics and Discourse of the Temple, the Dogmatists and the Radicals, these differ mainly in their interpretation of the Role Chance has when administering justice and finding judgement. Dogmatists believe that the letter of the law is to be followed however archaic it may be, and a judge must uphold local legal codes sternly only grasping for the Divine Element of Chance and Fate itself when absolutely necessary. Radicals on the other hand are absolute adherents of the principle that Fate and only Fate can give true and righteous justice and often travelling Judges of this School of thought ignore local customs, laws and codes moreso than their Dogmatist brothers.</p> <h2>The Faith in the World</h2> <p>The worldly influence of the faith ended with the <a href="Golden Empire" class="internal-link">Golden Empire</a>. Since then the Osafar has been of little account in the politics of <a href="Tal" class="internal-link">Tal</a>, and the Ashcult has spread instead into many temples across Tal and the Purple Sea, though everyone still pays ceremonial homage to the Great Kiln and the Osafar. The present Osafar is the first in a long time to seek a part in politics, and he has called on the faithful to support any undertaking that would douse the New World in the ashes of Sochor (see Ashcult).</p>
