<p>The Pillars of Myan, named after the <a href="Myan-Bond" class="internal-link">Myan Bond</a> between the will of the world and the <a href="Elemental" class="internal-link">Elementals</a> of Thandar's Circle, are a group of Elementalists originating from the students of <a href="Szuan" class="internal-link">Szuan</a> himself. Elementalists, sometimes conflated entirely with the broader term "Channelers", are a group of mages that harness the power of the Elements and enter bonds with Myan, who seek to learn and become more sentient by accompanying these Scholars. The Four Pillars of the inner Circle, Fire, Wind, Earth & Water, were held in high regard and their study was more popular than that of the six transitory Elements.</p> <p>Among these Elementalists, there are 10 schools known as the Pillars, that specialize in one of the 10 Elemental states of the old Circle of <a href="Thandar" class="internal-link">Thandar</a>. The Pillars were once a royal institution within the later Empire of <a href="Naigan" class="internal-link">Naigan</a>, but many of them fell into dishonour or full disrepair during the <a href="Period of the Bleeding Heron" class="internal-link">Period of the Bleeding Heron</a>. Essentially all magical research and practice of the Yppori of Tal was centralized within these Circles, as the ultimate expression of what Thandar had discovered and Szuan perfected.</p> <h4>The Ten Pillars</h4> <p>The inner Pillars of Earth, Water, Wind and Fire, and the transitory Pillars of Magma, Storm, Lightning, Frost, Ash and Seafire are mapped onto the Circle of Thandar.</p> <p>In the current day, the Pillar of Fire and its three adjacent Pillars of Lightning, Ash and Seafire survived the Era of the Heron and adopted religious iconography, gaining the protection of the Ashcult of <a href="Sochor" class="internal-link">Sochor</a>, to whose faithful ash has always been earth born of fire. The Pillars of Earth, Magma and Frost survived within Tal as well. The Great <a href="Hanshi" class="internal-link">Hanshi</a> outlawed the Pillars in the late 9th century under the often called "Divine Edict", and instead founded the "School of Chorol", which unified Elemental studies and Magical research under a royal institution on the islands of the Purple Sea; the Pillar of Wind was essentially made its core.</p> <p>The remaining Pillars were largely forced to flee the lands of Tal and live on only in small traditions abroad. The Pillar of Storm is rumoured to have fled to the shores of the Bihra of the Kareli, where its rites are still practised in secret. The Pillar of Water has almost vanished; some say its remaining Master Channelers fled north into the Sultanate of the Elves, and rumours persist of other remnants reaching the Safiri islands of the Moon's Gate to the west.</p>
