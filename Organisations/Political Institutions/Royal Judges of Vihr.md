<p>The Royal Judges of <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a>, commonly called the Eagles, are the judges of the crown. They hear the Crown Pleas, the cases of blood, treason, coin and the highway that the king has reserved to himself in every part of the realm, and since the reforms of Jakub II "the Good" they also hear the appeals of the common people against the sentences of their lords. They were founded by Ras Zervind I "the Eagle" in 268PA, and they take their name from him. In a realm whose justice is divided between the Var, the Gri, the bishops and the free cities, they are the one body of judges that answers to the throne alone, and the one institution that has always tried to make Vihr one realm under one law. They have never had the power to do it.</p>  
<h2>History</h2>  
<h3>The Eagle's Judges</h3>  
<p>The lords who submitted to Viago I kept the right to judge their own people by the Old Right, the spoken custom of the Vihrmen, and for a generation the crown judged nobody but its own household and its own domain. Zervind I, who came to the throne in 262PA, would not leave it at that. In 268PA he declared four kinds of case to be the Crown Pleas, which only the king could judge in any part of the realm: the shedding of blood, treason against the crown, the false striking of coin and robbery on the king's highway. He chose judges from among his own household and sent them riding circuit through the realm to hear them, with his seal of the eagle on their writs. The lords could not refuse them without refusing the king, and the judges became known as the king's eyes, and later as the Eagles. Zervind is said to have earned his own name from them, for it was said that nothing was done in his realm that the Eagle did not see.</p>  
<h3>Within Limits</h3>  
<p>The settlement of the Lords' Crisis in 384PA fixed the bounds that the Eagles have kept ever since. The Var and their Gri were confirmed in the justice of their own lands, the bishops' courts in the cases of the clergy and of faith, marriage, oaths and wills, and the crown kept the Crown Pleas and nothing more. Quarrels between the Var themselves belong to the <a href="Honoured Assembly" class="internal-link">Honoured Assembly</a>. The Eagles went on riding their circuits, but they could only judge what the settlement left them.</p>  
<p>When <a href="Radosław of Szievek" class="internal-link">Radosław of Szievek</a> completed the <a href="Speculum Regni" class="internal-link">Speculum Regni</a> in 741PA, its Book of the Crown became the handbook of the Eagles, and from then on they learned their law at Szievek. The free cities brought them a new duty. In <a href="Pretko" class="internal-link">Pretko</a>, where the crown keeps no governor, a royal judge is the only officer of the crown and hears the cases of blood and treason, while in <a href="Szulbod" class="internal-link">Szulbod</a> the high justice belongs to the Royal Starost instead.</p>  
<h3>Atej</h3>  
<p>The darkest hour of the Eagles came in the Grain Rising of 854. Ras Jerziv II sent the royal judge Ignac Wrona into Breza with agents of the <a href="Vaergriff" class="internal-link">Vaergriff</a>, and Wrona sat beside the High Inquisitor <a href="Teron Maz" class="internal-link">Teron Maz</a> and the Gri of Trydlew at the Judgements of Atej in 855PA. There was no standing court in the realm that could try a rising, and the improvised tribunal judged nine hundred Sarloni ringleaders and put them to death on a single day. The Sarloni have called Wrona the Crow of Atej ever since, and the Eagles have never quite washed off his name.</p>  
<h3>The Reforms of Jakub II</h3>  
<p>Ras Jakub II "the Good", the first of the Farmer Lords, had seen the Judgements as a young man, and in 889PA he gave the Eagles the permanent institutions they had never had. His Statutes, added to the Speculum Regni as its fifth book, founded the Royal Tribunal at <a href="Zavlech" class="internal-link">Zavlech</a>, a standing court under the First Judge that hears appeals in the Crown Pleas and in the disputes of the crown's charters. They seated a salaried royal judge in the capital of every Vadcy, and they granted the Peasants' Plea, by which any free man or woman of the land may appeal the sentence of their lord to the royal judge of their Vadcy. The Honoured Assembly let the Statutes pass because they left the courts of the Var untouched, and the Var have made sure ever since that the Plea is a narrow door.</p>  
<h2>Organisation</h2>  
<p>The Eagles are headed by the First Judge of the Royal Tribunal, who presides over the bench at Zavlech. Below the Tribunal sit the royal judges of the nine Vadcy, one in each capital, and the royal judges of the free cities, and the circuit judges still ride through the crownlands as they did in the days of Zervind I. Every royal judge carries the seal of the eagle on their writs and is trained in the Speculum Regni, most of them at Szievek University, and most have completed the Tregesia and been ordained Ministers of the Church before they take the seal, as the First Judge always has. No law bars a woman from the royal bench, but every royal judge seated so far has been a man, while women Ministers have long sat as judges in the bishops' courts. The Eagles are paid by the crown and can be removed only by the Ras.</p>  
<p>The Eagles hear the Crown Pleas of blood, treason, coin and the highway wherever they are committed, the Peasants' Plea against the sentences of the lords, and the disputes that arise from the charters of the crown. They have no say over the clergy, who answer to the bishops' courts, or over quarrels between the Var, which belong to the Honoured Assembly, and in every other matter the lords judge their own lands.</p>  
<h2>Trial and Sentence</h2>  
<p>The Eagles judge by witnesses, by sworn oaths and by the written law of the Speculum. Since the procession of the Kar-Zavr under <a href="Vormil I" class="internal-link">Vormil I</a> they may also call a sworn mage to read the truth of a witness or the trace of a crime, under the Book of the Crown. The bishops' courts refuse such testimony and trust only oaths and witnesses, and the Var despise it and call it "the mage's word". The Eagles will not grant the <a href="Candle Oath" class="internal-link">Candle Oath</a> in the Crown Pleas, and a lord accused before them cannot send a champion in their place.</p>
<p>The sentences of the Crown Pleas are hard. Blood and robbery on the highway are punished by hanging, false coin by burning, and treason is sent before the Ras. Lesser crown convicts are held in the many prisons and cells of the realm, such as the Flowerkeep near Trydowka, and only the most dangerous or most valuable prisoners pass to the Vaergriff and the <a href="Iron Hole" class="internal-link">Iron Hole</a>. In the courts of the Var the old blood price can still be paid, so that the same killing may cost a lord's son silver in his father's court and his neck before an Eagle, which is one more reason why so few such cases ever reach one.</p>  
<h2>The Eagles Today</h2>  
<p>The Eagles are respected in the towns and in the crownlands, and they are tolerated, at best, in the halls of the Var. A royal judge who rides into a Vadcy to hear a case of blood against the son of a great lord can find every door shut and every witness gone, and the Var have a hundred ways of keeping the Peasants' Plea from ever reaching him. It is because the Eagles so rarely reach the nobility that the <a href="Vaergriff" class="internal-link">Vaergriff</a> has become, unofficially, one of the only institutions of real accountability for the lords of Vihr. The reformers around the Kisras <a href="Waśko Ralpia" class="internal-link">Waśko</a> want to give the Eagles the reach that the Statutes of Jakub did not, and the Var know it. The present First Judge, Anzelm Morawiec, an aged Minister who has held the seat since 909PA, owes it to the friends of the Var at court, and he has moved slowly on every reform the Kisras has put before the Tribunal.</p>
