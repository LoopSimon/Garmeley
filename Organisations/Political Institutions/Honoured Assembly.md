<p>The Honoured Assembly is the council of the great lords and the Church of Iztaw that sits beside the throne of <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a>, and the body through which the Rascy has been governed alongside its kings since 384PA. It grew from the council of the Horn Lords of the old Federation, was forced into being by the Lords' Crisis of 377PA, and has been the central axis of Vihri constitutional politics ever since: the place where the Var, the Church and the Crown measure their strength against one another, and, for the most part, do so without drawing swords.</p>
<h2>Composition</h2>
<p>Each of the nine <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vadcy</a> sends its Var, or Varina, in person. The eight ancient lordships of Trazlova, Szievek, Rovgi, Opuzna, Breza, Selbia, Levazci and Tloky have sat since the beginning, and the Var of Torry has sat since the Vadcy was created in 870PA. A seat belongs to the Vadcy and passes with its title. The Gris and the lesser landholders below them have no seat of their own, and their voice in the Assembly is that of their Var.</p>
<p>The Church sits through the High-Bisop of Iztaw, currently Orthel II, who is both the Ras's spiritual leader and formally his subject. He may send the Archbishop of Vihr in his place, and the Church's other bishops attend when the Assembly turns to matters of faith or the succession. The Throne is represented by the Sekretar of Vihr, <a href="Tadeuz Pilsud" class="internal-link">Tadeuz Pilsud</a>, who holds the only seat in the Assembly occupied by a man not of noble blood. The Ras himself does not sit among the members, but he may address the Assembly in person.</p>
<p>The Assembly is presided over by the Foreman, whose office is the third most prestigious in Vihr, ranking directly behind the throne and the high seat at Iztaw. By long custom it has gone to the Var of Trazlova, and it is held today by Osran of House Lawchlaw.</p>
<h2>Powers</h2>
<p>The Assembly began as a consultative council, and it has never entirely lost the manner of one. The Ras calls on it for counsel and for consent, and the prerogatives that the lords won in 377PA, which the crown recognised in law for the first time, are its to defend. It has also inherited much of what the Horn Lords once did: it arbitrates disputes between the Var, and the great hosts of the realm are not raised without it. When a dynasty fails it chooses the next. In the Vihri War of Succession it acclaimed <a href="Zawis I" class="internal-link">Zawis</a> of Nediv over the Szinmo claimant Dobrov, and in 692PA the Assembly and the Gethric Bishops elected Miskomir</p>
<p>The settlement of 377PA also fixed the limits of the Assembly. The lords acknowledged the formal supremacy of the crown and the autonomy of the Church in matters of faith, and neither has been reopened without trouble since.</p>
<h2>Roots</h2>
<p>The Assembly claims descent from the periodic gatherings of the Horn Lords at Tlokyemy, where the chiefs of the <a href="Vihrmen Federation" class="internal-link">Vihrmen Federation</a> met to coordinate their campaigns and settle disputes between the tribes. No lord held authority over the others by right, and the most solemn pacts were sworn at the Ullkustone of Trydlew, before Ollku, god of river and sea. The Federation ended in 241PA, when Viago of the Zav proclaimed the Kingdom of Vihr, but the lords did not forget what they had been. The Unification Wars were fought by the lords of the south and of the Tlok River, who would not exchange the loose sovereignty of the Federation for a ruler they had no part in making. When they lost, at Tlok Valley in 246PA and at Glancz in 251PA, they kept their lands and understood their loyalty to be a bargain, with ancient prerogatives as its price. In the century and more that followed, neither the crown they had accepted nor the Church they had funded showed any interest in writing those prerogatives down.</p>
<h2>The Lords' Crisis</h2>
<p>The Church of Iztaw grew steadily richer and more sophisticated through the 4th century, and by its middle years its wealth and influence rivalled the crown's. In 375PA the throne passed to Zervind II, a man of forty-six who had spent his life as a secondary figure at his elder brother's court and arrived without the authority the crown demanded. High-Bisop Morzin I recognised in him a king who needed guiding more than he needed ruling. The Ras's correspondence began to pass through the clerical office, appointments were made on Morzin's counsel, and the presence of the Candleguard in the capital was accepted as a sign of royal piety rather than as an occupation. Within two years the kingdom was governed from Iztaw in all but name, and the Ras's movements and decisions were mediated through the High-Bisop's office.</p>
<p>The eight great lordships, whose armies were the strength that neither the crown nor the Church could replace, answered in the summer of 377PA by marching on Zavlech. The Candleguard stood down. Zervind was released. Morzin I understood that his position was impossible, and the lords, having what they came for, did not disband. The negotiations lasted several months and produced a settlement that neither the lords nor the Church would have drawn up from a clean slate. The eight lordships were recognised as hereditary Vadcy, with their titles and territorial prerogatives given legal standing for the first time. In return the lords acknowledged the crown's supremacy and the Church's autonomy in religious matters, and Morzin kept his office, chastened. The council the lords had demanded was established that same year as the League of Lords, and in 384PA it was formalised as the Honoured Assembly.</p>
<p>Zervind II reigned eleven more years in the narrowed space the crisis had left him, the last four of them under the formalised Assembly, which carried on the business of the realm with a confidence that rose as the king's own fell. He died in Zavlech in 388PA, reportedly without having once spoken publicly of 377PA. The name that later generations gave him, Kneelgood, was coined in satire after his death. It describes a king who knelt to the Church when he should have stood, and then to the lords when they left him no choice.</p>


<h2>Since 384PA</h2> <p>The balance the Assembly was founded on has been tested often. In 870PA, in the bitter years after the <a href="War of Horn and Fang" class="internal-link">War of Horn and Fang</a>, when many in Vihr held the clergy to have betrayed and sabotaged the country, the Council of 870 carved the Vadcy zi <a href="Torry" class="internal-link">Torry</a> out of the lands of Iztaw. It was seen as a clear move against the Church's encroaching power, it cost Iztaw extensive holdings, and it caused uproar among the elite of the realm. It also added a ninth Var to the Assembly's benches. When the canonisation of Andre of Trydlew was proclaimed at the Conclave of 876PA, the Assembly took care that Vihr should remember him as the last great knight of a terrible war and not as the man who lost Uhl.</p> <h2>The Assembly Today</h2> <p>On paper the balance still favours the Var and the Church, but under <a href="Vormil III" class="internal-link">Vormil III</a>, who is popular with the commonfolk and has the loyalty of the Sekretar, it has tilted back toward the throne. Pilsud has frequently clashed with the Var, and many of them fear him. The Ras's heir, <a href="Waśko Ralpia" class="internal-link">Waśko</a>, wantsto restrict the Clergy's role in governance, which would run straight through the Church's seat in the Assembly. Meanwhile Prince Jakub has committed the <a href="Knights of the Unicorn" class="internal-link">Knights of the Unicorn</a> to the <a href="Garmish Succession Crisis" class="internal-link">Garmish Civil War</a> against his father's expressed wishes. The Order is rooted in the same aristocracy that fills the Var's benches, and the war has put the Assembly's loyalties to the test.</p> <p>The Foreman, Osran, is of House Lawchlaw, a cadet branch of the royal House of Ralpia. That century-long kinship has not always held on the chessboard of Vihri politics, and what he does with the office in the coming years will matter a great deal to the balance of the realm.</p>
