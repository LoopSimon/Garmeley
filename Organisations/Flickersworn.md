<p>The Flickersworn are the champions of the <a href="Candle Oath" class="internal-link">Candle Oath</a> in <a href="Vihr" class="internal-link">Vihr</a>, the warriors who swear over the candle in another's name and fight for their cause. Any party to an oath may name a champion, but in Vihr the practice has become the business of the great houses, which keep excellent fighters on retainer for the duty, and a Var who goes to an oath without a Flickersworn of their own is rare. The best of them are among the most famous people of the realm, and the worst are hired killers with a priest's blessing.</p>  
<h2>Name</h2>  
<p>The name comes from the oath that every champion swears beside their lord's, that they fight in good faith for a cause they believe true, and from the candle that burns while they fight. To be flickersworn is to be sworn to the candle for another, and the name is older than the trade. A champion who has fought their first oath keeps the stub of its candle, and most Flickersworn wear it at their belt.</p>  
<h2>History</h2>  
<p>Champions have fought Candle Oaths in the Vihri lands for as long as there have been oaths, and in the old days they were most often a lord's own knights, kinsmen or sworn men. The most famous of the early champions was not Vihri at all but the Wermian knight <a href="Dazhek Winghelm" class="internal-link">Dazhek Winghelm</a>, who fought more than thirty oaths in the 5th century, and whose fame made the hiring of a famous champion a matter of prestige. Ras Boris I "the Pious" tried to bind the champions who fought before the crown to his Ideal of Virtue, so that only chaste men might take the candle. The court called them the "virgin candles", and the demand did not outlive him.</p>  
<p>The settlement of 384PA left the Var the justice of their own lands and the Honoured Assembly the quarrels between them, and the Candle Oath remained the one judgement that no Var could refuse once it had been granted. As the great houses came to settle their gravest quarrels on holy ground, the champion became a retainer like any other, and by the 8th century the richest houses kept them as they kept falconers and masters of horse. Foreign champions were prized from early on. Calina of Kisnau, a Pilvian, fought for the Gri of Ahmsk in the Long Candle of the 8th century, and the duellists of <a href="Grevenna" class="internal-link">Grevenna</a> have been sought after ever since for their reputation as skilled combatants.</p>  
<h2>The Retainer</h2>  
<p>A Flickersworn is paid twice, a yearly retainer for being in a house's service and a purse for every oath they fight, and the purse is the greater the more dangerous the cause. Many are low-born, foreign or former adventurers who have found that a lord pays better for a single morning on holy ground than a company pays for a season. Some are women, and a few of the most famous champions of the oath have been. Between oaths they serve as sworn swords, bodyguards and masters of arms, and in some houses they are the most trusted servants the lord has.</p>  
<p>The trade is unforgiving. A champion who dies has lost their lord's cause, and a champion who yields has lost it and their name with it, for no house will hire a Flickersworn who has yielded an oath. A champion who wins too often is feared, and a lord with a famous Flickersworn finds that many quarrels are settled before any candle is lit.</p>  
<h2>The Flickersworn Today</h2>  
<p>The most famous Flickersworn of the realm is <a href="Boleslaw the Bull" class="internal-link">Boleslaw the Bull</a>, the sworn sword of the Kisras <a href="Waśko Ralpia" class="internal-link">Waśko</a>, who is no soldier himself and has never needed to be. The Var of <a href="Rovgi" class="internal-link">Rovgi</a>, Zeslav zi Wogrov, has kept the Pilvian <a href="Intonina Duson" class="internal-link">Intonina Duson</a> for the last decade. The most feared is <a href="Carguez di Mindano" class="internal-link">Carguez di Mindano</a>, the fencer who fights for the Var of <a href="Opuzna" class="internal-link">Opuzna</a>.</p>  
<p>In 922PA Carguez fought for Var Ilia of Opuzna in the Candle Oath brought by House Sribo of <a href="Selbia" class="internal-link">Selbia</a>, which accused Ilia of the rape of Yeliza zi Sribo. Her brother <a href="Beladoz the Black" class="internal-link">Beladoz the Black</a>, a young noble taller and stronger than his opponent, took the candle for his sister and was cut apart by Carguez before the eyes of the court. Few in Vihr believe the verdict, the feud between Selbia and Opuzna has not cooled since, and the oath is the one that the reformers at court point to when they say that justice in Vihr goes to whoever can afford the better sword.</p>
