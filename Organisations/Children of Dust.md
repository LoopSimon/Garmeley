<p>The Children of Dust are a loose and largely clandestine network of scholars, occultists, historians, and adventurers united by a shared obsession: the recovery of knowledge from the great fallen civilisations of south-western Ilrakhan, mainly <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Rakh</a>, <a href="Palmunia" class="internal-link">Palmunis</a>, and <a href="Addacia" class="internal-link">Addacia</a>. They have no formal membership rolls, no recognised headquarters, and no institutional charter. Most people who encounter their work do not know they exist. The name itself is known only within their own circles and to those few outsiders who have had cause to look carefully at the margins of the Wasteland expeditions, the resettlement surveys of the eastern Addacian plateau, and the trade caravans of the Beheran interior.</p>

<p>What binds the Children of Dust is less ideology than appetite. Each member pursues their own questions be it a particular ruin, a particular text, a particular thread of Palmunian religious practice or Rakhian statecraft, and the network exists primarily to share access, resources, and information across those individual obsessions. Cooperation is pragmatic rather than principled. That said, a common conviction runs beneath the diversity of individual agendas: that the <a href="Excavators Guild of Butera" class="internal-link">Excavators Guild of Butera</a> and its institutional monopoly on Rakhian archaeology has produced a controlled, partial, and politically sanitised account of the ancient world, and that genuine discovery requires working outside it.</p>

<h2>Methods of Access</h2>

<p>The Children of Dust have developed several overlapping approaches to reaching sites the Guild controls, restricts, or has not yet identified. The most significant of these is their quiet embedding within the resettlement efforts that the nations of <a href="Grivil" class="internal-link">Grivil</a> and <a href="Hainach" class="internal-link">Hainach</a> have undertaken in the more habitable stretches of the former Addacian plateau and the eastern margins of Rakh. Colonial surveys, road-laying expeditions, and settlement parties move through landscapes thick with ancient remains; a scholar attached to such an effort as surveyor, cartographer, or physician attracts little scrutiny. What they find, they record. What they recover, they move quietly.</p>

<p>The second approach runs through the Beheran desert. The nomadic peoples of Behera, principally the <a href="Beheran" class="internal-link">Inori and the Mignoi</a>, have traversed the interior routes of the south-western world for generations, passing through lands that were Palmunian long before they became anything else. The Children of Dust have cultivated relationships with both peoples over decades, trading knowledge, medicine, and material goods for access to sites the nomads know but have no reason to excavate, and for passage through territories no outside scholar could navigate alone. The ruins of ancient Palmunis are not all coastal. Many of its most significant sites lie in the interior, beyond the reach of the Guild's maritime approach and surrounded by the hostile city-states of <a href="Bassara" class="internal-link">Bassara</a>.</p>


<p>The conventional western approach, along the Smoking Sea coast, is largely under Guild jurisdiction and the interior routes are Bassaran. The Children of Dust, working through Inori and Mignoi contacts, have made more progress on access to interior sites than any other outside group, though at considerable personal risk to those members who have undertaken such expeditions.</p>

<p>Among the more significant and most carefully guarded relationships in the network's recent history is its connection to the <a href="Pastelliora" class="internal-link">Pastelliora</a>, a mercantile association of financiers operating across Cevolia, Ur, and Sertova, whose interests in south-western Ilrakhan include both the Guild's coastal operations and the prospective resettlement of Rakhian territory. The precise nature of the arrangement between the Children of Dust and elements within the Pastelliora is not publicly known; rumour within scholarly circles suggests that certain Pastellioran merchants have provided funding, shipping access, and cover for Children of Dust expeditions in exchange for advance knowledge of significant discoveries and their commercial implications. If true, this would place the Pastelliora in the contradictory position of financing both the Excavators Guild and the network most actively working to circumvent it, from a purely mercantile perspective entirely coherent.</p>
