<p>The Candleguard is the militia of the Gethric Church. It is not an order of knights or an army in the field but a body of armed clergy, raised to keep cathedrals and great churches, and it is part of the clergy as surely as the priests it protects. Its badge is the Gethric candleflame, the same flame that stands on the banner of Iztaw. A detachment can be found at almost every major cathedral or church in the cities of the Gethric world, but only in Gethric countries and communities: it has no place in lands that do not follow the Prophet, and in Trinic lands a Bishop may not raise one.</p>
<h2>Origins</h2>
<p>The name comes from the custom of the candle. In the first years of the faith it was said that the flame of Gethric would find a candle in every family, in every home and castle, and the lighting of a candle for the Prophet became the commonest act of Gethric devotion. The guard that stood watch at the sacred places came to be called the Candleguard for it. The Church of Iztaw was founded one year after Gethric's death, and the Candleguard grew up in its first generations, as the Church gathered relics, treasure and pilgrims in the holy city and needed arms to keep them. Its seat is the Voldom at Iztaw, the cathedral named for the first High-Bisop.</p>
<h2>Organisation</h2>
<p>The Candleguard has no commander of its own. It is administered through the ranks of the clergy, and those ranks give it its shape. The High-Bisop of Iztaw is its supreme authority, and the Archbishops, Primati and Patriarchs hold it for their regions. Within a diocese the Bishop is the lawful lord of its guard. The Deacon, who is the right hand of the Bishop in the direct service and protection of the faith, commands the guard from day to day. The Vicar handles its pay, quarters and arms, and where a diocese has no Deacon, as in a Bishopric that works closely with an order of knights, the Vicar commands as well. A major parish keeps a small detachment under its Father or Minister. The rank and file are Disciples and Priests who have taken the Candleguard's oath, men who serve the Church with arms as other clerics serve it with books.</p>
<h2>The Settlement of 377PA</h2>
<p>The Candleguard once stood in the capital. Under Ras <a href="Zervind II" class="internal-link">Zervind II</a>, High-Bisop Morzin I made the guard a sign of royal piety, and its presence at Zavlech grew until the Candleguard held the king in all but name, and the kingdom was governed from Iztaw. The eight great lords marched on the capital in the summer of 377PA, and the Candleguard, formidable as it was, had no answer to the combined strength of the Vadcy and stood down. The settlement that followed gave the Church its autonomy in religious matters, and it fixed the Candleguard to church grounds: it may keep the cathedral, the cloister and the sanctuary, and it may not march. The Church has accepted the limit since, though it has never forgotten what the guard once was.</p>
<h2>Beyond the Church Grounds</h2>
<p>A body that cannot leave the churchyard is of little use outside it, and the Church found its answer in hiring. The Candleguard and the clergy that command it often employ adventurers, men and women of pure spirit, to do their bidding beyond church grounds: to escort relics, to carry sealed letters, to hunt the daemon-haunted and the heretical. Such hirelings wear no badge, and the Church does not claim them. In the years of the <a href="Gethric Pogroms" class="internal-link">Gethric Pogroms</a> it was accused of using them to do the work that its own guard was forbidden to do.</p>
<h2>The Candleguard Abroad</h2>
<p>In Zavlech a detachment keeps the cathedral of the Bishop. In Izhevk in Grachy the Candleguard, there called the Candleflame Guard, watches the Church of Remembrance and the Nightingale Library inside it, one of the great archives of Nostroya. Outside the holy city the duty is mostly quiet: standing at the doors, guarding relics, keeping order at great feasts, and walking the Silver Procession. Its soldiers swear to the Church and not to any lord, a fact the lords of Vihr have not forgotten.</p>
