<p>The Ashcult is the church of <a href="Sochor" class="internal-link">Sochor</a>: the priesthood that keeps the faith, judges in the name of the god and serves the Osafar in the <a href="Great Kiln" class="internal-link">Great Kiln</a>. It is named for the ash that is its holy substance, and its temples stand in every land where Sochor is worshipped, though nowhere as thickly as in <a href="Tal" class="internal-link">Tal</a> and the Purple Sea.</p>

<h2>Origins</h2> <p>The Ashcult grew out of the Holy Work that the followers of Sochor began when the god ascended in 275PA. In 281PA it recognised the three year old Chusfun as the reincarnation of Sochor, declared him Ngalas and raised him to the office of Osafar, and with him the Ashcult became an institution. In 283PA Laizin, King of the Ran, converted and was proclaimed Guardian of the Ngalas, and the alliance of throne and church founded the <a href="Golden Empire" class="internal-link">Golden Empire</a>. For nearly two and a half centuries, until the last Golden Emperor was killed in 532PA, the Ashcult stood at the heart of the rule of Tal.</p> <h2>The Osafar and the Ngalas</h2> <p>Osafar is the title of the leader and high priest of the Ashcult, the Voice of Ash, who sits in the Great Kiln. He is not always a Ngalas, but every Ngalas has been made Osafar. A Ngalas is a reincarnation of the Prophet of Sochor, known by the fire within: he seems unable to be wounded, glows from within and gives off a heat as if he were burning alive. Four Ngalas are counted since the Prophet himself: Chusfun, Juzoko, Nugen and the present Osafar, <a href="Hien-Ro" class="internal-link">Hien-Ro</a>. A Ngalas is found in childhood and raised to the office when it falls vacant, as Hien-Ro was on the death of his predecessor Pyon Meley, who was not a Ngalas. Chusfun was raised at once, for there had been no Osafar before him. When no Ngalas lives, the Elders of Ash choose the Osafar from among the priests of the Kiln.</p> <h2>Orders</h2> <p>Beneath the Osafar the Elders of Ash, a council of senior priests who live in the Kiln, advise him and keep its rites. The ordinary priests tend the temples of Tal and the Purple Sea and hand out the ash that is carried from the Kiln. Acolytes go out on their personal pilgrimages, and those who finish them may be ordained as Judges, who wander the world giving judgement by the law of the faith and by the Coin, the ceremonial coin through which the priests channel the power of their god. The only armed order of the church are the Brandgivers, the guardians of the Great Kiln and its priests.</p> <h2>Influence</h2> <p>The worldly influence of the Ashcult died with the Golden Empire, and the Osafar have been of little account in politics for a long time. Instead many temples of the Ashcult have risen across the Purple Sea and Tal, though all pay ceremonial homage to the Kiln and the Osafar. Hien-Ro is the first Osafar in a long time to seek a part in politics. Unlike Pyon Meley he has declared the New World of <a href="Sirenna" class="internal-link">Sirenna</a> the destined paradise of the children of Sochor, and he has called on all devout worshippers to support any undertaking that would douse the southern continent in the holy ashes of the god. How many of the kings and queens of Tal will follow him is not yet clear.</p>
