<p>The Pastelliora is a group of financiers and merchants independent from any specific nation, operating mostly in <a href="Cevolia" class="internal-link">Cevolia</a>, Ur & <a href="Sertova" class="internal-link">Sertova</a>. Their primary concerns are the flow of trade and wealth across south-western Ilrakhan and the investment opportunities overseas discoveries have brought with them, as such not only do they employ a variety of mercenary bands but also have directly sponsored many voyages and explorers attempting to find new and safer naval routes. A relatively new association, they have yet to come into conflict with any major political power and rely almost entirely on soft power and mercantile influence. </p>
<p>The Pastelliora has been in active cooperation with the <a href="Excavators Guild of Butera" class="internal-link">Excavators Guild</a> and has even considered the re settlement of the <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">rakhi</a> coast as a potential investment opportunity.</p>
<p>The Pastelliora have found their greatest fortune in the new world in the <a href="Narzoi Slavetrade" class="internal-link">Narzoi Slavetrade</a>. They have entrenched themselves in a number of its slave routes, and the profits have made them impossibly rich. They have also begun the construction of farms and plantations in the new world that are worked by slaves, binding their wealth to the trade far more firmly than any single cargo could. Stadtholder <a href="Jan van de Spiegel" class="internal-link">Jan van de Spiegel</a> of the Blijgrat League is rumoured to have connections to some of its members. It has not been free of risk for them, and in 926PA the pirate <a href="Kylian Juz" class="internal-link">Kylian Juz</a> seized a Cevoli vessel loaded with Narzoi slaves, a loss that struck hard at the profiteers behind the trade. Among its leading members is Gabriella Mile, the proprietor of the Battvian auction house Pireggi & Mile, whose plans for private plantations in the new world have raised questions as to the future of the association itself</p>
