<p>The Knights of the Gryphon were the foremost chivalric order of the <a href="Urland" class="internal-link">Kingdom of Urland</a> and one of the oldest continuously existing military institutions in the region of <a href="Ur" class="internal-link">Ur</a>, predating the kingdom itself, the Gethric conversion and in their foundational mythology even the Rakhian conquest that had first subjugated the Urien peoples three centuries before Urlands founding. Their heraldic symbol was a knight mounted on the back of a <a href="Gryphon" class="internal-link">Gryphon</a>, the same crest sported by Urbridge today.</p>

<h2>Origins</h2>

<p>The <a href="Gryphon" class="internal-link">Gryphon</a> has always been the apex predator of Ur and in 927PA, knights of the Order of Urbridge still ride out into the mountains on the rare and almost always fatal ambition of taming one. That they continue to do so reflects that the knights see themselves of descendants of people who actually accomplished the feat.</p>

<p>When the <a href="Rakh" class="internal-link">Empire of Rakh</a>moved against the tribes of the Ares and Urna in approximately 310BA, the surviving Rakhian sources recorded an encounter with "Urien barbarians on winged lions", creatures not native to southern Rakh, rendered in their accounts as something between wonder and terror. The warriors of the Ares tribe, fighting the most powerful empire on the continent from the hills and valleys they knew, had tamed Gryphons and ridden them into battle. Whether this was widespread practice or the achievement of a handful of extraordinary individuals whose legend was then amplified by terrified imperial scribes is not recoverable from the sources.</p>

<p>The formal institution of the Knights of the Gryphon emerged within the <a href="Arester" class="internal-link">Kingdom of Arester</a> in the centuries following the collapse of Rakhian rule, an order founded explicitly to embody and perpetuate the tradition of the Gryphon riders, its heraldry a direct reference to what their precursors had accomplished. The orders earliest members were Turvik in their religious practice, their oaths sworn to the old gods rather than to the Prophet. They were powerful enough within Arester to sometimes exceed the authority of local nobles, martial orders in this period derived institutional weight from their collective landholding, military capacity and the prestige of their membership, and the Knights of the Gryphon had all three in abundance. When Urland absorbed Arester, the Knights were accommodated rather than subordinated, too powerful and too prestigious to simply fold into the new kingdoms feudal hierarchy, they continued operating with the considerable autonomy that their preexisting status provided.</p>

<h2>The Gethric Transformation and Saint Dorian</h2>

<p>The Gethric conversion of Urland under King Edric I "the Zealous" in the early 4th century PA filtered gradually into the Knights of the Gryphon alongside the broader religious changes in the kingdom, but the orders theological character changed slowly and unevenly if at all, the Knights long resisted the religious ideals taking over. What ultimately transformed them was not the conversion itself but a man born three centuries later.</p>

<p><a href="Dorian" class="internal-link">Dorian the Kind</a>, born in 641PA to common Garmish stock, was a knight whose understanding of the Gethric faith produced what no cleric had quite managed: a coherent marriage of chivalric virtue and Gethric devotion in which the knightly vow was not merely a social contract between lord and vassal but a sacred oath whose weight before the Prophet equalled any act of worship. His theology was not abstract, it was expressed through the life of a warrior who held his blade and his faith to the same standard and expected everyone around him to do the same. When Dorian founded a the Chapter of the Shields of Summer within the Knights of the Gryphon he was planting something inside the order that grew until it defined the whole.</p> 
<p>The Shields of Summer Chapter attracted the orders most zealous Gethric members, accumulated its own lands and reputation, and by the time of Dorians death in 681PA had become the dominant faction within the Knights rather than a subordinate suborder. His canonisation that same year gave the chapter he had founded a patron Saint and the order as a whole a theological identity. The Knights of the Gryphon that existed in 690PA when the Hobgoblins emerged from the Kortopi Mountains were, in their spiritual character, a Dorianic institution. Their ancient heraldry and their foundational mythology of the Gryphon riders were unchanged while everything beneath it had been remade.</p>
