<p>This Othiri military organisation was formed in the aftermath of large scale border conflicts with Meadrath in the 8th century PA, it seeks to unite the people of the west against the growing influence of Meadi Imperialism and is less of a religious and more of a cultural institution. The Knights are based in the Fortress of Cherelais in southern <a href="Cimfon" class="internal-link">Cimfon</a> and have recently come under criticism for causing conflicts and distress with Halfling Cities in the <a href="Freelands" class="internal-link">Freelands</a>. The Knights of the Green Tree oversee and command various powerful magical artifacts such as the Red Blade and the Black Plate, they have an overall rather liberal attitude towards magic and various members use the arcane or divine while riding in plate armour.</p>
<p>The <a href="Meadi Othiri War" class="internal-link">Meadi-Othiri War</a> of 824 - 827PA was the defining crucible of the Knights of the Green Tree as an institution, and the event that transformed them from a classical chivalric order into the paramilitary organization they remain today. Founded in the aftermath of 8th century border conflicts with Meadrath to serve as Cimfons standing eastern defence, the Knights under Grand Master Lord Fionnor, known across Othirdon as Ebonfoil for his enchanted black armour that no blade had ever pierced, held the Cherelais Pass throughout the war's duration. Their full strength was committed only at the Battle of Red Oak, where they fought alongside Cimfons remaining nobility and the Lissvalley longbowmen in the engagement that ended the war.</p> 
<p>Fionnor himself killed Count Adalbert von Lindsfall "the Smaragd Knight" in single combat before both armies after von Lindsfall had cut down Mael Sidgwyn I in the melee, a duel that cemented Ebonfoils personal legend so thoroughly that his name long outlasted his own memory. He survived the battle, though accounts of his wounds vary considerably.</p> <p>Watching Cimfons conventional forces fail repeatedly in open engagement while the highland harassment succeeded, Fionnor spent the decades following Red Oak systematically rebuilding the order around the lessons of the campaign. The classical chivalric structure that had defined the Knights from their founding was progressively subordinated to a paramilitary model emphasizing mobility, terrain knowledge, and the capacity to sustain long term defensive operations along the Cimfon eastern border, what Fionnor himself reportedly described as "making the mountains do what the horses could not". The orders relationship with the Halfling communities of the Freelands, already complicated by longstanding disputes over land and sacred sites in the eastern highlands, did not improve during this period of institutional hardening. The Knights increasing identification as a specifically Locni defensive institution, oriented entirely against Meadi pressure from the east, left less room for the accommodation of foreign communities within their immediate vicinity than the Knights original Panrity foundations might have implied.</p>
